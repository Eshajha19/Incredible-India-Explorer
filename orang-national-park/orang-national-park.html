Orang National Park

Discover the "Mini Kaziranga" situated on the northern banks of the Brahmaputra River. Explore sprawling wetlands, floodplain grasslands, and a thriving population of rhinos and tigers.

Heritage & Ecosystem

History & Wetlands

Establishment

Established as a sanctuary in 1985 and declared a National Park in 1999. Orang holds a rich history and is an integral part of Assam's protected area network along the Brahmaputra.

Floodplain Grasslands

The park's geography mimics Kaziranga, featuring marshes, streams, and grasslands that flood seasonally, providing an incredibly fertile ecosystem for flora and fauna.

Tiger Reserve

Recognized as a Tiger Reserve in 2016, Orang boasts a high density of Bengal Tigers, thriving on the robust prey base found within the dense vegetation.

Fauna

Flagship Species

The iconic inhabitants of the Brahmaputra floodplains.

One-Horned Rhinoceros

Vulnerable

Orang contains a significant population of rhinos, carefully protected through intensive anti-poaching measures.

Endangered

Conservation

Protecting Orang

Preserving the intricate balance of the floodplain ecosystem requires ongoing effort and vigilance.

  • Anti-poaching Measures: Intense patrolling and surveillance to protect the rhino population.
  • Habitat Restoration: Managing invasive species and maintaining grassland health.
  • Community Participation: Engaging local communities in eco-tourism and wildlife protection.
  • Wildlife Monitoring: Utilizing camera traps and tracking for tiger and rhino populations.
Environment

Wetland Dynamics

The Brahmaputra River defines the life cycle of Orang National Park.

  • Seasonal Flooding: Annual floods deposit nutrient-rich silt, rejuvenating the grasslands.
  • Water Bodies: Numerous beels (oxbow lakes) act as breeding grounds for fish and birds.
  • Ecological Balance: The wetland acts as a natural sponge, preventing severe downstream flooding.
