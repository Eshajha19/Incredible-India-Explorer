RAMSAR WETLAND · AHMEDABAD, GUJARAT

Nalsarovar Bird Sanctuary

Gujarat's largest natural wetland and a dazzling haven for thousands of migratory birds, including flamingos and pelicans.

01 · Wetland of International Importance

Ramsar Site

Nalsarovar was designated a Ramsar Site in 2012, recognised as a wetland of international importance under the Ramsar Convention. It is Gujarat's largest natural wetland and a critical stopover on the Central Asian Flyway, supporting over 200 species of birds. The Ramsar designation highlights its global significance for migratory waterbird conservation and the ecosystem services it provides to local communities.

02 · Shallow Lake Ecosystem

Wetland Ecology

Nalsarovar is a shallow freshwater lake fed by the Sabarmati River system and monsoon rains. Its open waters, emergent vegetation, and surrounding marshes create a mosaic of habitats supporting diverse aquatic life. The lake's depth varies seasonally, creating exposed mudflats that attract wading birds, while submerged vegetation provides food for waterfowl and supports a rich fish population.

03 · Pink Visitors

Flamingos

Nalsarovar hosts thousands of greater and lesser flamingos during the winter months, painting the lake in stunning shades of pink. These iconic birds filter-feed on algae, crustaceans, and tiny invertebrates in the shallow waters. The flamingo population at Nalsarovar is among the largest in western India, making it a premier destination for wildlife enthusiasts and photographers.

04 · Spot-billed Beauties

Pelicans

The spot-billed pelican, a near-threatened species, is a regular visitor to Nalsarovar. These large waterbirds gather in flocks to feed on fish caught in their distinctive throat pouches. Nalsarovar provides crucial foraging and roosting habitat for pelicans during their migratory stay, alongside other waterbirds like cormorants, herons, and storks.

05 · On the Water

Boating

Rowboats and motorboats offer visitors a unique way to explore the lake and get close to the birdlife. Boating tours operate from the sanctuary's main entrance, taking visitors through channels lined with reeds and aquatic vegetation. Early morning and late afternoon safaris provide the best opportunities to observe birds in their natural behaviour while enjoying the serene landscape.

06 · Birder's Paradise

Bird Watching

Nalsarovar is a premier bird-watching destination with over 200 recorded species including migratory ducks, geese, cranes, and waders. Key species include the great white pelican, common crane, Eurasian wigeon, northern pintail, garganey, and the endangered sarus crane. The best season is November to March when winter migrants arrive in large numbers, transforming the lake into a vibrant avian spectacle.

09 · Did you know?

Interesting Facts

  • 🦩 Over 10,000 flamingos visit Nalsarovar every winter, creating a spectacular pink carpet across the lake.
  • 🇮🇳 Nalsarovar was designated as a Ramsar Site in 2012 and declared a Bird Sanctuary in 1969.
  • 🌊 The lake covers approximately 120 km² during the monsoon and shrinks to about 80 km² in summer.
  • 👁️ Nalsarovar is one of the few places in India where you can see both greater and lesser flamingos together.
  • 🌿 The surrounding Sanand and Viramgam tehsils depend on the wetland for groundwater recharge and livelihoods.
