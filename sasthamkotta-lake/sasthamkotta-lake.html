01 · Origins

History

Sasthamkotta Lake takes its name from the nearby Sasthamkotta Temple, dedicated to Lord Sastha (Ayyappa). Ancient texts and local folklore speak of the lake as a sacred water body blessed by the sage Parashurama. For centuries it has served as a vital freshwater reservoir for the Kollam region, sustaining settlements, agriculture, and trade along the Kerala coast.

Sasthamkotta is the largest freshwater lake in Kerala, fed entirely by rainfall and underground springs — it has no visible inlet or outlet. This unique hydrological feature keeps its waters exceptionally pure and free from salinity, making it a primary drinking water source for the Kollam district and surrounding areas.

03 · Wetland of International Importance

Ramsar Importance

Sasthamkotta Lake was designated a Ramsar Site in 2002, recognized as a wetland of international importance under the Ramsar Convention. Its unique ecosystem supports a rich diversity of aquatic life and provides critical ecosystem services including water purification, groundwater recharge, and flood regulation. The designation highlights the lake's global significance for biodiversity conservation and sustainable water management.

04 · Rich Aquatic Life

Biodiversity

The lake hosts a remarkable array of freshwater species including indigenous fish varieties such as channa, catfish, and carps. Its margins and surrounding wetlands provide habitat for numerous bird species including cormorants, herons, kingfishers, and migratory waterfowl. The aquatic vegetation and plankton communities form the base of a productive food web that supports the entire ecosystem.

05 · Protecting the Resource

Water Conservation

As the primary drinking water source for Kollam, Sasthamkotta Lake faces challenges from encroachment, siltation, and pollution. Conservation efforts led by the Kerala State Wetland Authority include catchment area protection, regulation of sand mining, desiltation programs, and community awareness initiatives. The lake's unique closed-basin hydrology makes it especially vulnerable to over-extraction and contamination.

06 · People of the Lake

Local Communities

Several villages around Sasthamkotta Lake depend on its waters for drinking, irrigation, and fishing. The lake holds deep cultural and religious significance, with the nearby Sasthamkotta Temple drawing pilgrims who bathe in its holy waters. Local communities have traditionally practiced sustainable water use and now play a key role in conservation and monitoring efforts.

09 · Did you know?

Interesting Facts

  • 💧 Sasthamkotta is the only lake in Kerala with no visible inlet or outlet — it is fed entirely by underground springs and rainfall.
  • 🇮🇳 It was designated a Ramsar Site in 2002, recognising its global importance as a freshwater wetland.
  • 🌿 The lake's water is so pure that it requires minimal treatment before being supplied as drinking water to Kollam city.
  • 🐟 Over 30 species of fish have been recorded in the lake, many of which are endemic to Kerala's freshwater systems.
  • 🏔️ The lake is surrounded by lush hills and rubber plantations, creating a picturesque landscape in the Kollam district.
