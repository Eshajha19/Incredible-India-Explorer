01 · High-Altitude Wetlands

Montane Wetlands

Montane wetlands are high-altitude ecosystems found above 1,800 m in the Western Ghats. They form in shallow depressions where rainfall and mist collect, creating saturated conditions that support unique plant and animal communities. Longwood Shola represents one of the finest examples of these montane wetlands, acting as natural sponges that release water slowly throughout the year and sustain the headwaters of perennial streams.

02 · Ancient Forest Patches

Shola Forest Ecosystem

Shola forests are stunted, dense tropical montane forests found in the sheltered valleys and slopes of the Nilgiris, Anamalais, and Palani hills. Characterised by gnarled trees draped in moss, lichens, and ferns, these forests thrive in the misty highlands. The Shola-grassland matrix creates a mosaic of habitats where the wetland pockets collect and filter rainwater, recharging the underground aquifers that feed rivers across Tamil Nadu and Kerala.

03 · Found Nowhere Else

Endemic Species

The Shola wetlands harbour a wealth of endemic species adapted to the cool, humid conditions. The Nilgiri tahr (Nilgiritragus hylocrius), Nilgiri langur (Semnopithecus johnii), and Nilgiri pipit (Anthus nilghiriensis) are among the iconic fauna. Endemic plants include Impatiens species (balsams), orchids like Habenaria, and Strobilanthes—many of which are found only in isolated Shola fragments.

04 · Protecting the Catchment

Water Conservation

Shola wetlands are critical water towers for the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve. Their thick leaf litter and sponge-like soils absorb monsoon rains, releasing water steadily through the dry months. Conservation efforts focus on preventing habitat fragmentation, controlling invasive species like wattle and eucalyptus, restoring native Shola vegetation, and maintaining the hydrological integrity of these montane wetland systems for downstream communities.

05 · Richness in Isolation

Biodiversity

The Shola-grassland-wetland complex supports extraordinary biodiversity. Over 150 species of birds, 30+ mammal species, and countless amphibians, reptiles, and insects have been recorded. The wetlands are breeding grounds for endemic frogs and dragonflies, while the surrounding Shola canopy provides refuge for the endangered lion-tailed macaque and the Malabar giant squirrel. The floral diversity includes rhododendrons, laurels, and myrtles unique to these high-altitude refugia.

08 · Did you know?

Interesting Facts

  • 🌧️ Shola forests act as cloud-traps, capturing moisture from passing mists and funneling it into the ground.
  • 🌱 The word "Shola" is derived from the Tamil word cōlai, meaning a dense, evergreen thicket.
  • 🐸 Several endemic amphibian species, including the Nilgiri wart frog, rely exclusively on Shola wetland pools for breeding.
  • 💧 A single hectare of Shola forest can hold up to 2 million litres of water in its soil and leaf litter.
  • 🌍 The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, home to Longwood Shola, is part of the Western Ghats — a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the world's eight hottest biodiversity hotspots.
