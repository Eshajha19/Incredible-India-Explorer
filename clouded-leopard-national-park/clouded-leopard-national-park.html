Clouded Leopard National Park

Venture into the dense, misty tropical evergreen forests of Tripura. Discover India's only national park dedicated to the elusive, arboreal Clouded Leopard.

Explore Wildlife View Map
Ecosystem

Forests & Avifauna

Evergreen Ecosystem

The park boasts thick tropical evergreen and semi-evergreen forests, intertwined with rich bamboo and cane brakes, crucial for carbon storage.

Birdwatching Paradise

A haven for birders, supporting over 150 species including Hornbills, Green Pigeons, Barbets, and numerous migratory birds.

Conservation History

Established within the Sipahijala Wildlife Sanctuary in 2007, it represents a crucial milestone in protecting Northeast India's unique biodiversity corridor.

Fauna

Mammals of the Canopy

Discover the secretive wildlife adapted to the dense foliage.

Clouded Leopard

Vulnerable

An incredibly elusive, arboreal cat with striking cloud-like spots. It possesses the longest canine teeth relative to skull size of any feline.

Capped Langur

Vulnerable

A distinctive primate often seen traversing the high forest canopy in troops.

Barking Deer (Muntjac)

Least Concern

Small, solitary deer known for their sharp, bark-like alarm calls when predators are near.

Slow Loris

Endangered

A rare, nocturnal primate with large eyes, relying on stealth and toxic secretions for defense.

Trekking

Nature Trails

Experience the forest closely through guided walks.

  • Guided Walks: Explore the complex layers of the evergreen forest on foot with experienced forest guards.
  • Birdwatching: Early morning trails offer the best chances to spot rare hornbills and barbets.
  • Best Season: Winter (November to March) provides the most pleasant climate for extended trekking.
Conservation

Protecting the Habitat

Dedicated efforts to preserve Tripura's ecological heritage.

  • Camera Trapping: Essential for monitoring the secretive Clouded Leopard population.
  • Habitat Restoration: Active management of bamboo and cane ecosystems to support native primate species.
  • Community Eco-tourism: Engaging local communities to reduce dependency on forest resources.
Facts

Did You Know?

This is the only national park in India specifically named after the Clouded Leopard.

The Clouded Leopard is the State Animal of Tripura.

The park is an essential wildlife corridor connecting various habitats in the Northeast Indian bio-geographic zone.
