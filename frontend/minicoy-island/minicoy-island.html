Minicoy Island

Geographically and culturally distinct from the rest of Lakshadweep, Minicoy is home to a historic lighthouse, a thriving tuna fishing tradition, a large lagoon and the Mahl-speaking community with close ties to the Maldives.

~4.8 sq km Total Land Area
1885 Lighthouse Built
Mahl Local Language
2nd Largest Island in Lakshadweep

🗼 Lighthouse

The Minicoy Lighthouse, built by the British in 1885, stands as one of the oldest and tallest lighthouses in Lakshadweep. It has long guided ships navigating the busy sea lanes near the Nine Degree Channel.

Visitors who climb its spiral staircase are rewarded with sweeping views of the island's palm groves, lagoon and surrounding reef, making it one of Minicoy's most recognisable landmarks.

  • Built by the British in 1885
  • One of the oldest and tallest lighthouses in Lakshadweep
  • Offers panoramic views of the island and lagoon

🐟 Tuna Fishing

Tuna fishing is central to Minicoy's economy and identity, with local fishermen traditionally using the pole-and-line method — a selective technique that targets tuna one at a time without large nets.

Processed and dried tuna, known locally as "Maas," is both a staple food and an important trade product, connecting Minicoy's fishing traditions to those of the nearby Maldives.

  • Traditional pole-and-line tuna fishing
  • Tuna fishing is the backbone of the local economy
  • Dried tuna ("Maas") is a staple food and trade product

🗣️ Mahl Language

Unlike the rest of Lakshadweep, where Malayalam is widely spoken, Minicoy's people speak Mahl, a language closely related to Dhivehi, the national language of the Maldives.

This linguistic difference reflects Minicoy's closer historical and cultural ties to the Maldives compared to the northern islands of Lakshadweep, giving the island a distinct identity within the Union Territory.

  • Mahl is closely related to Dhivehi, spoken in the Maldives
  • Distinct from Malayalam, spoken elsewhere in Lakshadweep
  • Reflects Minicoy's historical ties to the Maldives

🌊 Lagoons

Minicoy is home to one of the largest and most picturesque lagoons in Lakshadweep, stretching along the island's western side and enclosed by a long, curving reef.

The calm, shallow waters of the lagoon support water sports, traditional boat racing, and a rich variety of marine life, making it a centrepiece of daily life on the island.

  • One of Lakshadweep's largest lagoons
  • Enclosed by a long, curving reef
  • Supports water sports and traditional boat racing

🐠 Marine Biodiversity

The waters around Minicoy support a wide variety of marine life, including colourful reef fish, sea turtles, and healthy coral formations, thanks to the island's relatively undisturbed reef ecosystem.

The surrounding waters are also an important habitat for tuna and other pelagic fish species, underscoring the close link between the island's biodiversity and its fishing-based economy.

  • Rich coral reefs and colourful reef fish
  • Sea turtles found in the surrounding waters
  • Important habitat for tuna and other pelagic fish

🎎 Culture

Minicoy's culture is shaped by its seafaring traditions, with the island organised into traditional wards called "Avahs," each with its own community hall and identity, reflecting a matrilineal social structure unique within Lakshadweep.

Traditional dance forms like Lava and Bandiya, performed during festivals, along with distinctive boat-building skills, highlight Minicoy's cultural closeness to Maldivian traditions.

  • Island organised into traditional wards called "Avahs"
  • Matrilineal social structure, distinct within Lakshadweep
  • Traditional Lava and Bandiya dances performed at festivals
