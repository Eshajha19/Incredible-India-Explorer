Language Origins & Script

A Tibeto-Burman Legacy

The Mizo language (also known historically as Lushai) belongs to the Kukish branch of the Tibeto-Burman family of languages. It is the principal language of the Mizo people, predominantly spoken in the Indian state of Mizoram, as well as in parts of Manipur, Tripura, and neighboring countries.

Writing System: Mizo uses a Roman-based orthography rather than claiming an indigenous script. The alphabet was established by Christian missionaries, J.H. Lorrain and F.W. Savidge, in the late 19th century. The orthography has since evolved and is standardized to effectively represent the tonal nature and unique phonetics of the Mizo language.

📚 Mizo at a Glance

  • Classification: Sino-Tibetan > Tibeto-Burman > Kukish > Mizo.
  • Script: Roman script (Latin alphabet).
  • Native Name: Mizo ṭawng.
  • Region: Mizoram, India.

Cultural Heritage

Mizo Literature & Culture

The transition from an oral tradition to a written literature began in the late 19th century. Early Mizo literature consisted primarily of folk tales, songs, and translated religious texts. Today, Mizo boasts a vibrant literary culture encompassing poetry, fiction, and essays that capture the ethos of the Mizo people.

Mizoram's rich culture is deeply interwoven with its language. Traditional festivals such as Chapchar Kut and vibrant folk dances like Cheraw (the bamboo dance) are celebrated with songs and poetry composed in Mizo ṭawng, keeping the linguistic heritage alive and thriving.
