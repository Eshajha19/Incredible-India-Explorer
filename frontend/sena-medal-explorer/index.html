📜 History of the Sena Medal

The Sena Medal (literally, the "Army Medal") was instituted on 26 January 1960 by the Government of India with the approval of the President of India. It was created to recognise such individual acts of exceptional devotion to duty or courage as have special significance for the Army — acts of great merit that fall just below the threshold of the higher peacetime gallantry awards such as the Ashoka Chakra.

The medal was part of the post-independence reorganisation of India's honours system, which set out to indigenise decorations and retire colonial-era awards. It was gazetted alongside a family of service medals on 17 June 1960 — the Vishisht Seva Medal series, the Sainya Seva Medal, the Videsh Seva Medal, the Nao Sena Medal (Navy) and the Vayu Sena Medal (Air Force). The Sena Medal is thus the Indian Army's counterpart to the Navy and Air Force service gallantry medals.

The first Sena Medals were conferred in 1961, and the award has been announced twice every year since — on Republic Day (26 January) and Independence Day (15 August).

The 1999 Bifurcation

Originally a single decoration, the Sena Medal was bifurcated from 1 February 1999 into two distinct classes to reflect the nature of the act being recognised: the Sena Medal (Gallantry) for courageous acts in the face of the enemy or counter-insurgency firefights, and the Sena Medal (Devotion to Duty) for exceptional non-gallantry service. From the same date, the Government instituted a monthly stipend (initially ₹250, later revised to around ₹2,000) for recipients of the gallantry variant.

Growth & Legacy

Over six decades, the Sena Medal has become one of the most frequently conferred honours of the Indian Army, rewarding everything from close-quarter counter-terrorism engagements in Jammu & Kashmir and the North-East to sustained leadership at extreme altitudes along the Siachen Glacier and the Line of Actual Control. A bar is authorised for every subsequent award, with the first bar to the Sena Medal (Gallantry) conferred in 2012.

📋 Eligibility Criteria

The Sena Medal is awarded for "such individual acts of exceptional devotion to duty or courage as have special significance for the Army." It may be awarded for gallantry or for distinguished service performed by a soldier not in the face of the enemy, which gives the medal a dual character — part gallantry decoration, part general commendation for the Army.

🪖 Who Can Receive It

All ranks of the Indian Army — officers, junior commissioned officers, and other ranks — including personnel of the regular forces, reserve commissions, and the Territorial Army.

⚔️ Gallantry Acts

Direct acts of courage and valour involving personal risk to life, such as decisive actions in counter-insurgency firefights, ambushes, and close-quarter operations against militants.

🛡️ Devotion to Duty

Sustained exceptional service in non-combat roles — prolonged leadership at high-altitude posts, engineering feats under fire, and meritorious operational support duties.

🌙 Peacetime Character

Wartime exploits in declared conflicts fall under the higher gallantry series (Param Vir Chakra, Maha Vir Chakra, Vir Chakra). The Sena Medal principally honours peacetime and low-intensity operations.

🕯️ Posthumous Awards

The medal may be conferred posthumously when the qualifying act itself demonstrates exceptional valour or devotion — typically around a tenth of annual awards honour fallen soldiers.

💰 Gallantry Stipend

Recipients of the gallantry variant receive a monthly allowance from the Central Government — set at ₹250 in 1999 and revised to around ₹2,000 per month, with a like amount for each bar.

Exclusions & Limits

  • Army only: Civilians, and personnel of the Navy, Air Force, and paramilitary organisations are not eligible — the Navy and Air Force have their own equivalent medals.
  • Individual acts: The award recognises individual acts of exceptional significance to the Army, not routine performance of standard duties.
  • Bars: Each subsequent award of the Sena Medal attracts an additional bar on the ribbon.

🏅 Medal Design & Insignia

Cast in silver, the Sena Medal is a restrained, martial design that has remained unchanged since 1960. Recipients append the post-nominal letters SM after their names.

🪙

The Medal

A circular silver medal of about 35 mm diameter. The obverse bears a bayonet pointing upwards — a symbol of military readiness and the soldier's blade. It is suspended from a straight-bar suspender and is named and dated on the edge.

🛡️

The Reverse

The reverse depicts a standing soldier on sentry duty, with the legend "सेना मेडल" (Sena Medal) in Hindi along the upper rim — honouring the ordinary soldier the award exists to celebrate.

🎗️

The Ribbon

The 32 mm ribbon is crimson red with a single thin central silver-grey stripe — red 15 mm, central stripe 2 mm, red 15 mm. The full-size medal is worn on the left breast; miniatures accompany mess dress, and a ribbon bar serves for everyday uniform.

➖

Bars & Post-Nominals

A bar is attached to the ribbon for every subsequent award of the medal. In formal lists, recipients are denoted by the post-nominal SM, and multiple awards by repeated bars.

Specifications at a Glance

  • Metal: Silver, circular, approx. 35 mm diameter
  • Obverse: A bayonet, pointed upwards
  • Reverse: A standing soldier with the Hindi legend "सेना मेडल" above
  • Ribbon: 32 mm wide — red, with a thin central silver-grey stripe
  • Suspension: Straight-bar suspender; named and dated on the edge
  • Subsequent awards: A bar on the ribbon for each further award

🎖️ Types of Awards

Since the 1999 revision, the Sena Medal is conferred in two variants that reflect the nature of the act honoured, together with a bar for repeated awards.

SM(G)

⚔️ Sena Medal (Gallantry)

Awarded for acts of gallantry in the face of the enemy or in counter-insurgency operations — firefights, ambushes, and high-risk operations where the soldier displays conspicuous personal courage. This is the more celebrated variant, and it qualifies the recipient for the monthly gallantry stipend.

SM(D)

🪖 Sena Medal (Devotion to Duty)

Awarded for exceptional devotion to duty not in the face of the enemy — sustained leadership at extreme altitudes, exemplary engineering and support under hostile conditions, and meritorious service of special significance to the Army. It serves as the Army's highest general commendation.

BAR

➖ Bar to Sena Medal

Every subsequent award of the Sena Medal — whether gallantry or devotion to duty — adds a bar to the existing ribbon, worn above the medal. The first bar to the Sena Medal (Gallantry) was conferred in 2012, and bars remain a mark of repeated valour.

Before 1999: The Unified Medal

Prior to 1 February 1999, a single Sena Medal served both purposes — gallantry and distinguished service — without distinction on the medal itself. The bifurcation brought greater clarity and allowed the Government to attach the monthly stipend to the gallantry class.

⚙️ Selection Process

The Sena Medal follows the standard chain-of-command route of Indian Army honours, designed to be rigorous, decentralised at initiation, and centrally validated before Presidential approval.

1

Unit Recommendation

The Commanding Officer of the concerned unit or formation initiates the process, recommending personnel on the basis of acts of exceptional devotion to duty or courage.

2

Chain of Command

Recommendations move up through the chain of command — brigade, division, and corps headquarters — each level scrutinising the facts and endorsing deserving cases.

3

Army HQ Scrutiny

Army Headquarters validates each case against the eligibility criteria, confirming that the act holds special significance for the Army and is not routine duty.

4

Approval & Announcement

Approved lists are submitted for final sanction and are announced twice a year — on Republic Day (26 January) and Independence Day (15 August).

5

Investiture

The medal, citation, and warrant are presented by the President of India or an authorised representative at Rashtrapati Bhavan or at designated field locations.

6

Posthumous Conferment

Where the award is made posthumously, the honours are presented to the next of kin at the investiture, honouring the fallen soldier's sacrifice.

Assessment Focus

  • Exceptional nature: The act must go beyond the normal call of a soldier's duty
  • Special significance: The act must hold particular importance for the Indian Army
  • Personal risk: For gallantry — direct engagement with danger to life
  • Sustained merit: For devotion to duty — prolonged, exemplary service rather than a single gesture
  • Verification: Factual scrutiny at every level of the chain of command

From the frozen heights of Siachen to the dense forests of Jammu & Kashmir, the Sena Medal has honoured soldiers of every rank. Below are some of the most notable recipients across the years.

Major Saurabh Suyal

Bar to Sena Medal (Gallantry) · 2012

Recipient of the first Bar to the Sena Medal (Gallantry), awarded in 2012 for repeated acts of bravery across operations.

Ladakh Scouts officer honoured for distinguished service in high-altitude operations along the Line of Actual Control in Ladakh during tense border standoffs.

Impact of the Honour

  • Announced twice a year on Republic Day and Independence Day, with dozens of awards each cycle
  • In 2025 alone, 58 Army personnel received the Sena Medal (Gallantry) on Independence Day
  • Around a tenth of annual Sena Medals are conferred posthumously — testament to close-quarter valour
  • Recipients of the gallantry variant draw a monthly stipend from the Central Government
  • The medal serves as the Indian Army's equivalent of the Nao Sena Medal (Navy) and Vayu Sena Medal (Air Force)

🕰️ Interactive Timeline

Trace the evolution of the Sena Medal from its institution in 1960 to the present day.

1960

Institution. The Sena Medal is instituted on 26 January 1960 with Presidential approval and gazetted on 17 June 1960, alongside the Vishisht Seva Medal series, Sainya Seva Medal, Videsh Seva Medal, Nao Sena Medal and Vayu Sena Medal.

1961

First Conferments. The first Sena Medals are awarded, marking the start of the Army's own modern gallantry tradition.

1999

Bifurcation & Stipend. From 1 February 1999 the medal splits into Sena Medal (Gallantry) and Sena Medal (Devotion to Duty). The Government introduces a monthly stipend — initially ₹250 — for gallantry recipients.

2012

First Bar. Major Saurabh Suyal receives the first Bar to the Sena Medal (Gallantry), formalising the recognition of repeated valour.

2017

Stipend Revised. The monthly gallantry stipend is revised upward to around ₹2,000, with an equivalent amount attached to each bar.

2020s

Modern Operations. The medal continues to be conferred in volume for counter-insurgency operations and high-altitude border missions, including the 2023–26 cycles honouring soldiers along the Line of Actual Control and in Jammu & Kashmir.

Today

Ongoing Legacy. With dozens of awards announced every Republic Day and Independence Day, the Sena Medal remains the Indian Army's most personal badge of exceptional devotion to duty and courage.
