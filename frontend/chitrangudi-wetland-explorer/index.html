Chitrangudi Wetland

Known locally as "Kanamoovadi", this vital bird sanctuary in Tamil Nadu is a haven for nesting and migratory birds.

A Historic Bird Sanctuary

Chitrangudi Bird Sanctuary, locally revered as "Kanamoovadi" (meaning a place where birds nest), is located in the Ramanathapuram district of Tamil Nadu. Established as a protected area in 1989, it encompasses a traditional irrigation tank surrounded by lush vegetation.

Ramsar Recognition

In recent years, Chitrangudi has been recognized under the Ramsar Convention, highlighting its international importance as a wetland that supports critical biodiversity and local hydrological cycles.

Wetland Ecology & Habitat

The sanctuary is characterized by a large, shallow tank that fills during the Northeast Monsoon. The surrounding bunds are lined with ancient trees, primarily Tamarind, Fig, and Silk Cotton, which provide essential nesting sites for colonial waterbirds.

Tamarind Groves

Massive, ancient tamarind trees line the water's edge, offering dense canopy cover and sturdy branches for large nesting colonies.

Silk Cotton Trees

Bombax ceiba trees bloom in the dry season, providing nectar for birds and insects, and their tall structure serves as lookout points for raptors.

Bird Diversity & Nesting

Chitrangudi is famous for its nesting populations of large waterbirds. The sanctuary comes alive between October and March when migratory species arrive to breed alongside resident birds.

Conservation & Community

The survival of Chitrangudi is deeply intertwined with the local communities. Traditional water management practices ensure the tank retains water long enough for the birds to complete their breeding cycles.

  • Local fishers practice sustainable harvesting, avoiding disturbance to nesting zones.
  • The Forest Department maintains the bunds and prevents invasive species from choking the water body.
  • Eco-tourism initiatives provide alternative livelihoods, incentivizing the protection of the bird colonies.
