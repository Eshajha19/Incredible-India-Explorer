✒️ Writing system

Devanagari script

Kumaoni is written in Devanagari, the same script as Hindi and Sanskrit. Older Kumaoni manuscripts and inscriptions also used a distinct regional Kumaoni script, a Devanagari variant now largely out of everyday use.

The word कुमाऊँनी (Kumaoni) breaks into कुमाऊँ — the Kumaon region — plus the suffix -नी, meaning "belonging to." The candra-bindu (ँ) nasalises the vowel, a sound common across Pahari languages.

Common Kumaoni words

🎭 Culture & literature

A language sung as much as spoken

Aipan folk art

White rice-paste patterns painted on a red ochre (geru) ground decorate thresholds and courtyards for festivals and rites of passage — geometric motifs that echo Kumaoni oral tradition's love of pattern and repetition.

Jhoda & Chholiya

Circular jhoda folk dances and the sword-dance Chholiya are performed at weddings and harvest festivals, sung in Kumaoni verse passed down through generations.

Written literature

Gumani Pant is widely regarded as the first Kumaoni poet of note, and later writers built a small but growing body of Kumaoni prose, poetry, and radio drama.

Jagar oral epics

Jagar ballads — sung invocations to local deities and ancestral spirits — preserve Kumaoni history and myth through overnight ritual performance.

UNESCO's Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger lists Kumaoni as vulnerable / unsafe — still spoken by many, but under pressure from Hindi in schools, media, and cities, which makes projects like this one part of active conservation.
