Why Kheda became a test of satyagraha

01 · CAUSE

Agricultural distress

Crop failure and poor harvest conditions placed cultivators under severe financial pressure while land-revenue demands remained a central grievance.

→
02 · PROTEST

Collective refusal

Peasants organized around the demand for revenue relief and pledged to withhold payment where the campaign's conditions were met.

→
03 · OUTCOME

Negotiation and relief

The campaign produced negotiations and concessions in revenue collection, becoming an important example of organized non-violent resistance.

Peasant grievances

  • Severe crop and agricultural distress
  • Difficulty meeting land-revenue demands
  • Demand for suspension or relief in collection
  • Need for collective action rather than isolated petitions

Outcome and historical significance

Negotiation and revenue relief

The campaign demonstrated how disciplined collective action could bring a revenue dispute into negotiation. Its immediate results are best understood in the context of revenue collection arrangements and the government's willingness to accommodate cultivators facing hardship.

Why it mattered

Kheda helped develop the practical methods of satyagraha: local organization, public pledges, non-violence, communication, volunteer networks and disciplined collective action. It also strengthened the political role of Vallabhbhai Patel and created experience later relevant to wider nationalist mobilization.
