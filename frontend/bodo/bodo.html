🌿 Language & Heritage

बड़ो Bodo

Discover the ancient Bodo language, rich oral traditions, and cultural heritage of Assam's indigenous communities.

🗣️

Language Classification

Family: Sino-Tibetan (Bodo-Garo branch)

Region: Assam, West Bengal, Meghalaya

Script: Devanagari (official)

Speakers: ~1.5 million+

Status: Official language of Assam

📖

Cultural Heritage

Folk Music: Boro songs, Kherai music

Folk Dance: Bagurumba, Bardwi Sikhla

Festivals: Bwisagu (Bihu), Domashi

Oral Traditions: Folk tales, epics, proverbs

Traditional Attire: Dokhona, Jwmgra

✍️ Writing System

Devanagari Script

Bodo is officially written in the Devanagari script, with efforts to preserve and promote the language.

🔤

Devanagari Script

Bodo uses the Devanagari script with specific diacritics to represent its unique sounds and phonemes.

बड़ो Bodo
📜

Historical Scripts

Historically, Bodo was written using the Assamese script and the Roman script, before Devanagari was adopted officially.

বড়ো Bodo (Assamese script)
🌍

Roman Script Usage

The Roman script is also used in informal contexts, especially in digital communication and among younger generations.

Bodo Bodo (Roman)

"ख'रोम" Khorom "Hello / How are you?"

📚 Literary Heritage

Literature & Culture

Explore the rich literary traditions and cultural contributions of the Bodo community.

📝

Oral Literature

Bodo has a rich tradition of oral literature including folk tales, myths, legends, and proverbs passed down through generations.

🎵

Folk Music

Traditional Bodo folk music includes songs about nature, love, and daily life, often performed during festivals and community gatherings.

💃

Folk Dance

The Bagurumba dance is the most famous Bodo folk dance, performed during celebrations with colorful traditional attire and rhythmic movements.

📖

Modern Literature

Contemporary Bodo literature includes poetry, short stories, and novels exploring themes of identity, culture, and social change.

📍 Regional Information

Region Assam & Surrounding Areas
Major Districts Kokrajhar, Baksa, Udalguri, Chirang
Cultural Centers Kokrajhar, Bodoland Territorial Region
Neighboring Regions West Bengal, Meghalaya, Bhutan

Bodo is primarily spoken in the Bodoland Territorial Region of Assam, with speakers also found in parts of West Bengal and Meghalaya.
