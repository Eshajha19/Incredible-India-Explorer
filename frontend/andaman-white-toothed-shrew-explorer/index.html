Andaman White-toothed Shrew

Crocidura andamanensis - A rare endemic mammal of the Andaman Islands.

Introduction & Scientific Name

The Andaman White-toothed Shrew (Crocidura andamanensis) is a highly elusive, nocturnal mammal endemic to the Andaman Islands. As its name suggests, it belongs to the genus Crocidura, characterized by its unpigmented, white teeth.

Classification

  • Kingdom: Animalia
  • Phylum: Chordata
  • Class: Mammalia
  • Order: Eulipotyphla
  • Family: Soricidae
  • Genus: Crocidura
  • Species: C. andamanensis

Distribution & Habitat

Endemic to South Andaman Island. It inhabits primary and secondary tropical evergreen forests, typically residing in the dense leaf litter and undergrowth where moisture levels are high.

Behaviour & Diet

Like most shrews, it has an incredibly high metabolic rate and must eat constantly to survive. It is a voracious insectivore, feeding on ants, beetles, larvae, and other small soil-dwelling invertebrates. It is solitary and highly territorial.

Reproduction

Little is known about its specific breeding habits in the wild. However, members of the Crocidura genus typically have short gestation periods and give birth to small litters of 2 to 5 altricial young.

Interesting Facts

  • It was thought to be extinct for decades until a population was rediscovered in the early 2000s.
  • Its white teeth are an evolutionary trait distinguishing it from the red-toothed shrews (Soricinae) found in colder climates.

Conservation Status & Threats

Classified as Critically Endangered by the IUCN. Its highly restricted range makes it extremely vulnerable to habitat loss. Major threats include deforestation for agriculture, logging, and the introduction of feral cats and dogs.

Protected Areas

Its known habitat falls within the protected forests of the South Andaman Forest Division, including areas near Mount Harriet National Park.
