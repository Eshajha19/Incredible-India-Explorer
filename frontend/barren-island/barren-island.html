Barren Island

An uninhabited volcanic island in the Andaman Sea, home to India's only confirmed active volcano — a stark, ash-covered landscape ringed by rich coral reefs and deep offshore marine life.

🌋 Volcano & Eruptions

Barren Island is home to the only confirmed active volcano in South Asia, rising to 354 m above sea level from a caldera formed by earlier eruptions. Its first recorded eruption dates to 1787, and it has erupted more than a dozen times since, with recent activity in 1991, 1994–95, 2005 (shortly after the Indian Ocean tsunami), 2017, and most recently 2022.

Eruptions are typically Strombolian in style — periodic bursts of lava, ash and gas rather than a single catastrophic explosion — and are monitored from a safe distance by the Geological Survey of India, since the island itself is uninhabited and landing is restricted.

🪨 Geological History

Barren Island sits along the Andaman-Sumatra subduction zone, where the Indian tectonic plate is pushed beneath the Burma microplate — the same geological setting that produces frequent seismic activity across the Andaman Islands. The volcano is part of the Alpide volcanic belt that extends from the Mediterranean through the Himalayas into Southeast Asia.

The island's terrain is dominated by a horseshoe-shaped caldera, roughly 2 km wide, open toward the west and formed by a prehistoric eruption that collapsed the original volcanic cone. A newer cone has since grown inside this caldera from repeated lava flows and ash deposits.

🐠 Marine Life

The waters surrounding Barren Island are considered among the best deep-sea diving sites in India, with steep volcanic drop-offs, black sand slopes and clear visibility that attract experienced divers.

  • Pelagic species including reef sharks, barracuda and occasional manta rays
  • Healthy coral growth on the island's submerged volcanic slopes
  • Schools of reef fish and macro life along the drop-offs
  • Dive access is permitted only with authorised operators from Port Blair; landing on the island itself is restricted

🦎 Wildlife

Barren Island's harsh, ash-covered terrain supports little vegetation and no permanent human population, but pockets of hardy plant life persist in the caldera's more sheltered areas alongside a small number of resilient land species.

  • Feral goats, descendants of animals introduced in the 19th century
  • Seabirds that nest along the island's rocky coastline
  • Sparse scrub vegetation adapted to volcanic ash and rocky soil
  • Rich offshore marine biodiversity along the coral-fringed slopes
