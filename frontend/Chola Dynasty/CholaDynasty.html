Historical Overview

The Chola Dynasty (c. 9th–13th centuries CE) emerged in southern India and became renowned for its strong maritime network, centralized administration, temple building and patronage of arts. At their height the Cholas maintained influence across South India, Sri Lanka and parts of Southeast Asia.

Rajendra I

Maritime expeditions and conquest up to the Ganges; established port at Gangaikonda Cholapuram.

Naval Expansion & Trade

The Cholas developed a powerful navy that protected merchant shipping, enabled overseas expeditions to Southeast Asia, and helped secure maritime trade routes for spices, textiles and precious goods.

The Chola navy was organized with local shipyards, naval officers, and state-supported merchant fleets.
Key ports included Puhar, Nagapattinam and ports on the Coromandel Coast linking to Southeast Asia.

Temple Architecture

Chola temple architecture is characterized by massive vimanas, intricate stone sculpture and well-planned temple complexes. The Brihadeeswara Temple at Thanjavur (built under Rajaraja I) stands as a UNESCO-recognized masterpiece.
