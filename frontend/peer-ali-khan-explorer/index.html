Biography

From bookseller to revolutionary organiser

Peer Ali Khan is remembered as a freedom fighter who worked clandestinely in Patna during the Revolt of 1857. The Government of India's Unsung Heroes profile describes him as a bookseller who played a leading role in organising the July 1857 rising in the Patna district. It also notes that his birthplace is uncertain because the surviving accounts do not agree.

Contemporary colonial records describe Peer Ali as a bookseller living in Patna and connect his house and correspondence with the planning of resistance. These records must be read critically because they were produced by the colonial authorities investigating and prosecuting the uprising.

What is clear across the surviving accounts is his central place in Patna's July 1857 resistance and his refusal to abandon the cause when captured.

The Revolt of 1857

Peer Ali Khan's contribution in Patna

Patna became an important centre of resistance in July 1857. Peer Ali's role connected local organising, communications and the preparation of a wider rising.

📜

Read the colonial record critically

Contemporary Commissioner William Tayler's account calls Peer Ali a leader of the outbreak and describes papers found at his house. Those descriptions are valuable primary evidence, but they also reflect the viewpoint of a colonial official investigating an uprising.
