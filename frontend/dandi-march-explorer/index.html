Why salt?

A daily necessity became a political symbol

British colonial salt laws restricted the production and sale of salt and imposed a tax that affected an everyday necessity. Gandhi chose salt because the issue could connect a national political struggle to ordinary people's lives.

The march was conceived as a disciplined act of non-violent resistance and became a major opening of the Civil Disobedience Movement in 1930.

Public participation

As the march progressed, people gathered for meetings, welcomed marchers and joined wider salt-law protests. The route therefore became a moving platform for public mobilization rather than simply a walk from one place to another.

Dandi and beyond

From salt law violation to national response

At Dandi

On 6 April 1930 Gandhi symbolically broke the salt law by producing salt from seawater. The act gave the Civil Disobedience Movement a simple, visible form that could be repeated by people elsewhere.

National response

Salt-making and salt-law defiance spread across India. Arrests, demonstrations and boycotts intensified, while the movement drew substantial international press attention.
