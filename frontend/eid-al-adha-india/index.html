Spiritual Roots

Significance & Spirit of Eid al-Adha

Honoring Prophet Ibrahim's unshakeable faith, selflessness, and total submission to Divine Will.

📖

Story of Prophet Ibrahim & Ismail

Eid al-Adha commemorates Prophet Ibrahim\'s willingness to sacrifice his beloved son Ismail in obedience to God\'s command. Recognizing his devotion, God substituted a ram, establishing an eternal symbol of devotion over material attachment.

🕋

Culmination of Hajj Pilgrimage

Celebrated on the 10th day of Dhu al-Hijjah, Eid al-Adha coincides with the completion of the annual Hajj pilgrimage in Mecca, where millions of Muslims from around the world stand together in unity at Mount Arafat.

🕊️

Lessons in Selflessness & Compassion

The true spirit of Eid al-Adha lies not in ritual alone, but in sacrificing ego, selfishness, and wealth for the greater good of society, ensuring that food and care reach the poorest communities.

Social Justice & Philanthropy

The 3-Part Charity Rule (Qurbani Values)

Islamic tradition mandates that the sacrificial meat is divided into three equal portions to eliminate hunger and spread joy.

Morning Congregations

Namaz-e-Eid at Historic Eidgah Grounds

On Eid morning, tens of thousands gather in open Eidgah grounds and historic mosques in fresh attire for special prayers.

01

Ghusl & Fragrance

Purifying bath, applying non-alcoholic natural Attar, and wearing clean Kurta-Pyjama, Sherwani, or Pathani suit.

02

Takbeer Chants

Reciting the sacred Takbeer (Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar...) on the way to the Eidgah ground.

03

Namaz & Khutbah

Offering two Raka\'at congregational prayers led by the Imam, followed by the sermon on sacrifice and morality.

04

Gale Milna & Eidi

Warm triple-embrace (Gale Milna) wishing "Eid Mubarak!", followed by distributing Eidi gifts to children.
