🏛️ Karnataka · c. 950 – 1343 CE

Hoysala Dynasty Explorer

Follow the tiger-slayer Sala from the misty hills of Malnad to the heart of the Deccan. Discover the dynasty that raised the star-shaped temples of Belur, Halebidu, and Somanathapura — where soapstone turned into lace and every pillar became a story.

From Malnad Hills to Empire

A Dynasty Born of a Legendary Tiger Slaying

According to legend, the Hoysala line began when the young Sala slew a tiger at the command of the Jain sage Sudatta near Angadi in the Malnad hills of Karnataka — and a fighting tiger (Sala-tiger) became the dynasty's royal crest. The Hoysalas rose from these Western Ghat foothills to rule the southern Deccan between roughly 950 and 1343 CE.

Their first capitals stood at Belur and Halebidu (Dwarasamudra). At the dynasty's height under Vishnuvardhana and Veera Ballala II, the Hoysalas controlled most of present-day Karnataka and parts of Tamil Nadu and Andhra, patronising Kannada and Sanskrit literature, Jain and Hindu faiths, and above all a brilliant school of temple architecture.

🦁 The Tiger-Slayer

  • c. 950–1343 CE — Nearly four centuries of Hoysala rule over the southern Deccan.
  • Malnad Origins — The dynasty emerged from the hill country of the Western Ghats.
  • Belur & Halebidu — Twin temple capitals, later joined by Somanathapura.
  • Royal Emblem — The "Sala-fighting-tiger" crest, struck on coins and carved on temple walls.
Kings & Conquerors

Major Rulers of the Hoysala Line

From the legendary founder to the emperors who built the great temples and defied the Delhi Sultanate.

🦁

Sala (Legendary Founder)

The mythic tiger-slayer who, according to the tradition, founded the dynasty and gave it its name and emblem.

🗺️

Nripa Kama II (c. 1006–1046)

Regarded as the first historical ruler, he established the Hoysala domain in the Malnad region.

👑

Vishnuvardhana (1108–1152)

The greatest conqueror-builder — he pushed the empire across the river Kaveri and commissioned the Chennakesava temple at Belur.

⚔️

Veera Ballala II (1173–1220)

Defeated the Western Chalukyas and Kalyani rivals to make the Hoysalas the dominant power of the southern Deccan.

🏗️

Narasimha III (1263–1292)

Patron of the Keshava temple at Somanathapura, built in 1268 by his minister Soma Dandanayaka.

🛡️

Veera Ballala III (1292–1342)

The last great king, who held the Delhi Sultanate at bay for decades before falling at the siege of Madurai in 1343.

🛕 Hallmarks of Hoysala Architecture

  • Star-Shaped Plan: Temples follow a multi-pointed stellated ground plan instead of the usual rectangle.
  • Soapstone Carving: Soft chloritic schist let sculptors cut lace-like detail that rivals ivory work.
  • Lathe-Turned Pillars: Cylindrical pillars polished in lathe-like detail, no two quite alike.
  • Sculpted Friezes: Bands of elephants, horses, and scenes from the epics girding the temple walls.
Masterpieces in Stone

The Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas

The Hoysalas perfected one of India's most ornate temple styles. In 2023, the "Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas" — the Chennakesava temple at Belur, the Hoysaleshwara temple at Halebidu, and the Keshava temple at Somanathapura — were inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Belur's Chennakesava temple (1117 CE) is famed for its lathe-turned pillars and the exquisitely carved bracket figures (Madanikas). Halebidu's Hoysaleshwara temple, begun around 1121 CE, carries thousands of carved figures in bands around its walls, while Somanathapura's star-shaped Keshava temple (1268 CE) is the most perfectly preserved of them all.

Milestones

Chronology of the Hoysala Dynasty

From a legendary tiger-slaying to the fall of Dwarasamudra.

c. 950

Origins in the Malnad

The Hoysalas emerge as chieftains in the Western Ghat hill country; the legendary founder Sala gives the dynasty its name.

1006–1098

Founding Kings

Nripa Kama II and Vinayaditya consolidate the Malnad domain, ruling as feudatories before asserting independence.

1117

Vishnuvardhana's Triumph

Defeat of the Cholas at Talakad extends Hoysala power south of the Kaveri; the Chennakesava temple is begun at Belur.

1173–1220

Zenith under Ballala II

Veera Ballala II crushes the Western Chalukyas and rises to the foremost power of the southern Deccan.

1268

Somanathapura

Minister Soma Dandanayaka builds the star-shaped Keshava temple, the finest of the late Hoysala works.

1310–1343

Resistance and Fall

Malik Kafur sacks Dwarasamudra in 1310; Veera Ballala III resists the Sultanate until his death at Madurai, ending the dynasty in 1343.
