Chittara Art

The sacred geometric folk paintings of Karnataka's Devaragudda village.

Historical Overview

Chittara is a traditional folk art form practiced by the Deewaru community in the Shimoga district of Karnataka, particularly in the village of Devaragudda. The word 'Chittara' translates to 'painting' or 'picture' in the local language. Historically, these intricate geometric patterns were painted on the walls of homes during auspicious occasions, weddings, and harvest festivals to invoke divine blessings.

Region of Origin

Deeply rooted in the Malnad region of Karnataka, the art form relies on the natural surroundings of the Western Ghats. The women of the community are the primary custodians of this art, passing down the complex geometric formulas from mother to daughter through oral tradition and hands-on practice.

Traditional Color Palette

Chittara art strictly uses natural, earth-derived pigments. The base is typically the red mud of the region, while the patterns are drawn using:

White (Rice Paste)
Brown (Red Earth/Mud)
Black (Roasted Rice Husk)
Yellow (Turmeric)
Red (Kumkum/Stone powder)

Materials Used

Artists use simple, locally sourced tools. The 'brushes' are often made from chewed bamboo sticks or fronds of the areca nut tree. The canvas is the freshly plastered mud wall of the home, prepared with a smooth layer of cow dung and clay.

Geometric Motifs

Unlike narrative art forms, Chittara is purely geometric and symbolic. The patterns are composed of interconnected lines, triangles, and squares that represent elements of nature and agriculture:

  • The Peacock (Savala): Highly stylized using triangles, symbolizing grace and the monsoon.
  • The Paddy Sheaf (Batta): Represents agricultural prosperity and the harvest.
  • The Temple Chariot (Ratha): Drawn during festivals to signify divine movement.
  • The Geometric Grid: Interlocking squares and diamonds that form the foundational 'net' of the universe.
