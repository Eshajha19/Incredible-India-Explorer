Irula இருளர் மொழி  /  ërḷa

Irula is an independent Southern Dravidian language, closely related to Old Tamil with features shared with Kannada, spoken by the forest-dwelling Irula community of the Nilgiri hill tracts.

Classification & script

Irula was long dismissed as a "broken" mix of Tamil and Kannada, until linguist Kamil Zvelebil demonstrated in 1955 that it is its own Southern Dravidian language.

Dravidian

It has no independent script of its own: in Tamil Nadu, Irula is written using the Tamil script; across the border in Kerala, speakers use the Malayalam script. Its sound system adds a set of centralized vowels (e.g. [ɨ ɘ æ ɵ ʉ]) not found in standard Tamil.

Where it's spoken

Irula speakers are concentrated around the Nilgiri hill tracts, at the point where three states meet.

Tamil Nadu

The largest Irula population, across the Nilgiris, Coimbatore, Erode and Periyar districts — roughly 190,000 people by the 2011 census.

Kerala

Communities in the Palakkad district, including the Attappady and Walayar hill tracts, where Irula is written in Malayalam script.

Karnataka

Smaller settlements near the Karnataka side of the Nilgiri–Western Ghats border region.

Cultural heritage

The Irula are traditionally a forest-dwelling community, and their language carries that world in its vocabulary and oral literature.

Forest livelihood

Historically, the Irula relied on the forest for a living — gathering non-timber forest products, honey collection, and traditional snake- and rat-catching, a skill still used to protect grain stores from crop-destroying rodents.

Oral tradition

Much of Irula culture survives as spoken word rather than text: folk songs, riddles, legends and dances, including the Malanadu sub-clan's rock paintings and performances like the Doddu Aatam (Gaur play).

Belief & identity

Most Irula follow Hindu practice alongside animist traditions centred on ancestral and guardian spirits; some communities have also adopted Christianity. Genetic studies have identified the Irula as a close proxy for the region's ancient ancestral population.

Status today

Recognised as a Scheduled Tribe in Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka, UNESCO lists Irula as "vulnerable" — spoken daily by elders and in villages, but under pressure as younger generations shift toward Tamil.
