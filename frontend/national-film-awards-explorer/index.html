📜 History of the National Film Awards

The National Film Awards are the most prestigious and honoured film awards in India. Established in 1954, they were conceived to recognise and encourage the production of films of "a high aesthetic and technical standard with educational and cultural value".

From State Awards to National Film Awards

The awards were initially called "State Awards", recommended by the Film Enquiry Committee of 1949. At the very first ceremony, held for the films of 1953, the President's Gold Medal for the All-India Best Feature Film went to the Marathi film Shyamchi Aai. The scheme was renamed the National Film Awards at the 15th ceremony in 1967.

Institutional Administration

From 1973 until 2020, the awards were administered by the Directorate of Film Festivals under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting. Since 2021, the National Film Development Corporation of India (NFDC) administers the ceremony, along with the International Film Festival of India and the Indian Panorama.

Presented by the President

All the awards are conferred by the Hon'ble President of India at a solemn ceremony — held on 3 May each year to commemorate the release of Raja Harishchandra (1913), India's first full-length feature film. The Dadasaheb Phalke Award, the lifetime achievement honour for Indian cinema, is presented alongside the National Film Awards.

Growth and Significance

What began with two President's Gold Medals, two certificates of merit, and silver medals for a dozen regional films has grown into a pan-Indian celebration that covers films made in more than 20 languages, from mainstream blockbusters to parallel cinema, documentaries, and writing on cinema.

🏆 Award Categories

The National Film Awards are given in three sections: Feature Films, Non-Feature Films, and Best Writing on Cinema. Each category carries a medallion — the Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus) for the most prestigious awards and the Rajat Kamal (Silver Lotus) for the rest — along with a certificate and a cash prize.

🎞️

Feature Films

Honours cinematic achievement in full-length films — direction, acting, music, lyrics, and technical crafts — plus regional awards for every major Indian language.

📽️

Non-Feature Films

Celebrates excellence in documentaries, short films, and animation — the most acclaimed being Best Non-Feature Film.

📝

Best Writing on Cinema

Encourages the study, appreciation, and critical understanding of cinema through books, articles, reviews, and film scholarship.

🌺

Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus)

The highest tier of the awards. Six feature-film categories — Best Feature Film, Best Director, Best Children's Film, Best Popular Film, Best Debut Film, and Best AVGC (Animation) — receive the Golden Lotus.

🥈

Rajat Kamal (Silver Lotus)

The second tier, given for Best Actor, Best Actress, all supporting roles, playback singing, music direction, technical crafts, and the regional-language film awards.

🏅

Special Awards

Includes the Dadasaheb Phalke Award (lifetime achievement), the Nargis Dutt Award for national integration, the Indira Gandhi Award for a debut film of a director, and the Most Film-Friendly State award.

Key Feature Film Categories

  • Best Feature Film — the President's Golden Lotus for the year's finest film
  • Best Director — a Golden Lotus honour for outstanding direction
  • Best Actor & Best Actress — the celebrated "Bharat" and "Urvashi" honours, now presented as Silver Lotus awards
  • Best Popular Film Providing Wholesome Entertainment — recognising mainstream cinema of national value
  • Best Debut Film of a Director — the Indira Gandhi Award for promising new filmmakers
  • Best Children's Film — celebrating films crafted for young audiences
  • Best Feature Film in each language — separate awards for films in the languages of Schedule VIII of the Constitution

🎬 Best Feature Film — The Golden Lotus

The Best Feature Film is the crowning honour of the National Film Awards, presented with the Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus), a certificate, and a cash prize (₹3,00,000 as of recent years). The producer of the winning film receives the award, and the film is screened at the annual International Film Festival of India.

Legendary director Satyajit Ray holds the record for the most wins — six of his films won the award: Pather Panchali, Apur Sansar, Charulata, Goopy Gyne Bagha Byne, Seemabaddha, and Agantuk.

A National Roll of Honour

The winners span every major film industry of India — from the debut of Pather Panchali that put Indian cinema on the world stage, to the pan-Indian phenomenon of Baahubali — a living archive of the country's cinematic excellence.

🎭 Acting Awards

Separate awards for actors and technicians were instituted in 1968 for the films of 1967. The first Best Actor and Best Actress awards went to Uttam Kumar and Nargis respectively — originally named the "Bharat" and "Urvashi" awards before being renamed.

🎭 Best Actor

The premier acting honour, presented with the Rajat Kamal (Silver Lotus). First won by Uttam Kumar (1967) for Antony Firingee and Chiriyakhana.

👩‍🎤 Best Actress

The premier actressing honour. First won by Nargis (1967) for Raat Aur Din — a performance remembered as a milestone of Indian cinema.

🤝 Supporting Roles

Best Supporting Actor and Best Supporting Actress recognise the essential craft of actors who elevate the ensemble.

🎶 Music & Singing

Best Male and Female Playback Singer, Best Music Director, and Best Lyricist celebrate the voices and melodies that define Indian films.

Technical Crafts: The Heart of Indian Cinema

From the golden-era craft of V. K. Murthy to contemporary visual-effects pioneers, the technical awards ensure every department of filmmaking — in front of and behind the camera — receives its rightful national recognition.

📋 Selection Process

Every year, entries are invited for films released in the previous year. The selection is designed to be transparent and independent — neither the government nor the administering organisation influences which films win.

1

Entries Invited

Films produced across the country in all Indian languages are entered. More than 100 feature and 100 non-feature films are deemed eligible each year.

2

Juries Constituted

The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting constitutes separate juries for the Feature Film, Non-Feature Film, and Best Writing on Cinema sections, comprising people distinguished in cinema, the arts, and humanities.

3

Two-Tier Screening

Since the 57th National Film Awards, the feature-film section follows a two-tier process: entries are first shortlisted by five regional panels, and the winners are then decided by a national panel.

4

Dadasaheb Phalke Award

The lifetime achievement honour is decided separately by a committee of eminent film personalities, specially constituted for the purpose. No entries are invited for this award.

5

Conferred by the President

The winners receive their medallions, cash prizes, and certificates from the President of India at the annual ceremony, followed by the screening of award-winning films for the public.

What the Juries Weigh

  • Aesthetic & Technical Excellence — the cinematic form and craft of the film
  • Social Relevance — meaningful engagement with the cultures and concerns of India
  • Unity & Integration — contributions to national integration and unity through cinema
  • Educational & Cultural Value — films that inform, inspire, and elevate
  • Independence of Judgment — the juries function without external influence

⏳ Timeline

1913

Raja Harishchandra Released

Dadasaheb Phalke releases India's first full-length feature film on 3 May — the date on which the National Film Awards ceremony is now held every year.

1949

Film Enquiry Committee

The committee recommends the institution of State Awards for films to encourage films of high aesthetic and technical standard.

1954

First Awards Presented

The inaugural "State Awards" are presented for the films of 1953. Shyamchi Aai wins the President's Gold Medal for Best Feature Film.

1967

Renamed National Film Awards

The scheme is renamed at the 15th ceremony, and separate awards for artists and technicians are introduced.

1968

First Actor & Actress Honoured

Uttam Kumar and Nargis become the first Best Actor ("Bharat") and Best Actress ("Urvashi") winners.

1973

Directorate of Film Festivals

The Directorate of Film Festivals, under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, begins administering the awards.

2007

Two-Tier Selection Begins

From the 57th National Film Awards, feature films are shortlisted by five regional panels before the national panel decides the winners.

2021

NFDC Takes Over

The National Film Development Corporation of India assumes administration of the awards, continuing their legacy of celebrating Indian cinema.

2023

Aattam Wins Best Feature Film

The Malayalam film Aattam (The Play) wins the Golden Lotus for Best Feature Film at the 70th National Film Awards.

🌟 Notable Winners

Across more than seven decades, the National Film Awards have honoured the giants of Indian cinema — a roll of honour that reads like the history of the nation's screens.

🎬

Satyajit Ray

The master of world cinema and the most honoured director in the award's history — six Best Feature Film wins from Pather Panchali to Agantuk.

🎭

Kamal Haasan

A three-time Best Actor winner — Moondram Pirai, Nayakan, and Indian — celebrated across Tamil and pan-Indian cinema.

🎭

Amitabh Bachchan

Four-time Best Actor winner — Agneepath, Black, Paa, and Piku — the most awarded actor in the category's history.

⭐

Shabana Azmi

Among the most honoured actresses, winning for performances in Ankur, Paar, and other landmark films of the parallel cinema movement.

🌟

Mammootty & Mohanlal

Two legends of Malayalam cinema who have each won the Best Actor award multiple times, dominating the national honours for over a decade.

👑

Nargis & Uttam Kumar

The pioneering first recipients of the Best Actress and Best Actor honours, whose performances defined a generation of Indian cinema.

🌺

Sridevi

The icon of Indian cinema, posthumously awarded the Best Actress honour for her final film Mom.

🏆

Baahubali: The Beginning

S. S. Rajamouli's epic won the Best Feature Film Golden Lotus in 2015, becoming a symbol of modern pan-Indian cinema.

🌍

Pather Panchali

Satyajit Ray's debut in 1955 took the Golden Lotus and put Indian cinema on the world stage for the first time.

Dadasaheb Phalke Award Recipients

Presented alongside the National Film Awards, this lifetime achievement honour has been conferred on legends such as Devika Rani, Satyajit Ray, Lata Mangeshkar, Amitabh Bachchan, Rajinikanth, and Mohanlal.
