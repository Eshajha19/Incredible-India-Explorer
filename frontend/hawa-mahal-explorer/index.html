🪟 Jaipur, Rajasthan · 1799 CE

Hawa Mahal Palace of Winds · Jaipur

Discover Hawa Mahal, the iconic five-storey honeycomb marvel of Jaipur, Rajasthan. Built in 1799 CE by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh and designed by Lal Chand Ustad, this pink and red sandstone crown features 953 delicate latticed jharokhas that engineer natural air conditioning through the Venturi effect.

📍 Pink City, Jaipur, Rajasthan 👑 Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh (1799 CE) 🪟 953 Intricately Carved Jharokhas 🌬️ Venturi Air-Cooling Architecture
Location & Setting

The Crown of the Pink City

Perched on the edge of the City Palace complex in the heart of Jaipur's walled Pink City (a UNESCO World Heritage city in Rajasthan), Hawa Mahal ("Palace of the Breeze") is one of India's most recognized architectural icons.

Rising directly above Badi Chaupar at the intersection of Jaipur's planned royal grid, the palace was constructed from local red and pink sandstone. Its facade resembles the crown of Lord Krishna, whom Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh devotedly worshipped, blending Rajput architectural finesse with Islamic Mughal filigree.

Geographic Coordinates: 26.9239° N, 75.8267° E · Elevation: 431 m above sea level.

🏰 Hawa Mahal at a Glance

  • Location: Badi Chaupar, J.D.A. Market, Pink City, Jaipur, Rajasthan
  • Commissioned By: Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh (Grandson of Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II)
  • Chief Architect: Lal Chand Ustad (1799 CE)
  • Storeys: 5 distinct tiers (50 feet / 15 meters in height)
  • Total Jharokhas: 953 casements with fine sandstone lattice work (jali)
  • Primary Materials: Pink and red sandstone with white lime (chuna) accents
Historical Origins

Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh & The Royal Zenana

A masterpiece designed to provide royal women an unobserved window to the vibrant life and royal processions of Jaipur.

Purdah System & The Royal Women

In the late 18th century, the royal women of the Kachhwaha Rajput dynasty observed strict purdah (seclusion from public gaze). Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh commissioned Hawa Mahal as an extension of the Zenana (women's royal quarters) of the City Palace, allowing royal ladies to witness daily street life, bustling bazaars, and grand festive processions (such as Teej and Gangaur) completely unseen by the public.

Inspiration from Khetri Mahal

Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh was deeply captivated by the open-air wind-catching design of the Khetri Mahal in Jhunjhunu, Rajasthan. He commissioned royal architect Lal Chand Ustad to build an even grander structure in Jaipur, fusing Krishna-bhakti symbolism with groundbreaking climate-responsive engineering.

Five-Storey Pyramid

The 5 Distinct Levels of Hawa Mahal

Rising like a tiered honeycomb crown without regular stairs — instead connected by smooth pedestrian ramps.

Tier 1 · Ground

Sharad Mandir

The autumn chamber where royal autumn celebrations were held, featuring a large open courtyard with fountains.

Tier 2

Ratan Mandir

The gem chamber adorned with dazzling stained glasswork and mirror mosaics reflecting kaleidoscopic light across the halls.

Tier 3

Vichitra Mandir

The unique sanctum where Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh spent quiet hours worshipping Lord Krishna and composing poetry under the pen name 'Brijnidhi'.

Tier 4

Prakash Mandir

The chamber of light featuring open-air terraces on either side that illuminate the uppermost levels with natural daylight.

Tier 5 · Pinnacle

Hawa Mandir

The palace of winds at the very apex, where cool breezes sweep through, giving the entire monument its poetic name.

Architectural Brilliance

953 Jharokhas & Venturi Cooling Effect

Hawa Mahal is a world-class triumph of passive environmental architecture and climate-responsive engineering.

🪟

953 Latticed Jharokhas

Each of the 953 projecting bay windows (jharokhas) is carved with intricate sandstone jali filigree screens, tiny fluted domes, and delicate chhatris that disperse intense desert sunlight while maintaining complete privacy.

🌬️

The Venturi Cooling Principle

Air entering through the narrow jali apertures experiences pressure contraction followed by rapid expansion inside the chambers (the Venturi Effect), dropping ambient indoor air temperatures by 5°C to 8°C naturally without mechanical power.

👑

Mukut (Crown) Geometry

The pyramidal exterior rises at an 87-degree incline with a base barely 1.5 feet (45 cm) thick at the top levels, resembling the feathered crown (Mukut) of Lord Krishna with breathtaking vertical equilibrium.

🚶

Ramps Instead of Stairs

Because royal Rajput women wore heavy layered zardozi lehengas and royal palanquins were transported inside, the upper floors are connected by wide, gently inclining ramps rather than conventional staircases.

Chronology

Historical Timeline of Hawa Mahal

From 18th-century Rajput poetry and royal processions to modern UNESCO global heritage status.

1799 CE

Construction by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh

Royal architect Lal Chand Ustad completes the 5-tier sandstone palace of winds, dedicating its crown shape to Lord Krishna.

1876 CE

Jaipur Becomes the "Pink City"

Maharaja Sawai Ram Singh paints the entire city — including Hawa Mahal — terracotta pink to welcome Albert Edward, Prince of Wales.

1968 CE

State Protected Monument

Rajasthan State Department of Archaeology and Museums assumes protection and opens the interior courtyards to visitors.

2006 CE

Major Structural Conservation & Stained Glass Restoration

Comprehensive scientific conservation restores the original lime mortar stucco, water fountains, and imported colored Belgian stained glass.

2019 CE

UNESCO World Heritage City Inscription

Jaipur's historic walled city — with Hawa Mahal as its signature visual emblem — is formally inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Trivia & Insights

Fascinating Facts About Hawa Mahal

Curious architectural wonders and historical quirks of Jaipur's Palace of Winds.

🚪

No Front Entrance

Hawa Mahal has no direct entrance from the main street. Visitors and royals enter through an imperial gate located at the rear side of the City Palace complex.

🌈

Stained Glass Kaleidoscope

When morning sunlight strikes the Ratan Mandir's colored glass panels, the entire interior glows in vibrant rubies, amethysts, and emeralds without electric light.

📐

Extraordinary Slim Profile

Despite rising 50 feet high over five floors, the top three storeys have the thickness of just a single room, measuring barely 1.5 feet at the peak!

🪙

Poet King's Sanctuary

Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh composed hundreds of celebrated devotional Braj Bhasha poems here under the nom de plume Brijnidhi.
