Estuary Formation

Where the sweet waters of the river meet the saline sea.

The Aghanashini Estuary is formed where the Aghanashini River, one of the few undammed rivers in the Western Ghats, meets the Arabian Sea. This mixing of freshwater and seawater creates a unique brackish environment.

Unlike many other rivers, the Aghanashini remains free-flowing, allowing natural sediment transport and maintaining the ecological integrity of the estuary and its surrounding mangroves.

The Estuarine Gradient

The gradual transition from freshwater to marine conditions creates distinct ecological zones, each supporting specialized flora and fauna.

Economy

Fisheries

A lifeline for coastal communities.

The estuary serves as a crucial nursery ground for a variety of commercially important fish and crustacean species. The nutrient-rich waters support a thriving artisanal fishing industry.

Sustainable fishing practices, such as the use of traditional nets and seasonal fishing bans, are essential to maintain fish stocks and the livelihoods of local fishermen.

Global Recognition

Ramsar Site

Recognized in 2023 for its outstanding ecological value.

The Aghanashini Estuary was designated as a Ramsar Site in 2023, acknowledging its role in supporting biodiversity and providing ecosystem services. It is part of a cluster of wetlands in Karnataka that highlight the state's commitment to conservation.

People

Local Communities

The symbiotic relationship between the estuary and the people of Honnavar.

The estuary is integral to the cultural and economic life of the local fishing communities. Traditional knowledge of the tides, fish behavior, and mangrove ecology is passed down through generations.

Community-led conservation initiatives, supported by local NGOs and the forest department, aim to balance livelihood needs with ecological preservation.
