🦁 National Bravery Award for Girls

Geeta Chopra Award

A prestigious National Bravery Award presented to courageous girls who have displayed exceptional valour, presence of mind, and selfless service in the face of danger — named in memory of Geeta Chopra, a young girl whose life was tragically cut short.

Year Instituted: 1978 Category: Bravery Age Group: 6–18 years

History of the Geeta Chopra Award

The Geeta Chopra Award is one of the five categories of the National Bravery Awards (now known as the Bal Shakti Puraskar), instituted by the Indian Council for Child Welfare (ICCW) in 1978. The award is named in memory of Geeta Chopra and her brother Sanjay Chopra, two young children who were kidnapped and murdered in New Delhi in 1978. Despite being held captive, the siblings showed remarkable courage and refused to succumb to their captors' demands.

The tragic case shocked the nation and led to the creation of two separate awards: the Geeta Chopra Award, given to a girl who displays exceptional bravery, and the Sanjay Chopra Award, given to a boy. Together, they form part of India's broader National Bravery Awards scheme, which recognises children aged 6–18 who perform acts of courage and selfless service.

Since its inception, the Geeta Chopra Award has honoured dozens of young girls who have risked their lives to save others, survived devastating natural disasters, foiled attacks, and demonstrated extraordinary courage in the face of grave danger. The awardees are announced each year on Republic Day (26th January), and the children ride on caparisoned elephants in the Republic Day Parade down Rajpath in New Delhi.

In 2018, the awards were restructured and renamed the Bal Shakti Puraskar under the Ministry of Women and Child Development, but the Geeta Chopra Award continues as a named category within the broader scheme.

Eligibility Criteria

The Geeta Chopra Award has specific eligibility requirements to ensure it reaches the most deserving young heroines:

  • Gender: The award is exclusively for girls. Boys are honoured under the separate Sanjay Chopra Award.
  • Age: The recipient must be between 6 and 18 years of age at the time of the act of bravery.
  • Indian citizenship: The applicant must be a citizen of India.
  • Genuine act of bravery: The act must involve a real, physical risk to the child's own life — e.g., saving someone from drowning, fire, an animal attack, a kidnapping, or a natural disaster.
  • Time frame: The act of bravery must have occurred between 1st July of the previous year and 30th June of the current year.
  • Recommendation required: Nominations must be submitted by recognised bodies — district/state governments, NGOs, schools, or the ICCW. Self-nominations are not accepted.
  • Posthumous eligibility: The award may be given posthumously if the child lost her life during the act of bravery.

Selection Process

The selection of the Geeta Chopra awardee is a rigorous, multi-stage process designed to verify the authenticity and magnitude of the act of bravery:

1

Nomination

Nominations are invited from 1st July to 30th September each year. District authorities, school principals, NGOs, and state ICCW chapters submit applications with evidence, news clippings, and police/medical reports.

2

State-Level Screening

State ICCW chapters review the nominations and shortlist candidates. They conduct preliminary verification of the bravery act and forward the strongest cases to the national ICCW office.

3

National Committee

A high-powered national committee — comprising representatives from the ICCW, the Ministry of Women and Child Development, police, and child welfare experts — reviews the shortlisted nominations.

4

Field Verification

The committee may send field investigators to verify the facts — interviewing witnesses, police, medical staff, and the nominee herself. This ensures every claim is genuine.

5

Final Selection

The committee selects one girl for the Geeta Chopra Award (the most outstanding act of bravery by a girl) and other awardees for the general National Bravery Awards categories.

6

Republic Day Ceremony

The awards are presented by the Prime Minister of India (or the President) in a ceremony around Republic Day. The awardees ride on caparisoned elephants in the Republic Day Parade.

Award Significance

The Geeta Chopra Award holds immense significance in India's national landscape for several reasons:

🦁

Celebrating Girl Power

The award sends a powerful message that girls are as courageous and resilient as boys. By having a dedicated category for girls, the award challenges gender stereotypes and inspires young women across India to act fearlessly.

🕊️

Memorialising Geeta Chopra

The award keeps alive the memory of Geeta Chopra, who showed extraordinary courage in the face of her captors. Her story reminds the nation that even the youngest can display remarkable fortitude.

🏛️

National Recognition

Winning this award places the recipient among India's nationally recognised heroes. Awardees are felicitated by the Prime Minister, ride in the Republic Day Parade, and receive media coverage that amplifies their story nationwide.

🎓

Educational Benefits

Awardees receive financial assistance for education, including scholarships and tuition waivers at participating institutions. This ensures that the young heroine's courage is rewarded with long-term opportunities.

🤝

Community Inspiration

The award stories are widely disseminated through schools, media, and community programmes. They serve as case studies in moral education, inspiring other children to act with courage and selflessness in their own lives.

📜

Civic Engagement

Many awardees go on to become civic leaders, police officers, and social workers. The award serves as a launching pad for a life of public service, as the recognition and support it provides open doors to future opportunities.

Notable Recipients

The Geeta Chopra Award has been conferred on many courageous girls. Below are some notable recipients.

Recipients Across Decades

Since 1978

Over 40 girls have received the Geeta Chopra Award since its inception. Each was honoured for a unique act of extraordinary bravery — from saving siblings from drowning to fooling kidnappers and rescuing people from burning buildings.

Rescue from Drowning

Multiple Years

Several recipients have been honoured for saving lives from drowning — pulling siblings, classmates, or even strangers from rivers, wells, and flooded areas, often at great risk to themselves.

Foiling Crime

Multiple Years

Some awardees received the honour for foiling kidnappings, robberies, or attacks — using presence of mind to alert police, escape captivity, or shield younger children from harm.

Disaster Rescue

Multiple Years

Several recipients were honoured for rescuing people from natural disasters — including floods, earthquakes, and fires — demonstrating courage well beyond their years.

Timeline of Key Events

  1. 1978

    Tragedy & Inception

    Geeta and Sanjay Chopra are kidnapped and murdered. The nation is shocked. The ICCW institutes the Geeta Chopra Award and Sanjay Chopra Award in their memory.

  2. 1979

    First Awards

    The first National Bravery Awards, including the Geeta Chopra Award, are presented on Republic Day 1979.

  3. 1980s

    Annual Tradition

    The awards become an annual tradition, with awardees riding on caparisoned elephants in the Republic Day Parade down Rajpath.

  4. 1996

    Expanded Recognition

    The ICCW expands the awards to include more categories and recognises bravery acts during natural disasters and communal violence.

  5. 2001

    Posthumous Awards

    The awards gain prominence for posthumously honouring children who lost their lives saving others, including during the 2001 Gujarat earthquake.

  6. 2008

    30th Anniversary

    The 30th anniversary of the Geeta Chopra Award is observed, with over 700 children honoured across all categories since inception.

  7. 2018

    Bal Shakti Puraskar

    The awards are restructured under the Ministry of Women and Child Development and renamed Bal Shakti Puraskar. The Geeta Chopra Award continues as a named category.

  8. 2020s

    Continuing Legacy

    The award continues to honour courageous girls every Republic Day, keeping Geeta Chopra's memory alive and inspiring new generations.

Interesting Facts

🐘 Elephant Ride on Republic Day

Awardees ride on caparisoned elephants in the Republic Day Parade down Rajpath — a tradition that makes them visible to millions of spectators and television viewers across India.

💰 Financial Support

Awardees receive a cash prize and, in many cases, ongoing educational scholarships that support them through college, ensuring their bravery is rewarded with opportunity.

🏛️ Prime Minister's Reception

The awardees are personally felicitated by the Prime Minister of India in a ceremony before Republic Day — a moment many describe as the proudest of their young lives.

👤 Named After a Child

The award is one of the few national honours named after a child. Geeta Chopra was just 10 years old when she was kidnapped and murdered — making the award uniquely poignant.

🔄 Two Sibling Awards

The Geeta Chopra Award (for girls) and the Sanjay Chopra Award (for boys) are a matched pair — both named after the siblings who were victims of the same 1978 case.

🌍 International Recognition

The National Bravery Awards, including the Geeta Chopra Award, are recognised internationally as one of the most distinctive child-honour programmes in the world.
