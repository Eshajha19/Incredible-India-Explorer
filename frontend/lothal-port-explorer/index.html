⚓ Gulf of Khambhat · Indus Valley Maritime Metropolis

Lothal Ancient Port Explorer

Step onto the burnt-brick wharf of Lothal, the world’s oldest known tidal dockyard city. Built by Harappan master engineers around 2400 BCE, Lothal connected ancient India with Mesopotamia, Bahrain, and Oman.

Harappan Citadel & Harbor

The Mound of the Dead turned Maritime Emporium

Discovered in 1954 by renowned archaeologist Dr. S.R. Rao of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI), Lothal (meaning "Mound of the Dead" in Gujarati) is located near the Gulf of Khambhat in the Bhal region of Gujarat.

Lothal flourished between 2400 BCE and 1900 BCE as one of the most prominent southern posts of the Indus Valley Civilization. Unlike inland Harappan cities, Lothal was purpose-built as an ocean trading hub, featuring an acropolis, lower town, grid-patterned brick streets, subterranean drainage systems, and a massive artificial dockyard basin.

🏺 Harappan Key Facts

  • Discovery Year: Excavated 1955–1962 by Dr. S.R. Rao (ASI).
  • Geographic Position: Sabarmati and Bhogavo river basin near Gulf of Khambhat.
  • UNESCO Status: Nominated on UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List.
  • Key Export: Polished carnelian beads, ivory, copper, and shell ornaments.
Milestones of Lothal

Location & Historical Timeline

Tracking Lothal's emergence from a local bead-craft village to the premier Bronze Age maritime port of the ancient world.

c. 2400 BCE

City Foundation & Dockyard Construction

Harappan settlers transform the indigenous village into an urban port city, constructing the 218m x 37m brick dockyard basin.

c. 2350 BCE - 2000 BCE

Peak Golden Era of Mesopotamian Trade

Direct maritime commerce flourishes with Akkad and Ur (Meluhha trade). Bead factories produce millions of polished carnelian gemstones.

c. 2000 BCE

Catastrophic Riverine Flood

A massive flood destroys the acropolis and shifts the Bhogavo river course, prompting rebuilt brick bunds and raised platforms.

c. 1900 BCE - 1600 BCE

Late Harappan Period & Urban Decline

Changing river channels and desiccation lead to gradual decline of maritime traffic, transitioning into a rural farming settlement.

1954 CE

ASI Discovery & Modern Preservation

Dr. S.R. Rao excavates the site, establishing the Archaeological Museum at Lothal showcasing 800+ excavated artifacts.

Hydraulic Mastery

The World's Earliest Tidal Dockyard

Lothal's engineers possessed sophisticated knowledge of ocean tides, sluice gates, and salinity control.

🏗️

Burnt-Brick Basin Engineering

A rectangular basin measuring 218m in length and 37m in width built of kiln-fired mud bricks, capable of berthing 30 ocean ships simultaneously during high tide.

🌊

Inlet Channel & Lock Gate

Connected to the tidal estuary of the Bhogavo River. A wooden sluice gate maintained water levels during low tide to prevent ship grounding.

💎

Carnelian Bead Manufacturing

Lothal contained a state-of-the-art bead factory equipped with two-room kilns, copper drills, and agate raw material imported from the Narmada valley.

📜

Meluhha Trade with Mesopotamia

Cuneiform inscriptions in Akkadian cities mention trade with "Meluhha" (Indus region), importing timber, carnelian, lapis lazuli, and ivory.

🏛️ Key Artifact Discoveries

  • Persian Gulf Button Seal: Steatite circular seal depicting two leaping gazelles, proving direct Gulf trade (Dilmun/Bahrain).
  • Terracotta Boat Models: Detailed clay models showing masted sailing vessels with oars.
  • Standardized Chert Weights: Binary and decimal cubic stone weights for precision trade taxation.
  • Painted Jar with Crow & Fox: Ceramic vessel depicting fables similar to the Panchatantra.
Artifact Marvels

Archaeological Treasures of Lothal

Excavations at Lothal yielded over 18,000 artifacts revealing an organized society of merchants, artisans, and harbor authorities.

Unique among Harappan sites, Lothal produced a steatite button seal originating from the Persian Gulf island of Bahrain (ancient Dilmun), verifying that Harappan sea captains navigated the Arabian Sea across 2,000 kilometers of open water.
