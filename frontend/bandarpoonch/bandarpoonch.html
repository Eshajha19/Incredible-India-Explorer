Bandarpoonch Explorer

Rising to 6,316 metres in the western Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, Bandarpoonch — meaning "Monkey's Tail" — is one of the most distinctive and spiritually significant peaks of the Yamunotri massif. Its long, curving ridgeline gave it its evocative name, and its glaciers feed the sacred Yamuna river.

Geography, Glaciers & History

The Sacred Tail of the Himalayas

Bandarpoonch stands in the Uttarkashi district of Uttarakhand, within the Govind Pashu Vihar National Park. Its name — derived from Sanskrit "Vanara" (monkey) and "Puchcha" (tail) — refers to the graceful, elongated ridge that sweeps from the main summit, resembling the curling tail of the monkey god Hanuman.

The peak is the hydrological origin of the Yamuna River: the Bandarpoonch glacier feeds the Yamunotri, the sacred spring that pilgrims reach on the Char Dham yatra. This dual significance — as a trekking destination and a source of the Yamuna — makes Bandarpoonch uniquely important in Indian geography and culture.

The first recorded ascent of Bandarpoonch was made in 1950 by a Swiss expedition. Its twin satellite summit, Kalanag (6,387 m), also known as Black Peak, lies just north and is often climbed as an adjacent objective from the same base camp.

Trekking Guide

Routes, Camps & Conditions

Essential information for planning a safe and rewarding Bandarpoonch trek or summit attempt.

🥾

Base Route

The most-used approach starts from Sankri village (1,920 m), trekking through Taluka and Seema to reach the base camp near the Bandarpoonch glacier at ~4,500 m. The route passes through dense oak and rhododendron forests before ascending into high alpine terrain.

⛺

Camp Strategy

A two-camp structure is standard: Camp 1 at ~5,200 m on the main glacier, followed by Camp 2 at ~5,800 m on the upper snowfield. Summit attempts are launched pre-dawn from Camp 2 to take advantage of stable early-morning snow conditions.

🗓️

Best Season

May–June (pre-monsoon) and late September–October (post-monsoon) offer the clearest windows. Monsoon (July–August) brings heavy snowfall and increased avalanche risk at altitude. Winter months see the route closed due to extreme cold and snowfall at lower camps.

📋

Permits & Logistics

An Inner Line Permit from the District Magistrate's office in Uttarkashi is required. The peak falls within Govind Pashu Vihar National Park, requiring an additional forest department entry permit. Most teams use Sankri as the last motorable point for supply drops.
