Little Andaman

🏖️ Butler Bay

Butler Bay, located on the southeastern coast of Little Andaman, is a pristine crescent-shaped beach stretching over 2 kilometers. Known for its golden sand, clear turquoise waters, and gentle palm-fringed shoreline, it is one of the most picturesque beaches in the Andaman Islands.

The bay faces the open Indian Ocean, making it an exceptional surfing destination (see the Surfing tab). Beyond surfing, the beach offers excellent swimming conditions during calm seasons, beachcombing, and spectacular sunrise views. The area behind the beach features coastal vegetation and provides access to nearby forest trails.

Highlights

  • 2+ km crescent beach with golden sand
  • Clear turquoise waters — ideal for swimming (seasonal)
  • Prime surfing location with consistent swells
  • Stunning sunrise views over the Indian Ocean
  • Access point for forest treks to White Surf Waterfall

💧 Waterfalls

Little Andaman is home to several stunning waterfalls, the most famous being White Surf Waterfall (also called Whisper Wave Waterfall). Located about 6.5 km from Hut Bay, this multi-tiered cascade drops approximately 60 meters through dense evergreen forest, creating a spectacular white curtain of water — hence the name "White Surf".

Other notable waterfalls include Whisper Wave Waterfall and the smaller cascades along the island's perennial streams. Most waterfalls are accessible via forest trails that wind through the island's protected rainforest, offering opportunities to spot endemic birds, butterflies, and occasionally wild elephants that swim between islands.

Key Waterfalls

  • White Surf Waterfall: 60m multi-tiered cascade, 6.5 km from Hut Bay
  • Whisper Wave Waterfall: Scenic forest waterfall near the coast
  • Seasonal streams: Numerous smaller cascades during monsoon (May–September)

🌳 Evergreen Rainforests

Over 80% of Little Andaman is covered by tropical evergreen rainforest, part of the Andaman Islands rainforest ecoregion — one of the most biodiverse regions in India. These ancient forests have evolved in isolation, resulting in high endemism among flora and fauna.

The forests are characterized by towering dipterocarp trees (gurjan, hollong), dense canopy layers, lianas, epiphytes, and a rich understory. They serve as critical habitat for the Andaman wild pig, Andaman horseshoe bat, numerous endemic bird species (Andaman woodpecker, Andaman drongo, Andaman serpent eagle), and act as corridors for swimming elephants that move between islands.

Forest Highlights

  • Primary evergreen rainforest covering ~600 km²
  • High endemism — many species found nowhere else
  • Critical elephant corridor (swimming elephants)
  • Timber species: gurjan, hollong, padauk, marjan
  • Medicinal plants and rare orchids in the understory

🏄 Surfing at Butler Bay

Butler Bay is recognized as one of India's premier surfing destinations. The beach's exposure to the open Indian Ocean generates consistent swells, particularly during the pre-monsoon (March–May) and post-monsoon (September–November) seasons. The sandy bottom and reef-free breaks make it relatively safe for intermediate surfers.

The main break at Butler Bay offers both left and right-handers. Wave heights typically range from 3–6 feet, with occasional larger swells during cyclonic systems. The Andaman administration and local surf clubs have begun promoting surfing tourism, with basic board rentals and guidance available in Hut Bay during peak season.

Surfing Details

  • Best season: March–May & September–November
  • Wave type: Beach break (sandy bottom), lefts & rights
  • Typical size: 3–6 ft (1–2 m), up to 8+ ft on big swells
  • Skill level: Intermediate (currents can be strong)
  • Facilities: Basic board rental in Hut Bay (seasonal)
  • Access: 20-min drive from Hut Bay + short walk

🦋 Wildlife & Marine Life

Little Andaman's isolation has created a unique wildlife sanctuary. The island is a critical habitat for the dugong (sea cow) — India's state animal of Andaman & Nicobar — which feeds on seagrass meadows in the shallow coastal waters. Green and hawksbill sea turtles nest on the island's beaches, particularly at Butler Bay and nearby coves.

Terrestrial wildlife includes the endemic Andaman wild pig, Andaman horseshoe bat, saltwater crocodiles in mangrove creeks, and a rich avifauna with over 30 endemic bird subspecies. The island also serves as a corridor for Asian elephants that swim between Little Andaman, Great Nicobar, and other islands — a remarkable natural phenomenon.

Key Species

  • Dugong: Vulnerable; seagrass beds around the island
  • Sea Turtles: Green & Hawksbill nesting (Dec–Mar)
  • Asian Elephant: Swimming corridor between islands
  • Saltwater Crocodile: Mangrove creeks & coastal areas
  • Endemic Birds: Andaman woodpecker, drongo, serpent eagle, crake
  • Marine Life: Coral reefs, reef fish, dolphins offshore

🗺️ Geography & Geology

Little Andaman is the fourth-largest island in the Andaman archipelago, located at the southern end of the Andaman group, separated from Rutland Island by the Duncan Passage. The island is roughly oval-shaped, measuring approximately 30 km north–south and 28 km east–west.

Geologically, the island is part of the accretionary prism formed by the subduction of the Indian Plate beneath the Burma Microplate. The terrain rises from coastal plains to a central ridge reaching ~450 m at its highest point. The coastline alternates between sandy beaches (Butler Bay, RK Bay, Jackson Creek), rocky headlands, and mangrove-lined creeks. Freshwater streams radiate from the central highlands, creating the island's waterfalls and sustaining its rainforests.

Geographic Facts

  • Area: ~730 km² (4th largest in Andamans)
  • Coordinates: 10°36′N 92°30′E
  • Highest point: ~450 m (central ridge)
  • Separated from: Rutland Island by Duncan Passage (~15 km)
  • Main settlement: Hut Bay (northeast coast)
  • Geology: Accretionary prism — sedimentary & volcanic rocks
  • Climate: Tropical monsoon (2000–3000 mm annual rainfall)
