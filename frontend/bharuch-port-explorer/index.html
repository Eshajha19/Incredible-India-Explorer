Bharuch Barygaza

Trace the Indian Ocean port described by the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, where Narmada commerce met Roman, Arab and coastal traders.

Historical background

Bharuch, ancient Barygaza, occupied the Narmada's tidal mouth in Gujarat. Its river access and position at the head of the Gulf of Khambhat made it a major western gateway for centuries, from early historic India through the medieval period.

River gateway

Goods from inland Gujarat and the Deccan reached ocean-going merchants through the Narmada.

Roman connections

The first-century Periplus records Barygaza's pilots, customs officials and busy foreign exchange.

Living entrepôt

Greek, Roman, Arab and Indian sailing traditions met in its markets and anchorages.

Exports and imports

  • Exports: cotton textiles, agate and carnelian beads, ivory, spices and indigo.
  • Imports: wine, coral, bullion, glassware, metals and Mediterranean luxury goods.

Trade note

Monsoon knowledge and local pilots were essential for crossing the Gulf's strong tides and shifting channels.

Timeline

  1. c. 3rd century BCE Inland and coastal exchange expands around the Narmada.
  2. 1st century CE Barygaza appears as a leading port in the Periplus.
  3. 7th century CE Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang notes Bharukaccha as a regional centre.
  4. Medieval period Changing channels and rival Gujarat ports reshape its maritime role.
