📜 History of the Award

The Dadasaheb Phalke Award is India's highest award in the field of cinema, given by the Government of India and presented annually at the National Film Awards. It honours the recipient for their "outstanding contribution to the growth and development of Indian cinema".

The Father of Indian Cinema

The award is named after Dhundiraj Govind Phalke (1870–1944), popularly known as Dadasaheb Phalke and often regarded as "the father of Indian cinema". A pioneering filmmaker, he wrote, produced, and directed Raja Harishchandra (1913) — India's first full-length feature film — and went on to craft the visual grammar that later generations of Indian cinema would build upon.

Genesis and Vision

The award was introduced by the Government of India in 1969, the birth centenary year of Dadasaheb Phalke, to commemorate his monumental contribution to Indian cinema and to recognise the lifetime achievements of those who have carried his legacy forward.

Significance and Impact

Widely considered the equivalent of a lifetime achievement honour for Indian film, the award transcends regional and language divides. Its recipients span Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, and Assamese cinema — a living archive of the people who shaped India's screens.

Institutional Framework

The award is sponsored by the Directorate of Film Festivals, under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, and is conferred by the President of India at the National Film Awards ceremony. It is selected by a committee of eminent personalities from the Indian film industry.

One Award, Every Year

Since 1969, the Dadasaheb Phalke Award has been conferred annually, honouring one outstanding personality of Indian cinema each year — making it the most exclusive honour in the country's film world.

📋 Eligibility Criteria

🎥 Who Can Be Honoured

Individuals who have made an outstanding contribution to the growth and development of Indian cinema — including producers, directors, actors, music directors, lyricists, cinematographers, and technicians.

🌟 Lifetime Achievement

The award is a lifetime achievement honour, recognising the totality and enduring impact of a person's work across their career rather than a single film.

🇮🇳 All Indian Languages

Recipients may belong to any of India's film industries — Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Assamese, and more — reflecting the unity of Indian cinema.

🕊️ Posthumous Recognition

The award may be conferred posthumously. To date, Prithviraj Kapoor (1971) and Vinod Khanna (2017) are the only two posthumous recipients.

🏅 No Age Bar

There is no age restriction — recipients have ranged from veteran giants of the golden era to the youngest-ever winner, Mohanlal (65 at the time of conferment).

📝 Selection by Peers

The recipient is selected by a committee of eminent personalities from the Indian film industry, ensuring the honour is decided by the very people who understand cinema.

⚙️ Selection Process

The selection process ensures that India's highest cinema honour goes to the most deserving film personality of each year.

1

Nominations Invited

Nominations are invited for eminent personalities who have contributed outstandingly to the growth and development of Indian cinema during their lifetime.

2

Eminent Committee Review

A committee comprising eminent personalities from the Indian film industry considers the nominations and evaluates each candidate's body of work.

3

Recommendation

The committee recommends a single outstanding personality whose career has made a lasting and defining contribution to Indian cinema.

4

Conferment at National Film Awards

The President of India confers the Dadasaheb Phalke Award at the National Film Awards ceremony, presenting the Swarna Kamal medallion, a shawl, and the cash prize.

What the Committee Weighs

  • Outstanding Contribution: Sustained, transformative impact on Indian cinema
  • Growth & Development: Role in advancing Indian film art, craft, and industry
  • Legacy: Influence on audiences, peers, and future generations
  • Pan-Indian Reach: Contribution across languages and regions of Indian cinema
  • Lifetime Body of Work: The totality of a career rather than any single achievement

🏅 Medal Design

The Dadasaheb Phalke Award comprises a prestigious Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus) medallion, a shawl, and a cash prize of ₹1,000,000 (₹10 lakh).

🏅

Swarna Kamal Medallion

The centrepiece of the award — a Golden Lotus, the highest emblem of the National Film Awards family. The lotus, India's national flower, symbolises purity, creativity, and the timeless bloom of cinematic art.

🧣

The Shawl

A traditional shawl accompanies the medal, honouring the recipient in the ceremonial manner reserved for the nation's greatest achievers.

💸

Cash Prize

A cash prize of ₹1,000,000 (₹10 lakh) is presented along with the medal, recognising the lifetime dedication of the awardee to Indian cinema.

📜

Citation & Scroll

The honour is announced with an official citation, recording the outstanding contribution of the recipient for posterity.

A Place of Honour in Indian Film History

The Swarna Kamal is the same golden lotus used across the National Film Awards, making the Dadasaheb Phalke Award the crowning accolade of India's most prestigious film honour system.

Legends Across Languages

The roll of honour reads like a history of Indian cinema itself — from the first lady of Indian cinema, Devika Rani, and the Nightingale of India, Lata Mangeshkar, to the Shahenshah of Bollywood, Amitabh Bachchan, the Superstar Rajinikanth, and the youngest-ever recipient, Mohanlal.

⏳ Timeline

1870

Dadasaheb Phalke Born

Dhundiraj Govind Phalke is born on 30 April 1870, destined to become the father of Indian cinema.

1913

Raja Harishchandra Released

Phalke directs India's first full-length feature film, Raja Harishchandra, laying the foundation of Indian cinema.

1944

Phalke Passes Away

Dadasaheb Phalke dies on 16 February 1944, leaving behind a legacy that would inspire a national honour in his name.

1969

Award Instituted & First Conferred

On his birth centenary, the Government of India institutes the Dadasaheb Phalke Award and confers it on Devika Rani at the 17th National Film Awards.

1971

First Posthumous Award

Prithviraj Kapoor becomes the first posthumous recipient, with his son Raj Kapoor accepting on his behalf.

1984

Satyajit Ray Honoured

The maestro of world cinema, Satyajit Ray, receives the award, cementing its standing as a global marker of film excellence.

2018

Amitabh Bachchan Conferred

The Shahenshah of Bollywood, Amitabh Bachchan, is honoured for a career spanning more than five decades and over 200 films.

2023

Mohanlal, Youngest Recipient

Malayalam superstar Mohanlal becomes the youngest-ever recipient at 65, honoured at the 71st National Film Awards ceremony in September 2025.

✨ Interesting Facts

🎭 The Only Father & Sons

Prithviraj Kapoor (1971), Raj Kapoor (1987), and Shashi Kapoor (2014) are the only father and sons to have received the award.

🎤 The Only Sisters

Melody queens Lata Mangeshkar (1989) and Asha Bhosle (2000) are the only sisters to have received the award.

👬 Two Pairs of Brothers

B. N. Reddy (1974) and B. Nagi Reddy (1986), along with B. R. Chopra (1998) and Yash Chopra (2001), are the two pairs of brothers honoured.

🕊️ Only Two Posthumous Honours

Prithviraj Kapoor (1971) and Vinod Khanna (2017) remain the only posthumous recipients in the award's history.

👑 The First Lady of Cinema

First recipient Devika Rani founded Bombay Talkies (1934), the first Indian public limited film company, and debuted in Karma (1933) — the first Indian English-language film.

🌺 The Golden Lotus

The award is presented with the Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus) medallion — the same emblem used across the National Film Awards, making it the crowning National Film honour.

💍 Beware of Imposters

Private events like the "Dadasaheb Phalke International Film Festival" and "Dadasaheb Phalke Excellence Awards" are unrelated to the official Government of India award.

🇮🇳 A Pan-Indian Honour

Recipients represent eight or more languages — Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, and Assamese — celebrating the unity of Indian cinema.
