Tulu

/ˈt̪uɭu baːsɛ/ · Tuḷu Bāse

A Dravidian language of the Arabian Sea coast — spoken across Dakshina Kannada and Udupi in Karnataka, and northern Kasaragod in Kerala. Native speakers call themselves Tuluvas, and their homeland Tulu Nadu.

Writing System

The Tigalari script

Tulu's historical script — also called Tulu Lipi or Tulu-Grantha script — is a Southern Brahmic script descended from Grantha, sharing common ancestry with the Malayalam script. Today, Tulu is most commonly written in the Kannada script, alongside efforts to revive Tigalari.

Script timeline

  • 9th c. CETigalari evolves from the Grantha script, itself descended from Brahmi.
  • 1159 CEEarliest known Tigalari-Tulu stone inscription, Sri Veeranarayana temple, Kulashekara.
  • 15th c. CEMedieval Tulu inscriptions written in Tigalari script.
  • 17th c. CEEpic works Sri Bhagavato and Kaveri composed in Tigalari script.
  • TodayKannada script dominates; Tigalari is being reintroduced in Tulu Nadu schools by the Karnataka Tulu Sahitya Academy (est. 1994).

The common Tulu greeting

There's no separate "good morning" or "good evening" in everyday Tulu — Namaskāra covers any time of day, usually followed by asking after someone's wellbeing.

Language Family

Dravidian roots

Tulu belongs to the South Dravidian branch — and, notably, was the first language to split away from Proto-South Dravidian, which is why it preserves grammatical features not found in Tamil or Kannada.

Dravidian family → South Dravidian branch → Tulu splits earliest, followed later by the ancestors of Tamil, Kannada, and Malayalam.

Tulu, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, and Malayalam together are informally called the Pancha Dravida (five major Dravidian) languages.

Linguists note unusual features in Tulu, such as pluperfect and future‑perfect verb tenses formed without auxiliary verbs — a trait rare among Indian languages.

Where It's Spoken

Tulu Nadu — the coastal homeland

Tulu is concentrated along a narrow coastal strip between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, straddling the Karnataka–Kerala border.

  • Dakshina Kannada, Karnataka
    Core Tulu-speaking district, centred on Mangaluru (Kudla in Tulu).
  • Udupi, Karnataka
    Southern Udupi is majority Tulu-speaking.
  • Kasaragod, Kerala
    Northern Kasaragod forms the southern edge of Tulu Nadu.
  • Diaspora pockets
    Smaller communities in Chikkamagaluru, Shivamogga, Bengaluru, Mumbai, and the Gulf.
Literature & Culture

An oral epic tradition

Tulu culture is carried as much through performance and sung epic as through the written page.

  • ✎

    Pardana

    Sung oral epic poems — Tulu Nadu's core literary tradition, preserved through generations of ritual performers.

  • ✎

    Siri & Koti-Chennayya

    Two of the best-known Tulu epics, recounting legendary local figures still honoured in ritual worship (bhuta kola).

  • ✎

    Sri Bhagavato & Kaveri

    17th-century written epics composed in the Tigalari script — rare surviving examples of pre-modern Tulu literature.

  • ✎

    Yakshagana

    The coastal Karnataka dance-theatre tradition, performed through the night with elaborate costume, percussion, and improvised dialogue.
