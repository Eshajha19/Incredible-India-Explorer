🏛️ Badami, Aihole & Kalyani · c. 543 – 1189 CE

Chalukya Dynasty Explorer

Journey to the heartland of South Indian temple architecture. Discover the Chalukyas — who checked Emperor Harsha's southward expansion along the Narmada, pioneered the hybrid Vesara style at Aihole and Badami, and built UNESCO World Heritage monuments at Pattadakal.

Rulers of Karnata

Architects of Deccan Empires

The Chalukya Dynasty ruled large parts of Southern and Central India between the 6th and 12th centuries CE across three distinct branches: the Badami Chalukyas (c. 543–753 CE), the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi, and the Western Chalukyas of Kalyani (c. 973–1189 CE).

They transformed the Malaprabha river valley into a grand laboratory of structural experiments, giving birth to the Vesara architectural tradition that harmonized Nagara (North) and Dravida (South) temple forms.

🏛️ Chalukya At a Glance

  • Era: c. 543 – 1189 CE
  • Capitals: Vatapi (Badami), Aihole, Kalyani
  • Languages: Kannada, Sanskrit
  • Key Architectural Complexes: Pattadakal (UNESCO), Aihole Durga Temple, Badami Cave Temples
Monarchs & Conquerors

Major Chalukya Rulers

Key kings who expanded imperial borders and shaped South Indian history.

👑

Pulakeshin I (r. c. 543–566 CE)

Founder of the Badami Chalukya lineage who fortified the hill fortress of Vatapi (Badami) as the imperial capital.

⚔️

Pulakeshin II (r. 610–642 CE)

The greatest Chalukya emperor who defeated Harsha of Kanauji on the banks of the Narmada and earned international fame, recorded in the Aihole Inscription.

🛡️

Vikramaditya I (r. 655–680 CE)

Restored Chalukya power and unity after the Pallava invasion of Badami, regaining control over the Deccan.

🛕

Vikramaditya II (r. 733–744 CE)

Triumphant king who conquered Kanchipuram thrice, spared Pallava monuments out of admiration, and built Virupaksha Temple at Pattadakal.

👑

Tailapa II (r. 973–997 CE)

Founder of the Western Chalukya Empire of Kalyani who overthrew the Rashtrakutas and revived Chalukya sovereignty.

📜

Vikramaditya VI (r. 1076–1126 CE)

Great Western Chalukya emperor who established the Chalukya-Vikrama Era and patronized Bilhana (author of Vikramankadevacharita) and Vijnaneshwara.

🏛️ Architectural Masterpieces

  • Aihole: Known as the "Cradle of Indian Temple Architecture" featuring over 120 stone temples (e.g., Durga Temple, Lad Khan Temple).
  • Badami Cave Temples: Four rock-cut cave shrines dedicated to Lord Vishnu, Shiva, and Jain Tirthankaras.
  • Pattadakal (UNESCO): Royal coronation site displaying a unique synthesis of Northern Nagara and Southern Dravida architectural styles.
  • Vesara Style: The hybrid architectural movement born under Chalukya patronage.
Stone Legacy

Architectural Legacy

The Chalukyas created an enduring architectural legacy that served as the foundational school for later Hoysala, Rashtrakuta, and Vijayanagara builder guilds. At Pattadakal, Queen Lokamahadevi commissioned the grand Virupaksha Temple to celebrate Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas.

Their rock-cut reliefs at Badami display dynamic sculptural mastery, while the horseshoe-shaped apse of Durga Temple at Aihole remains a rare masterpiece of ancient design.

Chronology

Chalukya Dynasty Timeline

Key historical milestones from Badami to Kalyani.

543 CE

Founding of Badami Lineage

Pulakeshin I establishes the Chalukya dynasty at Vatapi (Badami).

c. 618 CE

Battle of Narmada

Pulakeshin II repels Harsha's southern expansion; Ravikirtti authors the Aihole Inscription.

c. 740 CE

Construction of Pattadakal Temples

Vikramaditya II builds Virupaksha Temple after victories over the Pallavas.

973 CE

Kalyani Chalukya Revival

Tailapa II overthrows Rashtrakutas and re-establishes Chalukya power from Kalyani.

c. 1189 CE

Decline

Western Chalukya Empire splits into regional successor states (Hoysalas, Seunas/Yadavas, Kakatiyas).
