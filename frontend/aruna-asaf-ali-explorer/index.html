Heroine of the 1942 Movement

Aruna Asaf Ali

The woman who hoisted the Indian tricolour at Gowalia Tank Maidan on 9 August 1942 and became the enduring face of the Quit India Movement.

16 July 1909 — 29 July 1996

Explore her story The flag hoisting

Aruna Asaf Ali, c. 1942

Biography

Aruna Asaf Ali (née Ganguly) was born on 16 July 1909 in Kalka, Punjab, into a Bengali Brahmo family. She was educated in Lahore and Nainital, and after her marriage in 1928 to Asaf Ali — a prominent Congress leader two decades her senior — she entered the Indian freedom struggle.

She participated in the Salt Satyagraha of 1930 and was arrested, marking the first of several imprisonments during the Civil Disobedience Movement. After her release she remained active in underground Congress work and became a familiar figure in Bombay's nationalist circles.

Her defining moment came on 9 August 1942, the day after the All India Congress Committee passed the Quit India Resolution in Bombay. With most senior Congress leaders arrested overnight, the 33-year-old Aruna slipped past police cordons, reached the Gowalia Tank Maidan (now August Kranti Maidan), and hoisted the Indian National Congress tricolour before a vast crowd — an act that transformed her overnight into the symbol of the Quit India Movement.

After 1947 she moved into democratic politics, social work, and publishing. She was elected the first Mayor of Delhi in 1958, remained active in the Socialist and later Communist movements, and was awarded the International Lenin Peace Prize in 1964. In 1997, posthumously, she was honoured with the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award. She passed away on 29 July 1996 in New Delhi.

The Quit India Movement (1942)

On 8 August 1942, the All India Congress Committee (AICC) met at the Gowalia Tank Maidan in Bombay and passed the historic "Quit India" Resolution, demanding an immediate end to British colonial rule. Mahatma Gandhi gave the movement its battle cry: "Karenge ya Marenge" — "We shall do or die."

The British responded with mass arrests overnight. Gandhi, Nehru, Azad, Patel, Prasad, and the entire senior Congress leadership were taken into custody before dawn on 9 August. With no recognised leadership left, the movement exploded into a spontaneous nationwide uprising that the British struggled to suppress for the next two years.

Aruna Asaf Ali's Leadership Role

When the senior leaders were arrested, the burden of leadership fell on younger Congress workers. Aruna Asaf Ali, then 33, became one of the most prominent figures to emerge from this vacuum. Her actions on the morning of 9 August — defying a police ban, hoisting the Congress flag at the very maidan where the resolution had been passed the previous evening — turned her into the symbol of the movement.

After the flag hoisting she went underground and, for nearly three years, coordinated Congress activities from hiding. She travelled under the alias "Mrs. B. D. Bharadwaj", edited the underground bulletin Inquilab, mobilised students and workers, and kept the lines of communication open between scattered Congress units across India. The British announced a reward of ₹5,000 for her capture.

Her underground period became the basis of her legend. The young woman who had been a relatively minor Congress worker in 1941 was, by 1944, one of the most recognised faces of the Indian freedom struggle.

The Movement's Impact

  • Mass uprising: Across India, railways, post offices, police stations, and government buildings were attacked or destroyed by protesters.
  • "Parallel governments": In Midnapore (Bengal), Ballia (UP), and Satara (Maharashtra), nationalists briefly established parallel administrations.
  • British realisation: The scale and spontaneity of the revolt convinced the British that they could no longer hold India after the war.
  • Final push: Although suppressed militarily by 1944, Quit India made independence after World War II inevitable.

The Gowalia Tank Flag Hoisting — 9 August 1942

The defining moment that made Aruna Asaf Ali the face of Quit India.

What Happened

On the night of 8–9 August 1942, the British police swooped down on Bombay and arrested every senior Congress leader who had addressed the AICC session — Gandhi, Nehru, Patel, Azad, Prasad, Kriplani, and others. By dawn, the maidan at Gowalia Tank, where the Quit India Resolution had been passed the previous evening, was ringed by police and declared off-limits.

A huge crowd nevertheless gathered at the maidan on the morning of 9 August, expecting to hear from their leaders. When it became clear that no senior leader would come, Aruna Asaf Ali, then 33, climbed the maidan's flagpole stand and hoisted the Indian National Congress tricolour. The crowd erupted in cries of "Inquilab Zindabad!" and "Mahatma Gandhi ki jai!".

The act electrified the gathering. Within hours, news of the flag hoisting spread across Bombay, then across India. The British authorities, who had expected the arrests of the leadership to demoralise the movement, instead found themselves facing a nationwide uprising with no single head to cut off.

Why It Mattered

  • Symbolic defiance: Hoisting the Congress flag in defiance of an explicit police ban was an act of open rebellion, not just protest.
  • Leadership vacuum filled: The gesture demonstrated that the movement would continue even without its recognised leadership.
  • Nationwide morale: Reports of the flag hoisting became the spark that lit simultaneous uprisings across the country.
  • Personal legend: The 33-year-old was transformed overnight from a Congress worker into "the heroine of 1942".
