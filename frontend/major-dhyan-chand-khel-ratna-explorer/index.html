📜 History of the Khel Ratna

The Khel Ratna ("Jewel of Sport") is the highest sporting honour of India. It was instituted in 1991–92 by the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, Government of India, under the name Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna Award, in memory of the sixth Prime Minister of India. It was created to give the nation an apex sporting honour — a sporting counterpart to the Bharat Ratna in civilian life.

The inaugural recipient in 1991–92 was chess Grandmaster Viswanathan Anand, then a rising 22-year-old. The choice of a chess player sent a clear message: the Khel Ratna would celebrate world-class excellence across the entire breadth of sport — not merely the most popular or commercialised disciplines.

In its early years the award evaluated a single year of performance. Following the suggestions of the 2014 award selection committee, the criteria were revised in February 2015 to evaluate a sportsperson's performance over a period of four years at the international level. Today the Khel Ratna sits at the apex of India's six-part National Sports Awards framework, alongside the Arjuna Award, Dronacharya Award, Major Dhyan Chand Award (lifetime), Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar, and the MAKA Trophy.

A Decade-by-Decade Rise

From a single honouree in most early years, the Khel Ratna has grown into a powerful national institution. The 1990s recognised trailblazers in individual sports; the 2000s mirrored India's medal push in Olympic disciplines; and from the 2010s onwards the list blossomed with women champions and para-athletes. In 2021, the year of the renaming, a record 12 sportspersons were honoured at once — including the entire Tokyo 2020 medal contingent. As of the 2024 National Sports Awards, 62 honours have been conferred across three decades.

🔄 Renaming History — In Honour of the Wizard

For thirty years the award was known as the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna Award. On 6 August 2021, the Government of India renamed it the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, honouring one of the greatest sportspersons the country has ever produced. Announcing the decision, Prime Minister Narendra Modi noted that he was responding to "many requests from citizens across India."

🏑

The Hockey Wizard

Major Dhyan Chand (1905–1979), widely regarded as the greatest field hockey player of all time, led India to Olympic gold medals in 1928, 1932, and 1936 and scored more than 400 international goals across a two-decade career.

📅

A Birthday That Became a National Day

Dhyan Chand was born on 29 August — the date now celebrated as National Sports Day every year. The National Sports Awards investiture ceremony is traditionally held on or around this date at Rashtrapati Bhavan.

🥉

Timed With Hockey's Resurrection

The renaming came days after the Indian men's hockey team won a memorable Olympic bronze at Tokyo 2020 — its first Olympic medal in 41 years — giving the tribute to the hockey legend an especially resonant moment.

📋 Eligibility Criteria

The Khel Ratna is awarded to Indian sportspersons for their "spectacular and most outstanding performance in the field of sports over a period of four years" at the international level. Since the February 2015 revision of the scheme, performances are assessed over the preceding four-year cycle rather than a single year.

🇮🇳 Indian Sportspersons

Open to sportspersons of Indian nationality who have delivered extraordinary international performances in recognised sports disciplines.

🥇 Four-Year Performance Window

Performance over the preceding four years is evaluated — covering major competitions such as the Olympic Games, Paralympic Games, Asian Games, Commonwealth Games, and World Championships.

🏅 Wide Discipline Coverage

The award covers Olympic sports, non-Olympic disciplines (such as billiards and yachting), and Paralympic sports — including, in recent years, individual para-athletes and even doubles pairs.

📄 Nomination Route

Nominations are accepted only through the prescribed route — National Sports Federations, the Indian Olympic Association, State Governments, and other notified bodies — with not more than two sportspersons nominated per discipline.

🗓️ Annual Deadline

Nominations for a given award year close on 30 April (or the last working day of April), after which the selection machinery takes over.

🚫 No Repeat for the Same Discipline

A Khel Ratna recipient cannot later receive the Arjuna Award for the same discipline — the two honours sit on the same ladder of national sporting recognition.

⚙️ Selection Process

The selection of a Khel Ratna winner is a rigorous, multi-layered process administered annually by the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports.

1

Call for Nominations

National Sports Federations, the Indian Olympic Association, State Governments, and other recognised sports bodies propose candidates for the award year.

2

Nomination Window Closes

Nominations must be submitted by 30 April (or the last working day of April), with a maximum of two sportspersons per discipline.

3

Selection Committee Evaluation

A twelve-member Selection Committee — chaired by a retired judge — evaluates performances on a 100-point scale: 80 points for sporting performance over the four-year window and 20 points for qualities such as leadership and discipline.

4

Government Approval

The committee's recommendations are submitted to the Union Minister of Youth Affairs & Sports for final approval and the announcement of the year's awardees.

5

Presidential Investiture

Recipients are conferred the award by the President of India at a ceremonial investiture at Rashtrapati Bhavan, traditionally held on or around National Sports Day (29 August).

What the Committee Weighs

  • Medal & Ranking Record: Medals at the Olympics, Paralympics, Asian Games, and Commonwealth Games, and top world rankings
  • World-Level Victories: World Championship and World Cup performances, weighted by the cyclicality of each sport
  • Consistency: Sustained excellence across the entire four-year evaluation window
  • Leadership & Conduct: Sportsmanship, discipline, and leadership qualities off the field

🏅 Scope & Categories

Unlike awards such as the Arjuna or the Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar, the Khel Ratna is a singular, indivisible apex honour — it has no tiers, grades, or sub-categories. What it does span is the widest possible world of sport.

🥇 Olympic Sports

Shooting, badminton, athletics, wrestling, boxing, hockey, tennis, table tennis, gymnastics, and chess — the disciplines through which India's modern sporting resurgence has been built.

🏃 Paralympic Sports

Para-athletics, para-shooting, para-badminton, and para high jump — with an unbroken run of para champions honoured from 2017 onwards, including Devendra Jhajharia and Avani Lekhara.

🎱 Non-Olympic Sports

Billiards and snooker, and yachting — proof that the honour celebrates world-beating excellence beyond the Olympic roster, as with Geet Sethi and Pankaj Advani.

👥 Individuals, Pairs & Teams

Mostly an individual honour, the Khel Ratna has also recognised pairs and teams — from the yachting duo of Motivala and Garg to the badminton world No. 1 pair, Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty.

Position in the National Sports Awards Family

The Khel Ratna crowns a wider national framework. The six core honours work as a pyramid:

  • Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna — apex honour for outstanding sportspersons
  • Arjuna Award — consistent outstanding performance (instituted 1961)
  • Dronacharya Award — excellence in coaching (instituted 1985)
  • Major Dhyan Chand Award — lifetime achievement in sport
  • Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar — organisations promoting sport (instituted 2009)
  • MAKA Trophy — top performing university

🎖️ Medal & Certificate

Medallion · Citation · Cash Prize

Each recipient receives a set of honours that makes the award immediately recognisable:

  • Medallion: a gold-plated medal bearing the national emblem, presented on the national tricolour ribbon
  • Certificate: a formal citation scroll recording the recipient's sporting achievements
  • Cash Prize: ₹25 lakh — the prize amount, revised upward over the years to match the growing prestige of the honour

The award is formally conferred by the President of India at an investiture ceremony at Rashtrapati Bhavan, on or around 29 August — National Sports Day.

🕰️ Awardees Timeline

Trace the evolution of the Khel Ratna — and the champions who defined each era — from its 1991 inception to the present day.

1991–92

Inception. The Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna Award is instituted. Chess Grandmaster Viswanathan Anand becomes the first recipient — a signal that the honour transcends mainstream popularity.

1997–98

First Cricketer. Sachin Tendulkar becomes the first cricketer to receive the honour, at the age of 24, cementing the award's place in the national imagination.

2001

Youngest Recipient. Shooter Abhinav Bindra receives the award at just 18 — years before his historic individual Olympic gold at Beijing 2008.

2009

Three for the First Time. Mary Kom, Vijender Singh, and Sushil Kumar are jointly honoured — the first year three athletes share the award.

Feb 2015

Criteria Revision. The evaluation window shifts from a single year to a four-year performance cycle, reflecting the Olympic cycle of modern sport.

2016

Post-Rio Wave. P. V. Sindhu, Dipa Karmakar, Jitu Rai, and Sakshi Malik are honoured — a record four in a single year after India's best-ever Olympic Games.

2017

Para Pioneers. Javelin thrower Devendra Jhajharia becomes the first para-athlete joint recipient, alongside hockey's Sardar Singh.

2020

Cash Prize Raised. The prize money is increased to ₹25 lakh, and five champions — including Rohit Sharma, Rani Rampal, and Manika Batra — are honoured.

6 Aug 2021

Renamed & Record Year. The award is renamed the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna, and a record 12 recipients — led by Olympic javelin gold medallist Neeraj Chopra — are honoured after Tokyo 2020.

2022–23

New Frontiers. Table tennis star Sharath Kamal is honoured in 2022, followed in 2023 by badminton's world No. 1 doubles pair — Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty.

2024

Modern Champions. Shooter Manu Bhaker, World Chess Champion D. Gukesh, hockey captain Harmanpreet Singh, and para high-jumper Praveen Kumar receive the honour, conferred on 17 January 2025.

💡 Interesting Facts

♟️

A Chess Grandmaster Led the Way

Viswanathan Anand, India's first chess Grandmaster, was the inaugural Khel Ratna recipient — underscoring that the honour celebrates excellence in any sport, not just the most popular ones.

🔫

Youngest Winner, Olympic Gold Later

Abhinav Bindra was just 18 when he won the award in 2001 — and went on to claim India's first individual Olympic gold medal at Beijing 2008.

🎱

A Two-Discipline Double

Pankaj Advani is the only sportsperson to win the honour across two disciplines — both billiards and snooker.

📉

Some Years Saw No Winner

In a few years — including 2008 and 2014 — no sportsperson met the bar, a reminder that the Khel Ratna is reserved for truly exceptional performances.

🥇

A Record 12 in 2021

The renamed award's first year also produced its largest-ever cohort — 12 champions, including the entire Tokyo 2020 medal contingent.

🏏

More Cricketers Than You'd Think

Sachin Tendulkar, M. S. Dhoni, Virat Kohli, and Rohit Sharma have all received the honour — even though cricket has no officially recognised National Sports Federation.

📅

Tied to National Sports Day

The award is traditionally conferred around 29 August — the birthday of Major Dhyan Chand, celebrated across India as National Sports Day.

⚖️

One Honour, Many Layers

A Khel Ratna winner cannot later receive the Arjuna Award for the same discipline — the apex honour sits at the very top of the national sports pyramid.
