Great Nicobar Island

The largest island in the Nicobar group and India's southernmost territory — home to Indira Point, dense tropical rainforest, the Shompen and Nicobarese communities, and rare endemic wildlife.

History, Geography & Wildlife

📜 History

Great Nicobar has long been home to the indigenous Shompen and Nicobarese communities, among the least-contacted tribal groups in India. The island remained largely undeveloped through the colonial period and after independence, with settlement and infrastructure concentrated around Campbell Bay.

The island was devastated by the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, which caused significant loss of life, submerged parts of the coastline, and permanently shifted the shoreline near Indira Point. In 2013, UNESCO recognised the Great Nicobar Biosphere Reserve as part of its Man and Biosphere Programme in recognition of the island's ecological significance.

🗺️ Geography

Great Nicobar is the largest and southernmost island in the Nicobar group, covering approximately 921 sq. km. It lies in the eastern Indian Ocean between the Bay of Bengal and the Andaman Sea, and is geographically closer to Sumatra, Indonesia, than to mainland India.

The island's terrain includes undulating hills, dense tropical forest, freshwater streams and sandy beaches, rising to its highest point at Mount Thullier (642 m). The Galathea River is the island's principal river system.

📍 Indira Point

Indira Point marks the southernmost tip of Indian territory, named in honour of former Prime Minister Indira Gandhi. A lighthouse here guides ships travelling toward Malaysia and the Malacca Strait.

The point was severely affected by the 2004 tsunami — the lighthouse subsided by roughly 4.25 metres, permanently pushing the coastline inland. Due to this damage, the immediate area has restricted access.

🌳 Campbell Bay National Park

Established in 1992, Campbell Bay National Park spans roughly 426 sq. km in the northern part of Great Nicobar and forms part of the Great Nicobar Biosphere Reserve. The park protects tropical evergreen forest, tree ferns and a rich orchid understorey.

  • Nature trails include the Kalpong Trail, Great Nicobar Trail and Mount Thullier Trail
  • Home to the world's largest moth, the Atlas Moth
  • Open to visitors from October to May
  • Heavily affected by the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami

🦎 Flora & Fauna

Great Nicobar is known for exceptionally high endemism. Its evergreen forests host rare tree ferns, orchids and the gymnosperm Podocarpus wallichianus, alongside diverse wildlife found nowhere else in India.

  • Nicobar Megapode — an endangered mound-nesting bird endemic to the islands
  • Giant Leatherback turtles nesting at Galathea Bay
  • Crab-eating macaque and Nicobar long-tailed macaque
  • Saltwater crocodiles, dugongs, dolphins and whales offshore
  • Giant robber crabs and the Nicobar pigeon

🌦️ Climate

Great Nicobar has a tropical, humid climate influenced by the surrounding Indian Ocean, with warm temperatures year-round and heavy monsoon rainfall. The island lies in Seismic Zone V, the highest earthquake-hazard zone on India's seismic map, and remains vulnerable to cyclones and tsunamis.
