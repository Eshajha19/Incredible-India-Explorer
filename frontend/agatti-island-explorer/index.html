🏝️ Lakshadweep · Agatti Island

Agatti Island Explorer

A coral island so narrow you can see two different blues from the runway. Agatti is Lakshadweep's only airport, its most accessible lagoon, and the starting point for almost every trip to the archipelago.

Agatti in cross-section — tap a layer to jump in

✈ Airport

The only runway in Lakshadweep

Agatti Airport (IATA code AGX) is the single airstrip serving the entire 36-island Lakshadweep archipelago — every scheduled flight, medical evacuation, and supply run funnels through it. It sits on a narrow strip of coral island with lagoon water close on both sides, so the final approach is low and slow over turquoise water before the runway appears as a thin pale line among the palms. It's a small, functional terminal rather than a shopping-and-lounges airport — the view is the amenity.

Because the runway is short, only turboprop aircraft can use it, which keeps flight frequency and seat counts low — book well ahead, especially around the October–May peak season.

🪸 Coral Reefs

A reef you can reach from the beach

Agatti's lagoon is ringed by a shallow fringing reef close enough to shore that first-time snorkelers can reach live coral within minutes of wading in. Divers head slightly further out to sites where the reef wall drops away and the coral gardens open up.

🌊

Agatti Reef

The main fringing reef around the lagoon — sandy patches between coral heads, good visibility, and a gentle introduction for new divers and snorkelers.

🕳️

Agatti Channel

A deeper channel cut through the reef where currents bring in bigger pelagic fish — a step up for certified divers.

🪸

Reef's End

Coral gardens on the edge of the reef, mixing colourful hard coral with open sandy stretches favoured by reef fish.

⚠️

Handle with care

Like reefs everywhere, Agatti's coral is sensitive to bleaching and physical contact — reputable operators brief divers on reef-safe fins, no-touch rules, and reef-safe sunscreen.

🏖 Beaches

Coral-sand shorelines on both sides

Because Agatti is so narrow, its beaches wrap almost the whole island — the lagoon side is calm and shallow, good for wading and swimming with kids, while the ocean-facing edge sees a bit more surf.

🏝️

Agatti Beach

The island's main white coral-sand beach, with warm shallow water that stays swimmable well out from shore.

💧

Lagoon Beach

Calmer, glassier water on the lagoon side — the best spot for an easy first swim or a sunset walk.

🌴

Palm-lined shore

Most of the coastline is shaded by coconut palms right down to the sand — bring water shoes, since much of the shallows are coral rubble, not soft sand.

🤿 Water Sports

Built for time in the water

Local dive centres and the island's resort run most activities, with equipment and certified instructors on hand — no need to bring your own gear.

🤿

Scuba diving

Beginner-friendly sites close to shore, plus deeper dives for certified divers along the reef wall and channel.

🥽

Snorkelling

The shallow, calm lagoon makes this the easiest way to see coral and reef fish — suitable for non-swimmers with a life vest.

🚤

Glass-bottom boat

A dry option for seeing the reef and its fish from above the water — popular with families and non-divers.

🛶

Kayaking

Self-paced paddling along the calm lagoon shoreline, at your own pace and away from boat traffic.

🎣

Deep-sea fishing

Half-day boat trips out past the reef for bigger open-water species.

⛵

Island hopping

Short boat rides to nearby uninhabited islands like Bangaram, Thinnakara, and Parali for a quieter stretch of sand.

🐢 Marine Life

What's actually down there

Agatti's lagoon and reef support a dense mix of reef fish alongside larger visitors passing through the channel — most sightings happen on a single snorkel or dive.

🐢

Sea turtles

Regularly seen grazing on seagrass beds in the shallower parts of the lagoon.

🐟

Reef fish

Triggerfish, snappers, sweetlips, and butterflyfish crowd the coral heads in dense, colourful schools.

🦈

Reef sharks

Small reef sharks patrol the channel and drop-offs — generally shy and not a concern for divers.

🐠

Rays

Rays are a common sighting gliding over the sandy patches between coral formations.

🐡

Barracuda & jackfish

Larger open-water fish often seen cruising in loose schools near the channel and reef edge.

🐬

Dolphins

Boat trips beyond the reef occasionally spot dolphin pods, particularly early morning.

🧳 Plan Your Trip

Before you book

Lakshadweep is a restricted Union Territory — visiting Agatti takes a bit more paperwork than a typical beach trip, but the process is well established for tourists.

Agatti · Practical notes

  • Every visitor — Indian or foreign — needs an entry permit from the Lakshadweep Administration before travelling; apply well in advance of your flight.
  • Foreign nationals face additional clearance and are limited to a smaller set of tourist islands.
  • Best time to visit is roughly October to May, outside the monsoon, when seas are calmer and visibility for diving is at its best.
  • Accommodation on the island itself is limited — book resort stays well ahead, especially in peak season.
  • Onward island-hopping to nearby atolls runs by boat and is tide-dependent, so build slack into tight itineraries.
