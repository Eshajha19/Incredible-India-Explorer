🕊️ International Award for Non-Violence

Gandhi Peace Prize

India's prestigious international award recognising contributions toward social, economic, and political transformation through non-violence and other Gandhian values.

Year Instituted: 1995 Award Amount: ₹1 Crore First Recipient: Julius K. Nyerere (1995)

History of the Gandhi Peace Prize

The Gandhi Peace Prize, officially named the International Gandhi Peace Prize, was instituted by the Government of India in 1995 to commemorate the 125th birth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi. The award honours individuals and institutions that have made significant contributions toward social, economic, and political transformation through non-violence — the core principle of Gandhi's philosophy of Satyagraha (truth-force).

The prize was conceived not merely as a national honour but as an international award, reflecting Gandhi's global influence on peace movements, civil rights, and non-violent resistance. Recipients have included heads of state, grassroots organisations, and global institutions whose work embodies Gandhian values.

Unlike India's civilian honours (Bharat Ratna, Padma Awards), the Gandhi Peace Prize is open to any person, institution, or organisation regardless of nationality, race, language, or religion. The award is administered by the Ministry of Culture, Government of India, and is announced each year on 2nd October — Gandhi Jayanti.

Year Instituted

1995 — The 125th Birth Anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi

The Gandhi Peace Prize was instituted on 2nd October 1995, marking the 125th birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi. The Government of India, under Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao, established the award as a fitting tribute to Gandhi's enduring legacy of non-violence, truth, and social justice.

The first award was conferred the same year on Julius Kambarage Nyerere, the former President of Tanzania, for his contributions to non-violent struggle against colonialism and his commitment to African unity and social transformation.

The award was designed to be an annual recognition, though in some years no recipient was selected. The prize continues to be administered by the Ministry of Culture and remains one of India's most prestigious honours for international peace and non-violence.

Eligibility Criteria

The Gandhi Peace Prize is distinguished by its open eligibility. Unlike most national honours, it is not restricted to Indian citizens. The criteria are deliberately broad to reflect Gandhi's universal appeal:

  • Open to all: Any person, institution, or organisation — regardless of nationality, race, language, caste, religion, or gender — is eligible.
  • Non-violent contribution: The recipient's work must embody the Gandhian principles of non-violence (Ahimsa), truth (Satya), and social, economic, or political transformation through peaceful means.
  • No posthumous award: The prize is not conferred posthumously, except in extraordinary circumstances where the recipient passed away after the jury's decision was made.
  • Self-nomination not required: Nominations are invited from eminent persons, institutions, and governments. The jury also has the power to consider names suo motu (on its own motion).
  • No age or field restriction: The award recognises contributions across all fields — peace, human rights, education, environment, rural development, science, and culture — provided the work reflects Gandhian values.

Selection Committee

The Gandhi Peace Prize recipient is selected by a five-member jury chaired by the Prime Minister of India. The composition of the jury ensures a blend of executive authority, judicial wisdom, and cultural eminence:

Chairperson

Prime Minister of India

The Prime Minister serves as the chairperson of the jury, providing executive oversight to the selection process.

Member

Chief Justice of India

The Chief Justice of India brings judicial eminence and constitutional authority to the jury's deliberations.

Member

Leader of the Opposition

The Leader of the single largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha ensures bipartisanship in the selection process.

Member

Two Eminent Persons

Two eminent persons are nominated to the jury, bringing domain expertise in peace, non-violence, and social transformation.

The jury meets annually to evaluate nominations and its decision is final and binding. The award is announced on Gandhi Jayanti (2nd October).

Prize Components

💰

Cash Award

A cash prize of ₹1 Crore (10 million Indian Rupees) is presented to the recipient. For institutions/organisations, the amount may be shared between the institution and its key contributors.

📜

Citation

A citation scroll describing the recipient's contributions to non-violence and social transformation, signed by the Prime Minister.

🪙

Medallion

A commemorative medallion bearing the likeness of Mahatma Gandhi and a Sanskrit inscription from the Bhagavad Gita.

🕊️

Handcrafted Scroll

A handcrafted scroll created by traditional Indian artisans, celebrating the recipient's commitment to Gandhian values.

Notable International Recipients

The Gandhi Peace Prize has been awarded to individuals and institutions from across the world, reflecting its international character.

Julius K. Nyerere

1995 (First Recipient)

Former President of Tanzania. Honoured for his non-violent struggle against colonialism and his commitment to African unity, rural development, and social justice.

Nelson Mandela

2000

Former President of South Africa. Awarded for his lifelong non-violent struggle against apartheid and his commitment to truth, reconciliation, and racial equality.

John Hume

2001

Northern Irish politician and Nobel Peace laureate. Honoured for his non-violent campaign for peace in Northern Ireland and the Good Friday Agreement.

Grameen Bank

2000 (Shared with Muhammad Yunus)

Bangladeshi microfinance organisation. Awarded for its pioneering work in poverty eradication through microcredit, embodying Gandhi's vision of economic self-reliance for the poorest.

UNESCO

2014

The United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organisation. Honoured for its decades of work promoting peace, education, and cultural understanding across nations.

Yohei Sasakawa

2018

Japanese philanthropist and WHO Goodwill Ambassador. Honoured for his decades of work toward the elimination of leprosy and his commitment to Gandhian values of service to the marginalised.

Interesting Facts

🌍 An International Award

The Gandhi Peace Prize is one of the few Indian government awards that is open to non-Indians. Recipients have come from Tanzania, South Africa, Northern Ireland, Bangladesh, Germany, Japan, and beyond.

💰 Among the Richest Indian Awards

The cash component of ₹1 Crore makes it one of the highest-value awards conferred by the Government of India.

📅 Announced on Gandhi Jayanti

The recipient is announced each year on 2nd October — Mahatma Gandhi's birthday — linking the award directly to the legacy it commemorates.

🔄 Years Without a Recipient

The jury is not obligated to select a recipient every year. In several years — including 2004–2008 and 2010–2013 — no award was conferred, reflecting the jury's commitment to maintaining the award's prestige.

👥 Shared Awards

The prize has been shared between individuals and institutions — e.g., Nelson Mandela and Grameen Bank (1999) — to recognise both personal leadership and institutional impact.

🏛️ Administered by the Ministry of Culture

Unlike the Padma Awards (administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs), the Gandhi Peace Prize is administered by the Ministry of Culture, reflecting its cultural and international character.
