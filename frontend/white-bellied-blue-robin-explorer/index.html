White-bellied Blue Robin

Sholicola albiventris - A rare endemic bird of the Western Ghats.

Introduction & Scientific Name

The White-bellied Blue Robin (Sholicola albiventris) is a small passerine bird endemic to the high altitude shola forests of the Western Ghats in South India. Previously classified under the genus Myiomela, recent molecular studies placed it in the genus Sholicola.

Distribution & Habitat

It is restricted to the shola-grassland mosaics of the southern Western Ghats, primarily in Kerala and Tamil Nadu, at elevations above 1500 meters. These birds prefer the dense, dark undergrowth of montane evergreen forests.

Behaviour & Diet

This species is highly terrestrial and secretive, often foraging on the forest floor among leaf litter. It feeds primarily on insects, larvae, and small invertebrates. It is known to flick its wings and tail frequently while moving.

Breeding

Breeding occurs during the pre-monsoon and monsoon months (April to August). The nest is a cup made of moss and roots, usually placed in a bank or among rocks. The clutch size is typically two eggs.

Conservation Status & Threats

Currently listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. The primary threats include habitat fragmentation due to the expansion of tea and cardamom plantations, invasive plant species altering the understory, and climate change affecting the delicate shola ecosystem.

Protected Areas

Found in several protected areas including Eravikulam National Park, Anamalai Tiger Reserve, and Periyar Tiger Reserve.

Interesting Facts

  • Despite its name, it is not closely related to the true robins of Europe.
  • It has a distinctive, melodious whistling call that echoes through the misty sholas.
