Brand Origins & The Swadeshi Spirit

Born out of the Indian Independence Movement, Parle pioneered indigenous food manufacturing to offer Indians wholesome, affordable treats.

Founder Vision
🏭

Mohanlal Dayal Chauhan

In 1929, Silk trader Mohanlal Dayal Chauhan purchased an abandoned factory between Vile Parle and Andheri in Mumbai. Inspired by Mahatma Gandhi's Swadeshi movement, he traveled to Germany to master confectionery manufacturing techniques.

First Sweets
🍬

12 Craftsmen & German Machinery

The company started with just 12 family members working as confectioners. The first products were orange candies, peppermint sweets, and boiled toffees. Legend has it the founders were so busy launching production they forgot to name the company, so it was named after the locality—Parle.

Biscuit Revolution
🍪

1939: Parle Gluco Launch

During the British Raj, biscuits were expensive luxury items imported for the elites. In 1939, Parle manufactured Parle Gluco—an affordable, energy-dense glucose biscuit crafted for common Indian families and soldiers during WWII.

"Bharat Ka Apna Biscuit"

Parle Gluco was created as an act of Swadeshi self-reliance, breaking the monopoly of British biscuit brands like Huntley & Palmers and United Biscuits in colonial India.
