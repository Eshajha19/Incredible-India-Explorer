Sultanpur Wetland

A Paradise for Migratory Birds Near Delhi

Gurugram District, Haryana, India

View Bird Checklist View Map
Ecology

A Thriving Freshwater Ecosystem

Dynamic Habitats

This freshwater wetland features seasonal water level fluctuations that create diverse micro-habitats, including open water, marshes, grasslands, and scattered woodland patches essential for different bird groups.

Rich Food Web

The ecological importance of Sultanpur lies in its prolific food web. The nutrient-rich waters support abundant aquatic insects, molluscs, and small fish, which in turn sustain hundreds of resident and migratory bird species.

Seasonal Visitors

Winter: Peak migration brings thousands of birds. Summer: Resident species dominate the landscape. Monsoon: Brings crucial wetland regeneration.

Avian Wonders

Flyways and Feathers

An important stopover on the Central Asian Flyway.

Painted Stork

Near Threatened

A beautifully patterned resident bird that forms noisy nesting colonies in the acacia trees surrounding the wetland.

Northern Pintail

Winter Migrant

Arriving from Europe and Siberia, these elegant dabbling ducks flock to the shallow waters during the peak winter months.

Bar-headed Goose

High-Altitude Flyer

One of the world's highest flying birds, migrating over the Himalayas to spend the winter in the Indian plains.

Eurasian Spoonbill

Winter Migrant

Easily identified by its spatulate bill, which it sweeps side-to-side in the shallow mud to catch small aquatic prey.

Bird Photography

Sultanpur is a haven for wildlife photographers. Purpose-built photography hides and strategically placed observation towers offer incredible vantage points. For the best lighting and bird activity, early morning sunrise photography is highly recommended. Always practice responsible wildlife photography: minimize noise, do not disturb nesting birds, and stick to the marked trails. A telephoto lens (400mm+) is ideal.

Conservation

Protecting the Habitat

  • Protected Area Management: Declared a National Park in 1991 to elevate protection status and enforce anti-poaching measures.
  • Water Conservation: Artificial pumping and habitat restoration efforts maintain water levels during dry seasons to support resident fauna.
  • Eco-Tourism Initiatives: Educational programs and visitor awareness centers help promote responsible tourism and wetland conservation.
Facts

Did You Know?

  • Located less than an hour's drive from Delhi, making it an accessible urban escape.
  • Despite its compact size (1.42 sq km), it supports over 250 bird species annually.
  • Designated as a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance.
  • A globally recognized Important Bird Area (IBA).
  • The best time to visit is from October to March for peak winter migration.
