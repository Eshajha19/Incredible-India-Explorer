Bhagat Singh

"Inquilab Zindabad!" - The intellectual revolutionary who shook the foundations of the British Empire.

The Scholar Revolutionary

Born on September 28, 1907, in Banga, Punjab, Bhagat Singh was not merely a man of action but a profound intellectual. Deeply influenced by the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919) and the Nankana Sahib incident, he abandoned his school studies to join the non-cooperation movement.

Unlike many of his contemporaries, Bhagat Singh was an avid reader of European revolutionary literature, socialism, and Marxism. He co-founded the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA), aiming not just for the transfer of power, but for a complete socialist revolution that would end the exploitation of man by man.

Historical Timeline

Revolutionary Activities

Saunders Assassination (1928)

To avenge the death of Lala Lajpat Rai, who succumbed to injuries sustained during a police lathi charge, Bhagat Singh and his comrades mistakenly killed British police officer John Saunders in Lahore.

Central Assembly Bombing (1929)

Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw low-intensity smoke bombs into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi. They threw the bombs to protest repressive laws, deliberately allowing themselves to be arrested to use the court as a platform for their message.

Hunger Strike in Jail

While imprisoned, he led a historic 116-day hunger strike demanding equal rights, better food, and dignity for Indian political prisoners compared to their European counterparts.
