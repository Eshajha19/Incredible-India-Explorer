Origins of Resistance

Political, Economic, Social & Military Causes

Decades of colonial expansion and exploitation fueled discontent across all sections of Indian society.

👑

Political Causes & Annexations

Lord Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse annexed Satara, Sambalpur, Nagpur, and Jhansi. The aggressive annexation of Awadh (1856) outraged both rulers and sepoys.

🪙

Economic Exploitation

Heavy land revenue taxation, destruction of traditional Indian handicrafts by British manufactured imports, and eviction of peasants created severe agrarian distress.

📜

Social & Religious Concerns

Fears of forced religious conversion by Christian missionaries and British social legislations created deep anxiety among conservative Hindu and Muslim populations.

⚔️

Military Grievances & Trigger

Indian sepoys faced pay discrimination, lack of promotion, and racial arrogance. The introduction of Enfield Rifle cartridges greased with cow and pig fat served as the immediate spark.

Impact & Transformation

Before vs. After 1857 & Transfer of Power

Before 1857 (EIC Rule)

  • Ruled directly by the British East India Company under Royal Charters.
  • Doctrine of Lapse actively expanding British territorial annexation.
  • Indian sepoys formed the vast majority of military strength with low officer representation.

After 1857 (British Crown Rule)

  • Government of India Act 1858 transferred power to the British Crown (Queen Victoria).
  • Abolition of EIC and Doctrine of Lapse; Princely States guaranteed status quo.
  • Reorganization of Indian Army (martial race theory, increased British troop ratio).
  • Sowed seeds of modern Indian nationalism and unity.
