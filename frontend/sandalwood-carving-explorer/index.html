Historical Overview

2,000 Years of
Fragrant Heritage

From ancient trade routes and Vijayanagara temples to Mysore's royal court and modern GI-protected exports — the story of Karnataka's most celebrated craft.

Sandalwood (Santalum album) has been India's most prized aromatic timber for over two millennia. Karnataka's Western Ghats region produces some of the world's finest heartwood — dense, fragrant, and uniquely suited to fine carving. The craft of sculpting this wood into devotional idols, decorative objects, and architectural panels has been sustained by hereditary artisan communities (shilpis) across Mysore and its surrounding regions.

Unlike most regional crafts, Karnataka's sandalwood carving is inseparable from its royal and temple histories. The Wadiyar dynasty of Mysore transformed it into a diplomatic art form — gifting ornate sandalwood pieces to kings and foreign dignitaries. Today, Geographical Indication (GI) tags protect the craft, while government craft schools and e-commerce platforms are opening new markets for the next generation of artists.
