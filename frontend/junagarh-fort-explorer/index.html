👑 Bikaner, Rajasthan · Rathore

Junagarh Fort Explorer

Rising from the flat plains of Bikaner, Junagarh Fort is one of the few great Rajasthan citadels built on level ground — a red sandstone masterpiece of palaces, courtyards and decorative art created for Raja Rai Singh in the late 1500s.

The Old Fort of Bikaner

An Introduction to Junagarh Fort

Junagarh Fort stands in the heart of Bikaner, Rajasthan, the capital of the former princely state of the same name. Unlike the hilltop citadels of Jodhpur or Chittorgarh, Junagarh was built on flat ground — a deliberate choice that makes it one of India's most unusual and best-preserved fortress-palaces.

Commissioned in 1589 by Raja Rai Singh and completed in just five years, the fort combines Rajput military might with the decorative arts of Mughal and Gujarati traditions. Its name, meaning "Old Fort", was given in the early 1900s by Maharaja Ganga Singh — a mark of respect for the centuries the fort had already guarded Bikaner.

📜 At a Glance

  • Location: Bikaner, Rajasthan, India.
  • Built: 1589–1594 CE by Raja Rai Singh.
  • Setting: Flat ground — rare for a Rajasthani fort.
  • Stone: Red sandstone, with marble and gold leaf inside.
  • Museum: Opened to the public in 1961.
A Fort for a Warrior Raja

History and Raja Rai Singh

Raja Rai Singh, the sixth ruler of Bikaner (r. 1571–1612), was one of the most celebrated soldiers of his age — a trusted general of the Mughal emperor Akbar, honoured with the title of Rao and gifted extensive jagirs for his service in imperial campaigns. He used his wealth and influence to raise a fortress worthy of his standing.

Begun in 1589 and completed in 1594, the fort was first named Chintamani, after the legendary wish-granting jewel. It served as the seat of the Bikaner court for centuries and, unlike many Rajasthani forts, was never captured by assault. In the early 20th century Maharaja Ganga Singh renamed it Junagarh — "Old Fort" — by which it is known today.

🏛️ The Fort at a Glance

  • Raja Rai Singh: Warrior-general of Akbar and founder of the fort.
  • Chintamani: The fort's original name — "the wish-granting jewel".
  • Ganga Singh: Renamed it Junagarh in the early 1900s.
  • Never captured: The fort has never fallen to an enemy assault.
  • Museum 1961: Opened to the public by the royal family.
Walls, Bastions and Gates

Fortifications

Junagarh's defences protect it on every side — walls of red sandstone studded with bastions and pierced by four major gates.

🧱

Walls and Bastions

The fort's circuit of walls runs about 986 metres around the complex, reinforced by 37 bastions — vantage points that allowed the garrison to sweep every approach with fire.

🌅

Suraj Pol — Sun Gate

The principal eastern gate, named for the sun god Surya and aligned so the rising sun floods its arch — the ceremonial entrance of the fort.

🌙

Chand Pol — Moon Gate

The western gate, named for the moon, completing the fort's sun-and-moon pairing and guarding the approach from the old city.

🚪

Karan Pol

An inner gate named in honour of the Karan Singh era, one of the successive barriers that slowed any attacker who passed the outer walls.

🛡️

Daulat Pol and Fateh Pol

Further gates of the circuit — Daulat Pol ("wealth") and Fateh Pol ("victory") — each a named statement of the dynasty's ambitions.

⚔️

Never Breached

Despite sieges and shifting alliances across 400 years, no enemy ever carried Junagarh's walls — a record few Indian forts can claim.

Palaces of the Bikaner Court

Royal Palaces

Inside the walls, successive maharajas built and embellished a series of palaces, each reflecting the taste of its builder.

🏰

Chandra Mahal

The oldest palace in the fort, built by Raja Rai Singh himself — the original royal residence of the founder and the heart of the early fort.

👑

Karan Mahal

Built by Maharaja Karan Singh (r. 1631–1674), this durbar hall became the ceremonial seat of the Bikaner court for generations.

⚜️

Anup Mahal

The administrative heart of the fort, built by Anup Singh (r. 1669–1698) — its chambers glowing with gold leaf and painted panels.

🌸

Phool Mahal

A pleasure palace of Maharaja Ganga Singh, its painted ceiling of flowers and birds among the finest interiors in the fort.

🎭

Ganga Mahal

Built for Ganga Singh's European-style entertainments, its rooms included a ballroom where the maharaja hosted colonial-era guests.

☁️

Badal Mahal

The "Palace of Clouds", its walls and ceilings painted with monsoon scenes — dark rain clouds and falling rain that delighted Bikaner's desert court.

🏯 Courtyards at a Glance

  • Chandra Mahal courtyards: The oldest open courts of the fort.
  • Daulat Khana: Treasury courtyard where the state's wealth was stored.
  • Har Mandir: The fort's royal temple, set within its own court.
  • Zenana courts: Private courtyards of the royal women.
Open Courts and Cool Retreats

Courtyards

Like all great Rajasthani forts, Junagarh is organised around a series of courtyards that link its palaces, temples and service buildings. These open courts bring light and air into the dense complex, and served as the stages for court ceremonies, festivals and the everyday life of the zenana.

The most important are the courts of the Chandra Mahal, the oldest part of the fort; the Daulat Khana, the treasury courtyard; and the court of the Har Mandir, the fort's royal temple dedicated to the family deity. Each courtyard is framed by jharokha balconies and carved sandstone facades.

Art Inside the Stone

Decorative Interiors

Junagarh is celebrated less for its scale than for the exquisite decoration of its interiors — among the finest surviving examples of Rajput palace art.

🖌️

Painted Walls and Ceilings

From the monsoon clouds of the Badal Mahal to the floral panels of the Phool Mahal, the fort's walls are covered in vibrant miniature-style painting.

✨

Gold Leaf

The Anup Mahal glows with gold-leaf decoration on its ceilings and panels — the administrative palace made radiant as a display of state power.

🪟

Mirror Work and Jharokhas

Delicate mirror inlay, coloured glass and carved sandstone balconies combine to scatter light through the royal chambers.

🧵

Textiles and Carpets

The museum preserves the palace's furnishings — fine carpets, embroidered hangings and court textiles that once lined these rooms.

🖼️

Miniature Paintings

The Bikaner school of miniature painting flourished under royal patronage, and many fine examples are preserved in the fort museum.

⚔️

Weapons Gallery

The museum's armoury displays swords, daggers, shields and firearms of the Bikaner rulers, many inlaid with gold and set with gems.

Traditions in Stone

Architectural Influences

Junagarh is a meeting place of three great traditions — Rajput fort-building, Mughal courtly art and Gujarati decorative craft.

🏰

Rajput Tradition

The fort's bastions, gates and courtyards follow the classic Rajput military plan — the same family of fortifications as the great forts of Mewar and Marwar.

🕌

Mughal Influence

Rai Singh served at Akbar's court, and his fort shows it — Mughal-style domes, arches and the formal garden tradition appear throughout the complex.

🌸

Gujarati Craft

Jali screens, mirror work and the fine painted ornament of the interiors reflect the Gujarati decorative arts brought north by travelling craftsmen.

Key Dates

Historical Timeline

1589

Raja Rai Singh begins construction of the fort, first named Chintamani.

1594

The fort is completed in just five years, a remarkably fast build.

1631–1674

Karan Singh's reign adds the Karan Mahal durbar hall.

1669–1698

Anup Singh builds the gold-leafed Anup Mahal.

1888–1943

Ganga Singh's long reign adds the Phool Mahal and Ganga Mahal.

Early 1900s

The fort is renamed Junagarh — "Old Fort" — by Ganga Singh.

1961

The fort museum opens to the public, preserving the collections of the Bikaner court.

Did You Know?

Interesting Facts

A Fort on Flat Ground

Junagarh is one of the only major Rajasthan forts not built on a hill — its strength came from walls and guns, not cliffs.

"Old Fort" — But Not the Oldest

Renamed Junagarh in the 1900s, the fort is actually newer than many of Rajasthan's "newer"-sounding citadels.

37 Bastions

The walls are studded with 37 bastions, giving the garrison overlapping fields of fire around the entire circuit.

Sunrise at Suraj Pol

The Sun Gate faces due east, and each sunrise lights the ceremonial arch of the fort's main entrance.

Built in Five Years

Rai Singh's fortress was raised in just five years (1589–1594) — an astonishing pace for a complex of this scale.

Royal Museum Since 1961

The Junagarh museum, opened by the Bikaner royal family, displays weapons, paintings, costumes and ceremonial objects of the court.
