🥁 Gujarat · 1932 – Present

Dhollywood Explorer

Step into Gujarati cinema, named for the dhol drum that beats through its music. From Narsinh Mehta (1932), the first Gujarati talkie, to National Award-winning classics and a new wave of urban storytelling, discover the films and artists behind Gujarat's own "Dhollywood".

Where Hollywood Meets the Dhol

From Narsinh Mehta to a Thriving Regional Industry

Gujarati cinema traces its roots to the silent era, when numerous Gujarati and Parsi filmmakers were active across early Indian studios — including director Rustomji Dhotiwala, whose 1919 silent film "Bhagat Soordas" drew on a story by Gujarati writer Champshi Udeshi. The industry's talkie era formally began on 9 April 1932 with "Narsinh Mehta", directed by Nanubhai Vakil and based on the life of the revered saint-poet.

The years after independence saw a surge in production, with 74 Gujarati films made between 1946 and 1952 alone. The industry earned its nickname "Dhollywood" from the dhol, a double-headed drum central to Gujarati music, while some also call it "Gollywood" — a blend of Gujarat and Hollywood.

🎞️ At a Glance

  • First talkie: Narsinh Mehta (1932), directed by Nanubhai Vakil.
  • Industry hub: Ahmedabad and Mumbai.
  • Name origin: "Dhollywood" — named after the dhol drum; also called "Gollywood".
  • 100-film milestone: Reached in 1961, the year Gujarat and Maharashtra became separate states.
From Saint-Poets to Urban Stories

Notable Films of Dhollywood

The films that marked Gujarati cinema's biggest milestones, from its founding talkie to its modern urban revival.

🎥

Narsinh Mehta (1932)

The first Gujarati talkie, directed by Nanubhai Vakil, dramatising the life of the saint-poet Narsinh Mehta.

🎨

Liludi Dharti (1968)

Gujarati cinema's first colour film, marking a technical leap for the industry in its golden era.

🏅

Kanku (1969)

Adapted from a 1936 short story, this film won the National Film Award for Best Feature Film in Gujarati.

🌆

The Better Half (2010)

Directed by Ashish Kakkad, this film launched the "Urban Gujarati Film" trend, moving stories from villages to cities.

🎭

Bey Yaar (2014)

A buddy drama that became a landmark of Dhollywood's 2010s revival, drawing urban and youth audiences back to cinemas.

🎬

Chhello Divas (2015)

A college-life comedy-drama that became one of the highest-grossing Gujarati films of its era.

🏆 Voices Behind the Camera

  • Nanubhai Vakil: Director of Narsinh Mehta (1932), the film that founded Dhollywood.
  • Naresh Kanodiya: Widely regarded as Gujarati cinema's first megastar, rising to fame in the 1980s–90s.
  • Upendra Trivedi: Veteran actor-director central to Dhollywood's golden-age films.
  • Nirupa Roy: Made her acting debut in the Gujarati film Ranakdevi (1946) before becoming a major Bollywood star.
Directors, Writers & Performers

Award-Winning Artists of Dhollywood

Nanubhai Vakil laid the foundation of Gujarati talkies with Narsinh Mehta in 1932, and the industry's post-independence surge brought forward performers who would shape its golden age. Naresh Kanodiya became the industry's first true megastar, his popularity enduring even through the difficult years of the 1990s when audiences began drifting away from regional cinema.

Actress Nirupa Roy began her career in the Gujarati film Ranakdevi in 1946, years before she became one of Hindi cinema's most recognised faces, frequently cast as the mother in Amitabh Bachchan's landmark films. Her early roots in Dhollywood reflect the industry's long history of nurturing talent that would later shine on the national stage.

Milestones

Chronology of Gujarati Cinema

From silent-era beginnings to a modern, urban-driven revival.

1919

Silent-Era Roots

Parsi-Gujarati director Rustomji Dhotiwala releases "Bhagat Soordas", one of many silent films shaped by Gujarati filmmakers of the era.

1932

Narsinh Mehta Releases

The first Gujarati talkie premieres, directed by Nanubhai Vakil — widely regarded as the true beginning of Dhollywood.

1946–1952

Post-Independence Surge

74 Gujarati films are produced in this period alone, many drawing on saints, mythology and folk tales.

1961

The 100-Film Milestone

Gujarati cinema crosses its 100th film the same year Gujarat and Maharashtra separate into distinct states.

1968–1969

Colour and National Recognition

"Liludi Dharti" brings Gujarati cinema its first colour film, followed a year later by "Kanku" winning a National Film Award.

2010s–Present

The Urban Revival

Films like "The Better Half", "Bey Yaar" and "Chhello Divas" usher in a new, urban-focused wave of Dhollywood storytelling.
