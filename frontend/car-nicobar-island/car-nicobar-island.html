Car Nicobar

A flat, coral-fringed island and the administrative headquarters of the Nicobar district — home to the largest Nicobarese population, sweeping coconut plantations, quiet beaches and a distinct island culture.

Geography, Culture & Everyday Life

🗺️ Geography

Car Nicobar is a low-lying, roughly oval island in the northern Nicobar group, unlike the hillier, forested islands further south. Its flat terrain and sandy soil are ringed by coral reefs and fringing beaches.

Malacca, on the island's western coast, serves as the administrative headquarters of the Nicobar district, connecting the island to Port Blair by air and sea.

  • Flat, low-lying island in the northern Nicobar group
  • Surrounded by coral reefs and sandy beaches
  • Malacca is the Nicobar district's administrative headquarters

🎎 Culture

Car Nicobar is home to the largest population of the Nicobarese, an indigenous community with their own language, customs and communal way of life organised around villages rather than individual land ownership.

Traditional round or oval huts on stilts, communal feasts, and the annual Ossuary Feast (a ceremony honouring ancestors) are central to Nicobarese cultural life, alongside more recent Christian influences introduced in the 19th century.

  • Home to the largest Nicobarese population in the islands
  • Traditional stilt huts and community-based village life
  • Ossuary Feast honouring ancestors is a key cultural event

🥥 Coconut Plantations

Coconut palms dominate the island's landscape, planted in dense groves that shape both its scenery and its economy. Nearly every household is connected to coconut cultivation in some way, whether for food, oil or trade.

Copra (dried coconut kernel) production has traditionally been a mainstay of the local economy, with coconut products used in cooking, construction and craftwork across Nicobarese villages.

  • Dense coconut groves cover much of the island
  • Copra production is a long-standing local industry
  • Coconut products used in food, construction and crafts

💰 Economy

Alongside coconut and copra production, the island's economy includes fishing, small-scale agriculture, and government services centred around Malacca, since the island hosts key administrative offices for the Nicobar district.

An Indian Air Force base on the island also contributes to local employment and infrastructure, while sea and air links to Port Blair support trade and the movement of goods and people.

  • Coconut, copra production and fishing form the traditional economy
  • Government offices in Malacca support local employment
  • Indian Air Force base contributes to infrastructure and jobs

🏖️ Beaches

Car Nicobar's coastline features quiet, coral-fringed beaches such as those near Malacca and Kakana, with calm, shallow waters that reflect the island's flat, sheltered geography.

Traditional hodi (dugout canoe) races are held along the coast during local festivals, combining the island's beach culture with its seafaring traditions.

  • Coral-fringed beaches near Malacca and Kakana
  • Calm, shallow coastal waters
  • Traditional hodi boat races held during festivals

📜 History

Car Nicobar has long been inhabited by the Nicobarese, with historical trade contacts across the Bay of Bengal. Danish and later British administrations established a presence in the wider Nicobar Islands from the 18th and 19th centuries onward.

Christian missionary activity from the 1800s introduced significant cultural and religious change, while the island later became part of independent India's Union Territory of Andaman & Nicobar Islands, retaining strong Nicobarese self-governance in village affairs.

  • Long history of Nicobarese settlement and regional trade
  • Danish and British colonial contact from the 18th-19th centuries
  • Part of the Andaman & Nicobar Union Territory since Indian independence
