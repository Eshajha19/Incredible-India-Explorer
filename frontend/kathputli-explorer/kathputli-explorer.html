📍 Rajasthan, India

Kathputli

Rajasthan's iconic string puppet tradition, combining ancient storytelling with vibrant craftsmanship.

Explore the Tradition
—  History & Origins  —

A Legacy of Storytelling

The origins of Kathputli (from Kath meaning wood and Putli meaning doll) can be traced back over a thousand years to the desert state of Rajasthan. Historically, these puppets were crafted and performed by the Bhat community, an agricultural group who doubled as traveling entertainers and storytellers.

These performances served not only as entertainment but also as a powerful medium for moral education, social awareness, and oral history. The tradition enjoyed royal patronage for centuries before evolving into a popular folk art form that continues to charm audiences today.

—  The Performance  —

The Magic of the Strings

Manipulation

Unlike complex marionettes, Kathputlis are typically controlled by a single continuous string looped over the puppeteer's fingers, creating distinct, jerky, and rhythmic movements.

The Stage (Tambu)

Performances are held in a makeshift tent called a Tambu. The puppeteers remain hidden behind a brightly colored curtain, bringing the wooden figures to life.

Music & Voice

The performance is accompanied by the high-pitched squeaking of a bamboo reed instrument called a Boli, along with traditional dholak beats and passionate folk singing by the narrators.

Narrative Style

Stories often revolve around historical heroes like King Vikramaditya or Amar Singh Rathore, blending myth, romance, and bravery with interactive dialogue and humor.

—  The Cast  —

Character Showcase

Amar Singh Rathore

The Hero (Raja)

Appearance: Wears a grand Rajput turban, a vibrant angarkha (tunic), and sports an impressive, fierce mustache.

Role: Often the central protagonist, representing honor, bravery, and the legendary history of Rajasthan.

Anarkali

The Dancer

Appearance: Adorned in a beautiful, heavy ghagra (skirt) that flares dramatically when spun.

Role: Provides mesmerizing dance interludes, symbolizing beauty and grace within the royal courts.

The Snake Charmer

Folk Entertainer

Appearance: Dressed in traditional nomadic attire, often holding a pungi (flute) and accompanied by a fabric snake.

Role: Represents rural village life, adding humor, mysticism, and local color to the main narrative.

—  Craftsmanship  —

Making a Kathputli

1

Wood Selection

Artisans select lightweight Mango wood (Aam) or Ardu wood for the head and torso to ensure easy manipulation.

2

Carving & Shaping

The wood is meticulously carved to form distinct facial features—large eyes, prominent noses, and expressive lips.

3

Painting

The carved head is painted with a base coat, followed by intricate details using vibrant, oil-based colors.

4

Costume & Decoration

Old fabrics, sequins, and mirror-work are used to sew traditional Rajasthani outfits (ghagras and turbans).

5

String Attachment

Puppets usually lack legs; their trailing skirts hide the absence. Strings are attached to the head and the back for manipulation.
