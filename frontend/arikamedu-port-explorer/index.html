Arikamedu Ancient Port Explorer

Step into Arikamedu, the legendary 2,000-year-old Indo-Roman trade emporium identified as Poduke in Greco-Roman records. Discover Mortimer Wheeler's famous brick warehouses, Arretine redware pottery, and glass bead craft.

Poduke of the Erythraean Sea

The Coromandel Emporium of Antiquity

Situated on the right bank of the Ariyankuppam River near Puducherry, Arikamedu (historically Arikamedu / Poduke) was a prominent ancient port city on the East Coast of India.

Identified by scholars as Poduke mentioned in Ptolemy's Geographia and the 1st-century Greco-Roman text Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, Arikamedu served as a vital commercial hub connecting the early Chola kingdom region with the Roman Empire and Southeast Asia from 200 BCE to 200 CE.

🏛️ Arikamedu Snapshot

  • Location: Ariyankuppam river mouth, 4 km south of Puducherry.
  • Historic Identification: Poduke in Greco-Roman trade manuals.
  • Excavations: Sir Mortimer Wheeler (1945), Jean-Marie Casal (1947), Vimala Begley (1989-1992).
  • Key Exports: Indo-Pacific glass beads, fine muslin textiles, gemstones, shell objects.
Chronology of Arikamedu

Location & Historical Timeline

Tracking Arikamedu's transition from an early iron-age coastal settlement to a major Mediterranean trading emporium.

c. 200 BCE

Early Megalithic Settlement

Fishermen and bead crafters settle along the Ariyankuppam river estuarine bank.

1st Century BCE - 1st Century CE

Peak Indo-Roman Trade Hub

Roman merchants establish a permanent factory colony. Import of Mediterranean amphorae wine jars and Arretine tableware.

2nd Century CE

Industrial Bead Manufacturing Era

Arikamedu becomes the primary global manufacturing workshop for drawing Indo-Pacific glass and semi-precious stone beads.

1945 CE

Sir Mortimer Wheeler Excavations

Sir Mortimer Wheeler conducts scientific stratigraphical excavations, proving Roman trade chronologies in South Asia.

Global Networks

Trade Routes & Mediterranean Partners

Arikamedu connected Roman merchants from Italy and Egypt with Indian artisans and Southeast Asian markets.

🏛️

Roman Empire & Mediterranean

Direct maritime commerce with Rome, importing amphorae containing Italian wine (Falernian), Spanish olive oil, and garum fish sauce.

📿

Indo-Pacific Glass Bead Export

Arikamedu glass and stone beads were exported across the Indian Ocean to Sri Lanka, Thailand, Vietnam, and Indonesia.

🧵

Muslin & Silk Textiles

Fine woven cotton textiles and dyed muslins produced in Tamil country were highly valued by Roman patricians.

⛵

Coromandel Coastal Galleys

Served as a coastal transshipment port connecting the Bay of Bengal trade lanes with Malabar and Ceylon.

🔍 Key Archaeological Discoveries

  • Arretine Red-Gloss Pottery: Stamped Roman tableware produced in Arezzo, Italy, dating from 30 BCE to 45 CE.
  • Roman Amphora Fragments: Over 1,000 double-handled amphora shards bearing Mediterranean clay signatures.
  • Brick Warehouse Structure: A 50-meter-long brick warehouse with drainage sluices built along the riverbank.
  • Engraved Gem Intaglios: Intaglios carved from quartz depicting Cupid, Augustus, and Roman eagles.
Scientific Stratigraphy

Archaeological Findings of Arikamedu

Excavations led by Sir Mortimer Wheeler in 1945 established Arikamedu as an archaeological benchmark for South Asian history. By cross-dating stamped Roman Arretine pottery of known Mediterranean manufacture with local Megalithic ceramics, Wheeler established precise chronologies for Indian archaeology.

Later excavations by Dr. Vimala Begley revealed that Arikamedu was an active industrial craft hub long before Roman contact, continuing its bead-making tradition well into the medieval period.
