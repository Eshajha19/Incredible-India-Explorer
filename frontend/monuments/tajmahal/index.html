📖 About

The Taj Mahal

A mausoleum built as a testament to love, blending Persian, Islamic, and Indian architectural traditions.

The Taj Mahal is an ivory-white marble mausoleum on the right bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh. It was commissioned in 1631 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan to house the tomb of his beloved wife, Mumtaz Mahal. It also houses the tomb of Shah Jahan himself.

The tomb is the centrepiece of a 17-hectare (42-acre) complex, which includes a mosque and a guest house, and is set in formal gardens bounded on three sides by a crenellated wall.

Construction of the mausoleum was completed in 1653, employing thousands of artisans and craftsmen. The Taj Mahal is regarded by many as the best example of Mughal architecture and a symbol of India's rich history.

📜 History

Historical Background

The story of love, loss, and an architectural masterpiece.

👑

Emperor Shah Jahan

The fifth Mughal emperor, Shah Jahan, commissioned the Taj Mahal as a mausoleum for his favourite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who died during childbirth in 1631.

💕

Mumtaz Mahal

Born Arjumand Banu Begum, she was the emperor's beloved wife. Her death left Shah Jahan heartbroken, leading him to build the world's most beautiful tomb in her memory.

🏗️

Construction

Construction began in 1632 and took 22 years to complete. Over 20,000 artisans, including architects, calligraphers, and stonecutters, were employed.

📜

Legacy

The Taj Mahal has become a global symbol of love and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World, attracting millions of visitors annually.

🏛️ Architecture

Mughal Architectural Style

A masterpiece blending Persian, Islamic, and Indian architectural traditions.

⬜

White Marble

The Taj Mahal is built from Makrana white marble, which changes colour throughout the day.

🕌

Main Dome

The iconic onion-shaped dome rises to 73 metres (240 feet) and is surrounded by four smaller domes.

📏

Four Minarets

Four towering minarets, each 40 metres (130 feet) high, flank the mausoleum.

🎨

Decorative Elements

Intricate calligraphy, floral motifs, and precious stone inlay work adorn the walls.

✨ Highlights

Architectural Highlights

The Taj Mahal's most remarkable features.

01

The Great Gate

The monumental entrance gate features 11 smaller domes and intricate calligraphy.

02

The Tomb Chamber

The octagonal chamber houses the cenotaphs with exquisite marble screens.

03

Pietra Dura Inlay

Precious stones including lapis lazuli, jade, and turquoise are inlaid into the marble.

04

Calligraphy

Verses from the Quran are inscribed in calligraphy on the main gateway and tomb walls.

05

Reflecting Pool

A long water channel with reflecting pools mirrors the mausoleum.

06

Perfect Symmetry

The entire complex is perfectly symmetrical, reflecting the Mughal concept of paradise.

🏆 UNESCO

UNESCO World Heritage

Recognized as a masterpiece of human creative genius.

The Taj Mahal was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, recognized as "the jewel of Muslim art in India and one of the universally admired masterpieces of the world's heritage."

It is listed under Criteria (i): representing a masterpiece of human creative genius, and (ii): exhibiting an important interchange of human values over time.

💡 Facts

Interesting Facts

Fascinating details about the Taj Mahal.

01

Changing Colours

The marble changes colour throughout the day — pinkish in the morning, white in the afternoon, and golden in the moonlight.

02

Optical Illusion

The minarets are slightly tilted outward so that in an earthquake, they would fall away from the main tomb.

03

20,000 Artisans

Over 20,000 workers were employed in the construction of the Taj Mahal.

04

Precious Stones

The Taj Mahal features inlay work with 28 types of precious and semi-precious stones.

05

Black Taj Myth

Legend says Shah Jahan planned a black marble Taj Mahal across the river, but this remains a myth.

06

7 Wonders

The Taj Mahal was named one of the New Seven Wonders of the World in 2007.
