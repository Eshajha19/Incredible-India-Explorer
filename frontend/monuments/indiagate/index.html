📖 About

India Gate

A solemn tribute to the soldiers of the British Indian Army who sacrificed their lives in World War I and the Afghan Wars.

The India Gate is a war memorial located in New Delhi, India. It stands as a tribute to the 70,000 soldiers of the British Indian Army who lost their lives in World War I (1914-1918) and the Third Anglo-Afghan War (1919).

Designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens, the India Gate is an imposing 42-metre high archway, reminiscent of the Arc de Triomphe in Paris. The monument was originally called the All India War Memorial and was inaugurated on February 12, 1931, by Lord Irwin, the Viceroy of India.

At the base of the India Gate, the Amar Jawan Jyoti (Flame of the Immortal Soldier) was added in 1971 to honor the soldiers who died in the Indo-Pakistan War of 1971. The flame burns continuously as a reminder of their sacrifice.

🏛️ Architecture

Architectural Design

A masterpiece of imperial architecture.

🏛️

Arch Design

India Gate is a 42-metre high archway inspired by the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, blending European and Indian architectural elements.

📝

Inscriptions

The walls are inscribed with the names of over 13,000 soldiers, along with the words of the 1914-1918 war.

🖌️

Stone Carving

The monument features intricate stone carvings and decorative elements that reflect the grandeur of the British Raj.

🌟

Amar Jawan Jyoti

The eternal flame at the base of the India Gate is a later addition that enhances the memorial's significance.
