📖 About

The Red Fort

A magnificent Mughal fortress that served as the main residence of the Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years.

The Red Fort is a historic fort in Delhi that served as the main residence of the Mughal emperors. It was commissioned by Emperor Shah Jahan in 1639 and completed in 1648. The fort is named for its massive red sandstone walls, which stand as a testament to Mughal architectural excellence.

Covering an area of 254 acres, the Red Fort is a masterpiece of Mughal architecture, blending Persian, Timurid, and Indian architectural styles. It was the political and ceremonial center of the Mughal Empire until 1857.

Today, the Red Fort is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of India's most iconic landmarks, symbolizing the country's rich cultural heritage and the Prime Minister's annual Independence Day address.

📜 History

Historical Background

The story of Delhi's imperial fortress.

👑

Emperor Shah Jahan

Shah Jahan commissioned the Red Fort in 1639 when he decided to move his capital from Agra to Delhi, establishing Shahjahanabad.

🏗️

Construction

Built between 1639 and 1648, the fort took 9 years to complete, employing thousands of artisans and craftsmen.

⚔️

1857 Rebellion

The fort was the site of significant events during the Indian Rebellion of 1857, after which it became a British military garrison.

🇮🇳

Independence Day

Since 1947, the Prime Minister of India hoists the national flag and delivers a speech from the fort's ramparts on Independence Day.

🏛️ Architecture

Mughal Architectural Style

A masterpiece of Mughal architecture blending Persian, Timurid, and Indian styles.

🧱

Red Sandstone

The fort is built from red sandstone, giving it its distinctive color and name. The walls rise to heights of 33 meters.

🏰

Lahore Gate

The main entrance to the fort, the Lahore Gate, is a magnificent structure with intricate ornamentation and a defensive barbican.

🕌

Delhi Gate

The southern gate of the fort, featuring ornate arches and decorative elements typical of Mughal architecture.

💎

Marble Pavilions

Inside the fort, white marble pavilions and palaces showcase the finest Mughal craftsmanship and artistry.

✨ Major Structures

Inside the Red Fort

Explore the magnificent structures within the fortress.

01

Diwan-i-Aam

The Hall of Public Audience, where the emperor would meet the general public and hear their grievances.

02

Diwan-i-Khas

The Hall of Private Audience, where the emperor held private meetings with nobles and foreign dignitaries.

03

Rang Mahal

The "Palace of Colors" was the residence of the emperor's wives and featured beautiful decorations and a marble fountain.

04

Moti Masjid

The "Pearl Mosque" built by Aurangzeb, featuring pristine white marble and exquisite craftsmanship.

05

Hayat Bakhsh Bagh

The "Life-Bestowing Garden" with lush greenery, water channels, and pavilions creating a paradise-like atmosphere.

06

Defensive Walls

The massive 2.4 km long walls with bastions, battlements, and a moat provided formidable defense against invaders.

🏆 UNESCO

UNESCO World Heritage

Recognized as a masterpiece of Mughal architecture.

The Red Fort was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007 as part of the Red Fort Complex. It is recognized for its exceptional Mughal architecture and its historical significance as the political and ceremonial center of the Mughal Empire.

UNESCO describes the Red Fort as "a masterpiece of Mughal architecture" and "an outstanding example of the fusion of Persian, Timurid, and Indian architectural traditions."
