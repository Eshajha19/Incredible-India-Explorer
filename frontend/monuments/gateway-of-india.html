🌊 Mumbai's Icon

Gateway of India

Mumbai's historic waterfront landmark, standing tall at the edge of the Arabian Sea since 1924.

📍

Location

Address: Apollo Bandar, Colaba, Mumbai, Maharashtra 400001, India

Coordinates: 18.9219° N, 72.8346° E

Neighborhood: Colaba, South Mumbai

Overlooking: Arabian Sea

📜 History

Historical Background

Built to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary in 1911.

Construction Period: 1911 – 1924

Architect: George Wittet

Architectural Style: Indo-Saracenic

Purpose: To commemorate the arrival of King George V and Queen Mary

Inauguration: December 4, 1924

Height: 26 meters (85 feet)

Material: Yellow basalt and reinforced concrete

The Gateway of India was built to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary to Bombay (now Mumbai) in December 1911. The foundation stone was laid in 1911, and the monument was completed in 1924. It was designed by the Scottish architect George Wittet in the Indo-Saracenic architectural style, which combines elements of Indian and Islamic architecture.

The monument served as a ceremonial entrance to India for British viceroys and governors. Ironically, the last British troops to leave India after independence marched through the Gateway of India in 1948, symbolizing the end of British rule.

🏛️ Architecture

Architectural Grandeur

A masterpiece of Indo-Saracenic architecture blending Indian, Islamic, and European styles.

⛩️

Indo-Saracenic Style

A fusion of Indian, Islamic, and European architectural elements, characterized by arches, domes, and intricate stonework.

🏛️

Central Dome

The central dome is 15 meters in diameter and is flanked by four smaller domes, creating a majestic silhouette against the Mumbai skyline.

📏

Stonework & Arches

The monument is built from yellow basalt and reinforced concrete, featuring intricate arches and latticework that showcase exquisite craftsmanship.

🌊

Waterfront Location

Strategically positioned at the edge of the Arabian Sea, the Gateway offers stunning views of the waterfront and the iconic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel.

Architectural Highlights

  • Central Dome: 15 meters in diameter, with four smaller domes
  • Height: 26 meters (85 feet) from the ground
  • Material: Yellow basalt from the Deccan region
  • Design: Indo-Saracenic style with Islamic arches and Indian decorative elements
  • Friezes: Intricate stone friezes depicting scenes from Indian history
⛵ Mumbai Harbour

Gateway to the Harbour

The monument's relationship with Mumbai's historic waterfront.

The Gateway of India stands at the edge of Mumbai's historic harbour, serving as a symbolic entrance to the city. The monument overlooks the Arabian Sea and offers breathtaking views of the waterfront, including the iconic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel, the Elephanta Caves ferry, and the bustling Mumbai Port.

The harbour has been a crucial part of Mumbai's identity, serving as a gateway for trade, travel, and cultural exchange for centuries. The Gateway of India is a testament to this maritime heritage.

Key Features:

  • View of the Arabian Sea and Mumbai skyline
  • Ferry services to Elephanta Island (Elephanta Caves)
  • Historic waterfront promenade
  • Iconic photo opportunity with the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel

Important Events

The Gateway of India has witnessed many significant moments in Indian history.

The foundation stone was laid by King George V and Queen Mary during their visit to Bombay.

1924

Inauguration

The Gateway of India was officially inaugurated on December 4, 1924, by the Viceroy, Lord Reading.

1948

End of British Rule

The last British troops marched through the Gateway of India, symbolizing the end of British colonial rule in India.

2008

26/11 Terror Attacks

The Gateway of India and the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel became symbols of resilience after the 2008 Mumbai attacks.
