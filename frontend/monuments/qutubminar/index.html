📖 About

Qutub Minar

The world's tallest brick minaret and a masterpiece of Indo-Islamic architecture.

The Qutub Minar is a 73-metre (240 ft) tall minaret located in Delhi, India. It is the world's tallest brick minaret and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Construction began in 1192 by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, the first ruler of the Delhi Sultanate, and was completed by his successors.

The tower is made of red sandstone and marble, featuring intricate carvings and inscriptions in Arabic. It stands within the Qutub Minar complex, which includes several other historical monuments, including the Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque and the famous Iron Pillar.

The Qutub Minar is one of Delhi's most iconic landmarks, attracting millions of visitors annually who come to admire its architectural grandeur and historical significance.

⚙️ Iron Pillar

The Iron Pillar

A marvel of ancient Indian metallurgy.

The Iron Pillar of Delhi is a 7.21-metre (23.7 ft) high iron pillar that stands in the Qutub Minar complex. It is famous for its rust-resistant composition and has stood for over 1,600 years without significant corrosion.

The pillar was originally erected in Udayagiri by Chandragupta II Vikramaditya and later moved to Delhi. It bears inscriptions in Sanskrit and is a testament to the advanced metallurgical skills of ancient India.

The Iron Pillar is a major tourist attraction and a symbol of India's ancient scientific achievements.

Key Features

  • 🔩 Height: 7.21 metres (23.7 ft)
  • ⚖️ Weight: Approximately 6 tonnes
  • 📜 Inscriptions in Sanskrit
  • 🛡️ Rust-resistant composition
  • 🏛️ Originally from Udayagiri
🏆 UNESCO

UNESCO World Heritage

Recognized as a masterpiece of Indo-Islamic architecture.

The Qutub Minar was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993 as part of the Qutub Minar Complex. It is recognized for its exceptional architectural and historical significance, representing the fusion of Islamic and Indian architectural traditions.

UNESCO describes the Qutub Minar as "an outstanding example of early Indo-Islamic architecture" and "a masterpiece of the art of brick construction."
