Nilgiri Laughingthrush

Montecincla cachinnans - An endemic bird of the Nilgiri Hills.

Introduction & Scientific Name

The Nilgiri Laughingthrush (Montecincla cachinnans) is a striking, endangered bird species endemic to the high-altitude shola forests of the Nilgiri Hills in the Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu. It was recently reclassified into the genus Montecincla.

Taxonomic Classification

  • Kingdom: Animalia
  • Phylum: Chordata
  • Class: Aves
  • Order: Passeriformes
  • Family: Leiothrichidae
  • Genus: Montecincla
  • Species: M. cachinnans

Distribution & Habitat

Highly restricted to the Nilgiri massif above 1200 meters. It relies heavily on the fragmented shola forest patches and the dense understory of Lantana and Strobilanthes thickets.

Physical Characteristics

A medium-sized, distinctly colored bird with a greyish-brown crown, a bold black eye stripe bordered by white, and bright yellow underparts. Its bill is black and slightly curved.

Diet & Behaviour

Omnivorous and highly social, usually foraging in noisy groups of 5 to 15 individuals. They feed on berries, nectar, insects, and small invertebrates found in the canopy and mid-story.

Breeding

Breeds between January to April. They build cup-shaped nests in dense bushes. Both parents, and sometimes helper birds from the flock, assist in feeding the chicks (cooperative breeding).

Conservation Status & Threats

Listed as Endangered by the IUCN. The primary threats are the destruction of shola forests for tea plantations, the spread of invasive weeds (like Lantana camara) that choke the native understory, and forest fires.

Ecological Importance

Acts as a vital seed disperser for native montane forest plants, helping to maintain the genetic diversity and regeneration of the shola ecosystem.

Protected Areas

Found within the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, particularly in Mukurthi National Park and the Dodabetta peak region.

Interesting Facts

  • It is the official state bird of the Nilgiris district.
  • Its loud, cackling "laughing" calls are a signature sound of the high-altitude shola forests.
