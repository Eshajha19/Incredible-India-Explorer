🌳 Chhattisgarh · Kanger Valley

Tirathgarh Falls Explorer

Deep inside Kanger Valley National Park, the Kanger River's Munga Bahar tributary breaks into a milky-white, multi-tiered cascade dropping roughly 91 metres through dense Bastar forest.

📍 Location & Water Source

Where the falls come from

Tirathgarh Falls lies on the western edge of Kanger Valley National Park in the Bastar district of Chhattisgarh, about 35 km southwest of Jagdalpur near Darbha. The water comes from the perennial Munga Bahar nallah, a tributary stream that feeds into the Kanger River — the lifeline of the entire national park and, ultimately, part of the Godavari river system.

  • Location: Kanger Valley National Park, Bastar district, Chhattisgarh
  • Distance: ~35 km southwest of Jagdalpur, via Darbha
  • Water source: Munga Bahar nallah, tributary of the Kanger River
  • Total height: ~91 m (300 ft) block waterfall
  • Structure: Multi-tiered / segmented cascade in three main steps
  • Best season: Year-round; fullest in monsoon, clearest milky-white flow post-monsoon (Oct–Feb)

🌦️ Seasons

The Falls Across the Year

Fed by a perennial nallah, Tirathgarh flows all year, but its character shifts noticeably between the monsoon flood and the clear, milky-white flow that follows it.

🌲 Forest Ecosystem & Geology

Kanger Valley's Living Backdrop

Tirathgarh sits inside Kanger Valley National Park, one of India's densest protected forests — 200 sq km of moist deciduous sal and bamboo cover stretching from the falls east to the Kolab river on the Odisha border. The falls plunge over sedimentary rock layers typical of the Bastar plateau, the same limestone geology that has carved the nearby Kutumsar and Kailash caves. The park is also home to the Bastar hill myna, Chhattisgarh's state bird, alongside leopards, wild dogs, flying squirrels and mugger crocodiles basking on the Kanger River's sandbanks.

Getting There

Tirathgarh is reached by road from Jagdalpur via Darbha, entering Kanger Valley National Park from its western boundary.

Travel Notes

  • Best season: Year-round; October–February for the clearest milky-white flow
  • Park timings: Kanger Valley National Park is open 1 November – 30 June
  • Access: A stepped staircase lets visitors descend to view multiple tiers up close
  • Nearest town: Jagdalpur (~35 km) · Nearest railhead: Jagdalpur Railway Station
