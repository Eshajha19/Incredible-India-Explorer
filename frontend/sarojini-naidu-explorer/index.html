🇮🇳 The Nightingale of India · 1879–1949

Sarojini Naidu Explorer

Poet, freedom fighter, and tireless advocate for women's rights — discover the life and legacy of Sarojini Naidu, the first Indian woman to serve as President of the Indian National Congress and the first woman Governor of an Indian state.

Born: 13 February 1879, Hyderabad Nickname: The Nightingale of India First Woman: Congress President (1925)

Biography

🎓

Early Life & Education

Born on 13 February 1879 in Hyderabad to a Bengali Brahmin family, Sarojini Chattopadhyay was a prodigy in languages and poetry. Her father, Dr. Aghorenath Chattopadhyay, was a scientist and educationist, while her mother, Varada Sundari Devi, was a noted Bengali poetess. After topping the Madras Presidency matriculation at twelve, she was sent to King's College, London and later Girton College, Cambridge on a scholarship from the Nizam of Hyderabad — making her one of the first Indian women to receive a formal British university education.

✍️

The Poet Awakens

While in England, she met poets like Arthur Symons and Edmund Gosse, who encouraged her to write about India in English. Her first poetry collection, The Golden Threshold (1905), published in London, made her an overnight literary sensation. Her lyrical, musical verse earned her the title "The Nightingale of India" (Bharat Kokila) from Mahatma Gandhi. She followed it with The Bird of Time (1912) and The Broken Wing (1917).

🕊️

Entry into Freedom Struggle

The 1905 Partition of Bengal transformed her from poet to patriot. She met Gopal Krishna Gokhale in 1906 and through him was introduced to Mahatma Gandhi in 1914. Inspired by Gandhi's call for self-rule, she gave up her comfortable life and joined the Indian National Congress, becoming one of its most powerful orators — touring the country and addressing massive crowds in both English and Hindustani.

⚖️

Women's Rights Advocate

Sarojini Naidu believed that India's freedom was inseparable from the emancipation of its women. In 1917 she co-founded the Women's Indian Association (WIA) with Annie Besant and Margaret Cousins, campaigning for women's suffrage, education, and the abolition of purdah and child marriage. She led a delegation to the Secretary of State for India demanding the vote for Indian women — a demand partially conceded in the Government of India Act, 1919.

🏛️

Congress President (1925)

In 1925, at the Cawnpore (Kanpur) session of the Indian National Congress, Sarojini Naidu was elected President — the first Indian woman to hold that office, succeeding Anand Mohan Sahay. Her presidency symbolised the Congress's commitment to women's equal participation in the national movement, and she used the platform to urge mass mobilisation of women for civil disobedience.

🌅

Governor of Uttar Pradesh (1947–1949)

After India attained independence on 15 August 1947, Sarojini Naidu was appointed the first woman Governor of the United Provinces (present-day Uttar Pradesh), making her the first woman to serve as a Governor in independent India. She held office until her sudden death on 2 March 1949 in Lucknow while working at her desk in the Raj Bhavan.

Major Freedom Movements

🚢

Home Rule Movement (1916–1918)

Sarojini Naidu travelled across India with Annie Besant and Bal Gangadhar Tilak, demanding self-government (Swaraj) within the British Empire. Her fiery speeches mobilised thousands of women to join political activism for the first time.

🤝

Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)

A close ally of Gandhi, she toured the country urging Indians to boycott British schools, courts, goods, and titles. She publicly renounced her own government titles and picketed foreign-cloth liquor shops alongside women volunteers.

🧂

Civil Disobedience & Dharasana Satyagraha (1930)

After Gandhi's arrest following the Dandi Salt March, Sarojini Naidu led 2,500 satyagrahis to raid the Dharasana Salt Works. The brutal British response — with non-violent protesters beaten unconscious — shocked the world and became one of the most photographed events of the freedom struggle.

🇮🇳

Round Table Conferences (1931)

She accompanied Gandhi to London for the Second Round Table Conference — one of the few women delegates — advocating for Indian self-government before British leaders and the global press.

✊

Quit India Movement (1942)

Following the "Do or Die" call of 8 August 1942, she helped organise underground resistance after the arrest of senior Congress leaders. She was imprisoned for nearly twenty-one months in the Yerwada and Aga Khan Palace jails.

📚

Total Prison Terms

Across her public life, Sarojini Naidu spent a cumulative total of over two and a half years in British jails, including stints at Yerwada Central Jail, the Aga Khan Palace, and other prisons — enduring poor health and harsh conditions without ever renouncing the cause.

Literary Contributions

📖

The Golden Threshold (1905)

Her debut poetry collection, published in London with an introduction by Arthur Symons. The book introduced Indian themes — festivals, palanquin bearers, snake charmers, bazaars — to English readers with lyrical grace.

🐦

The Bird of Time (1912)

Her second collection, including the celebrated poem "In the Bazaars of Hyderabad". The poem paints a vivid sensory portrait of a traditional Indian marketplace, and is still taught in schools across India.

🪶

The Broken Wing (1917)

A more somber collection reflecting the anguish of World War I and the tightening grip of colonial rule, including "The Gift of India" — a tribute to Indian soldiers who died in Europe.

🎙️

Speeches & Writings

Naidu's collected speeches, The Speeches & Writings of Sarojini Naidu (1919), showcased her mastery of English oratory. She used poetry and rhetoric as instruments of political awakening, often quoting from her own verse during public addresses.

🎭

Mufti Mahmud & The Sceptred Flute

The Sceptred Flute (1943) was a retrospective anthology gathering her complete published verse. Her unfinished play Mufti Mahmud dramatised the pathos of princely decay under colonial pressure.

🌟

Legacy in Indian English Poetry

Naidu is widely regarded as a foundational figure of Indian English literature. Her work is regularly featured in anthologies of Indian poetry, and her birthday, 13 February, is commemorated as National Women's Day in India.
