The Legend

Muziris — First Emporium of India

Kodungallur, at the mouth of the Periyar River in Kerala, is archaeologically identified as Muziris (Muciri) — the legendary port that Pliny the Elder called "Primum Inscriptionis Emporium Indiae".

📜

Classical Sources

Mentioned in the Periplus Maris Erythraei (1st c. CE), Pliny's Natural History (77 CE), and Ptolemy's Geography (2nd c. CE) as a thriving emporium where "Yavana ships arrive with gold and leave laden with pepper."

🌊

The Great Flood of 1341

A catastrophic Periyar flood permanently altered the Malabar coast — silting Muziris harbour and giving birth to Cochin port. Kodungallur became a silent keeper of ancient maritime memories.

🏛️

Chera Capital

Between the 9th–12th centuries CE, Kodungallur (then Mahodayapuram) served as the royal capital of the Second Chera Kingdom under the Kulasekhara dynasty.

Syncretic Sanctuary

Where World Faiths First Landed

Kodungallur welcomed Christianity, Islam, and Judaism centuries before they reached most of India — all coexisting with ancient Hindu traditions.

Kodungallur's heritage sites tell the story of India's earliest interfaith encounters.

🕌

Cheraman Juma Mosque (629 CE)

India's first mosque, built by Malik Dinar during Prophet Muhammad's lifetime. Traditional Kerala architecture with a brass lamp lit continuously for 1,400 years.

⛪

St. Thomas Church (52 CE)

Tradition holds that St. Thomas the Apostle landed at Azhikode near Kodungallur, establishing Christianity in India 1,500 years before European missionaries.

🛕

Kodungallur Bhagavathy Temple

Ancient shrine to Goddess Kannaki/Bhadrakali, immortalized in the Tamil epic Silappatikaram. Famous for Bharani and Thalappoli festivals.

🕉️

Thiruvanchikulam Shiva Temple

Ancestral temple of the Chera emperors. The only Shaivite Paadal Petra Sthalam in Kerala, associated with saints Sundarar and Cheraman Perumal.
