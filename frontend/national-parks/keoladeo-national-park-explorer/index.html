🏛️ UNESCO World Heritage Site (1985) 💧 Ramsar Wetland of International Importance 📍 Bharatpur, Rajasthan

Keoladeo National Park Explorer

Step into one of the world's most vital bird sanctuaries — a man-made wetland mosaic hosting over 370 species of resident and Palaearctic migratory waterfowl along the Central Asian Flyway.

Global Importance

UNESCO World Heritage & Ramsar Wetland

Recognized globally for its exceptional biodiversity, artificial hydrology system, and critical role as a wintering sanctuary for endangered Palaearctic birds.

🏛️

UNESCO Natural Criterion (x)

Inscribed in 1985

Keoladeo Ghana National Park was inscribed on the World Heritage List under Natural Criteria (x) for containing the most important habitats for in-situ conservation of biological diversity, especially threatened waterfowl species.

  • Winter refuge for tens of thousands of migratory birds
  • Highest concentration of breeding storks, herons & cormorants in Asia
  • Protected under strict National Park and Wildlife Protection statutes
💧

Ramsar Wetland Site

Designated 1 October 1981

Designated as Ramsar Site No. 230, Keoladeo is a artificial wetland oasis engineered in the 18th century by Maharaja Suraj Mal. Water impounded from Ajan Bund feeds 10 distinct dyked blocks covering 29 km².

  • Complex canal network with sluice gates controlling water depths
  • Interconnected ecosystem of marshes, woodlands, and savannahs
  • Sustained by Chambal River pipeline and local monsoon inflows

Avian Migration

Central Asian Flyway & Siberian Crane Legacy

Every autumn, millions of birds navigate thousands of kilometers across high mountain passes and Central Asian steppes to winter in Keoladeo.

The Central Asian Flyway (CAF)

Keoladeo lies at the southern terminus of the Central Asian Flyway. Waterfowl breeding across Siberia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, and the Tibetan Plateau fly south over the Himalayas to find sanctuary in Bharatpur's warm, food-rich marshes.

Conservation Icon

The Siberian Crane Legend

Bharatpur was world-famous as the sole wintering refuge in India for the western/central population of the Critically Endangered Siberian Crane (Leucogeranus leucogeranus).

Traveling 6,400 km from the Ob River marshes in Russia, these magnificent snow-white birds wintered in Keoladeo until 2002. Today, Keoladeo remains a flagship symbol for international crane conservation efforts.
