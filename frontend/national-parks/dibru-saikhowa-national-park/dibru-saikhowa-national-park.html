Dibru-Saikhowa National Park

Explore a unique river island ecosystem formed by the Brahmaputra and Lohit rivers. Discover the famous feral horses, intricate wetlands, and a paradise for birdwatchers.

Explore Wildlife View Map
Landscape

River Islands & Ecosystem

River Island Formation

Bounded by the Brahmaputra, Lohit, and Dibru rivers, the park's topography is a dynamic maze of shifting sandbanks, river islands (saporis), and changing channels.

Seasonal Changes

The landscape is profoundly shaped by the monsoon. Seasonal flooding inundates the floodplain ecosystem, replenishing the soil and sustaining the marshlands.

Wetland Habitats

Oxbow lakes, swamps, and marshes form critical habitats for aquatic biodiversity, acting as natural water conservation systems and fish nurseries.

Flora & Fauna

Descendants of cavalry horses left behind after WWII, now living wild in the Churkey Sapori grasslands.

Endangered

An elusive predator navigating the dense semi-evergreen and deciduous forests of the park.

Avian Paradise

Endangered

Greater Adjutant Stork

Endangered

Birdwatching

A Haven for Birds

The diverse habitat makes it one of the most important bird areas in the world.

  • Migratory Season: Winter (Nov-Feb) attracts thousands of waterfowl from Siberia and Central Asia.
  • Resident Species: Hornbills, Kingfishers, Eagles, and Herons are present year-round.
  • Endangered Species: Critical habitat for the Jerdon's Babbler and Black-breasted Parrotbill.
Dynamics

Brahmaputra Ecosystem

The immense power of the rivers dictates the rhythm of life in the park.

  • Sediment Deposition: The rivers continually reshape the land, creating new saporis (islands) rich in nutrients.
  • Climate Resilience: The extensive wetlands act as a buffer against both floods and droughts.
  • Unique Safaris: Due to the terrain, exploration is primarily conducted via boat safaris on the rivers and channels.
