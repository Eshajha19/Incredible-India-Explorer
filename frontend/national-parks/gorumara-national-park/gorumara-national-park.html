Gorumara National Park

Venture into the heart of the Dooars region. Discover rolling grasslands, dense Sal forests, and the iconic Indian One-Horned Rhinoceros from panoramic watchtowers.

Explore Wildlife View Map
Ecosystem

Forests & Rivers

Moist Deciduous Forests

The park is dominated by dense Sal forests (Shorea robusta), interspersed with Teak, Simul, and Khair, forming a thick canopy that shelters diverse wildlife.

River Ecosystems

The Murti and Jaldhaka rivers flow through the park. These perennial water sources are the lifeline of the forest, attracting large herds of herbivores during dry seasons.

Rich History

Established as a reserve forest in 1895, declared a Wildlife Sanctuary in 1949, and upgraded to a National Park in 1994, reflecting over a century of conservation.

Fauna

Iconic Wildlife

Discover the majestic species that roam the Dooars.

Indian One-Horned Rhino

Vulnerable

The star attraction of Gorumara, known for its armor-plated appearance. They are frequently seen grazing in the riverine grasslands.

Asian Elephant

Endangered

Large herds migrate through the park's elephant corridors, heavily relying on the Jaldhaka and Murti rivers.

Indian Bison (Gaur)

Vulnerable

The largest extant bovine. These massive, muscular animals can often be spotted near the salt licks by the watchtowers.

Indian Leopard

Vulnerable

The apex predator of Gorumara, utilizing the dense Sal forest canopy and tall grasses for stealthy hunting.

Avifauna

Bird Species

A paradise for birdwatchers in the Sub-Himalayan foothills.

Great Hornbill

Vulnerable

An impressive bird with a massive casque, found in the upper canopies of the old-growth forests.

Scarlet Minivet

Least Concern

Strikingly colorful birds that flutter through the foliage in small, active flocks.

Kingfishers

Various

Frequently sighted diving for fish along the banks of the Murti and Jaldhaka rivers.

Observation

Watch Towers

Gorumara is famous for its strategically placed watchtowers offering panoramic views.

  • Jatraprasad: The most famous tower, named after a legendary forest elephant. Overlooks the Murti river.
  • Medla: Accessible via a unique buffalo cart ride. Overlooks the elephant corridor.
  • Chandrachur: Built deep in the forest, offering incredible views of wild vegetation and bison herds.
  • Chukchuki: The prime spot for birdwatchers, located at the edge of the park.
Experience

Safari Experience

Immerse yourself in the Dooars wilderness.

  • Jeep Safari: The most popular way to explore the dense trails and reach the various watchtowers.
  • Elephant Safari: Offers a unique, elevated perspective, often allowed only for guests staying at specific forest lodges.
  • Timings: Safaris operate in morning and evening shifts. The park remains closed during the monsoon (mid-June to mid-September).
