Jim Corbett National Park Explorer

Established in 1936, Jim Corbett is India's oldest national park and the site where Project Tiger was launched in 1973. Explore its Shivalik hills, the Ramganga River, dense Sal forests, and one of the country's densest Bengal Tiger populations.

History

From Hailey National Park to Corbett

Trace the park's journey from its founding in 1936 to becoming the birthplace of India's most important tiger conservation programme.

Geography

Shivalik Hills & the Ramganga River

Corbett spans 1,318 km² across the Shivalik foothills, ranging from 360 m to 1,040 m in elevation, with the Ramganga River and its reservoir carving through riverine belts, ravines, and the Patli Dun valley.

Flora

Sal Forests & Riverine Grasslands

Sal forest covers roughly 73% of the park, complemented by chaur grasslands, khair-sissoo riverine belts, and pine forest at higher elevations.

Fauna

Wildlife of Corbett

From Bengal Tigers to gharials basking along the Ramganga, Corbett supports a remarkably diverse range of species.

Flagship Species

The Bengal Tiger of Corbett

Corbett is where Project Tiger began in 1973 — and remains home to one of India's densest Bengal Tiger populations today.

Dense Sal forests, riverine cover, and abundant prey along the Ramganga floodplains make Corbett prime tiger habitat. As the pilot reserve for Project Tiger, its conservation model of anti-poaching camps and core-buffer zoning became the template later applied across dozens of other Indian tiger reserves.

Experience the Wild

Safari Zones in Corbett

Six distinct zones — Dhikala, Bijrani, Jhirna, Dhela, Durga Devi, and Sonanadi — each offering a different slice of the park's terrain and wildlife.

Plan Your Visit

Best Time to Visit

Open November through June; Dhikala zone closes mid-June to mid-November for the monsoon, but Jhirna zone stays open year-round.
