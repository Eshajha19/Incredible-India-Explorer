Indravati National Park

Venture into one of central India's finest tiger habitats. Explore the dense tropical forests, the majestic Indravati River, and the sanctuary of the endangered Wild Water Buffalo.

Heritage

History & Ecological Significance

Establishment

Established as a National Park in 1981, Indravati takes its name from the nearby Indravati River. In 1983, it was declared a Tiger Reserve under Project Tiger to protect the declining tiger population.

Conservation Initiatives

The park is a vital stronghold for the endangered Wild Water Buffalo. Intensive conservation initiatives have been put in place to monitor the species and prevent habitat loss and poaching.

Central Indian Forests

Serving as a crucial corridor, Indravati connects with neighboring reserves like Bhairamgarh and Pamed, ensuring genetic exchange for tigers and other large mammals.

River Ecosystem

The Lifeline: Indravati River

Origin & Course

The Indravati River originates in the Eastern Ghats of Odisha and flows westwards to join the Godavari River, forming the northern boundary of the National Park and acting as a natural frontier.

Riverine Habitats

The river sustains a diverse aquatic ecosystem and creates crucial riparian habitats along its banks. These wetlands are vital for the survival of the Wild Water Buffalo, which relies on the river for cooling and foraging.

Flora

Forest Ecosystem

The diverse terrain of Indravati includes undulating hills, river valleys, and plains, nurturing a rich combination of tropical moist and dry deciduous forests.

  • Deciduous Forests: Dominated by Teak, Sal, and Mahua, shedding leaves in the dry season.
  • Bamboo Forests: Extensive tracts of bamboo provide crucial forage and shelter.
  • Grasslands: Essential for supporting the park's large herbivore populations like Chital and Sambar.
  • Bird Diversity: Home to Hornbills, Peafowl, Hill Mynas, and several species of raptors.
  • Micro-habitats: Rich in reptiles, amphibians, and a stunning variety of butterflies.
Focus

Wild Water Buffalo

Extremely Rare
The wild water buffalo population has seen severe declines due to habitat loss and interbreeding with domestic cattle.
Dedicated Protection
Indravati remains one of the final protected strongholds where purebred wild buffaloes can still be observed in their natural habitat.
