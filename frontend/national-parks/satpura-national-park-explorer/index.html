Satpura Hills Overview

Origins

Declared a national park in 1981 and a Tiger Reserve in 2000, Satpura combines the Satpura, Bori, and Pachmarhi sanctuaries into one 2,133 km² reserve.

Satpura Range

"Satpura" comes from the Sanskrit words "Sat" (seven) and "Pura" (mountain), referring to the rugged range of sandstone peaks, gorges, and valleys the park sits within.

Denwa River

The Denwa River winds along the park's edge, feeding the Tawa Reservoir and forming the backdrop for the park's signature canoe safaris.

Wildlife Walking Safari

Satpura is one of the few parks in India where visitors can track wildlife on foot with an armed guard and naturalist.

Canoe Safari

Glide silently along the Denwa River — a quiet way to spot wildlife and birds at the water's edge without the sound of an engine.

Flora

Birdwatching

Home to over 300 bird species, with the Tawa Reservoir and Denwa Backwaters as prime birding spots between November and March.
