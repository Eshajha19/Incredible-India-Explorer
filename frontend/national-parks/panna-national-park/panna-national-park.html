Panna National Park

Discover the jewel of Madhya Pradesh, renowned for its incredible tiger conservation success, the pristine Ken River, breathtaking waterfalls, and diverse wildlife.

Explore Wildlife View Map
Heritage

History & Conservation

Establishment

Established in 1981, Panna National Park spans across the Panna and Chhatarpur districts. In 1994, it was declared India's 22nd Tiger Reserve under Project Tiger.

Tiger Reintroduction

After losing all its native tigers to poaching by 2009, Panna executed a globally recognized successful tiger reintroduction programme, bringing tigers from other reserves to revive the population.

UNESCO Biosphere

Designated as a Biosphere Reserve by the Ministry of Environment and Forests in 2011, and brought into the UNESCO World Network of Biosphere Reserves in 2020.

Water Ecosystem

The Lifeline: Ken River & Raneh Falls

Ken River

Originating from the Ahirgawan village, the Ken River flows northward through the park, serving as its primary water source. It supports a rich ecosystem and is home to endangered Gharials and Mugger crocodiles. Boat safaris offer a unique vantage point for bird watching and reptile spotting.

Raneh Falls

Located on the Ken River, Raneh Falls is famous for its mesmerizing canyon formed by purely crystalline granite and dolomite in varying shades of pink, red, and grey. The falls are most spectacular during and just after the monsoon season.

Fauna

Incredible Wildlife

Home to a wide variety of mammals, reptiles, and over 200 species of birds.

Bengal Tiger

Endangered

The apex predator of Panna, thriving after the successful reintroduction program.

Indian Leopard

Vulnerable

Elusive and agile cats that share the dense forest habitats with the tigers.

Sloth Bear

Vulnerable

Frequently seen foraging for termites and fruits in the rocky outcrops.

Indian Wolf

Endangered

Inhabits the buffer zones and grasslands, hunting in small packs.

Gharial

Critically Endangered

A fish-eating crocodile with a long, thin snout, found in the Ken River.

Flora

Rich Vegetation

The terrain consists of extensive plateaus and gorges. The park's vegetation is predominantly dry deciduous forest with vast open grasslands.

  • Teak Forests: Southern tropical dry deciduous teak dominating the landscape.
  • Bamboo Thickets: Dense bamboo clusters providing excellent cover for wildlife.
  • Grasslands: Tall grasses offering ideal camouflage for tigers and grazing for herbivores.
  • Medicinal Plants: A variety of indigenous herbs used in traditional medicine.
  • Bird Diversity: Over 200 species including vultures, eagles, and kingfishers.
Trivia

Interesting Facts

Success Story
From zero tigers in 2009, Panna has grown to host over 80 tigers today.
Canyon Wonders
The granite rocks at Raneh Falls change color from pink to grey depending on sunlight.
Vulture Haven
Panna's steep gorges are a crucial nesting ground for several endangered vulture species.
