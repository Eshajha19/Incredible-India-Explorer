Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park

Dive into a mesmerizing coastal ecosystem comprising 21 islands, vibrant coral reefs, and lush seagrass beds. A sanctuary for the gentle Dugong and playful dolphins.

Explore Marine Life View Map
Ecosystem

Coastal & Coral Ecology

Ocean Currents

The gulf acts as a mixing zone for currents from the Indian Ocean and the Bay of Bengal, creating a nutrient-rich environment that supports massive marine biodiversity.

Coral Reef Ecosystem

Housing over 100 species of hard corals, these reefs act as critical nurseries for reef fish and invertebrates. Active restoration projects are combatting climate change impacts.

Seagrass Beds (Blue Carbon)

The extensive seagrass meadows trap massive amounts of carbon (Blue Carbon), protect the coast from erosion, and serve as the primary feeding ground for Dugongs.

The 21 Islands

An archipelago stretching from Rameswaram to Tuticorin. These islands feature pristine sandy beaches, mangrove forests, and crucial nesting sites for marine turtles.

Marine Fauna

Denizens of the Deep

Discover the incredible species that call the Gulf of Mannar home.

Dugong (Sea Cow)

Vulnerable

Gentle marine mammals that graze exclusively on the park's seagrass meadows. Highly protected under recovery initiatives.

Bottlenose Dolphin

Near Threatened

Highly intelligent and social, frequently sighted playing in the wakes of boats near the Tuticorin coast.

Spinner Dolphin

Least Concern

Known for their spectacular acrobatic displays and spinning leaps out of the water.

Green Sea Turtle

Endangered

Utilizes the isolated sandy beaches of the 21 islands as vital nesting grounds during breeding season.

Reef Fish

Various

Hundreds of colorful tropical species navigate the complex architecture of the hard and soft corals.

Hard Corals

Threatened

The reef-builders of the ecosystem, highly sensitive to climate change and ocean warming.

Dugongs

The Sea Cow Sanctuary

The Gulf of Mannar is the last major stronghold for the Dugong in Indian waters.

  • Feeding: They can consume up to 40kg of seagrass daily, creating 'feeding trails' on the ocean floor.
  • Migration: Generally localized, but they track the health and availability of seagrass meadows.
  • Threats: Propeller strikes, entanglement in fishing nets, and habitat degradation.
Management

Conservation & Community

Protecting a marine biosphere requires a multifaceted approach involving local populations.

  • Reef Restoration: Utilizing artificial reef modules to encourage coral polyp settlement and growth.
  • Sustainable Fishing: Educating coastal communities and enforcing seasonal fishing bans.
  • Plastic Reduction: Initiatives to clean the beaches and prevent marine debris from choking the corals and turtles.
