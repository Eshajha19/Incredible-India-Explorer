Travancore Wolf Snake

Lycodon travancoricus - An endemic non-venomous snake of the Western Ghats.

Introduction & Scientific Name

The Travancore Wolf Snake (Lycodon travancoricus) is a species of colubrid snake endemic to the Western Ghats of India. It is a non-venomous, nocturnal predator that is frequently mistaken for the venomous krait due to its banded pattern.

Taxonomic Classification

  • Kingdom: Animalia
  • Phylum: Chordata
  • Class: Reptilia
  • Order: Squamata
  • Family: Colubridae
  • Genus: Lycodon
  • Species: L. travancoricus

Distribution & Habitat

Endemic to the southern Western Ghats, primarily found in Kerala and Tamil Nadu. It inhabits tropical moist deciduous forests, evergreen forests, and plantations, usually hiding under rocks or leaf litter during the day.

Physical Characteristics

A slender, medium-sized snake reaching up to 1 meter in length. Its dorsal side is dark brown to black with alternating transverse white or yellowish bands. The belly is whitish.

Diet & Behaviour

Strictly nocturnal and terrestrial. It preys primarily on skinks, geckos, and other small lizards. It is known to be highly active during the monsoon season.

Reproduction

Oviparous (egg-laying). Females lay clutches of 4 to 7 elongated eggs during the late summer or early monsoon months. The eggs are often deposited in decaying logs or termite mounds.

Conservation Status & Threats

Listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, though localized populations face threats from habitat destruction, deforestation for agriculture, and road mortality. It is also frequently killed out of fear due to its resemblance to the Common Krait.

Ecological Importance

Acts as a crucial mesopredator in the forest floor ecosystem, controlling the populations of small lizards and maintaining the balance of the detrital food web.

Protected Areas

Found in Periyar Tiger Reserve, Kalakkad Mundanthurai Tiger Reserve, and Neyyar Wildlife Sanctuary.

Interesting Facts

  • Unlike the krait, the Travancore wolf snake's scales are smooth and lack the prominent hexagonal vertebral row.
  • The species name "travancoricus" refers to the historical princely state of Travancore, where it was first described.
