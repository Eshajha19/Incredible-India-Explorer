Chalukya Dynasty of Vatapi · 7th Century CE

Emperor Pulakeshin II

The greatest monarch of the Badami Chalukyas, master of the Deccan, and the legendary military tactician who famously halted Emperor Harshavardhana’s southern expansion at the Narmada River.

🏛️ Quick Facts

Dynasty: Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi)
Reign Period: c. 610 – 642 CE
Capital: Vatapi (Modern Badami, Karnataka)
Region of Influence: Deccan Plateau, Maharashtra, Karnataka
Primary Source: Aihole Inscription (by Ravikirti)
Rise to Supremacy

Accession & Territorial Expansion

Pulakeshin II (born Ereya) ascended the throne after a bitter civil war against his uncle, Mangalesha, who attempted to deny him his rightful succession. Upon securing the capital of Vatapi, Pulakeshin faced a fragmented kingdom with numerous feudatories declaring independence.

Displaying brilliant military strategy, he systematically subjugated rebellious powers. He defeated the Kadambas of Banavasi, the Gangas of Talakad, and the Mauryas of Konkan. By launching naval assaults to capture Puri (modern Elephanta) and subduing the Latas, Malwas, and Gurjaras in the northwest, Pulakeshin established an undisputed Chalukya hegemony over the vast Deccan plateau.

⚔️ Master of the Deccan

His conquests successfully unified the fractured southern territories, earning him the prestigious title of Dakshinapatheshvara (Lord of the South). His sphere of influence formally stretched from the Narmada River in the north to the Kaveri River in the south.

Clash of Titans

The Battle of Narmada: Halting Harsha

The defining moment of Pulakeshin II's reign was his confrontation with Harshavardhana, the paramount sovereign of Northern India. Seeking to extend his empire into the Deccan, Harsha marched his massive army southwards, intending to cross the natural frontier of the Narmada River.

Pulakeshin organized a formidable defense, utilizing the rugged terrain of the Vindhyas and his specialized elephant corps. The Chalukyan forces decisively defeated Harsha's army, forcing the northern emperor to retreat and formally accept the Narmada River as the boundary between their two massive empires.

📜 Epigraphic Evidence

The victory is immortalized in the Aihole Inscription, which states poetically that Harsha (whose name means 'joy') had his joy melted away by fear upon facing Pulakeshin's elephant forces. Following this victory, Pulakeshin assumed the imperial title Parameshwara (Supreme Lord).

✍️ The Aihole Prashasti

Composed in flawless Sanskrit by the Jain court poet Ravikirti, the inscription is located at the Meguti Jain temple in Aihole (Karnataka). It is dated to 634 CE and serves as the primary historical anchor for 7th-century Indian chronology, detailing Pulakeshin's genealogy and conquests.

Historical Anchor

Significance of the Aihole Inscription

Unlike many periods of ancient Indian history shrouded in myth, Pulakeshin II's reign is exceptionally well-documented thanks to the Aihole Inscription. This Prashasti (eulogy) is a masterpiece of classical Sanskrit poetry, with Ravikirti boldly comparing his own literary prowess to that of the legendary Kalidasa and Bharavi.

The inscription provides an unparalleled, sequential account of Pulakeshin's military campaigns across India, serving as the most vital epigraphic text for reconstructing the political geography of the early medieval Deccan.

Governance & Legacy

Administration, Culture & Xuanzang's Visit

Xuanzang's Observations

The Chinese pilgrim visited Maharashtra during Pulakeshin's reign, describing the king as a just and powerful ruler whose subjects were fiercely loyal and whose warriors were courageous to the point of death.

Religious Tolerance

Though a devout Hindu who performed Vedic sacrifices, Pulakeshin patronized Jainism (evidenced by Ravikirti) and Buddhism, maintaining harmony across his vast, diverse empire.

Persian Diplomacy

Historical records and the Ajanta cave murals (Cave 1) suggest diplomatic exchanges with the Sassanid Persian Emperor Khosrow II, reflecting Vatapi's global prestige.

Timeline & Final Campaigns

c. 610 CE

Accession to the Throne

Defeats his uncle Mangalesha in a civil war to claim the Chalukyan crown, stabilizing a fractured kingdom.

c. 618 – 619 CE

The Battle of Narmada

Decisively halts Harshavardhana's southern march, establishing the Narmada River as the northern frontier.

634 CE

The Aihole Inscription

Court poet Ravikirti composes the famous eulogy detailing the zenith of Pulakeshin's territorial conquests.

c. 642 CE

Pallava Retaliation & Death

After earlier successes against the Pallavas, Pulakeshin is defeated by Narasimhavarman I, who captures and sacks the capital of Vatapi. Pulakeshin is believed to have died defending his capital.
