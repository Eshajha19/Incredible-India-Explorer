⚔️ Mewar · 8 March 1535

Siege of Chittorgarh 1535

When Sultan Bahadur Shah of Gujarat brought the new science of gunpowder artillery to the walls of Chittor, the defenders of Mewar answered with the oldest code of honour. On 8 March 1535, Rani Karnavati led the women of the fort into the flames of jauhar, and the warriors of Mewar made a last saka — the second and most famous of Chittor's three jauhars.

The Second Jauhar of Chittor

A Fortress, a Regent, and a Sultan

After Rana Sanga's crushing defeat at Khanwa (1527) and his death the following year, the Kingdom of Mewar fell into faction and decline. Rani Karnavati, the Bundi-born widow of Rana Sanga, governed as regent while an infant heir, Udai Singh, grew up. Meanwhile the Muzaffarid Sultan Bahadur Shah of Gujarat was carving out an empire to the south, sweeping up Malwa (1531) and Raisen (1532).

In December 1532 Bahadur Shah marched on Chittor for the first time. The siege was lifted only when Karnavati paid a heavy ransom and Gujarat withdrew in March 1533. But the threat never left the plateau. In 1534 the Sultan returned with a far larger host and the formidable artillery corps of his renegade Ottoman commander Rumi Khan — guns that no Rajput wall could long withstand.

🏰 At a Glance

  • December 1534 – 8 March 1535 — The second great siege of Chittorgarh.
  • Gujarat Sultanate Victory — Chittor sacked after a breach was blown by artillery.
  • Rani Karnavati — Regent of Mewar, led the women's jauhar.
  • Second Jauhar — ~13,000 women are said to have entered the flames.
Forces in the Field

The Belligerents

A rising artillery-based empire against a proud, outnumbered fortress kingdom — the 16th century's first great clash between cannon and Rajput chivalry.

🏹

Gujarat Sultanate

  • Ruler — Sultan Bahadur Shah (Muzaffarid dynasty)
  • Commander — Rumi Khan, chief of the artillery corps
  • Strength — A large army reported at 50,000–80,000 men with heavy siege guns
  • Tactics — Trenches, mines, and massed cannonade against the walls
🛡️

Kingdom of Mewar

  • Rana — Vikramaditya Singh (younger son of Rana Sanga)
  • Regent — Rani Karnavati; with Rani Jawahir Bai Rathor and Bagh Singh of Deola
  • Strength — A few thousand Rajput defenders against overwhelming odds
  • Tactics — Defensive walls, sorties, and a final suicidal saka

Why it mattered: Chittor's great fort seemed almost unconquerable — it had defied the Delhi Sultans for centuries. Rumi Khan's artillery broke that assumption forever. From this siege onward, the rajas of Rajasthan could no longer trust stone walls alone against gunpowder.

⚔️ The Verdict at Chittor

  • Gujarat Victory — The walls were breached in March 1535 and the fort stormed.
  • The Jauhar — Rani Karnavati and, by tradition, ~13,000 women entered the flames to avoid capture.
  • The Saka — The remaining warriors, led by Bagh Singh, made a last charge; thousands of Rajputs were slain.
  • Escape of the Heir — Infant Udai Singh was smuggled to safety by his nurse Panna Dai, keeping the Sisodia line alive.
Flames That Outlived the Conqueror

Why Chittor 1535 Echoes Still

Bahadur Shah could sack Chittor, but he could not hold it. The Sisodias returned to their plateau soon after his army departed, and Mewar's resistance became the defining legend of Rajput honour. The famous tale of Karnavati sending a rakhi to the Mughal emperor Humayun and invoking his aid is a later invention of the chronicler James Tod — the sober record shows a doomed but defiant defence.

Irony followed the victor. Humayun, alarmed by Bahadur Shah's rise, routed his army at Mandsaur later in 1535 and stripped him of Malwa and Gujarat. The Sultan was killed by the Portuguese at Diu in 1537, and the heir who escaped Chittor's flames — Udai Singh — would found Udaipur and father Maharana Pratap.

Milestones

Chronology of the Siege

From the downfall of Rana Sanga's empire to the death of Bahadur Shah — a decade that reshaped Mewar.

1527–28

Khanwa and the Fall of Sanga

Rana Sanga is defeated by Babur at Khanwa and dies the following year. Mewar, leaderless, descends into succession strife that weakens it for a generation.

1531–32

Bahadur Shah's Rise

The Sultan of Gujarat conquers Malwa (1531) and Raisen (1532). His ambitions now turn to the rich Rajput plateaus of Mewar.

December 1532

The First Siege

Bahadur Shah invests Chittor. Regent Karnavati negotiates, paying a heavy ransom; the Gujarat army withdraws by 28 March 1533.

December 1534

The Guns Return

Bahadur Shah returns with a vast army and Rumi Khan's artillery train. The outnumbered defenders brace for a fight to the end.

March 1535

The Walls Break

Gunpowder mines and cannonade smash a breach in Chittor's defences. Rani Jawahir Bai Rathor leads a fatal sortie against the attackers.

8 March 1535

The Second Jauhar

Karnavati leads the women of Chittor into the flames while the warriors, under Bagh Singh, charge out for the saka. Chittor is sacked.

1535–37

Aftermath

Humayun routs Bahadur Shah at Mandsaur and conquers Gujarat. The Sultan is killed by the Portuguese at Diu (1537); Udai Singh founds Udaipur.
