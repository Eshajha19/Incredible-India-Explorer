🏜️ Hanumangarh, Rajasthan · Indus Valley Civilization

Kalibangan Explorer

Step into Kalibangan, a major Harappan metropolis situated along the dried-up bed of the ancient Ghaggar River. Famous for unearthing the world's earliest excavated ploughed agricultural field, sacred fire altars, and a dual-mound fortified layout.

Bank of the Ancient Ghaggar

Early and Mature Harappan Phases

Located in the Hanumangarh district of Rajasthan, Kalibangan's name translates to "black bangles," inspired by the abundant fragments of terracotta bangles scattered across the ancient surface. Archaeological excavations reveal two distinct cultural phases: the Early Harappan (pre-Harappan) settlement characterized by distinctive pottery and defensive walls, followed by the Mature Harappan urban phase.

Unlike many other Harappan sites, Kalibangan provides clear stratigraphic evidence of transition from a planned proto-urban settlement into a fully integrated Mature Harappan city featuring standard brick sizes, weights, and administrative order.

🏛️ Site at a Glance

  • Location: Hanumangarh district, Rajasthan, India.
  • River Basin: Ghaggar River (ancient Sarasvati valley).
  • Key Discovery: World's earliest excavated ploughed field and ritual fire altars.
  • Layout: Dual mounds comprising a fortified citadel and a lower residential town.
Urban Planning & Layout

Fortified Citadel, Residential Layout & Drainage

Kalibangan exhibits classic Indus urbanism, divided into two primary fortified mounds reflecting social and administrative organization.

🏰

The Citadel (Western Mound)

Heavily fortified with mud-brick walls, containing massive brick platforms, administrative structures, and rows of ritual fire altars.

🏘️

Lower Town (Eastern Mound)

The residential sector laid out in a gridiron street plan with houses constructed of mud and baked bricks featuring courtyards.

🌊

Drainage & Sanitation

Houses utilized soakage jars and terracotta drain pipes to channel wastewater, maintaining hygienic street corridors.

🌾

Ploughed Field Evidence

South-east of the settlement lies a unique pre-Harappan ploughed field showing grid-pattern furrows for dual crop cultivation.

🔥 Sacred & Funerary Customs

  • Fire Altars: Series of brick-lined rectangular pits containing ash and animal bones found on citadel platforms and private homes.
  • Burial Types: Three distinct cemetery burial customs: extended inhumation, urn burials in circular pits, and rectangular graves.
  • Grave Goods: Pottery vessels, beads, and copper ornaments interred with the deceased.
Spiritual & Funerary Life

Fire Altars and Distinctive Burial Practices

One of Kalibangan’s most defining characteristics is the discovery of raised brick platforms housing rectangular fire altars (vedikas). These altars were discovered both in public administrative areas on the citadel and within domestic residences, indicating prominent ritualistic or sacrificial practices among the inhabitants.

The site's cemeteries reveal unique mortuary diversity, reflecting complex social structures and religious beliefs regarding the afterlife across different communities residing within the metropolis.

Chronology

Settlement Timeline & Decline

Tracing the evolution of Kalibangan from early agrarian occupation to Mature urbanism and eventual abandonment.

c. 3500 – 2600 BCE

Period I: Early Harappan Phase

Establishment of the fortified settlement, introduction of copper metallurgy, early pottery, and the famous ploughed field.

c. 2600 – 1900 BCE

Period II: Mature Harappan Metropolis

Reconstruction into a strictly planned dual-mound city with standardized brick sizes, seals, weights, and fire altars.

c. 1900 BCE Onwards

Decline and Abandonment

Gradual desiccation of the Ghaggar river system, ecological stress, and eventual desertion of the site.

Material Culture

Pottery, Craft Activities & Artifacts

Exquisite material remains reflecting high-level artisanal skill and commercial exchange.

🏺

Distinctive Pottery Fabrics

Six distinct fabric types of pottery, featuring fine buff and red wares painted with geometric motifs and animal figures.

⚖️

Weights, Seals & Terracotta

Steatite seals, cubical chert weights, and terracotta animal figurines showcasing administrative standardization.

💍

Bead-Making & Metallurgy

Shell, terracotta, and semi-precious stone beads alongside copper tools and ornaments manufactured in dedicated workshops.
