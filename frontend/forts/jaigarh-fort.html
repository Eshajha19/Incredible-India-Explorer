Jaigarh Fort

Guardian of Amer Fort, and home to the world's largest cannon on wheels.

History

Jaigarh Fort stands on a ridge known as Cheel ka Teela ("Hill of Eagles") in the Aravalli range, directly above Amer Fort near Jaipur, Rajasthan. It was built in 1726 by Sawai Jai Singh II, the founder of Jaipur city, on the foundations of earlier fortifications raised by his ancestors.

For centuries, Jaigarh served as the principal stronghold and cannon foundry of the Kachwaha Rajputs, and was never conquered in battle. It remained largely inaccessible to the public for much of the 20th century, which helped preserve its structures, armoury, and cannon foundry in excellent condition.

Builder

Jaigarh Fort was commissioned by Sawai Jai Singh II of the Kachwaha dynasty in 1726, primarily to strengthen the defences of Amer Fort and to house the state's artillery and cannon-casting foundry.

Jai Singh II, also a noted astronomer, is remembered for founding the city of Jaipur and for planning many of the fortifications and observatories associated with the Kachwaha rulers.

Architecture

Built almost entirely of local reddish sandstone, Jaigarh Fort follows a rugged, functional military layout designed for defence rather than display.

  • Massive Ramparts: Thick fortified walls run along the natural contours of the hill, connecting Jaigarh to Amer Fort.
  • Watchtowers: Multiple towers provide commanding views over the surrounding hills and the approach roads to Jaipur.
  • Cannon Foundry: One of the few surviving cannon foundries in Asia, where the fort's artillery, including the Jaivana cannon, was cast.
  • Diya Burj: A tall tower offering panoramic views, along with palace buildings, a granary, and a small museum of armoury.

Jaivana Cannon

Jaigarh Fort's most famous feature is the Jaivana Cannon, cast in the fort's own foundry in 1720. It is widely recognised as the largest cannon on wheels in the world, with a barrel roughly 20 feet long and a total weight of around 50 tonnes.

Despite its immense size, the Jaivana was fired only once, as a test shot, and was never used in actual warfare. Its massive scale was intended as much to demonstrate Kachwaha military strength as for practical use in battle.

Military Importance

Jaigarh Fort was purpose-built to protect Amer Fort and, later, the city of Jaipur, forming an outer line of defence in the Aravalli hills. Its cannon foundry made it self-sufficient in producing artillery, reducing dependence on outside sources of weaponry.

Thanks to its formidable defences and strategic hilltop position, Jaigarh Fort was never captured by an invading force, and it played a key role in safeguarding the Kachwaha rulers' seat of power for generations.

Interesting Facts

  • The Jaivana Cannon is recognised as the largest cannon on wheels ever built, yet it was fired only once.
  • Jaigarh Fort is connected to Amer Fort by a series of subterranean passages.
  • The fort was closed to the public for several decades in the 20th century, in part due to persistent legends of hidden treasure.
  • Jaigarh housed one of the very few cannon foundries in Asia during its time.
  • Despite its formidable arsenal, Jaigarh Fort was never conquered in battle.

FAQs

Who built Jaigarh Fort?

Sawai Jai Singh II of the Kachwaha dynasty built Jaigarh Fort in 1726, mainly to protect Amer Fort and house the royal cannon foundry.

What is special about the Jaivana Cannon?

The Jaivana is regarded as the largest cannon on wheels ever made, weighing around 50 tonnes, though it was fired only once as a test.

How is Jaigarh Fort connected to Amer Fort?

The two forts are linked by underground passages, allowing safe movement of people and supplies between them, especially during sieges.

Was Jaigarh Fort ever captured?

No. Jaigarh Fort was never conquered in battle, thanks to its strong hilltop defences and self-sufficient cannon foundry.

Why was Jaigarh Fort closed to visitors for years?

It remained largely off-limits for much of the 20th century, partly linked to long-standing legends about hidden treasure within the fort.
