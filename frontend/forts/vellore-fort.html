Vellore Fort

Overview

Vellore Fort is a massive 16th-century fortification located in Vellore city, Tamil Nadu, on the banks of the Palar river. It is widely regarded as one of the finest examples of military architecture in South India.

Spread over a large area and built almost entirely of granite, the fort boasts powerful double ramparts, a deep protective moat, and a unique fusion of Hindu and Islamic architectural elements. Its strategic location and robust design made it a prized possession for successive dynasties - from the Vijayanagara kings to the British.

History

Vellore Fort was built in the 16th century during the Vijayanagara Empire and remained a key stronghold through the turbulent centuries that followed. After the fall of Vijayanagara, the fort passed through the hands of the Bijapur Sultanate, the Mughals, the Marathas, and the Nawabs of the Carnatic.

The fort gained special significance after 1799, when the British imprisoned the family of Tipu Sultan and the last ruler of Sri Lanka, King Sri Vikrama Rajasinha, within its walls. It later became an important garrison town for the British East India Company, and its garrison was at the centre of the historic Vellore Mutiny of 1806.

Builder

Vellore Fort was built by the rulers of the Vijayanagara Empire, most notably during the reign of King Sadashiva Raya, under the supervision of the governor Chinna Bommi Reddy (Bommi Nayakar) in the mid-1500s.

The fort was designed as a formidable military installation - a reflection of the Vijayanagara kingdom's renowned expertise in fortification and granite construction. Over the following centuries, each successive ruling power strengthened and adapted the fort to its own defensive needs.

Vellore Mutiny

The Vellore Mutiny of 10 July 1806 was the first major armed uprising against the British East India Company, half a century before the 1857 Revolt.

The sepoys of the Vellore garrison revolted when the Company introduced new uniform regulations that forbade Indian soldiers from wearing religious marks and mandated a new leather-turbaned headgear, which deeply offended both Hindu and Muslim troops. The mutineers captured the fort and killed several British officers before being brutally suppressed by reinforcements from Arcot.

Although the uprising was quickly crushed, it is historically significant as a precursor to the broader Indian struggle for independence, and it forced the British to reconsider their insensitive policies toward native soldiers.

Architecture

Vellore Fort is celebrated for its splendid granite architecture and harmonious blend of Hindu and Islamic styles.

  • Jalakandeswarar Temple: A beautiful 16th-century Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Shiva, famous for its ornate gopuram (tower) and intricate carvings.
  • Church & Mosque: The fort compound also contains a small Christian church and the Ashtabujakaram mosque, showcasing religious coexistence within the fort.
  • Granite Walls: The entire fortification is built of massive granite blocks, a testament to the engineering skill of the Vijayanagara era.
  • Impressive Gateways: The main gateways are flanked by strong bastions and feature arched, heavily defended entrances.

Moat & Fortifications

The defensive system of Vellore Fort is considered one of the strongest in South India.

  • Deep Moat: The fort is encircled by a broad, deep moat that was historically kept filled with water and infested with crocodiles, making it nearly impossible to storm directly.
  • Double Ramparts: The fort features concentric double walls, providing layered defence against attackers.
  • Bastions & Rounded Towers: Loophole-studded bastions allowed defenders to fire at approaching enemies from protected positions.
  • Mound Fortification: An earthen mound on the outer side further absorbed cannon fire and shielded the inner walls.

Interesting Facts

  • The Vellore Mutiny of 1806 was the first sepoy uprising against the British, predating the 1857 Revolt.
  • The family of Tipu Sultan was imprisoned in the fort after the fall of Srirangapatna in 1799.
  • The last king of Sri Lanka, Sri Vikrama Rajasinha, spent his final years imprisoned within the fort.
  • The fort's moat was once stocked with crocodiles as a living barrier.
  • The Jalakandeswarar Temple inside the fort is a fine example of late Vijayanagara temple architecture.
  • The fort never fell to a direct frontal assault - it was usually taken through negotiation or betrayal.

Location & Map

Vellore Fort is located in the heart of Vellore city in northern Tamil Nadu, about 140 km from Chennai, on the banks of the Palar river.
