Chandragiri Fort

The glorious last capital of the majestic Vijayanagara Empire.

History

The history of Chandragiri Fort dates back to the 11th century when it was first built by the Yadava rulers. Over the centuries, it passed through the hands of various dynasties before coming under the control of the Vijayanagara Empire in 1367.

It rose to prominence during the rule of Saluva Narasimha Deva Raya. The fort served as an important provincial capital, safeguarding the rich southern territories of the empire.

Vijayanagara Empire

Following the disastrous Battle of Talikota in 1565 and the subsequent sack of Hampi (the main capital), the Aravidu dynasty—the fourth and final dynasty of the Vijayanagara Empire—relocated their capital first to Penukonda and finally to Chandragiri.

During its time as the capital, Chandragiri Fort witnessed the empire's twilight years, attempting to hold off the Deccan sultanates while continuing their rich tradition of art, literature, and temple patronage.

Raja Mahal

The crown jewel of Chandragiri Fort is undoubtedly the Raja Mahal (King's Palace). Unlike traditional Hindu architecture of the era which predominantly used wood, the Raja Mahal is constructed entirely of stone, brick, and lime stucco without any timber.

This three-story magnificent structure exemplifies the blending of Hindu and Islamic architectural styles (Indo-Saracenic), featuring graceful arches, intricate stucco work, and a majestic central tower (Shikara) capped with Hindu elements.

Architecture

The fort is encircled by massive stone walls and bastions designed to repel artillery fire. Apart from the Raja Mahal, another significant surviving structure is the Rani Mahal (Queen's Palace), similar in design but smaller, possessing a flat roof and elegant arches.

The fort layout originally included lush gardens, deep wells, and temples, showcasing the Vijayanagara mastery over civic planning and aesthetics even in a defensive stronghold.

Museum

Today, the Raja Mahal houses a splendid archaeological museum maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI). The museum exhibits a rich collection of artifacts unearthed from the region, including:

  • Bronze and stone sculptures of Hindu deities.
  • Ancient weapons, armory, and cannons used by the Vijayanagara army.
  • Panchaloha (five-metal alloy) idols dating back to the Chola and Vijayanagara periods.
  • Coins, copper plate inscriptions, and manuscripts offering glimpses into the empire's administration.

Interesting Facts

  • The Grant of Chennai: It was within the walls of Chandragiri Fort in 1639 that the British East India Company successfully negotiated and signed the treaty to acquire the land for building Fort St. George, which eventually became the city of Madras (Chennai).
  • Timber-less Palace: The Raja Mahal is famous for its complete absence of wood in its construction, utilizing a robust mix of stone and mortar to support its massive weight.
  • Tirupati Proximity: The fort is located in close proximity to the sacred Tirumala Venkateswara Temple, often serving as a resting place for royals on pilgrimage.

Location & Map

Chandragiri Fort is situated in the Tirupati district of Andhra Pradesh, about 15 kilometers from the holy city of Tirupati.
