Murud-Janjira Fort

The unconquered island citadel: A marvel of naval architecture that withstood the might of the Maratha, Portuguese, and British empires.

History

Murud-Janjira, originally known as 'Medhakot', has a history that dates back to the 15th century. It was initially a small wooden structure built by a Koli chief. In 1489, the fort was captured by Malik Ambar, a prominent minister of the Ahmadnagar Sultanate, who recognized its strategic potential.

The fort was subsequently developed and fortified by the Siddis, an ethnic group of African descent who served as naval chiefs for the Deccan Sultanates. Under the Siddi rule, Janjira transformed into an impregnable maritime stronghold. The name 'Janjira' is believed to be derived from the Arabic word 'Jazeera', meaning 'island'.

Despite being surrounded by the expanding Maratha Empire, the fort remained fiercely independent. It was only after the decline of the Siddi power and the advent of the British Raj that the fort's strategic importance waned, leading to its eventual abandonment.

Builder

The initial wooden structure was erected by local Koli fishermen. However, the transformation of Medhakot into the formidable stone fortress of Janjira was spearheaded by Malik Ambar in the late 15th century.

The most significant expansions and fortifications were carried out by the Siddi rulers, particularly Siddi Surul Khan and his successors, throughout the 16th and 17th centuries. Their expertise in naval warfare and coastal defense is evident in every stone of the fort.

Island Fort

Murud-Janjira is uniquely situated on an oval-shaped rock approximately 3 kilometers off the coastal village of Murud. This isolation was its greatest defense.

  • Natural Moat: The surrounding Arabian Sea acted as a natural moat, making direct assaults by land-based armies virtually impossible.
  • Maritime Access: The fort could only be accessed by boats, and the single entrance was carefully guarded and designed to be easily defended against naval boarding parties.
  • Self-Sufficiency: Despite its isolation, the fort contained three sweet water springs, ensuring the garrison could withstand prolonged blockades without running out of drinking water.

Defensive Features

The fort's reputation as 'unconquered' is rooted in its ingenious defensive design:

  • Rounded Bastions: The fort features 26 rounded bastions, which were more effective at deflecting cannonballs than flat walls.
  • Triple-Layered Walls: The main walls are exceptionally thick, ranging from 15 to20 feet, providing immense resistance to artillery fire.
  • Strategic Cannon Placement: The bastions were equipped with heavy cannons, including the famous 'Kalaal Bangadi', 'Chavri', and 'Landa Kasam', which could fire accurately at approaching enemy ships.
  • Deceptive Entrance: The main gate is positioned such that any attacking ship would be exposed to crossfire from multiple bastions before even reaching the entrance.

Architecture

The architecture of Murud-Janjira is a blend of indigenous Indian and Islamic military styles, adapted for coastal defense.

  • Stone Masonry: The fort is constructed using large blocks of laterite and basalt stone, bound together with lead and lime mortar, creating a structure of immense durability.
  • Palace Complex: Inside the fort, there are ruins of a palace, including the 'Diwan-e-Aam' (Hall of Public Audience) and 'Diwan-e-Khas' (Hall of Private Audience), indicating its role as an administrative center.
  • Mosques and Tombs: Several mosques and the tombs of Siddi rulers are present within the fort walls, reflecting the cultural and religious life of its inhabitants.

Battles

The fort's history is punctuated by numerous failed attempts to capture it:

  • Maratha Sieges: Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj launched multiple campaigns against Janjira, including a notable siege in 1676. Despite his naval prowess, the fort's defenses held firm.
  • Portuguese Conflicts: The Portuguese, who controlled nearby territories, frequently clashed with the Siddis but were never able to breach the fort's walls.
  • British Era: The British East India Company also attempted to subdue the fort, but it was only through diplomatic treaties and the gradual decline of the Siddi state that the fort came under indirect British influence, never by military conquest.

Interesting Facts

  • Murud-Janjira is one of the very few forts in India that remained unconquered throughout its active military history.
  • The fort's cannons were named with great reverence; it is said that the 'Landa Kasam' cannon was so powerful that its recoil had to be absorbed by a specially designed mechanism.
  • The Siddi rulers of Janjira were known for their exceptional naval skills and were often employed as admirals by the Deccan Sultanates.
  • Despite being surrounded by salt water, the fort's internal wells provided sweet, potable water, a mystery that added to its legendary status.

Location & Map

Murud-Janjira Fort is located off the coast of the village of Murud in the Raigad district of Maharashtra. It is accessible by ferry from the Murud jetty.
