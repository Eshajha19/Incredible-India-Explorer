Panhala Fort

The sentinel of the South: A majestic hill fort deeply intertwined with the legacy of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and the legendary escape to Vishalgad.

History

Panhala Fort boasts a rich and layered history that predates the Maratha Empire. Its origins trace back to the 12th century when it was constructed by the Shilahara dynasty, specifically under the reign of King Bhoja II. The fort served as a crucial administrative and military center for the dynasty, overseeing the trade routes that passed through the Sahyadri ranges.

Following the decline of the Shilaharas, the fort changed hands multiple times. It was controlled by the Yadavas of Devagiri, the Bahmani Sultanate, and later the Adil Shahi dynasty of Bijapur. Each ruling power left its architectural imprint on the fort, adding bastions, gates, and palaces to the existing structure.

However, Panhala's most glorious chapter began in the mid-17th century when it became a pivotal asset in the rising Maratha Empire. Its strategic location made it a frequent target for the Adil Shahi forces, leading to numerous sieges and battles that tested the resilience of its defenders.

Builder

The original foundation of Panhala Fort was laid by the Shilahara dynasty in the 12th century. King Bhoja II is credited with establishing the initial fortifications, utilizing the natural basalt cliffs of the region to create a defensible stronghold.

Subsequent expansions were carried out by the Adil Shahi dynasty in the 16th century, who added significant architectural elements, including the iconic Teen Darwaza and various palace structures. Later, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj extensively renovated and strengthened the fort in the 17th century, adapting it to the needs of the Maratha military machine and ensuring its role as a key defensive outpost.

Maratha Empire

Panhala Fort holds a special place in the annals of the Maratha Empire. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj first captured the fort in 1659, shortly after the famous encounter with Afzal Khan. Recognizing its strategic value in controlling the southern borders of his growing kingdom, Shivaji Maharaj made Panhala his headquarters for several months.

The fort served as a vital link in the Maratha communication and supply network, connecting the core territories around Pune and Raigad with the southern conquests. It was from Panhala that Shivaji Maharaj administered the southern provinces and planned further expansions. The fort's resilience during the Adil Shahi sieges became a symbol of Maratha determination and military prowess.

Sajja Kothi

One of the most poignant structures within Panhala Fort is the Sajja Kothi. This elegant, multi-storied pavilion is believed to have been the residence of the fort's commander or a high-ranking official during the Adil Shahi period.

The Sajja Kothi is renowned for its intricate architectural details, including arched windows, decorative brackets, and a commanding view of the surrounding valleys. According to local legend, it was from the balconies of this very building that Shivaji Maharaj observed the movements of the besieging Adil Shahi army, planning his daring escape to Vishalgad. Today, it stands as a serene spot for visitors to reflect on the fort's storied past.

Teen Darwaza

The Teen Darwaza, or Triple Gate, is the most iconic and architecturally significant entrance to Panhala Fort. Built during the Adil Shahi period, this massive structure is a masterpiece of military architecture.

As the name suggests, it consists of three consecutive gateways, designed to create a deadly choke point for any invading force. The gates are flanked by high, imposing walls and bastions, allowing defenders to rain down projectiles on attackers trapped between the gates. The intricate stone carvings and the sheer scale of the Teen Darwaza make it a highlight for any visitor to the fort.

Major Battles

Panhala Fort has witnessed several historic battles, the most famous being the events leading to the Battle of Pavan Khind in 1660.

  • Siege of Panhala (1660): The Adil Shahi general, Siddi Jauhar, laid siege to Panhala Fort with a massive army. Shivaji Maharaj, realizing the fort could not hold out indefinitely, negotiated a temporary truce to buy time.
  • The Escape to Vishalgad: Under the cover of a stormy night, Shivaji Maharaj executed a daring escape from Panhala, heading towards the safety of Vishalgad Fort. To ensure his safe passage, his loyal commander, Baji Prabhu Deshpande, along with 300 Maratha soldiers, held off the pursuing Adil Shahi army at the narrow Ghod Khind pass.
  • Battle of Pavan Khind: Baji Prabhu Deshpande and his men fought valiantly, sustaining heavy casualties but successfully delaying the enemy until they heard the cannon fire from Vishalgad, signaling Shivaji Maharaj's safe arrival. The pass was subsequently renamed Pavan Khind (Sacred Pass) in honor of their sacrifice.

Interesting Facts

  • Panhala is the largest fort in the Pune-Kolhapur region, sprawling over a significant area with a perimeter of approximately 7 kilometers.
  • The fort features an impressive network of hidden escape routes and secret passages, designed to facilitate the movement of troops and supplies during sieges.
  • Within the fort complex, there are several ancient temples, including the Ambabai Temple, which is highly revered by locals and visitors alike.
  • The fort's name is derived from the Marathi word "Panalha," which refers to a type of snake, possibly indicating the presence of such reptiles in the area in ancient times.

Location & Map

Panhala Fort is located in the Kolhapur district of Maharashtra, approximately 20 kilometers northwest of Kolhapur city. It is easily accessible by road and is a popular destination for history enthusiasts and trekkers.
