Bahu Fort

A 3,000-year-old sentinel overlooking the Tawi River, steeped in myth, history, and devotion.

History

The Bahu Fort stands on a plateau on the left bank of the Tawi River. Perhaps the oldest fort and edifice in the city of Jammu, its origins are believed to date back over 3,000 years. The fort has seen centuries of rise and fall of various empires, having been destroyed and rebuilt numerous times.

It was most notably rebuilt and refurbished by the Dogra rulers in the 19th century, giving it its current formidable appearance. Throughout its existence, it served as a primary defensive fortification for the Jammu region.

Builder

According to legend, the fort was originally constructed by Raja Bahulochan, the brother of Raja Jambu Lochan (who is credited with founding the city of Jammu). The current structure, however, is heavily influenced by the renovations and expansions carried out by the Dogra Kings, particularly Maharaja Gulab Singh and his successors in the 19th century.

Architecture

The fort is a stunning example of regional military architecture mixed with religious significance. Constructed primarily with sandstone and lime mortar, it features:

  • Thick, imposing defensive walls equipped with battlements and loopholes for cannons and muskets.
  • Octagonal watchtowers at intervals for panoramic surveillance of the Tawi river valley.
  • A massive entrance gate that accommodated elephants.
  • Subterranean chambers and secret escape routes typical of historic Indian forts.

Bawe Wali Mata Temple

Within the precincts of the fort lies the highly revered Bawe Wali Mata Temple, dedicated to the Hindu goddess Kali. She is considered the presiding deity of Jammu.

The temple attracts thousands of pilgrims every week, especially on Tuesdays and Sundays, which are considered auspicious. During the bi-annual Navratri festivals, the fort and temple complex host massive fairs (melas) where devotees gather in huge numbers to offer prayers.

Gardens (Bagh-e-Bahu)

Surrounding the fort is the beautifully landscaped Bagh-e-Bahu garden, terraced down the hillside towards the Tawi River. Inspired by the Mughal gardens of Kashmir, it features symmetrical layouts, manicured lawns, vibrant flower beds, and cascading water fountains.

The garden offers a serene environment and breathtaking panoramic views of Jammu city and the surrounding mountains, making it a favorite picnic spot for locals and tourists alike.

Interesting Facts

  • Macaque Population: The fort complex is home to a large population of rhesus macaque monkeys, considered sacred by many visitors.
  • The Tawi View: It provides the best vantage point for viewing the Tawi River, historically referred to as the Surya Putri (Daughter of the Sun).
  • Aquarium: The Bagh-e-Bahu garden complex houses India's largest underground aquarium.

Location & Map

Bahu Fort is located approximately 5 km from the city center of Jammu, situated on a rock face on the left bank of the Tawi River.
