Rajmachi Fort

The majestic twin-fort complex guarding the ancient trade routes of the Sahyadris.

History

Rajmachi Fort boasts a long and turbulent history. Historically, the fort was primarily under the control of the Maratha Empire, who recognized its strategic importance. It changed hands numerous times, passing between the Marathas, the Adil Shahi dynasty, the Mughals, and finally the British.

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj captured this fort in 1657 from the Adil Shahi ruler of Bijapur. The fort witnessed intense battles during the Mughal-Maratha wars and played a crucial role during the First Anglo-Maratha War in the 18th century.

Builder

While the exact original builders remain shrouded in early medieval history, the fort was significantly expanded and fortified by the Satavahana dynasty in the early centuries CE. Later, major renovations and reinforcements were carried out by the Maratha Empire under the reign of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj to enhance its defensive capabilities.

Twin Forts

Rajmachi is unique because it is not a single fort, but rather a sprawling complex consisting of two distinct citadels (Ballekillas) built on two adjacent peaks of the Sahyadri mountains:

  • Shrivardhan Fort: The taller of the two peaks (approx. 2710 feet), it served as a high-altitude observation post offering commanding views of the entire region.
  • Manaranjan Fort: Slightly lower (approx. 2510 feet), this fort is more expansive and features triple lines of massive fortification walls.

Trekking Route

Today, Rajmachi is celebrated as one of the most popular trekking destinations in Maharashtra, especially during the monsoon season when the Sahyadris are draped in lush greenery and waterfalls.

There are two primary trekking routes to the fort:

  • Lonavala Route: A long but relatively flat 15 km walk from Lonavala via Tungarli lake. It's a scenic and relaxed trail.
  • Kondhane Route: A steeper, more challenging ascent originating from Kondhane village in Karjat, which passes by the ancient Kondana Buddhist Caves.

Architecture

The architecture is primarily functional and military-oriented, utilizing the natural basalt rock of the Sahyadris. The fort complex features:

  • Massive Ramparts: Thick stone walls conforming to the contours of the hill.
  • Water Cisterns: Numerous rock-cut water cisterns that provided fresh water year-round.
  • Secret Escape Routes: Hidden doorways used for sudden sorties or escapes during sieges.
  • Ancient Temples: The base village (Udhewadi) houses the ancient Kal Bhairava temple.

Strategic Importance

The primary purpose of Rajmachi was to control the historical Bor Ghat—a prominent trade route that connected the coastal Konkan region (and its ports) to the Deccan Plateau.

By controlling Rajmachi, the ruling powers could monitor trade caravans, collect tolls, and prevent enemy armies from easily ascending from the coast into the Deccan heartland.

Interesting Facts

  • Fireflies Festival: During the pre-monsoon period (May-June), the forests around Rajmachi become illuminated by millions of fireflies, creating a magical spectacle.
  • Udhewadi Village: A small settlement of about 60 families exists at the plateau between the two peaks, providing rustic hospitality to trekkers.
  • Buddhist Connection: The proximity to the Kondana Caves indicates that the trade route was active and protected since ancient Buddhist times (1st Century BC).

Location & Map
