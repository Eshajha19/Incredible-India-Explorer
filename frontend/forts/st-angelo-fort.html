St. Angelo Fort

A historic Portuguese fortress overlooking the Arabian Sea.

Overview

St. Angelo Fort (also known as Kannur Fort or Fortaleza de Santo Angelo) is a historic fort built on the seashore near Kannur, Kerala. It stands on a rocky headland that juts into the Arabian Sea, at the northern end of the beach of Payyambalam.

Constructed in 1505, the fort is one of the oldest and best-preserved European forts in India. Its towering red laterite walls, bastions, and moat were designed to dominate the sea lanes of the Malabar Coast - a region that was at the heart of the global spice trade for centuries.

History

St. Angelo Fort was built in 1505 by Francisco de Almeida, the first Portuguese Viceroy of India, with the permission of the local Kolathiri (Kolathiri raja) rulers. It was the first Portuguese fort in the East and became a crucial stronghold for protecting Portuguese trade interests along the Malabar spice route.

The fort witnessed centuries of colonial rivalry. In 1663, it was captured by the Dutch, who held it for over a century. In 1772, the Dutch sold the fort to the Arakkal family (the only Muslim royal family in Kerala), and in 1790 the fort passed into British hands, remaining under their control until Indian independence.

Portuguese Heritage

St. Angelo is the oldest Portuguese fort in India, and it is forever tied to the dramatic story of the Portuguese presence on the Malabar Coast.

It was here that Francisco de Almeida established his base of operations. The fort was also the site of tragedy - de Almeida's son, Lourenco, was killed in a sea battle at Chaul in 1508, and the fort itself was besieged by the Zamorin of Calicut. Under the Portuguese, the fort served as a key naval station that protected their spice monopoly against rival European and Arab traders.

Dutch & British Rule

In 1663, the Dutch captured St. Angelo Fort from the Portuguese, and it remained in Dutch hands for more than a century. The Dutch rebuilt and strengthened the fort's defences, adding warehouses and barracks for their garrison.

In 1772, the Dutch sold the fort to the Arakkal (Ali Raja) family of Kannur, who were allies of the region's rulers. However, in 1790, the British East India Company seized the fort from the Arakkal rulers, and it subsequently served as a British military post on the Malabar coast until independence in 1947.

Architecture

The fort is a fine example of early European colonial military architecture adapted to tropical conditions.

  • Laterite Stone Walls: The fort is built almost entirely of laterite (red stone), giving it its distinctive earthy colour.
  • Bastions & Ramparts: High, thick ramparts punctuated by bastions provided sweeping fire over both land and sea approaches.
  • Moat: A dry moat surrounds part of the fortification, adding another layer of defence.
  • Old Cannons: Well-preserved cannons still point out to sea, silent reminders of the fort's military past.
  • Chapel: Remains of a chapel and residential quarters of the garrison survive within the fort.

Lighthouse Views

Adjacent to the fort stands the Kannur Lighthouse, built by the British in 1903. From the fort's elevated ramparts and the lighthouse precinct, visitors are treated to spectacular views of the Arabian Sea, the fishing harbour of Moppila Bay, and the emerald-green Dharmadam Island just offshore.

The combination of the ancient fort, the modern lighthouse, and the unbroken stretch of golden beach makes St. Angelo one of the most photographed coastal landmarks in Kerala.

Interesting Facts

  • St. Angelo is the oldest Portuguese fort in India, built in 1505.
  • It changed hands between the Portuguese, Dutch, Arakkal family, and British over its history.
  • The fort's walls are made of red laterite stone, giving it a unique appearance.
  • The nearby Kannur Lighthouse, built in 1903, offers stunning sea views from the fort.
  • Dharmadam Island, visible from the fort, was once a part of the mainland.
  • The fort was built with the permission of the Kolathiri raja, reflecting the political alliances of the spice trade era.

Location & Map
