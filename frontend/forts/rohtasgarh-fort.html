Rohtasgarh Fort

One of eastern India's largest hill forts, known for its massive gateways and rich medieval history.

Overview

Rohtasgarh Fort is one of the largest and most formidable hill forts in eastern India. It is perched on a plateau at the western end of the Kaimur hills in the Rohtas district of Bihar, at a height of about 1,500 metres above sea level.

The fort is celebrated for its massive gateways, towering ramparts, and the great stone mosque built by Sher Shah Suri. For centuries it was considered an impregnable stronghold, and its rich medieval history links it with the Sur dynasty, the Mughal empire, and the legends of the Kaimur region.

History

Rohtasgarh's origins are steeped in legend. According to tradition, the fort is associated with Raja Harishchandra, whose son Rohtas is said to have given the fort its name. Historically, the fort rose to prominence in the medieval period.

In 1538, Sher Shah Suri captured the fort and used it as a secure base and treasury, ruling the region from here while consolidating his empire. Later, the Mughal emperor Akbar posted his general Raja Man Singh (1598-1605) to Rohtasgarh, who built palaces and a grand baradari within the fort. The fort remained an important Mughal outpost until it passed to the British after the Revolt of 1857.

Builder

While the earliest fortifications of Rohtasgarh predate the medieval era, the fort as it stands today was largely built and expanded by Sher Shah Suri after he captured it in 1538. He constructed the fort's massive walls, gateways, and the great mosque.

The Mughal general Raja Man Singh, who governed the fort from 1598 to 1605, added palaces and a baradari (pillared hall), transforming the fortress into a grand administrative and residential complex at the peak of its glory.

Architecture

Rohtasgarh is a sprawling complex that reflects both Afghan (Suri) and Mughal architectural traditions.

  • Massive Stone Walls: Vast ramparts of dressed stone encircle the plateau, following its natural contours.
  • Sher Shah's Mosque: A beautiful mosque built by Sher Shah Suri, notable for its elegant arches and domes.
  • Rani Mahal: The Queen's palace, one of the finest surviving structures, with intricately carved pillars.
  • Baradari: A twelve-pillared hall built during Man Singh's tenure for audiences and gatherings.
  • Diwan-i-Am: The hall of public audience where the governor heard petitions.
  • Water Systems: Large stepped tanks and wells ensured water supply for the garrison.

Gates & Bastions

Rohtasgarh's most famous feature is its series of massive gateways, each a formidable structure in its own right.

  • Main Gateways: The fort's principal entrances are tall, arched gateways built of huge stone blocks, designed to be closed and defended against any assault.
  • Defensive Bastions: Rounded bastions and watchtowers along the walls provided commanding views over the passes below.
  • Fortified Ramp Approach: A steep, winding ascent from the plains led through successive gates, making a direct attack almost suicidal.
  • Impregnable Reputation: So strong were the gates and walls that the fort was long regarded as the most secure stronghold in eastern India.

Historical Significance

Rohtasgarh played a pivotal role in the political history of North India:

  • It was Sher Shah Suri's chief stronghold, from which he secured his treasury and organised the administration of his empire.
  • The fort served as a guardian of the route between the Gangetic plains and the Deccan.
  • Under the Mughals it was a strategic frontier fortress, governed by nobles such as Raja Man Singh.
  • After the Revolt of 1857, the British captured the fort, and it subsequently fell into disuse.
  • Its engineering influenced the design of the later Rohtas Fort in present-day Pakistan, also attributed to Sher Shah Suri.

Interesting Facts

  • Rohtasgarh is one of the largest hill forts in eastern India.
  • It stands on a plateau about 1,500 metres above sea level in the Kaimur hills.
  • The fort's name is linked to the legend of Raja Harishchandra and his son Rohtas.
  • Sher Shah Suri captured it in 1538 and made it his treasury stronghold.
  • Mughal general Raja Man Singh governed the fort from 1598 to 1605.
  • It remained unconquered through most of its history due to its massive gateways and natural defences.
  • The town of Sasaram, with Sher Shah Suri's famous tomb, lies nearby.

Location & Map

Rohtasgarh Fort is located in the Rohtas district of Bihar, about 40 km south-west of Sasaram, on a plateau of the Kaimur hills.
