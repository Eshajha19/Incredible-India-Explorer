Vijaydurg Fort

The 'Gibraltar of the East' - one of the oldest and most powerful sea forts of the Konkan coast.

Overview

Vijaydurg Fort, also known as Gheria, is one of the oldest sea forts on the Konkan coast of India. It stands majestically on the shore of the Arabian Sea near the town of Devgad in Maharashtra's Sindhudurg district.

Built largely of stone and spread across nearly 17 acres, the fort was an unassailable naval bastion. Its massive walls, towering bastions, and strategic position on a rocky promontory made it one of the most formidable fortresses on the western coast. The British, who admired its defensive strength, called it the "Gibraltar of the East".

History

The fort's origins date back to the late 12th century (around 1195 CE) when it was built during the Shilahara (Yadava) dynasty, making it one of the oldest forts in the region. For centuries it remained a secondary stronghold before rising to prominence under the Marathas.

In 1653, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj captured Vijaydurg from the Adil Shahi Sultanate of Bijapur and rebuilt it into a premier naval base. The fort was later expanded and strengthened by his son Sambhaji and, most famously, by the Maratha admiral Kanhoji Angre, who used it as his headquarters.

Vijaydurg withstood repeated assaults, including a major British naval bombardment in 1805, before finally falling to the British East India Company. Even then, it required a combined attack by land and sea to bring it down.

Builder

The original fort was constructed by the Shilahara (Yadava) dynasty in the 12th century. However, its most celebrated transformation came under Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, who captured the fort in 1653 and substantially reinforced its defences.

The fort reached the height of its power under Kanhoji Angre (c. 1698-1729), the legendary admiral of the Maratha navy, who made Vijaydurg the base of the powerful Angre (Angria) fleet that dominated the western Indian coast and challenged the European maritime powers.

Naval Significance

Vijaydurg was the strongest naval base of the Maratha Empire. From its protected anchorage, the Angre fleet launched daring attacks against the Portuguese, Dutch, and British ships plying the Arabian Sea trade routes.

  • Headquarters of the Angre (Angria) naval fleet for over three decades.
  • The sheltered harbour could hold a large fleet while the fort's guns commanded the sea approaches.
  • Repeatedly repelled British and Portuguese naval expeditions, including a failed British attack in 1750.
  • Controlled coastal trade and extracted tolls from European shipping along the Konkan coast.

The fort's success proved that an indigenous Indian navy could effectively resist the powerful European fleets of the 18th century.

Architecture

Vijaydurg's architecture is a masterpiece of military engineering. The fort's massive perimeter walls are up to 30 feet thick and were built with interlocking stones fitted together so precisely that, in several places, no mortar was needed.

  • Massive Ramparts: Thick stone walls rising directly from the rocky shore, making assault by sea nearly impossible.
  • Towers & Bastions: Strategically placed bastions provided overlapping fields of fire over land and sea.
  • Main Gateway: A fortified entrance protected by heavy doors and spikes, designed to withstand battering rams.
  • Water Reservoir: A large, rock-cut freshwater tank inside the fort ensured water supply during long sieges.
  • Bhavani Temple: A shrine to Goddess Bhavani within the fort complex reflects the devotion of the Maratha rulers.

Hidden Underwater Wall

One of Vijaydurg's most astonishing features is a massive underwater wall (sea wall) constructed in front of the fort. Stretching about 1,400 metres along the sea bed, it was a cleverly concealed defensive structure.

Built of stone below the waterline, the wall was deliberately kept just beneath the surface so that enemy warships would run aground on it at low tide, becoming easy targets for the fort's cannons. It was only revealed to the world decades later, after a major cyclone and low-tide conditions exposed it in 1970. This ingenious submerged fortification remains a testament to the advanced naval engineering of the Marathas.

Interesting Facts

  • Vijaydurg is among the oldest sea forts in India, with origins in the 12th century.
  • Its stone walls are up to 30 feet thick, among the thickest of any Indian fort.
  • The fort's walls in places use interlocking stones without mortar, a rare construction feat.
  • Kanhoji Angre used the fort to raid European shipping and collected tolls from passing vessels.
  • The fort withstood a massive British naval attack in 1805 that combined bombardment from both land and sea.
  • The hidden underwater wall remained a secret for centuries and was only discovered when exposed by tides in 1970.
  • The fort houses a freshwater reservoir, temples, and remains of palaces within its ramparts.

Location & Map

Vijaydurg Fort is situated on the western coast of India, near Devgad in the Sindhudurg district of Maharashtra, about 70 km from the town of Malvan. The fort juts out into the Arabian Sea on a rocky promontory at the mouth of the Vaghotan river.
