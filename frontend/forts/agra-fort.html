Agra Fort

Overview

Agra Fort is a massive red sandstone fortress on the western bank of the Yamuna river in Agra, Uttar Pradesh. For nearly a century, it served as the main residence of the emperors of the Mughal Dynasty, until the capital was shifted to Delhi in 1638.

Also known as the "Red Fort of Agra" or "Lal Qila," it is often overshadowed by the nearby Taj Mahal, yet it stands as one of the finest examples of Mughal military and palace architecture, blending defensive strength with imperial luxury.

History

The site of Agra Fort was originally an earlier brick fort held by Hindu Rajput rulers before it came under Mughal control. Emperor Akbar rebuilt it almost entirely in red sandstone starting in 1565, making it his primary residence and seat of power.

Successive emperors - Jahangir and especially Shah Jahan - added white marble palaces, mosques, and gardens within its walls. In a dramatic turn of history, Shah Jahan himself was imprisoned in the fort's Musamman Burj tower by his son Aurangzeb, where he reportedly spent his final years gazing at the Taj Mahal he had built for his wife.

Architecture

Agra Fort showcases the evolution of Mughal architecture - from Akbar's robust red sandstone military structures to Shah Jahan's refined white marble pavilions inlaid with precious stones.

  • Red Sandstone Walls: Massive double-walled ramparts rising about 70 feet, built by Akbar for defence.
  • Marble Pavilions: Shah Jahan's additions, such as the Khas Mahal and Sheesh Mahal, feature delicate marble screens and mirror-work.
  • Persian & Timurid Influence: Symmetrical layouts, arched gateways, and formal gardens reflect Central Asian design traditions adapted to Indian craftsmanship.
  • Semi-Circular Plan: The fort is built in the shape of a semicircle, with its straight side facing the Yamuna river.

Location

Agra Fort stands on the west bank of the River Yamuna in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, roughly 2.5 km northwest of the Taj Mahal. Its riverside position allowed Mughal emperors, including Shah Jahan during his imprisonment, a direct view of the Taj Mahal in the distance.

UNESCO Status

Agra Fort was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, recognised for its outstanding representation of Mughal military and civil architecture. It was among the first sites in India to receive this designation, alongside the Taj Mahal and Ajanta Caves.

Major Attractions

  • Jahangir Mahal: A grand red sandstone palace built by Akbar, blending Hindu and Central Asian styles.
  • Diwan-i-Am & Diwan-i-Khas: The halls of public and private audience, used by the emperor to meet subjects and nobles.
  • Sheesh Mahal: The "Mirror Palace," once used as a royal dressing room, with walls embedded with tiny mirrored glass pieces.
  • Musamman Burj: An octagonal marble tower where Shah Jahan was held captive, offering a distant view of the Taj Mahal.
  • Moti Masjid: The "Pearl Mosque," a serene white marble mosque built by Shah Jahan for the royal household.
  • Anguri Bagh: A charbagh-style ("grape garden") formal garden framed by marble pavilions.

Historical Significance

Agra Fort was the political and ceremonial heart of the Mughal Empire during its golden age, hosting the coronations, courts, and daily governance of emperors from Akbar to Aurangzeb.

It witnessed pivotal moments in Indian history, including Shah Jahan's imprisonment and the fort's later use as a British military garrison and administrative centre after 1803. Today, it remains a powerful symbol of Mughal authority, artistry, and the dramatic family history behind the Taj Mahal.

Interesting Facts

  • Agra Fort is sometimes called the "Red Fort of Agra," distinct from the Red Fort in Delhi built later by Shah Jahan.
  • The fort is estimated to have around 500 buildings in Bengali and Gujarati styles at its peak, though most were later demolished by Shah Jahan and the British.
  • Shah Jahan spent his final years imprisoned in the fort, reportedly viewing the Taj Mahal from the Musamman Burj.
  • The fort's massive walls and moat made it one of the most formidable Mughal military strongholds.
  • It served as the capital of the Mughal Empire until 1638, when Shah Jahan shifted the capital to Delhi.

FAQs

Who built Agra Fort?

Emperor Akbar began rebuilding the fort in red sandstone in 1565. His successors, Jahangir and Shah Jahan, later added marble palaces and mosques within it.

Is Agra Fort the same as the Red Fort in Delhi?

No. They are two different forts. Agra Fort predates the Red Fort in Delhi, which Shah Jahan built later after shifting the Mughal capital from Agra to Delhi.

Why is Agra Fort a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

It was inscribed in 1983 for its exceptional representation of Mughal architecture, combining military fortification with palatial residences.

Why was Shah Jahan imprisoned in Agra Fort?

His son Aurangzeb seized the throne and held Shah Jahan under house arrest in the Musamman Burj for the final years of his life.

How far is Agra Fort from the Taj Mahal?

Agra Fort is about 2.5 km northwest of the Taj Mahal, and both monuments are visible from each other along the Yamuna river.
