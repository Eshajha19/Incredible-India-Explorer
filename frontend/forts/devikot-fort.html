Devikot Fort

An ancient citadel at the heart of Bengal's early medieval history, echoing tales of the Palas, Senas, and early Muslim rulers.

History

Devikot, also historically known as Devkota or Kotivarsha, holds a paramount place in the ancient and medieval history of Bengal. The city was a major administrative center (Adhisthana) during the rule of the Gupta Empire, the Palas, and the Senas.

Following the decline of the Sena dynasty, Devikot gained prominence as the early capital and military stronghold of Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji, the Turkic conqueror who led the early Muslim expansion into Bengal in the early 13th century. The fort became the launchpad for his ill-fated campaign into Tibet.

Builder

The original fortifications of Devikot date back to antiquity, likely expanded during the Pala and Sena dynasties (8th-12th centuries). The most prominent structures whose ruins exist today, including the mosques and military barracks, were heavily modified and fortified by Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji and subsequent governors of the Delhi Sultanate in the 13th and 14th centuries.

Archaeological Importance

Devikot is a treasure trove for historians and archaeologists. Excavations at the site (Bangarh) have revealed continuous occupation from the Maurya period down to the medieval Islamic period.

The site has yielded numerous inscriptions, terracotta plaques, and architectural fragments that document the transition of Bengal's culture from a prominent center of Buddhism and Hinduism to an early Islamic administrative hub.

Architecture

The architecture of the Devikot Fort area displays a unique blend of pre-Islamic Bengal style and early Indo-Islamic military architecture. Key aspects include:

  • Earthen and Brick Ramparts: Massive brick fortifications built upon earlier earthen ramparts, typical of ancient Bengal forts.
  • Dargahs and Mosques: The ruins contain some of the oldest mosques in Bengal, utilizing stones and pillars from older structures.
  • Moats: Evidence of wide defensive moats fed by the nearby Punarbhaba River.

Historical Events

Devikot was the epicenter of several crucial events in Bengal's history:

  • Bakhtiyar Khalji's Death: After his disastrous military campaign in Tibet (1206), a defeated and ailing Bakhtiyar Khalji retreated to Devikot, where he was allegedly assassinated by his lieutenant Ali Mardan Khalji.
  • Early Sultanate Capital: It served as the capital of Bengal under the Khaljis before the administrative center was shifted to Lakhnauti (Gaur).

Interesting Facts

  • Kotivarsha Vishaya: In ancient Gupta inscriptions, the region was referred to as the prosperous district (Vishaya) of Kotivarsha.
  • Dargah of Ata-ullah: The fort area houses the famous Dargah of Maulana Ata-ullah, built during the reign of Sultan Sikandar Shah in the 14th century.
  • River Connection: The fort was strategically situated on the eastern bank of the Punarbhaba river, facilitating both defense and trade.

Location & Map
