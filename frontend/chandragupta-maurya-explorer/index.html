👑 Sovereign & Unifier of Ancient India

Chandragupta Maurya

The visionary founder of the Mauryan Empire who overthrew Nanda rule, defeated Seleucus I Nicator, and forged the subcontinent's first pan-Indian state under Chanakya's guidance (c. 321 – 297 BCE).

At a Glance: Key Historical Metadata

Full Name: Chandragupta Maurya (Sandrokottos in Greek texts)
Dynasty: Maurya Dynasty (Founder)
Historical Era: Ancient India / Iron Age (4th Century BCE)
Predecessor: Dhana Nanda (Nanda Empire)
Successor: Bindusara (Son)
Primary Seat: Pataliputra (Modern Patna, Bihar)

Early Life & Mentorship of Chanakya

Historically verified details about Chandragupta's origins are preserved across ancient Buddhist, Jain, and Greco-Roman traditions:

  • Buddhist Chronicles (Mahavamsa): Describe Chandragupta as belonging to the noble Moriya clan of Pipphalivana.
  • Greco-Roman Records (Justin & Plutarch): Note his meeting with Alexander the Great during Alexander's Punjab invasion (~326 BCE).
  • Jain & Sanskrit Texts: Record his alliance with Chanakya (Kautilya) at Taxila University, where Chanakya trained him in military tactics, covert intelligence, and statecraft.

🔍 Epigraphic records and rock edicts provide contemporary evidence of Mauryan governance and imperial boundaries.

Greek Connections & The Seleucid Treaty (305 BCE)

When Seleucus I Nicator sought to reclaim Alexander's eastern satrapies, Chandragupta confronted his forces near the Indus River around 305 BCE. The conflict culminated in a historic diplomatic treaty:

  • Territorial Cession: Seleucus surrendered Arachosia (Kandahar), Gedrosia (Balochistan), Paropamisadae (Kabul), and Aria (Herat).
  • War Elephants: Chandragupta provided Seleucus with 500 trained war elephants, which proved decisive at the Battle of Ipsus (301 BCE) in Asia Minor.
  • Embassy of Megasthenes: Seleucus sent ambassador Megasthenes to Pataliputra. Megasthenes wrote Indika, providing first-hand Greek accounts of Indian social life, royal courts, and city defenses.

🏛️ Megasthenes recorded Pataliputra as a grand riverine metropolis protected by 64 gates and 570 towers.

Later Life, Jain Tradition & Historical Legacy

Jain historical records (such as the Rajavali-kathe and epigraphs at Shravanabelagola) document that during a severe 12-year famine in South Bihar, Chandragupta abdicated around 298–297 BCE in favor of his son Bindusara.

He embraced Jain monastic vows under Acharya Bhadrabahu, traveled south to Karnataka, and spent his final days practicing Sallekhana (holy fasting unto death) on the hill now named Chandragiri.

🏛️ Unifier of Subcontinental History

Chandragupta Maurya established ancient India's first unified empire, laying administrative foundations that enabled economic prosperity, cultural exchange, and peace under Emperor Ashoka.
