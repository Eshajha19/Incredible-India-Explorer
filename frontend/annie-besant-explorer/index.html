Annie Besant

Leader of the Home Rule Movement

A British-born champion of Indian self-rule who became the first woman President of the Indian National Congress.

"India's fitness for Home Rule does not depend upon her people learning the art of government; they know it already."

Besant founded the All India Home Rule League at Madras on 1 September 1916, campaigning for dominion status for India within the British Empire — the same status already held by Canada and Australia.

Biography

Annie Besant (1847–1933), born Annie Wood in London, was a British social reformer, women's rights campaigner, and leading theosophist who went on to become one of the most influential figures in India's freedom struggle.

She first travelled to India in 1893 as a leader of the Theosophical Society, eventually settling at its international headquarters in Adyar, Madras, and serving as its president from 1907 until her death. She founded the Central Hindu College at Banaras in 1898, which later formed the nucleus of Banaras Hindu University.

Deeply drawn to India's political future, Besant turned from spiritual work to nationalist politics during the First World War, becoming, alongside Bal Gangadhar Tilak, one of the two central leaders of the Home Rule Movement.
