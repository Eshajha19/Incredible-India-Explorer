Karikili Wetland

A pristine Ramsar Site in Tamil Nadu, nurturing freshwater ecosystems and migratory waterbirds.

A Recognized Wetland of International Importance

Located in the Kancheepuram district of Tamil Nadu, Karikili Wetland is a vital freshwater ecosystem. In 2022, it was officially designated as a Ramsar Site, recognizing its international importance for biodiversity conservation. The wetland is part of a larger network of tanks and lakes that historically supported local agriculture and village water needs.

Ramsar Designation

The Ramsar Convention is an international treaty for the conservation and sustainable use of wetlands. Karikili's inclusion highlights its critical role in supporting endangered species and maintaining regional hydrology.

Freshwater Ecosystem & Aquatic Flora

Karikili is a freshwater wetland fed primarily by monsoon rains and runoff from the surrounding catchment area. The nutrient-rich waters support a diverse array of aquatic vegetation.

Water Lilies

Nymphaea species provide crucial surface cover, reducing water evaporation and offering shelter to small fish and amphibians.

Hydrilla & Pondweed

Submerged macrophytes that oxygenate the water column and serve as primary spawning grounds for native fish species.

Reed Beds

Phragmites and Typha dominate the margins, acting as natural bio-filters that trap sediments and heavy metals from runoff.

Birdwatching Paradise

Karikili is a haven for ornithologists and birdwatchers. During the winter months (November to February), the wetland hosts thousands of migratory birds arriving from Central Asia and Europe.

Northern Pintail: A striking migratory dabbling duck frequently seen in large flocks.
Garganey: A small duck that uses the wetland as a critical wintering ground.
Purple Heron: A resident wader that hunts stealthily among the reed beds.
Black-winged Stilt: Recognizable by its exceptionally long pink legs and contrasting black-and-white plumage.

Interesting Facts

  • Karikili Wetland spans approximately 58 hectares of protected water body and surrounding marshland.
  • It acts as a crucial flood buffer for the surrounding villages during the Northeast Monsoon.
  • The wetland supports over 100 species of resident and migratory birds.
  • Local communities actively participate in conservation efforts, recognizing the wetland's role in groundwater recharge.
