Beas Conservation Wetlands Explorer

Journey along the mighty Beas River — a lifeline of northern India, a Ramsar-designated riverine wetland, and the last refuge of the endangered Indus River Dolphin in India.

Global Importance

Ramsar Site & Riverine Wetland Ecosystem

Designated a Wetland of International Importance in 2020, the Beas Conservation Reserve protects a dynamic riverine ecosystem critical for aquatic biodiversity and migratory species.

💧

Ramsar Site No. 2448

Designated 2020

The Beas Conservation Reserve was designated a Ramsar Site for its riverine wetland ecosystem supporting threatened species, including the Indus River Dolphin, Indian Mahseer, and numerous migratory waterfowl along the Central Asian Flyway.

  • 6,429 hectares of riverine wetland habitat
  • Critical habitat for 8–10 Indus River Dolphins
  • Over 50 fish species, including endemic cold-water taxa
  • 200+ bird species recorded annually
🏞️

Beas River System

470 km Himalayan River

Originating from Beas Kund at Rohtang Pass (4,361 m), the Beas flows 470 km through the Kullu Valley and Punjab plains before merging with the Sutlej at Harike. Its braided channels, sandbars, and riparian forests create a dynamic riverine ecosystem.

  • Lifeline for millions in Himachal Pradesh and Punjab
  • Major tributary of the Indus River system
  • Supports hydroelectric projects and irrigation canals
  • Sacred river mentioned in the Rigveda as Vipasa
Hydrology & Ecology

River Ecosystem & Seasonal Dynamics

The Beas River undergoes dramatic seasonal transformations driven by Himalayan snowmelt and monsoon rainfall, shaping the rhythms of its aquatic life.

Flagship Species

Indus River Dolphin — Conservation Icon

The Beas Conservation Reserve is the only confirmed breeding habitat of the endangered Indus River Dolphin in India, representing the easternmost population of this rare species.

About the Indus River Dolphin

The Indus River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica minor) is a freshwater dolphin subspecies found only in the Indus River system. Blind and highly adapted to murky river waters, it navigates using echolocation. The Beas population of 8–10 individuals represents a critically important conservation stronghold.

Threats to Survival

Conservation Action

Protecting the Beas Dolphins

India's first dedicated dolphin passage at Vikas-1 Barrage, community-based monitoring programs, and active habitat restoration efforts are giving the Indus River Dolphin a fighting chance for survival in the Beas.
