🏔️ Dhauladhar Range, Himachal Pradesh

Hanuman Tibba Explorer

Standing at 5,982 metres in the Dhauladhar range of Himachal Pradesh, Hanuman Tibba is the highest peak of the Dhauladhar and one of the most iconic mountains visible from Manali. Named after Lord Hanuman, the monkey god of Hindu mythology, this majestic peak towers over the Beas River valley and serves as a dramatic backdrop to the popular Solang Valley, drawing trekkers and mountaineers from across the globe.

Geography, History & Significance

The Abode of Hanuman

Hanuman Tibba stands at the western end of the Dhauladhar range, in the Kullu district of Himachal Pradesh. The peak dominates the skyline above Manali and the Solang Valley, its massive pyramid-shaped summit visible from nearly every vantage point in the Kullu Valley. The mountain's name translates to "Peak of Hanuman" in Hindi, honouring the Hindu monkey god who is said to have rested here during his mythical flight to Lanka.

The Dhauladhar range forms the southern boundary of the Lahaul and Spiti district, rising abruptly from the lush green Kullu Valley. Hanuman Tibba, at 5,982 m, is the highest point of this range and feeds several glacial streams that flow into the Beas River. The mountain's north face is a dramatic wall of ice and rock, while its southern slopes descend through alpine meadows into the Solang Valley.

The first recorded ascent was made in 1939 by a British expedition, and the peak has since become a popular objective for intermediate mountaineers seeking a challenging but accessible Himalayan summit. The mountain also holds spiritual significance for the local Himachali people, who consider it a sacred abode of Lord Hanuman.

Trekking Guide

Routes, Camps & Conditions

Essential information for planning a safe and rewarding Hanuman Tibba trek or summit attempt.

🥾

Base Route

The standard approach begins from Solang Valley near Manali, trekking through alpine meadows and moraine fields to reach the base camp at approximately 4,200 m on the Beas Glacier. The trail passes through dense pine forests before emerging onto open grazing lands used by local Gaddi shepherds.

⛺

Camp Strategy

A two-camp structure is typical: Camp 1 at ~4,800 m on the glacier moraine below the southwest face, and Camp 2 at ~5,400 m on the upper snowfield. Summit attempts begin before dawn from Camp 2 to take advantage of firm, frozen snow conditions and stable early morning weather.

🗓️

Best Season

June–September offers the most stable weather for climbing. The Dhauladhar range receives heavy snowfall in winter and early spring, making the approach extremely dangerous. Late June to mid-July and late August to September provide the best weather windows with clear skies and firm snow conditions.

📋

Permits & Logistics

A trekking permit from the District Magistrate's office in Manali is required. Registration at the forest checkpost near Solang Valley is mandatory. All supplies must be carried from Manali, which is approximately 14 km from the trailhead. Local guides and porters can be hired in Manali town.

Nearby Attractions

Explore the Surrounding Region

Discover remarkable destinations within reach of the Hanuman Tibba area.

⛷️

Solang Valley

A popular adventure sports destination at 2,560 m near Manali, offering paragliding, skiing, and zip-lining. It serves as the primary base camp for Hanuman Tibba treks and provides stunning views of the Dhauladhar range.

~14 km away
🏔️

Rohtang Pass

A high mountain pass at 3,978 m connecting the Kullu Valley to the Lahaul-Spiti valley. Famous for its snow-covered landscape and panoramic views of surrounding peaks including Hanuman Tibba.

~51 km away
🏯

Hadimba Temple

An ancient cave temple dedicated to Goddess Hadimba, surrounded by a cedar forest in Manali. Built in 1553, it is one of the most visited temples in Himachal Pradesh.

~15 km away
🏔️

Deo Tibba

A 6,001 m peak adjacent to Hanuman Tibba in the Pir Panjal range, visible from the Solang Valley approach. A popular climbing objective for those seeking a more challenging summit.

~25 km away
