Chandrashekhar Azad

"Azad hi raha, azad hi rahunga." - The mentor and protector of India's most daring revolutionaries.

The Free Soul

Born Chandrashekhar Tiwari on July 23, 1906, in Bhavra, he adopted the name "Azad" (Free) during his first arrest for participating in the Non-Cooperation Movement. When asked by the magistrate for his name and address, the 15-year-old boldly declared his name was "Azad," his father's name was "Swatantrata" (Independence), and his residence was "Jail."

He was sentenced to 15 lashes, bearing each one with stoic defiance, cementing his legend among the youth. Azad became the undisputed chief of the revolutionary underground, acting as the mentor to Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru, while constantly evading the vast British intelligence network.

Revolutionary Organizations

Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)

Founded in 1924 by Sachindra Nath Sanyal, the HRA aimed to establish a "Federal Republic of United States of India" through armed revolution. Azad joined early and became a key strategist.

Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA)

In 1928, under Azad's leadership and alongside Bhagat Singh, the HRA was reorganized into the HSRA. The shift reflected a deeper ideological commitment to socialism and ending capitalist exploitation alongside British rule.
