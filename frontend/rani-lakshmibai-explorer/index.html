Rani Lakshmibai

"Main apni Jhansi nahi dungi!" - The Queen of Jhansi and icon of the 1857 Revolt.

The Warrior Queen

Born Manikarnika Tambe in 1828 in Varanasi, she was married to the Maharaja of Jhansi, Gangadhar Rao, and renamed Lakshmibai. Unusually for women of her time, she was educated at home and trained in martial arts, horse riding, and shooting.

When her husband died without a biological male heir, the British East India Company, under Lord Dalhousie's "Doctrine of Lapse," refused to recognize their adopted son, Damodar Rao, and annexed the state of Jhansi in 1854. This injustice ignited her fierce resistance.

Timeline of Resistance

Battles of the 1857 Revolt

Siege of Jhansi (1858)

British forces led by Sir Hugh Rose laid siege to Jhansi. Despite being heavily outnumbered, Lakshmibai defended the fort fiercely for weeks before escaping under the cover of night.

Battle of Gwalior (1858)

After joining forces with Tatya Tope, she captured Gwalior. In the final battle against the 8th King's Royal Irish Hussars, she fought valiantly in men's attire before succumbing to her wounds.

An Enduring Legacy

Rani Lakshmibai became a potent symbol of Indian nationalism and female empowerment. Sir Hugh Rose, her British adversary, famously described her as "the most dangerous of all Indian leaders" and "the best and bravest of all." Today, she is immortalized in folk songs, statues, and the women's regiment of the Indian National Army named in her honor.
