🚂 Revolutionary Movements · 9 August 1925

The Kakori Train Action & the Price of Rebellion

Four minutes after midnight on 9 August 1925, ten young revolutionaries of the Hindustan Republican Association stopped the Saharanpur–Lucknow passenger train at the village of Kakori and walked away with the government's treasury chest. The robbery of about ₹8,000 cost the British Raj nothing — yet within two years it sent four men to the gallows and transformed the revolutionary movement into the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association.

Why the Movement Turned Violent

The Road From Non-Cooperation to the Gun

When Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 after the violence at Chauri Chaura, hundreds of young nationalists felt betrayed. Having given up schools, colleges, and law practices for a struggle that ended without Swaraj, a new generation concluded that the path of constitutional protest and mass Satyagraha had failed — and that only armed revolution could win freedom.

In October 1924, Ram Prasad Bismil, Sachindra Nath Sanyal, and Jogesh Chandra Chatterjee founded the Hindustan Republican Association (HRA) in Kanpur. Its manifesto — drafted by Sanyal as Bandee Jeevan ("Imprisoned Life") — called for the establishment of a Federal Republic of India through revolution. For two years the HRA organised in secret across the United Provinces and Punjab, but it was cripplingly poor: weapons, ammunition, and travel cost money the young conspirators did not have.

🌍 At a Glance

  • 9 August 1925 — ten HRA revolutionaries loot the government treasury chest from a train at Kakori.
  • ≈ ₹8,000 seized — a fraction of the funds the HRA needed to arm itself.
  • 40 arrested, 28 tried in the Kakori Conspiracy Case at Lucknow.
  • 4 hanged — Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, Roshan Singh.
  • 1928 — survivors regroup as the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).

The Night Itself

The Action of 9 August 1925

Months of planning, a weapon-carrying march through the night, and a four-minute hold-up — this is what happened at Kakori.

01

The Boarding

Around 10:30 pm on 9 August, the revolutionaries boarded the 8 Down Saharanpur–Lucknow passenger train — some at Lucknow, others at nearby stations — so as not to draw attention. They carried revolvers and loaded clips hidden in their clothing and in baskets of sweets.

02

The Chain Is Pulled

Just past Kakori station, around 11:40 pm, the emergency chain was pulled and the train shuddered to a halt on open track. As the guard rushed to inspect, a group of young men moved through the carriages towards the guard's van where the treasury chest was kept.

03

The Treasury Is Taken

Overpowering the guard, the revolutionaries forced open the iron chest and seized the government treasury cash — about ₹8,000 intended for the district treasury. In the scuffle a shot was fired, wounding the guard. The gang melted into the fields and by dawn had dispersed across the countryside.

04

The Manhunt Begins

The robbery was a direct strike at the state itself. Within days, a wide police dragnet swept the United Provinces, offering rewards and raiding homes. Informers, a recovered Mauser pistol, and traces of a map of the province began to close the net around the HRA's leaders.

The Net Closes

Arrests & the Conspiracy Case

The British response was swift and total. Rewards were announced, and a special police team led by the Chief of Police in Lucknow began connecting the dots. One by one the conspirators were caught — often betrayed by informers offered clemency. Ram Prasad Bismil was arrested at the end of October 1925; Rajendra Lahiri at Banaras in November; Ashfaqulla Khan in December, tracked from a train in the guise of an Afghan horse dealer.

In all, more than 40 people were arrested and 28 were put on trial before the Sessions Court of Lucknow in what became known as the Kakori Conspiracy Case. The proceedings ran from 1925 to 1927 — a year and a half of hearings under tight police security, watched by the entire country as the accused turned the courtroom into a platform for the revolutionary cause.

⚖️ The Charges

  • Conspiracy to wage war against the King-Emperor.
  • Robbery of government property on a railway.
  • Murder — attempted and actual, over the wounding of the guard.
  • Unlawful possession of arms and ammunition.

The British framed the action not as theft but as an act of war, using the full machinery of the criminal law to destroy the HRA.

Sept 1925 Arrests

The First Arrests

Police sweep the United Provinces. Some accused surrender or are captured within weeks as informers begin testifying for the prosecution.

Oct–Dec 1925 Arrests

Leaders Captured

Ram Prasad Bismil is arrested in late October, Rajendra Lahiri at Banaras in November, and Ashfaqulla Khan in December — disguised, but recognised on a train.

1925–1927 The Trial

The Kakori Conspiracy Case

Twenty-eight accused face trial before the Sessions Court at Lucknow. The proceedings last about two years; the accused use the dock as a forum, refusing to beg for mercy.

1927 Sentences

Verdict: Four Death Sentences

The court sentences Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqulla Khan, Rajendra Lahiri, and Thakur Roshan Singh to death. Four others are transported for life; the remainder receive long terms of imprisonment.

1927 Sentences

Appeals Dismissed

Appeals to the High Court and the Privy Council fail. Petitions for commutation — including appeals from across the country — are rejected by the Viceroy.

17–19 Dec 1927 Aftermath

The Executions

Rajendra Lahiri is hanged at Gonda on 17 December 1927. On 19 December, Ram Prasad Bismil is hanged at Gorakhpur, while Ashfaqulla Khan and Roshan Singh are hanged together at Allahabad.

Feb 1928 Aftermath

The HRA Becomes the HSRA

Survivors including Chandrashekhar Azad gather at the Feroz Shah Kotla grounds in Delhi and reconstitute the organisation as the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, pledging to continue the armed struggle.

Why It Still Matters

Revolutionary Impact & the Road to 1929

Kakori gave the revolutionary movement what it had lacked — martyrs, a legend, and a lesson. The British won the trial and lost the propaganda war.

📣

Public Sympathy

The executions turned the accused into national heroes. Processions, poems, and protests followed the trials, and the romance of the young "Kakori martyrs" drew a new generation towards revolutionary politics.

🔄

From HRA to HSRA

With Bismil and Sanyal dead or jailed, Chandrashekhar Azad rebuilt the movement. In 1928 the Hindustan Republican Association became the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) — more disciplined, more socialist, and soon to be joined by Bhagat Singh.

🎯

A Blueprint for Action

The Kakori model — precise, symbolic strikes against the state's machinery — was refined in later actions: the Saunders killing in Lahore (1928) and the Delhi Assembly bombing of April 1929, in which Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt courted arrest.
