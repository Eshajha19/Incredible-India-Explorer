🐦 Western Ghats Endemic Bird

Wayanad Laughingthrush Explorer

Argya delesserti is a gregarious, ground-feeding songbird found only in the Western Ghats. Loud and chatty in flocks, yet notoriously hard to spot in the dense undergrowth it calls home.

🌿 Introduction

The Wayanad Laughingthrush (Argya delesserti) is a sociable, medium-sized songbird endemic to the Western Ghats of southern India. Despite its name, the species is not confined to the Wayanad plateau — it ranges much further, from south of Goa down to the southern hill ranges of Tamil Nadu.

It belongs to the laughingthrush group, birds named for their loud, chattering, almost human-like calls. Though vocal and often travelling in noisy flocks, the Wayanad Laughingthrush keeps to dense undergrowth, making it far easier to hear than to see.

First described by British physician-naturalist Thomas C. Jerdon in 1839, it remains one of the characteristic understorey birds of the Western Ghats' moist forests today.

🔬 Scientific Name

The species is scientifically known as Argya delesserti (Jerdon, 1839). The species epithet delesserti honours the French naturalist Adolphe Delessert, who collected the original specimens near Kotagiri in the Nilgiri Hills.

  • Binomial name: Argya delesserti (Jerdon, 1839)
  • Common names: Wayanad Laughingthrush
  • Synonyms: Pterorhinus delesserti, Garrulax delesserti, Dryonastes delesserti, Ianthocincla delesserti
  • Type locality: Kotagiri, Nilgiri Hills, Tamil Nadu

Note: Laughingthrush taxonomy has been revised several times as researchers study genetic relationships within the family. Some ornithological references list this species under the genus Pterorhinus rather than Argya — both names refer to the same bird.

🗺️ Distribution Map

The Wayanad Laughingthrush is patchily distributed along the Western Ghats, south of Goa, and becomes rarer toward the northern end of its range.

🗺️

Range: Goa → Castle Rock, Karwar & Dandeli (Karnataka) → Wayanad & Nilgiris → Brahmagiris → Ashambu Hills (Tamil Nadu / Kerala border)

  • Recorded near Goa, Castle Rock, Karwar, Dandeli, and Bhatkal in the north (uncommon here)
  • The Wayanad plateau and Nilgiri hills, its namesake stronghold
  • Brahmagiri Hills along the Karnataka–Kerala border
  • Southward to the Ashambu Hills near the Kerala–Tamil Nadu border

🌳 Habitat

This species occupies subtropical and tropical moist forests, favouring dense undergrowth, forest edges, and secondary growth from the lowland plains up into the higher hills of the southern Western Ghats.

Unlike the four other south Indian laughingthrush species, which are restricted to high-elevation shola-grassland habitats, the Wayanad Laughingthrush tolerates a broader elevational range — giving it a wider overall distribution.

🪶 Physical Characteristics

  • Upperparts: Dark brownish-grey plumage on the back and wings
  • Face: A broad black mask running through the eye
  • Throat: Distinctive clean white throat patch
  • Underparts: Muted grey breast transitioning to a rufous belly
  • Bill: Heavy bill, pale yellow on the lower mandible
  • Tail: Uniformly dark, darker than the back

It can be told apart from the similar rufous-vented laughingthrush by its pale lower mandible and the lack of rufous outer tail feathers.

👀 Behaviour

Highly gregarious, the Wayanad Laughingthrush usually moves in flocks of 6 to 15 birds, though gatherings of up to 40 have been recorded. Flocks stay low, skulking through undergrowth and rarely venturing into the open.

Their calls are loud and varied — shrill chattering, cackling, churring, and chirping notes, some of which resemble the calls of the rufous babbler. This vocal, hidden-in-plain-sound behaviour is exactly what gives laughingthrushes their name.

🍽️ Diet

The species forages mainly on the forest floor, flipping over fallen leaves and litter to uncover food. Its diet consists chiefly of insects, supplemented with fallen seeds and berries.

Foraging in flocks likely helps the birds spot predators and locate food more efficiently across the leaf litter of the forest understorey.

🪺 Breeding

Breeding has been recorded across a wide elevational range, from the plains to the higher hills of southern India. Nests are typically built low in dense vegetation, offering cover from predators.

Interestingly, more than a pair of adults have occasionally been observed attending a single nest, suggesting the species may practise cooperative breeding, with related "helpers" assisting in raising the young — a behaviour seen in several other social laughingthrush species.

🌍 Ecological Importance

By foraging through leaf litter for insects, the Wayanad Laughingthrush helps regulate invertebrate populations in the forest understorey. Its consumption of berries and seeds also contributes to seed dispersal within its habitat.

As a species tied closely to intact, moist Western Ghats forest, its continued presence in an area is a useful sign of a relatively undisturbed understorey ecosystem.

🛡️ Conservation Status

The IUCN Red List currently classifies the Wayanad Laughingthrush as "Least Concern" (assessed by BirdLife International in 2018, under the synonym Garrulax delesserti), reflecting its relatively wide range across the Western Ghats compared to other south Indian laughingthrushes.

"Least Concern" does not mean the species is free of pressure — its reliance on undisturbed moist forest understorey means continued habitat loss could shift its status in future assessments.

⚠️ Threats

  • Habitat loss: Clearing of moist forest and undergrowth for agriculture and plantations
  • Fragmentation: Roads and settlements breaking up continuous forest cover the species depends on
  • Infrastructure development: Expansion of roads, resorts, and hydropower projects across the Western Ghats
  • Understorey disturbance: Since it rarely leaves dense cover, degradation of undergrowth specifically affects this species more than canopy birds

🏞️ Protected Areas

The species has been recorded across several protected areas spanning its range in the Western Ghats, including:

  • Dandeli-Anshi (Kali) Tiger Reserve and Bhimgad Wildlife Sanctuary, Karnataka
  • Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary, Kerala
  • Silent Valley National Park, Kerala
  • Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, spanning Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu
  • Kalakad-Mundanthurai Tiger Reserve, near the Ashambu Hills, Tamil Nadu

✨ Interesting Facts

📛 A Name Bigger Than Its Range

Named after the Wayanad plateau, this bird actually ranges far beyond it — from south of Goa all the way to the Ashambu Hills near Kerala's southern tip.

🗣️ Loud but Elusive

Flocks can be heard chattering loudly from the undergrowth, yet spotting the birds themselves often takes patience — they rarely break cover.

🤝 Possible Helper Birds

Multiple adults tending a single nest hints at cooperative breeding, where non-parent birds may help raise chicks that are not their own.

🎖️ Named for a French Naturalist

Its species name delesserti honours Adolphe Delessert, who collected the type specimen near Kotagiri in the Nilgiri Hills.

🐦 One of Five Southern Specialists

It's one of five laughingthrush species found in the hills of south India — but the only one not restricted to the highest-elevation shola forests.
