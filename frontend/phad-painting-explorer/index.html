📜 Rajasthani Scroll Painting · Shahpura, Rajasthan

Phad Painting Explorer

Phad Painting is a 700-year-old scroll-based storytelling folk art form originating from Shahpura and Bhilwara in Rajasthan. Painted on long cotton scrolls measuring up to 30 feet, Phad paintings serve as mobile shrines carried by priest-singers (Bhopas) who unroll the scrolls at night to chant heroic epics of folk deities Pabuji and Devnarayan.

Centuries-Old Tradition

Mobile Temples & Wandering Bards

Practiced exclusively by the Joshi community of Rajasthan for generations, Phad painting is uniquely paired with performing arts. The Bhopa (priest) and Bhopi (priestess) travel from village to village, unrolling the multi-hued scroll while singing musical folk ballads accompanied by the stringed Ravanahatha instrument.

Every character facing each other inside the scroll represents interaction, and no character faces the viewer directly until the final ritualistic eye-opening ceremony (Nayan Unmilan).

📜 Phad Art Snapshot

  • Origin: Shahpura & Bhilwara, Rajasthan
  • Artisan Community: Joshi Lineage (Chhipa Clan)
  • Performers: Bhopa & Bhopi Priest-Singers
  • Musical Companion: Ravanahatha & Veena
  • Primary Deities: Folk Deities Pabuji & Devnarayan

In Phad painting, scale, color, and eye orientation establish character rank and emotion.

Narrative Cycles

Core Themes & Epics

The traditional Phad repertoire focuses on two major epic sagas.

🗡️ Epic of Pabuji

Chronicles the life of 14th-century Rathore prince Pabuji, venerated as a protector of cattle and savior against plagues. The scroll depicts his brave defense of cows and heroic martyrdom.

🐍 Epic of Devnarayan

Depicts Devnarayan (an incarnation of Lord Vishnu) leading the Gurjar community, fighting evil rulers, and performing miraculous healing arts.

Natural Canvas

Materials & Traditional Process

Phad paintings use 100% natural organic pigments mixed with gum and water.

Khaddar (Handspun Canvas)

Coarse cotton cloth starched with rice paste and burnished with moonstone.

Pilmachi (Yellow Paint)

Used to paint human skin tones and background structures.

Hinglu (Vermilion Red)

Mineral red used for royal garments, tents, and battle banners.

Kajal (Lampblack)

Charcoal soot used for precise black outlines and defining facial features.
