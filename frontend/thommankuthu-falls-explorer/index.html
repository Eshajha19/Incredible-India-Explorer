Thommankuthu Falls

📖 Introduction

The Seven Cascades of Keezharkuthu

A Staircase of Water

Thommankuthu Falls is located near Thodupuzha in the Idukki district of Kerala, nestled within the dense forests of the Western Ghats. The name "Thommankuthu" derives from the local legend of a tribal chief named Thomman who fell to his death here, and "kuthu" meaning waterfall in Malayalam.

Unlike single-drop waterfalls, Thommankuthu presents as a spectacular series of seven cascades arranged like a staircase down the mountain. The Keezharkuthu River, a tributary of the Thodupuzha River, creates this remarkable formation as it negotiates the rocky terrain over a distance of approximately 2.5 kilometres.

Water Source & Geography

The Keezharkuthu River originates from the high-altitude shola forests of the Annamalai hills. Fed by monsoon rains and perennial springs, the river maintains flow throughout the year, though volume varies dramatically with season. The upper cascades emerge from dense evergreen forest while the lower sections open into deciduous woodland.

Geologically, the cascades form where the river crosses bands of resistant laterite rock interspersed with softer sedimentary layers. Differential erosion has carved natural pools at each cascade level, creating the distinctive stepped profile that makes Thommankuthu unique among Kerala's waterfalls.

Upper Cascade (Step 7)

The highest point of the series

The uppermost cascade sits deep within evergreen forest, where sunlight filters through the dense canopy. Water emerges from a narrow rock fissure, creating a powerful jet that plunges 15 metres into a deep emerald pool below.

This step is the least accessible and most pristine — reaching it requires a challenging trek through dense forest. The pool is cold and deep, surrounded by moss-covered boulders and hanging ferns.

Drop: 15 metres
Access: Difficult
Swimming: Not recommended
🌳 Forest Environment

Western Ghats Wilderness

🌿

Evergreen Canopy

Upper reaches feature tropical wet evergreen forest with trees exceeding 40m. Rich in endemic species like Poeciloneuron indicum and Vateria indica. Dense understory with palms, canes, and ferns.

🦜

Bird Diversity

Over 150 bird species recorded including Malabar trogon, great hornbill, Nilgiri flycatcher, and endemic Malabar grey hornbill. Important habitat for Western Ghats endemics.

🦎

Reptile Haven

Home to king cobra, Malabar pit viper, Travancore tortoise, and numerous endemic lizards. The moist rocky habitat supports exceptional herpetofauna diversity.

🦋

Butterfly Paradise

Over 200 butterfly species including endemic Malabar banded peacock, southern birdwing (India's largest), and blue nawab. Riparian zones are particularly rich.

🐘

Large Mammals

Asian elephants, gaur, sambar deer, wild boar inhabit the forest. Occasional tiger and leopard sightings. Important corridor connecting to Idukki wildlife sanctuary.

🌸

Flowering Plants

Over 600 plant species including rare orchids, medicinal herbs, and endemic Impatiens. Seasonal blooms of Strobilanthes (Neelakurinji) every 12 years.

🌧️ Seasonal Variation

Best Time to Visit

Peak Flow

Monsoon (Jun–Sep)

All seven cascades at full volume with thundering rapids. Forest is lush green. Trekking trails can be slippery and leech-prone. Some upper cascades restricted for safety. Dramatic photography.

Best Time

Post-Monsoon (Oct–Feb)

Ideal conditions with healthy flow and safe trekking. Lower cascades perfect for swimming. Pleasant weather with clear skies. Best for photography and forest walks. Recommended visiting period.

Low Flow

Summer (Mar–May)

Water reduces to gentle streams. Some upper cascades may dry completely. Hot and humid conditions. Easier trekking but less spectacular waterfalls. Natural pools remain swimmable.

What to Carry

  • Sturdy trekking shoes with good grip
  • Water bottle and snacks
  • Leech socks (especially monsoon)
  • Rain gear (weather dependent)
  • First-aid kit for minor injuries

Safety Tips

  • Avoid upper cascades during monsoon
  • Swim only in designated safe pools
  • Stay on marked trails
  • Do not venture alone into deep forest
  • Respect wildlife — maintain distance

📍 Nearby Attractions

Places to Explore

Idukki Arch Dam

Asia's first and India's tallest arch dam at 168 metres. Built between two mountains — Kuravan and Kurathi. Spectacular engineering marvel with boating facilities in the reservoir.

45 km away

Kalvari Mount

Christian pilgrimage site with a 14-station way of the cross on a hilltop. Offers panoramic views of Idukki reservoir and surrounding hills. Particularly beautiful at sunset.

35 km away

Malankara Dam

Scenic reservoir on the Thodupuzha River ideal for boating and picnics. Surrounded by hills with beautiful sunrise and sunset views. Less crowded alternative to major tourist spots.

20 km away

Ilaveezhapoonchira

Valley surrounded by three hills with legend that Sita bathed here during exile. Rolling grasslands perfect for short treks. Popular filming location and photography spot.

40 km away

Nadukani Pavilion

Scenic viewpoint offering 360-degree views of Western Ghats. Popular for sunrise viewing and short walks. Located on the Munnar-Thekkady route with tea estates nearby.

55 km away

Anchal Bridge

Historic suspension bridge over Thodupuzha River offering scenic views. Popular for photography and short evening walks. Connects to local village trails and paddy fields.

15 km away
