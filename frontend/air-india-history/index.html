The Genesis (1932)

Tata Airlines: Where it all began

In 1932, visionary J.R.D. Tata founded Tata Airlines, marking the dawn of civil aviation in India. On October 15, 1932, he famously piloted a single-engine De Havilland Puss Moth carrying air mail from Karachi to Bombay, flying via Ahmedabad.

This early aviation history laid the foundation for what would become the flag carrier of a newly independent nation.

  • First commercial flight in India
  • Driven by J.R.D. Tata's passion
  • Initial focus on air mail services

Brand Identity & Aviation Heritage

The Maharaja

Created in 1946 by Bobby Kooka and Umesh Rao, the Maharaja mascot became one of the world's most recognizable advertising icons, symbolizing Indian hospitality and royal treatment.

The Centaur Logo

For decades, Air India's logo was the centaur, a stylized version of Sagittarius shooting an arrow in a circle representing the wheel of Konark, symbolizing speed, strength, and movement.

Aviation Heritage

From art collections to custom-designed aircraft interiors showcasing Indian motifs, Air India has been a flying ambassador of India's rich cultural heritage across the globe.
