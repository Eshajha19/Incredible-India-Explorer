🏆 Symbols & Meaning

Indian Sports Trophy Design

Every Indian sporting trophy is a crafted statement — a choice of shape, material, national symbol, sport motif and inscription. This explorer decodes the anatomy of India's most treasured trophies: why they look the way they do and what each element is meant to say.

Oldest Trophy: Durand Cup (1888) Highest Honour: Khel Ratna (1991–92) Statuette Tradition: Arjuna Award (1961)

The Anatomy of a Trophy

A trophy is a three-dimensional sentence. When a cricketer lifts the Ranji Trophy, when a football club hoists the Durand Cup, or when an athlete receives the Arjuna Award, they are holding a piece of design that encodes history, values and national identity in metal and wood.

Indian sporting trophies combine four layers of meaning: form (the shape chosen), material (the substance that carries prestige), symbol (national and mythological emblems), and inscription (names, mottos and dedications). Each layer answers a question: what is being honoured, by whom, and in whose name?

  • Colonial era (1888–1947): British-administered tournaments donated silver cups — the Durand Cup (1888), the Shimla Trophy (1904–05), the Ranji Trophy (1934). Trophies honoured viceroys, donors and royal patrons in the Western cup-and-urn tradition.
  • Republic & national awards (1947–1991): After independence the motifs turned Indian — the President's Cup adopted the Ashoka Lion Capital, Jawaharlal Nehru donated a silver snake-boat trophy (1952), and the national sports awards turned to Mahabharata figures: Arjuna (1961) and Dronacharya (1985).
  • Modern era (1991 onwards): The Khel Ratna medallion (1991–92) and redesigned bronze statuettes (2009) brought a contemporary, Oscar-style sculptural language to Indian honours.

Shapes & Forms

The outline of a trophy is rarely accidental — it mirrors the sport, the era, and the identity of the person or body it honours.

The Cup & Urn

Cricket • Classical

When Anthony de Mello proposed the national cricket championship trophy in 1934, his sketch was "a Grecian urn two feet high with a lid, the handle of which represented Father Time". The urn shape recalls a vessel poured in memory — fitting for a trophy named after the late Ranjitsinhji.

The Open Bowl

Football • Legacy

The Durand Cup and Santosh Trophy follow the classic silver-bowl form of old European football trophies. An open bowl is designed to be lifted and shown to the crowd — the physical act of raising the cup is the trophy's ritual.

The Statuette

Individual Honours

Arjuna Award (1961), Dronacharya Award (1985) and Dhyan Chand Award (2002) are bronze statuettes of their namesakes — Arjuna drawing his bow, Dronacharya as the guru, Dhyan Chand in hockey action. The form makes the ideal visible: the athlete becomes the trophy.

The Boat Replica

Water Sports

The Nehru Trophy is a silver replica of a chundan vallam (snake boat), placed on a wooden abacus — the exact boat type that raced before Prime Minister Nehru at Alappuzha in 1952. The trophy is the sport itself, shrunk into silver.

The Medallion

Highest Honour

The Khel Ratna (1991–92) is a medallion rather than a cup — a wearable honour designed for personal, portable recognition, in the tradition of state honours like the Bharat Ratna.

The Shield & Plaque

Team & Institution

Team and university honours such as the Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (MAKA) Trophy use shield and plaque forms — broad surfaces made to be engraved with a long roll of winners' names.

Materials & Their Meaning

Precious metal signals worth, but the choice is also historical — colonial silver, royal gold, and the bronze of India's national sports statuettes each tell a story.

Silver

Ranji Trophy • Nehru Trophy

Silver is the classic trophy metal — durable, lustrous and precious without being royal. The Ranji Trophy is struck in silver, and the Nehru Trophy (1952) was donated as "a rolling trophy made in pure silver, in the shape of a snake boat".

Gold

The Ranji Proposal

Gold has historically been reserved for the most elevated honours. When the Maharaja of Patiala claimed the right to fund the Ranji Trophy in 1934, he offered a gold cup valued at £500 in the design submitted by Anthony de Mello — a gesture of royal prestige.

Bronze

National Sports Awards

The Arjuna, Dronacharya and Dhyan Chand awards are bronze statuettes. Bronze is the sculptor's medium — classical, weighty and enduring. After the 2009 redesign, the statuettes received an "antique polishing" finish inspired by Hollywood honours.

Wood

Nehru Trophy Abacus

Wood appears where the trophy is rooted in a place. The Nehru Trophy's snake-boat replica rests on a wooden abacus — the traditional boat-builders' measuring platform — grounding a silver ornament in Kerala's carpentry craft.

Mixed Media

Silver + Wood

Many Indian trophies combine materials deliberately: silver on a wooden base, gold details on silver bodies, enamel for colour. The mix layers meanings — royal metal meets natural craft — and makes each trophy physically unique.

The 2009 Redesign

Design Modernisation

In 2009 the national sports statuettes were re-designed by Odisha artist Gajendra Prasad Sahu — 15-inch tall, roughly two-kilogram bronze figures with an antique polish, "more forceful and meaningful in their look", and far easier to hold than the earlier glass-boxed statuettes.

National Symbols on Trophies

After independence, India's sporting trophies began to carry the country's own iconography — the Lion Capital, the Ashoka Chakra and the national motto.

Ashoka Lion Capital

The State Emblem

The State Emblem of India — three lions of the Sarnath Lion Capital (c. 280 BCE) on an abacus bearing the Dharma Chakra — is the supreme national symbol. It crowns the Durand President's Cup, whose design features "a circular disc atop the Ashoka Lion Capital".

Ashoka Chakra

The 24-Spoke Wheel

The 24-spoke Dharma Chakra sits at the centre of the National Flag and is a recurring motif on Indian medals and trophy discs. The wheel symbolises motion, progress and the turning of justice — the perfect emblem for sport's endless forward movement.

"Satyameva Jayate"

The National Motto

The motto "Satyameva Jayate" ("Truth alone triumphs"), from the Mundaka Upanishad, is inscribed in Devanagari beneath the State Emblem. The Bharat Ratna medal sets the pattern: a platinum State Emblem with the motto on its reverse.

The Tricolour

Saffron • White • Green

Modern Indian trophies frequently echo the saffron, white and green of the National Flag in ribbons, enamel bands and podium bases — a quiet way of wrapping victory in the flag.

Emblem on State Honours

Bharat Ratna Pattern

India's civilian and sporting honours standardised the national emblem on their metal. The Bharat Ratna medallion's reverse carries a platinum State Emblem and the national motto — the same visual language later echoed on the President's Cup and award certificates.

Regulated Use

State Emblem Act, 2005

The Ashoka Lion Capital is not decorative — its use is governed by the State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005. When a trophy carries the emblem, it carries the authority of the Republic.

Sport-Specific Elements

The most beautiful trophies are those that mirror the sport itself — the trophy's form is the sport's signature.

Cricket

Urn • Father Time

The Ranji Trophy's classical urn form, with a Father Time handle, binds cricket's oldest championship to the game's timeless ritual — and to Lord's, where Father Time has watched over cricket since 1926.

Football

Silver Bowl • Three Trophies

Indian football's honours are won as cups. The Durand Cup hands its champions three trophies — the Durand Cup, the Shimla Trophy and the President's Cup — each with its own nickname and design story.

Water Sports

Snake Boat Replica

The Nehru Trophy is shaped like the chundan vallam itself — a 100-foot snake boat shrunk to hand-held silver. No trophy in the world ties its form more literally to the object of the sport.

Archery & Mythology

Arjuna with Bow

The Arjuna Award statuette shows the Pandava archer drawing his bow — the mythological embodiment of concentration and skill, and a permanent link between Indian sport and the Mahabharata.

Coaching

Dronacharya, the Guru

The Dronacharya Award honours coaches with a statuette of Arjuna's guru — the figure who turns talent into champions. The form literally makes the coach the hero of the honour.

Hockey

Dhyan Chand Statuette

The Dhyan Chand Award (2002) presents a statuette of the "Wizard of Hockey", whose three Olympic golds made Indian field hockey legendary — the trophy is the sportsperson.

University Sport

MAKA Trophy

The Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (MAKA) Trophy, awarded to the best-performing university, pairs the plaque form with the name of India's first Education Minister — an institutional honour for institutional sport.

Ritual of the Lift

Design for Celebration

Handles, necks and rims are designed to be gripped, raised and kissed. A trophy's ergonomics are part of its meaning — every trophy is built for a single iconic moment: the lift.

Names & Inscriptions

A trophy is often a memorial made in metal. The name engraved on the trophy — or the names engraved onto it — is the single most important design element, because it fixes who the honour belongs to and whom it remembers.

  • Named after players & patrons: the Ranji Trophy remembers Kumar Shri Ranjitsinhji of Nawanagar, the first Indian to play Test cricket; the Santosh Trophy honours Sir Manmatha Nath Roy Chowdhary, the Maharaja of Santosh and former IFA president; the Durand Cup is named for Sir Henry Mortimer Durand, the British India Foreign Secretary who founded it in 1888.
  • Named after leaders: the Nehru Trophy carries the name and signature of India's first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • Named after mythological figures: the Arjuna and Dronacharya awards take their names and forms from the Mahabharata.
  • Named after sportspersons: the Dhyan Chand Award honours the hockey wizard whose name now also crowns the renamed Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award (2021).
  • Engraved inscriptions: the Nehru Trophy bears the words "To the winners of the boat race which is a unique feature of community life in Travancore Cochin" above Nehru's signature — an inscription that reads like a letter.
  • Rolling names: many trophies engrave every champion's name around the base or band, turning the trophy into a roll of honour that grows richer each year.
  • Rolling vs. permanent: the Durand Cup and Shimla Trophy are rolling trophies (held for a year, returned), while the President's Cup is crafted afresh each year as a permanent keepsake — two very different inscription strategies in one competition.

The pattern repeats across India: name the trophy after the person, then let the trophy remember everyone after.

The Design Evolution

From colonial silver cups to national-emblem statuettes — a timeline of how India's trophy design came of age.

  1. 1888

    Durand Cup founded

    Asia's oldest football tournament begins at Shimla with a silver cup named for Sir Mortimer Durand.

  2. 1904–05

    Shimla Trophy donated

    Residents of Shimla present their own trophy ("The Artistry"), joining the Durand Cup as a rolling honour.

  3. 1934

    Ranji Trophy

    The Maharaja of Patiala funds a Grecian-urn trophy in memory of Ranjitsinhji; Vizzy offers a rival gold-plated cup for the same championship.

  4. 1941

    Santosh Trophy

    The IFA names its inter-state championship after the Maharaja of Santosh and later donates the trophy to the AIFF.

  5. 1947–50

    President's Cup

    The colonial Viceroy's Trophy is replaced by the President's Cup, gifted by Dr. Rajendra Prasad, bearing the Ashoka Lion Capital — the emblem era begins.

  6. 1952

    Nehru Trophy

    Jawaharlal Nehru donates a silver snake-boat trophy for Kerala's Vallam Kali after stepping into the winning Nadubhagam Chundan.

  7. 1961

    Arjuna Award

    India's first national sports honour for individuals — a bronze statuette of Arjuna the archer.

  8. 1985

    Dronacharya Award

    Coaches are honoured with a statuette of the guru Dronacharya.

  9. 1991–92

    Khel Ratna

    India's highest sporting honour is instituted as a medallion (originally the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna).

  10. 2002

    Dhyan Chand Award

    A lifetime-achievement honour introduced with a statuette of the hockey wizard.

  11. 2009

    Statuette redesign

    Arjuna and Dronacharya statuettes are re-designed by Gajendra Prasad Sahu — antique-polished bronze, Oscar-style, easier to hold.

  12. 2021

    Khel Ratna renamed

    The Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna becomes the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, uniting India's highest honour with its greatest hockey icon.
