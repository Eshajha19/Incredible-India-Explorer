⚔️ Manyakheta & Deccan · c. 753 – 982 CE

Rashtrakuta Dynasty Explorer

Explore one of the four great empires of the world described by Arab geographer Sulaiman. Discover the Rashtrakutas — who carved the monolithic Kailash Temple out of a single rock face at Ellora, dominated the Kannauj Tripartite Struggle, and enriched Kannada literature.

Imperators of the Deccan

Military Supremacy and Cultural Patronage

Founded by Dantidurga in 753 CE after overthrowing the Badami Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas established their capital at Manyakheta (Malkhed in modern Karnataka). Over two and a half centuries, they expanded from the Narmada to the Kaveri, repeatedly marching into Northern India to assert supremacy over Kannauj.

Alongside their formidable military, the Rashtrakutas were celebrated patrons of Jainism, Shaivism, and Vaishnavism, commissioning rock-cut architecture at Ellora and Elephanta and fostering Kannada works like Kavirajamarga.

👑 Rashtrakuta At a Glance

  • Era: c. 753 – 982 CE
  • Capital: Manyakheta (Malkhed, Karnataka)
  • Languages: Kannada, Sanskrit, Prakrit
  • Key Architectural Wonder: Kailash Temple (Cave 16), Ellora Caves
Monarchs & Conquerors

Major Rashtrakuta Rulers

Key emperors who forged military victories and created immortal cultural heritage.

👑

Dantidurga (r. 753–756 CE)

Founder of imperial Rashtrakuta power who defeated Chalukya Kirtivarman II and consolidated rule over Maharashtra and Gujarat.

🛕

Krishna I (r. 756–774 CE)

Monarch who commissioned the breathtaking rock-cut Kailash Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora, carved top-down from a cliff face.

⚔️

Govinda III (r. 793–814 CE)

Brilliant military strategist who led triumphant campaigns from the Himalayas to Kanchipuram, dominating the Pratiharas and Palas.

📜

Amoghavarsha I (r. 814–878 CE)

Scholar-king who ruled for 64 years; author of Kavirajamarga (earliest Kannada poetics work), patron of Jain scholar Jinasena, and builder of Manyakheta.

🛡️

Indra III (r. 914–929 CE)

Re-established Rashtrakuta hegemony in Northern India by capturing Kannauj and routing Mahipala I Pratihara.

🏹

Krishna III (r. 939–967 CE)

Last great conqueror who defeated Rajaditya Chola at the Battle of Takkolam (949 CE) and raised a victory pillar at Rameswaram.

🎨 Cultural Milestones

  • Ellora Cave 16 (Kailash Temple): Monolith excavated by removing 200,000 tonnes of rock, twice the area of the Parthenon.
  • Kannada Literature: Golden era of Adikavi Pampa, Sri Ponna, and royal patronage of early Kannada poetry.
  • Elephanta Caves: Trimurti Sadashiva sculpture created under Rashtrakuta realm Influence.
  • Religious Tolerance: Equal patronage to Digambara Jainism, Shaivism, and Islam for foreign Arab traders.
Art & Literature

Cultural & Architectural Contributions

The Rashtrakutas transformed the Deccan into a global center of art and intellectual discourse. Arab traveller Sulaiman ranked the Rashtrakuta Emperor among the four great world rulers alongside the Caliph of Baghdad, the Emperor of China, and the Emperor of Constantinople.

Their architecture stands as an pinnacle of rock-cut engineering, while their courts nurtured early Kannada grammar, Jain philosophy, and Sanskrit treatises.

Chronology

Rashtrakuta Dynasty Timeline

Key historical milestones from Dantidurga's rise to the Western Chalukya revival.

753 CE

Founding by Dantidurga

Dantidurga defeats Kirtivarman II Chalukya, establishing independent Rashtrakuta sovereignty.

c. 760 CE

Excavation of Kailash Temple

Krishna I begins the excavation of the monolithic Kailashnath Temple at Ellora.

814 CE

Ascension of Amoghavarsha I

Start of a 64-year golden age of peace, literature, and building of Manyakheta capital.

949 CE

Battle of Takkolam

Krishna III defeats Chola crown prince Rajaditya, establishing Rashtrakuta authority in Tamil country.

973–982 CE

Fall to Western Chalukyas

Tailapa II overthrows Kakka II, ending Rashtrakuta rule and establishing the Western Chalukya dynasty of Kalyani.
