Senapati Bapat

The fearless leader of the Mulshi Satyagraha and a towering figure in Maharashtra's revolutionary history.

The Making of a Revolutionary

Born Pandurang Mahadev Bapat on November 12, 1880, in Parner, Ahmednagar district, he was a brilliant student who traveled to Britain in 1904 to study engineering. However, his time in London exposed him to Indian revolutionaries like Savarkar, shifting his focus from engineering to the armed struggle for Indian independence.

The Title "Senapati"

He earned the title "Senapati" (Commander-in-Chief) during the Mulshi Satyagraha due to his exceptional leadership, strategic brilliance, and the deep respect he commanded from the local peasantry who viewed him as their military leader against oppression.

The Mulshi Satyagraha (1921-1924)

The Mulshi Satyagraha was a pivotal peasant movement against the construction of the Mulshi Dam by the Tata company, which threatened to submerge 54 villages in the Mulshi taluka of Pune, displacing thousands of farmers without adequate compensation.

The Grievance

The British colonial government and Tata Power acquired fertile agricultural land, offering meager compensation that would leave the Mavlas (local peasants) destitute.

Bapat's Leadership

Bapat organized the peasants, urging them to refuse compensation and physically block the surveyors and construction workers. He adopted Gandhian non-violence but maintained a militant organizational structure.

The Outcome

Though the dam was eventually built and the valley submerged, the Satyagraha forced the authorities to significantly increase compensation and set a precedent for future environmental and displacement movements in India.
