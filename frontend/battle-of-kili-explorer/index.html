⚔️ Kili Plains (Near Delhi) · 1299 CE

Battle of Kili Explorer

In late 1299 CE, Qutlugh Khwaja led a colossal Chagatai Mongol army into India with the explicit objective of taking Delhi. Rejecting advice to stay behind city walls, Sultan Alauddin Khalji marched out to the plains of Kili. Supported by generals Zafar Khan, Nusrat Khan, and Ulugh Khan, the Sultanate established a formidable defense line that broke the Mongol campaign and saved the capital.

Historical Context

The Mongol Threat to the Delhi Sultanate

During the 13th century, the Chagatai Khanate launched repeated incursions into the Indian subcontinent. Previous Mongol raids focused on plunder in the Punjab and borderlands, but in 1299 CE, Chagatai ruler Duwa Khan dispatched his son Qutlugh Khwaja with an army estimated at up to 200,000 horsemen for the outright conquest of Delhi.

As the massive Mongol host bypassed peripheral forts and surrounded Delhi, widespread panic gripped the city. Senior advisor Ala-ul-Mulk urged Sultan Alauddin Khalji to avoid open battle and negotiate terms. However, Alauddin resolutely refused, stating that a monarch who feared open combat was unfit to rule. He mobilized his full army and established a defensive camp at Kili, a few miles north of Delhi.

🏛️ At a Glance

  • Date: Late 1299 CE
  • Location: Kili Plains (Northern suburbs of Delhi)
  • Belligerents: Delhi Sultanate vs Chagatai Khanate (Mongols)
  • Sultanate Leaders: Sultan Alauddin Khalji, Zafar Khan, Nusrat Khan, Ulugh Khan
  • Mongol Leaders: Prince Qutlugh Khwaja, Hijlak, Targhi
  • Key Result: Strategic Sultanate victory; Chagatai withdrawal from India.
Chronology of Events

Timeline of the 1299 CE Campaign

Trace the course of events from the Mongol advance through the defense of Kili and the aftermath.

Late 1299 CE (Autumn)

Mongol Incursion Across the Indus

Qutlugh Khwaja leads a massive Chagatai army across the Indus. The Mongols bypass provincial garrisons, advancing directly towards Delhi with speed and determination.

Late 1299 CE

Refugees & Siege Panic in Delhi

Thousands of rural refugees flood into Delhi. Trade and supply lines are severed. Advisor Ala-ul-Mulk counsels caution, but Alauddin decides on open battlefield confrontation.

1299 CE (Battle Eve)

Deployment at Kili Plains

Alauddin marches his army out of Siri/Delhi and constructs a fortified camp at Kili with trenches, war elephants, and disciplined central reserves.

1299 CE (Battle Day)

Zafar Khan’s Heroic Charge

Zafar Khan commands the Sultanate's right wing and launches a fierce attack on the Mongol left wing under Hijlak. The Mongol flank breaks and flees.

1299 CE (Afternoon)

The Ambush & Last Stand of Zafar Khan

Zafar Khan pursues the fleeing Mongols for 18 miles. Qutlugh Khwaja executes a feigned retreat ambush. Refusing to surrender, Zafar Khan fights to the death.

Night / Following Days

Mongol Retreat & Qutlugh Khwaja’s Death

Terrified by the resilience of the Sultanate center and heavy casualties, the Mongols retreat. Qutlugh Khwaja dies of wounds on the return journey to Transoxiana.

Commanders & Leaders

Key Military Leaders

Profiles of the Sultanate generals and Chagatai commanders who directed the engagement.

Delhi Sultan

Sultan Alauddin Khalji

Supreme Commander of the Delhi Sultanate

Master military strategist and ruler of the Khalji Dynasty. His unyielding decision to fight in the open and his disciplined center formation anchored the defense of Kili.

Right Wing General

Zafar Khan (Hizabruddin)

Governor of Samana & Commander of Right Wing

Renowned for extraordinary bravery and aggressive tactics. His ferocious cavalry charge routed the Mongol left wing, though his aggressive pursuit led to his heroic last stand.

Chagatai Prince

Qutlugh Khwaja

Prince & Supreme Mongol Commander

Son of Chagatai Khan Duwa. Led the largest Mongol invasion force into India aimed at taking Delhi. He succeeded in ambushing Zafar Khan but suffered mortal wounds in the campaign.

Wing Commanders

Nusrat Khan & Ulugh Khan

Commanders of Left Wing & Reserves

Experienced generals and brothers of the Sultan who held the left flank and central reserves securely against Mongol probing attacks, ensuring the main army remained intact.

Tactics & Defense

Battle Strategy & Tactical Execution

Explore the defensive fortifications, combined arms, and tactical maneuvers deployed at Kili.

🛡️

Fortified Trench Line

Alauddin positioned his army behind a pre-dug line of defensive earthworks and trenches to neutralize the terrifying momentum of Mongol cavalry charges and prevent flank encirclement.

🐘

War Elephant Screen

Heavy war elephants armored in iron plates were positioned at regular intervals in front of the infantry and archers to act as mobile fortresses, disrupting Mongol horse archer sweeps.

⚔️

Aggressive Flank Charge

Zafar Khan's right wing launched a high-speed counter-charge against the Mongol left wing under Hijlak, catching the Mongol riders unprepared and driving them back in disarray.

🎯

Disciplined Central Reserve

While Zafar Khan pursued the enemy flank, Alauddin maintained tight control over the central reserve, refusing to break formation and ensuring the main defensive line stood unshakable.

Strategic Impact

Outcome & Consequences

The Battle of Kili was a resounding strategic victory for the Delhi Sultanate. Although Zafar Khan and his brave vanguard perished in the 18-mile pursuit ambush, the overall Mongol invasion force suffered devastating losses and failed in its primary objective of conquering Delhi.

Demoralized by the impenetrable Sultanate defense and the mortal wounding of Prince Qutlugh Khwaja, the Chagatai army broke camp under cover of night and hastily retreated to Transoxiana. Qutlugh Khwaja died during the withdrawal, and the victory permanently established Alauddin Khalji's reputation as the savior of Northern India from Mongol conquest.

📜 Key Outcomes

  • Delhi Saved: The threat of Mongol conquest of Delhi was averted.
  • Heroic Legacy: Zafar Khan's courage became legendary in medieval chronicles.
  • Mongol Casualties: Qutlugh Khwaja died of wounds; Chagatai forces withdrew.
  • Military Reform: Prompted Alauddin to reform the standing army and construct border fortresses.
