Since 1928

A cinema that grew along the backwaters

Malayalam film built its identity on literature and everyday life long before it built star systems — the timeline below follows that current.

1928 — 1938
The silent beginning

J.C. Daniel directed and produced Vigathakumaran, the first Malayalam feature film, shot largely around Thiruvananthapuram. Malayalam cinema found its voice a decade later with Balan (1938), its first talkie.

1965
A national breakthrough

Chemmeen, adapted from Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai's novel about a fishing village, became the first South Indian film to win the President's Gold Medal for Best Feature Film — putting Malayalam cinema on the national map.

1972 — 1980s
The New Wave

Adoor Gopalakrishnan's Swayamvaram opened a parallel-cinema movement built on restraint and social realism. G. Aravindan, K.G. George, Bharathan and Padmarajan followed with work that treated the everyday as worthy of the frame.

1980s — 1990s
The golden middle

A rare middle ground formed between art house and mass appeal — sharp writing, ensemble casts, and character actors carried films that were commercially popular without losing craft.

2010s — Present
New Generation cinema

A younger wave of writer-directors reset the industry's grammar — smaller casts, real locations, unresolved endings — and sent Malayalam titles to festivals and, eventually, Oscar shortlists.

Popular Films

Titles that shaped the industry

A cross-section — from the film that first won Malayalam cinema national recognition to the ones streaming worldwide today.

1965
Chemmeen
Dir. Ramu Kariat

A fishing-village romance that won Malayalam cinema its first national Best Feature honour.

1972
Swayamvaram
Dir. Adoor Gopalakrishnan

The film credited with launching Malayalam cinema's New Wave movement.

1981
Elippathayam
Dir. Adoor Gopalakrishnan

Winner of the British Film Institute's Sutherland Trophy, still studied for its visual restraint.

1993
Manichitrathazhu
Dir. Fazil

A psychological thriller that became one of Malayalam cinema's most remade stories across languages.

2019
Kumbalangi Nights
Dir. Madhu C. Narayanan

An ensemble family drama widely credited with defining the New Generation era's tone.

2019
Jallikattu
Dir. Lijo Jose Pellissery

India's official Oscar entry that year, built around a runaway buffalo and a village unravelling.

2021
The Great Indian Kitchen
Dir. Jeo Baby

A domestic drama, told almost entirely inside one kitchen, that sparked national conversation.

2024
Manjummel Boys
Dir. Chidambaram

A survival drama based on a real cave rescue that became one of Malayalam cinema's biggest box-office hits.

Directors

The filmmakers behind the frame

Six names across six decades — from the founders of the New Wave to the writer-directors resetting the industry today.

Adoor Gopalakrishnan
New Wave · Est. 1972

Founding figure of Malayalam art cinema; Swayamvaram, Elippathayam, Mathilukal.

G. Aravindan
New Wave · Est. 1974

Painter-turned-filmmaker known for Thampu and Kanchana Sita's meditative pacing.

K.G. George
Golden middle · Est. 1976

Sharp social satire in Yavanika and Adaminte Variyellu.

Padmarajan
Golden middle · Est. 1979

Novelist-director behind Thoovanathumbikal and Namukku Parkkan Munthiri Thoppukal.

Lijo Jose Pellissery
New Generation · Est. 2010

Angamaly Diaries, Ee.Ma.Yau., and Oscar entry Jallikattu.

Anjali Menon
New Generation · Est. 2009

Warm ensemble writing in Bangalore Days and Koode.

Recognition

Decades of national and global honours

National Film Award — Best Feature Film
Malayalam has taken this award more times than any other Indian language, starting with Chemmeen in 1965.
Kerala State Film Awards
The industry's own annual honours, run by the Government of Kerala since 1969.
International Film Festival of Kerala
One of Asia's most respected festivals, running annually in Thiruvananthapuram since 1996.
Academy Award submissions
India's official Oscar entry has come from Malayalam cinema several times in the last decade, including Jallikattu and 2018.
