Wada Houses

The grand courtyard mansions of the Maratha Empire.

Introduction to the Wada

The Wada is a traditional residential mansion found primarily in Maharashtra, particularly in Pune, Satara, and the Konkan coast. Flourishing during the Maratha Empire (17th-18th centuries), these structures were designed for large joint families, serving not just as homes but as centers of social, political, and economic life. They are masterclasses in passive cooling and spatial hierarchy.

Cultural Context & Climate Adaptation

🌬️ Natural Ventilation

The central open courtyard (Chowk) acts as a thermal chimney. Hot air rises and escapes through the top, drawing cool air in from the surrounding shaded verandahs, creating a continuous breeze even in peak summer.

☀️ Climate Adaptation

Deep overhanging eaves and thick laterite or brick walls protect the interiors from the harsh Deccan sun and heavy monsoon rains. The sloping roofs with Mangalore tiles ensure rapid water runoff.

🏛️ Social Hierarchy

The layout reflects strict social codes. The public reception areas (Diwankhana) are near the entrance, while the private family quarters and women's spaces (Antahpur) are located deep within, around the inner courtyards.
