Cultural Context

The Painted Mansions of the Marwaris

Located in the semi-arid region of Rajasthan, the Shekhawati region is famously known as the world's largest open-air art gallery. Between the 18th and early 20th centuries, wealthy Marwari merchants built grand *havelis* (mansions) to display their wealth and success.

These havelis were constructed around inner courtyards and were extensively decorated with vibrant frescoes, depicting themes ranging from Hindu mythology and local legends to the arrival of British trains and modern inventions.

Key Architectural Features

  • Fresco Paintings: Elaborate murals covering the interior and exterior walls.
  • Courtyards: Multi-courtyard layouts serving as the center of family life.
  • Jharokhas: Overhanging enclosed balconies providing ventilation and privacy.
  • Materials: Local stone, lime plaster, and timber.

Climate Adaptations

Designed for the Desert

The architecture of Shekhawati havelis is a masterclass in passive climate control, designed specifically for the extreme heat and aridity of the Thar Desert.

The thick walls provide high thermal mass, absorbing heat during the day and releasing it slowly at night. The central courtyards create a microclimate, drawing in cool air at the ground level while hot air escapes upwards. Narrow streets and overhanging *chhajjas* (eaves) maximize shading, keeping the building naturally cool.
