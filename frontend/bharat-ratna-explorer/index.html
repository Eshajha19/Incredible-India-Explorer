🇮🇳 Highest Civilian Honour of India

Bharat Ratna Explorer

Conferred in recognition of exceptional service or performance of the highest order in any field of human endeavour.

Year Instituted: 1954 Total Awardees: 53 First Recipient: C. Rajagopalachari (1954)

History & Legacy

Institution in 1954

The Bharat Ratna was instituted on 2 January 1954 by a presidential notification issued by the office of the Secretary to the President of India, Dr. Rajendra Prasad. It was established as India's premier civilian decoration to acknowledge achievements that transcend standard merit.

Evolution of Scope

Originally, the award statutes restricted conferment to achievements in Art, Literature, Science, and Public Service. In December 2011, the Government of India expanded the criteria to encompass "any field of human endeavour", paving the way for sports personalities and diverse icons.

Eligibility Criteria

  • Universal Access: Open to any person without distinction of race, occupation, position, or sex.
  • Highest Standard: Awarded for exceptional service or performance of the highest order.
  • No Monetary Grant: The award carries no monetary grant, but recipients enjoy top precedence in official protocol.
  • Non-Citizens Allowed: Though typically conferred on Indian citizens, naturalized citizens (Mother Teresa) and foreign nationals (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, Nelson Mandela) have also been honored.

Medal Design & Insignia

🍃

Peepal Leaf Shape

The medal is designed in the shape of a peepal leaf, measuring approximately 5.8 cm in length, 4.7 cm in width, and 3.1 mm in thickness, minted at the India Government Mint in Kolkata.

☀️

Embossed Platinum Sun

The obverse side features a platinum sun emblem surrounded by rays, below which the words "BHARAT RATNA" are embossed in silver-gilt Devanagari script.

🏛️

State Emblem & Motto

The reverse side displays the State Emblem of India (Lion Capital of Ashoka) carved in platinum, along with the national motto "Satyameva Jayate" in Devanagari script.

Selection Process

Recommendations for the Bharat Ratna are made directly by the Prime Minister of India to the President of India. No formal annual recommendation process or external public nominations are mandatory, though the Prime Minister evaluates widespread consensus and national impact.

1

Prime Minister's Evaluation

The Prime Minister selects candidates based on distinguished service of national magnitude.

2

Presidential Approval

The President approves and signs the Sanad (certificate) and decoration.

3

Ceremonial Presentation

Conferred at Rashtrapati Bhavan with full state ceremony.

Interesting Facts

Youngest Recipient

Sachin Tendulkar became the youngest recipient of the Bharat Ratna at age 40 when he was awarded in 2014.

Oldest Living Recipient

Dhondo Keshav Karve was awarded on his 100th birthday in 1958, making him the oldest living recipient at the time of conferment.

Posthumous Awards

Though initially not allowed in 1954, statutes were amended in 1955 allowing posthumous awards. Lal Bahadur Shastri was the first posthumous recipient in 1966.

Non-Citizen Honorees

Two foreign nationals—Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987) and Nelson Mandela (1990)—and one naturalized citizen—Mother Teresa (1980)—have received the honor.
