Reo Purgyil Explorer

Standing majestically at 6,816 meters, Reo Purgyil is the highest peak in Himachal Pradesh. This dome-shaped giant of the Zanskar Range rises above the Sutlej on the India–Tibet border, guarding a landscape of dramatic gorges, high-altitude villages, and ancient monasteries.

Geography, History & Significance

The Guardian of the Upper Sutlej

Reo Purgyil (6,816 m) and its slightly shorter twin Leo Pargial (6,791 m) form a striking twin-peak massif at the southern end of the Zanskar Range in the Western Himalaya, in the Kinnaur district of Himachal Pradesh. Rising directly from the banks of the Sutlej River, the massif marks the border between India and the Tibet region of China.

The mountain's name means 'the mountain that guards' — a fitting description, for this dome-shaped peak is often cloaked in swirling clouds and has long been venerated by the local people. The Spiti River, a right-bank tributary of the Sutlej, drains the northern face of the massif, carving one of the most dramatic river valleys in the Himalaya.

Geologically a dome structure, Reo Purgyil overlooks the western valleys of Tibet and the ancient trade routes of Kinnaur. Its remote, high-altitude setting and the lingering mystery around its early ascents have made it one of the most fascinating — and least visited — high peaks of the Indian Himalaya.

Trekking Information

Plan Your Reo Purgyil Expedition

Essential details for trekkers and mountaineers planning to explore the Reo Purgyil massif region.

🗓️

Best Season

June to September. Summer months offer the most stable conditions, though the massif frequently shrouds itself in cloud. Late September provides the clearest views of the twin peaks.

🥾

Trek Difficulty

Very Difficult. The approach involves high-altitude hiking above 4,500 m with exposed, technical sections. This is an expedition-grade peak for experienced mountaineers only.

⏱️

Duration

A full expedition typically takes 2–3 weeks round trip from Kaza or Nako, including acclimatization at the high camps on the glacier plateau south of the peak.

🏕️

Base Camp

Nako (3,620 m) serves as the primary gateway in Kinnaur. The trek follows the Spiti River valley, passing through the villages of Leo and Pooh before establishing base camp near the massif.

📋

Permits

Special inner-line and Protected Area Permits are required due to the proximity of the international border. Trekking agencies and the ITBP coordinate clearance in advance.

🏔️

Key Highlights

Panoramic views of the Zanskar Range, the twin summits of Reo Purgyil and Leo Pargial, the Sutlej gorges, and the ancient monasteries of Nako with their cliff-side Tibetan settlements.
