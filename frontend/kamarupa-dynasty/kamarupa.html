Historical Overview

A Kingdom of the Brahmaputra

Kamarupa was one of the earliest historically attested kingdoms of Northeast India. Its political centre developed in the Brahmaputra valley, corresponding broadly to parts of present-day Assam and adjoining regions.

The kingdom is known from inscriptions, literary traditions and archaeological remains. Its rulers maintained connections with wider North Indian political and cultural networks while developing a distinctive regional identity.

Kamarupa's history is commonly discussed through three major political phases: the Varman dynasty, the Mlechchha dynasty and the later Kamarupa Pala dynasty.

Historical Timeline

Centuries of Kamarupa

Follow the major political phases that shaped the kingdom's long historical development.

01
c. 4th century
VARMAN DYNASTY

Pushyavarman

Pushyavarman is traditionally regarded as the founder of the Varman line. The dynasty established an important early political foundation in the Brahmaputra valley.

02
c. 7th century
VARMAN DYNASTY

Bhaskaravarman

One of Kamarupa's best-known rulers, Bhaskaravarman maintained diplomatic links with Harsha of North India and appears prominently in the writings of the Chinese traveller Xuanzang.

03
c. 7th–9th century
MLECHCHHA DYNASTY

Salastambha

After the Varman period, political authority in Kamarupa passed to a line commonly known as the Mlechchha dynasty. Salastambha is associated with the beginning of this phase.

04
c. 9th–12th century
KAMARUPA PALA

Brahmapala

The later Kamarupa Pala line emerged in the region during the medieval period. Brahmapala is generally regarded as its founder.

05
c. 10th–11th century
KAMARUPA PALA

Ratnapala

Ratnapala was among the important rulers of the later Kamarupa political tradition. His reign is represented in surviving epigraphic records.
