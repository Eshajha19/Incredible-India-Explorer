Barehipani Falls: Giant Cascade of Similipal

Plunging 399 meters (1,309 feet) down ancient Precambrian volcanic cliffs in a thunderous two-tiered ribbon, Barehipani Falls is India's second-tallest waterfall, fed by the sacred Budhabalanga River in the core of Mayurbhanj's pristine tiger habitat.

Step 1: The Legendary Cascade

The Sacred Ribbon of Budhabalanga

Named 'Barehipani' after the local Mayurbhanj term 'Barahi' (twisted rope), the waterfall resembles a colossal braided silver rope descending over dark Meghasani cliff faces into a secluded, cloud-shrouded jungle valley.

River: Budhabalanga River
Type: Two-tiered tiered plunge waterfall
Rank: 2nd Tallest in India (399m / 1,309 ft)

UNESCO Biosphere

The Untamed Similipal Forest Ecosystem

Barehipani is surrounded by 2,750 km² of dense sal forests, deep gorges, and endangered wildlife sanctuaries.

Flora: Pristine Sal & Wild Orchids

Over 1,076 plant species and 94 varieties of exotic wild orchids carpet the Meghasani slopes under towering Shorea robusta (Sal) canopies.

Fauna: Melanistic Tigers & Elephants

Home to the world's only wild melanistic (black) Royal Bengal tigers, Asiatic wild elephants, leopards, Indian bison (Gaur), and giant flying squirrels.

Geology: Meghasani Volcanic Basalt

Ancient basaltic and spilitic lava flows formed dramatic concentric amphitheater rings dating to the Archean-Proterozoic boundary (2.8+ Ga).

Travel Seasons

Seasonal Variation & Park Visiting Windows

Similipal Tiger Reserve operates strictly on seasonal conservation schedules to protect breeding wildlife.

Winter Peak (November to February) — Best Season

The park reopens on November 1st after the monsoons. Temperatures range from a crisp 8°C to 22°C. The Budhabalanga River roars with fresh post-monsoon volume, mist shimmers over the canyon, and safari tracks are completely accessible.

Flow: Massive Silver Cascade & Dense Mist Park Access: Open (Permits required at Jashipur / Pithabata) Highlights: Wildlife safaris, orchid flowering, birdwatching
