⚔️ Central India · 1737–1738

Battle of Bhopal Explorer

In the winter of 1737, Peshwa Baji Rao I's swift Maratha cavalry trapped the Nizam of Hyderabad and his Mughal army near Bhopal. Cut off from supplies and reinforcements, the Nizam was forced to sue for peace — and the Treaty of Doraha handed Malwa to the Marathas, sealing their supremacy over central India.

The Maratha Ascendancy

A Campaign That Reshaped India

By the 1730s, the Maratha Empire under Peshwa Baji Rao I had become the most dynamic power in India. The Mughal Empire, weakened after Aurangzeb's death, could no longer hold its provinces. The Nizam of Hyderabad, Asaf Jah I, marched north in 1737 to check Maratha expansion into Malwa — and to defend the Mughal order he served.

Baji Rao responded with the tactics that made him legendary: fast-moving cavalry, deep raids, and the encirclement of a slower, heavier enemy. Near Bhopal, the Maratha horsemen surrounded the Nizam's army, cut its supply lines, and waited for hunger to do what battle could not.

🏛️ At a Glance

  • 1737–1738 — Fought near Bhopal, in present-day Madhya Pradesh.
  • Maratha Victory — A campaign won by encirclement and blockade.
  • Treaty of Bhopal — Malwa ceded to the Marathas; a heavy indemnity paid.
  • Maratha Supremacy — Central India fell under Maratha influence.
Masters of the Field

The Commanders

Two of the most capable military leaders of the eighteenth century met at Bhopal.

🛡️

Peshwa Baji Rao I

  • Role — Peshwa (Prime Minister) of the Maratha Empire
  • Command — The Maratha cavalry, famed for speed and mobility
  • Tactics — Encirclement, blockade, and cutting supply lines
👑

Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah I

  • Role — Nizam of Hyderabad, Mughal viceroy of the Deccan
  • Command — A large Mughal army with heavy artillery
  • Defeat — Trapped and starved into surrender at Bhopal

Why it mattered: Baji Rao's victory over the Nizam — the most powerful Mughal noble of his time — proved that the Maratha light cavalry could defeat the Mughal's heavy, artillery-based armies in the field.

⚔️ The Verdict of Bhopal

  • Decisive Maratha Victory — The Nizam's army was surrounded and forced to negotiate.
  • Malwa Ceded — The Marathas gained control of the Malwa region.
  • Indemnity Paid — The Nizam agreed to pay a large sum to the Marathas.
  • Mughal Decline — The defeat exposed the Mughal Empire's inability to defend its provinces.
The Balance of Power Shifts

Why Bhopal Was the Turning Point

Bhopal was not a single day of battle but a campaign of encirclement that ended in the Nizam's surrender. It demonstrated that the Maratha state could defeat the most powerful noble of the Mughal Empire on his own ground.

The victory opened the road to Maratha expansion across central India and beyond. Within a decade, the Maratha banner would fly from Delhi to the Deccan — and the Mughal Empire would never again command the obedience it once held.

The Pen After the Sword

The Treaty of Bhopal (1738)

The agreement signed after the campaign reshaped the political map of central India.

1738

Cession of Malwa

The Nizam agreed to cede the province of Malwa to the Marathas, ending Mughal control over the region.

1738

Indemnity Payment

The Nizam agreed to pay a large indemnity to the Marathas as the price of peace.

Aftermath

Maratha Supremacy

The treaty confirmed the Maratha Empire as the paramount power in central India, paving the way for its later expansion northward.

📜 Lasting Implications

  • Maratha Expansion — Malwa became a springboard for Maratha campaigns into the north.
  • Mughal Weakness Exposed — The empire could no longer protect its own provinces.
  • Rise of the Peshwa — Baji Rao I's reputation as a military genius was cemented.
  • Road to Panipat — The expansion that began at Bhopal would culminate in the Third Battle of Panipat (1761).
