Bengal & Bihar • 750 - 1174 CE

The Pala Dynasty

Great patrons of Mahayana Buddhism, builders of monumental universities, and masters of eastern India.

Historical Overview

The Pala Empire was an imperial power during the Late Classical period on the Indian subcontinent, centered in the region of Bengal and Bihar. Founded by Gopala in 750 CE, the dynasty ruled for about four centuries and brought stability and prosperity to the region. The Palas were staunch supporters of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, and their reign is considered the golden age of Bengal.

They maintained extensive diplomatic and trade relations with the Srivijaya Empire in Southeast Asia and the Abbasid Caliphate. The Palas are also renowned for their distinct style of sculpture and bronze casting, which influenced the art of Tibet and Southeast Asia.

Patrons of Buddhism

The Pala rulers were devout Buddhists and played a crucial role in the revival and spread of Mahayana Buddhism. They built massive monastic universities that attracted scholars from across Asia.

  • Vikramashila University: Founded by Dharmapala, it became the second most important center of Buddhist learning after Nalanda.
  • Somapura Mahavihara: Located in modern-day Bangladesh, this is the largest Buddhist vihara in the subcontinent and a UNESCO World Heritage site.
  • Odantapuri: Another major university founded by Gopala I.

Art & Education

The Pala period saw the flourishing of the 'Pala School of Art', characterized by exquisite bronze sculptures and illuminated palm-leaf manuscripts. Their artistic style heavily influenced the art of Nepal, Tibet, and Myanmar. The proto-Bengali language also began to take shape during this era, with the composition of the Charyapada, the earliest known literary work in the Bengali language.

Historical Timeline

750 CE

Gopala I is elected king, founding the Pala dynasty and bringing an end to a century of anarchy (Matsyanyaya).

770 CE

Dharmapala expands the empire and establishes Vikramashila University.

810 CE

Devapala reigns, considered the most powerful Pala emperor, conquering parts of northern and eastern India.

1040 CE

Atisa Dipankara, a famous Pala scholar, travels to Tibet to spread Buddhism.

1174 CE

The dynasty declines and is eventually replaced by the Sena dynasty.
