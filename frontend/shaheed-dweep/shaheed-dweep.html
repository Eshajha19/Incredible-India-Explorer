Shaheed Dweep

Formerly Neil Island, officially renamed Shaheed Dweep in 2018 — a small, laid-back island known for its natural rock bridge, turquoise beaches, coral reefs and the farms that feed Port Blair's tables.

18.9 km² Total Island Area
3 Main Beaches
~2 hrs Ferry from Port Blair
2018 Renamed Shaheed Dweep

🌉 Natural Bridge (Howrah Bridge)

Neil Island's best-known landmark is a naturally formed rock bridge carved entirely by wave and coral erosion, popularly nicknamed the "Howrah Bridge" after its namesake in Kolkata. It sits at the edge of Laxmanpur Beach, exposed at low tide and partly submerged at high tide.

The rock formation is fringed with small tide pools that reveal starfish, tiny crabs and colourful reef fish, making it as much a mini nature walk as a photo stop.

  • Best visited at low tide, when the arch and surrounding tide pools are fully exposed
  • Roughly a 15–20 minute walk along the beach from the Laxmanpur II entry point
  • Sunrise visits offer the softest light and the fewest crowds

🏖️ Beaches

Neil Island packs three distinct beaches into a very small footprint, each with its own character — from sunrise views to snorkelling bays to a sunset point.

  • Bharatpur Beach — a wide, calm bay near the jetty, popular for glass-bottom boat rides and snorkelling over shallow coral beds
  • Laxmanpur Beach (I & II) — a long stretch of soft white sand facing west, home to the Natural Bridge and a favourite spot for sunset
  • Sitapur Beach — known as "Sunrise Point," a quieter cove on the island's southeastern tip with a rockier shoreline

🐠 Coral Reefs

The shallow waters around Bharatpur and Laxmanpur beaches sit over living coral beds that are easily explored without diving certification, making Neil Island one of the more accessible reef destinations in the Andamans.

  • Glass-bottom boat rides from Bharatpur Beach for a dry view of the reef below
  • Shallow-water snorkelling suitable for beginners, with gear rented locally near the jetty
  • Certified scuba diving trips to nearby reef sites for spotting reef fish, moray eels and soft corals
  • Coral health, like elsewhere in the Andamans, is sensitive to bleaching events — operators encourage reef-safe sunscreen and no-touch snorkelling

🌾 Agriculture

Neil Island is often called the "vegetable bowl" of the Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Its fertile soil and settled farming community — many descended from Bengali and Bihari settlers relocated here after Partition — supply a large share of the fresh vegetables and fruit sold in Port Blair's markets.

  • Small family-run farms growing tomatoes, cabbage, papaya, banana and betel nut
  • Produce ferried daily to Port Blair, roughly two hours away by boat
  • Bicycle rides through the interior villages pass directly through working farmland

🧭 Tourism

Compact enough to explore almost entirely by bicycle or scooter, Neil Island trades the buzz of Havelock (Swaraj Dweep) for a quieter, slower pace — a single small market, a handful of guesthouses, and beaches within easy reach of one another.

It's most often visited as a day trip or short stopover between Port Blair and Havelock, connected to both by regular government and private ferries. The best time to visit is between October and May, avoiding the June–September monsoon.
