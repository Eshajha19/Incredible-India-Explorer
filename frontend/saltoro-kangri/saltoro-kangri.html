Saltoro Kangri Explorer

Rising to 7,742 metres in the remote Saltoro subrange of the Karakoram in Ladakh, Saltoro Kangri is the highest peak of the Saltoro Ridge and one of the most formidable summits in the Siachen region. Its massive ice-clad flanks guard the western approaches to the Siachen Glacier, making it a peak of immense geographical and strategic significance.

Geography, Glaciers & Significance

The Guardian of Siachen

Saltoro Kangri stands in the Saltoro subrange, the southernmost ridge of the Siachen Muztagh in the eastern Karakoram. The peak forms the western wall of the Siachen Glacier, the world's longest non-polar glacier at 76 km, and sits near the Line of Control between India and Pakistan — making it one of the most geopolitically sensitive summits on Earth.

The Saltoro Ridge, of which Saltoro Kangri is the crown, separates the Siachen Glacier on the east from the Nubra and Indus valleys on the west. Its glaciers feed both the Siachen system and the Nubra river, contributing to the broader Indus watershed that sustains millions in Ladakh and beyond.

The first recorded ascent was achieved in 1962 by an Indo-Japanese expedition, a landmark achievement in high-altitude mountaineering. Due to its proximity to the Siachen conflict zone, climbing activity has been extremely limited, and the peak remains one of the least-summitted 7,000-metre peaks in the world.

Technical Specifications

  • Elevation: 7,742 m (25,397 ft)
  • Prominence: 2,160 m
  • Coordinates: 35°36'N 76°59'E
  • Climbing Grade: Alpine D/E
  • First Ascent: 1962 (Indo-Japanese Expedition)
  • District: Leh, Ladakh
  • Sub-range: Saltoro, Siachen Muztagh
