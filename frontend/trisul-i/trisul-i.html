Trisul I Explorer

Rising to 7,120 metres in the Kumaon division of Uttarakhand, Trisul I is the highest of the three iconic Trisul peaks that dominate the skyline above the Nanda Devi massif. Named after the trident (trishul) of Lord Shiva, this sacred mountain has captivated pilgrims, trekkers, and mountaineers for centuries. Its three-pronged summit profile is visible from the holy town of Roopkund and the Auli ski resort, making it one of the most recognisable peaks in the Garhwal Himalayas.

Geography, Mythology & Significance

The Trident of the Himalayas

Trisul I stands in the Chamoli and Pithoragarh districts of Uttarakhand, forming part of the boundary between the Kumaon and Garhwal regions. The mountain derives its name from its distinctive three-peaked summit, which resembles the trishul — the celestial trident of Lord Shiva, one of the most sacred symbols in Hindu mythology.

The Trisul group comprises three peaks: Trisul I (7,120 m), Trisul II (6,660 m), and Trisul III (6,008 m). Together they form a dramatic horseshoe-shaped ridge surrounding the Trisul glacier system. The Trisul glacier feeds the Pindari and Kafni glaciers, which in turn give rise to important tributaries of the Sharda and Alaknanda rivers — both major headwaters of the Ganges.

The first recorded ascent was achieved in 1907 by T.G. Longstaff, making Trisul I one of the earliest 7,000-metre peaks ever climbed. The mountain holds deep spiritual significance for locals, who consider it the abode of divine energies. The famous Roopkund lake, known as the "Skeleton Lake" for the hundreds of ancient human remains found there, lies in the shadow of the Trisul massif.

Trekking Guide

Routes, Camps & Conditions

Essential information for planning a safe and rewarding Trisul trek or summit attempt.

🥾

Base Route

The standard approach begins from Lohajung (2,300 m) in Chamoli district, trekking through the Pindari glacier trail to reach the base camp near the Trisul glacier at approximately 4,500 m. The route passes through dense rhododendron and oak forests before ascending into high alpine terrain.

⛺

Camp Strategy

A three-camp structure is typical: Camp 1 at ~5,200 m on the glacier flat, Camp 2 at ~5,800 m on the upper snowfield, and Camp 3 at ~6,400 m for summit attempts. Pre-dawn starts from Camp 3 take advantage of stable early-morning snow conditions.

🗓️

Best Season

May–June (pre-monsoon) and September–October (post-monsoon) offer the clearest windows. Monsoon (July–August) brings heavy snowfall and increased avalanche risk. Winter months see the route closed due to extreme cold and heavy snowfall at lower camps.

📋

Permits & Logistics

An Inner Line Permit from the District Magistrate's office in Chamoli is required. The peak falls near the Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve, requiring an additional forest department entry permit. Most teams use Lohajung as the last motorable point for supply drops.
