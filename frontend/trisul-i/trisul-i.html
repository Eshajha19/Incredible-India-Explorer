Trisul I Explorer

Standing majestically at 7,120 meters, Trisul I is the highest of the three iconic peaks named after Lord Shiva's trident. It holds a legendary place in mountaineering history as the first 7,000-meter peak ever to be climbed, marking a golden milestone in Himalayan exploration.

Geography, History & Significance

The Trident of Shiva

Trisul I is the highest of the three Trisul massif peaks in the Kumaon region of the Garhwal Himalayas, Uttarakhand. The three peaks — Trisul I (7,120 m), Trisul II (6,690 m), and Trisul III (6,008 m) — form a striking silhouette resembling Lord Shiva's trident, after which they are named.

The mountain is located in the Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve, surrounded by pristine alpine meadows and glaciers. Its prominence and relatively accessible route made it a target for early Himalayan mountaineering, leading to its historic first ascent in 1907.

Today, Trisul I remains a sought destination for experienced trekkers and climbers. The standard route approaches via the Pindari Glacier and offers breathtaking views of the surrounding Kumaon peaks, including Nanda Devi, Nanda Kot, and Panchachuli.

Technical Specifications

  • Elevation: 7,120 m (23,360 ft)
  • Prominence: 1,586 m (5,203 ft)
  • Coordinates: 30°18'44"N 79°46'35"E
  • Climbing Difficulty: Hard / Technical
  • First Ascent: June 12, 1907
  • Expedition Team: T.G. Longstaff & Party
Trekking Information

Plan Your Trisul Expedition

Essential details for trekkers and mountaineers planning to explore the Trisul massif region.

🗓️

Best Season

May to June and September to October. The post-monsoon months offer clear skies and stable weather, while pre-monsoon provides moderate temperatures.

🥾

Trek Difficulty

Moderate to Difficult. The trek involves high-altitude hiking above 4,000 m with steep moraine sections. Prior Himalayan trekking experience is recommended.

⏱️

Duration

The Pindari Glacier trek typically takes 7–10 days round trip from Munsiyari, covering a distance of approximately 90 km with gradual altitude gain.

🏕️

Base Camp

Munsiyari (2,200 m) serves as the gateway. The trek proceeds through Khati village, Dwali, and Phurkia before reaching the Pindari Glacier snout.

📋

Permits

Trekking permits are required from the Forest Department in Munsiyari. The area falls within the Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve, and entry fees apply.

🏔️

Key Highlights

Panoramic views of Trisul I, Nanda Devi, Nanda Kot, and Panchachuli peaks. The trek passes through dense rhododendron forests and alpine meadows.
