📜 History of the Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar

The Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar (National Sports Promotion Award) was instituted in 2009 by the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, Government of India. Its Hindi name translates to "National Sports Promotion Award", and it was created to recognise contributions that had long gone uncelebrated.

While awards like the Khel Ratna and Arjuna Award honour individual sportspersons and the Dronacharya Award honours coaches, no national honour existed for the organisations — corporates, sports control boards, and voluntary organisations — whose funding, infrastructure, and programmes power the entire sports ecosystem.

The award was first conferred on 29 August 2009 at Rashtrapati Bhavan by the President of India, alongside the other National Sports Awards. The inaugural recipients were Tata Steel and the Railways Sports Promotion Board. The award scheme was subsequently revised in 2015, reorganising the categories into the four that exist today.

One of the Six National Sports Awards

The Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar is one of the six core awards that constitute India's National Sports Awards framework, alongside the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna, the Arjuna Award, the Dronacharya Award, the Major Dhyan Chand Award (lifetime achievement), and the Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (MAKA) Trophy. It is the only one of the six that honours organisations rather than individuals.

Growth & Legacy

Since its inception, the award has grown into a coveted institutional honour. As of 2024, 55 awards have been bestowed upon 42 different organisations and individuals. Several bodies — including the Services Sports Control Board (four awards), Tata Steel (three awards), and the Railways Sports Promotion Board — have won the honour multiple times, reflecting sustained, year-on-year commitment to sport.

🎯 Purpose & Objective

The objective of the award, as stated in the official scheme, is to "encourage and promote the involvement of corporates, voluntary organisations, sports control boards, etc., in the promotion and development of sports in the country."

Beyond mere recognition, the Puruskar is a strategic tool of national sports policy. By spotlighting exemplary organisations, it motivates other entities to step forward — whether by funding elite athletes, building academies, hiring sportspersons, or taking sport to underserved communities.

🏭 Celebrate Institutional Contribution

Honours the private and public sector organisations whose sustained investment makes sporting success possible — from grassroots coaching to elite podium finishes.

🤝 Encourage Corporate Participation

Incentivises companies to channel corporate social responsibility (CSR) funds, expertise, and resources into sports promotion and development.

🌱 Build the Grassroots Pipeline

Recognises bodies that identify and nurture budding young talent, expanding the talent pool from which India's future champions emerge.

💼 Champion Sportsperson Welfare

Rewards organisations that employ sportspersons and provide welfare measures, giving athletes security beyond their competitive careers.

🌍 Sports as a Force for Development

Highlights how sport can advance development objectives — peace, inclusion, safe play environments, and community well-being — across India.

🏟️ Strengthen Sports Infrastructure

Motivates the creation and upgradation of academies, stadiums, and training facilities that form the physical backbone of Indian sport.

📋 Eligibility Criteria

The award is open to any corporate entity — public or private — sports control boards, and non-governmental organisations (NGOs), including sports bodies at the state and national level, that have played a visible role in sports promotion and development over the preceding three years.

🏢 Who Can Apply

Corporate entities (public and private), sports control boards, and NGOs including state and national sports bodies with a demonstrable role in promoting and developing sport.

📈 Three-Year Track Record

Contributions are assessed over the preceding three years, ensuring that recognition reflects sustained, measurable commitment rather than one-off gestures.

👦 Nurturing at Scale

For the budding-talent category, an entity should have identified and nurtured at least 50 young sportspersons each year during the last three years.

💼 Employment Threshold

For the employment category, an entity should have recruited at least 50 sportspersons over the last three years, with meaningful roles and welfare measures.

🚫 Government Exclusion

Sports Departments of State and Central Governments mandated specifically for sports promotion are not eligible — the award targets corporate and voluntary participation.

📄 Documentation

Applicants submit audited accounts, memoranda of association, programme details, and supporting documents through the Ministry's dedicated online portal.

🏅 Award Categories

Following the 2015 revision of the scheme, the Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar is conferred in four categories, each recognising a distinct dimension of sports promotion and development.

👦 Identification & Nurturing of Budding Talent

Awarded to entities that systematically identify, train, and mentor young sportspersons — often 50 or more every year — building the grassroots pipeline for Indian sport. Notable winners include Tata Steel and the GoSports Foundation.

🤝 Encouragement to Sports through Corporate Social Responsibility

Recognises companies that channel CSR funds into sports — a minimum of 5% of gross profit or ₹2 crore across three financial years. Recipients include ONGC, Odisha Mining Corporation, and Rashtriya Ispat Nigam.

💼 Employment of Sportspersons & Welfare Measures

Honours organisations that provide careers and welfare to sportspersons — recruiting at least 50 over three years, with promotions and support structures. The Railways and Services Sports Control Boards excel in this category.

🌱 Sports for Development

For NGOs using sport as a tool for social development — safe play environments, school physical-education capacity, and peace-building programmes. Winners include the Ladakh Ski & Snowboard Association and Subroto Mukerjee Sports Education Society.

Discontinued Categories (Pre-2015)

Before the 2015 revision, the award also recognised two further categories — Establishment and Management of Sports Academies of Excellence and Financial Support for Sports Excellence — both of which were folded into or replaced by the current four-category structure. Early winners such as Tata Steel and Khel Evam Yuva Kalyan Vibhag received awards under these original categories.

⚙️ Selection Process

The selection process is designed to be rigorous, transparent, and driven by verifiable impact. It is administered annually by the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports.

1

Call for Nominations

The Ministry invites applications through its dedicated online portal from National Sports Federations, the Indian Olympic Association, industry chambers (FICCI, ASSOCHAM, CII), State Governments, and Sports Control Boards.

2

Open Application

Corporate entities and NGOs submit applications with audited financials, programme documentation, and evidence of sports-promotion activity spanning the preceding three years.

3

Multi-Faceted Scrutiny

Nominations are assessed against category-specific criteria — numbers of athletes nurtured, CSR outlays, sportspersons employed, and community programmes delivered.

4

Selection Committee Review

A high-level committee evaluates the shortlist, weighing the scale, sincerity, and verifiable impact of each entity's contribution to sports promotion and development.

5

Government Approval

Recommendations are submitted to the Government of India for final approval and announcement of the year's awardees.

6

Presidential Investiture

The winners receive a trophy and citation from the President of India at an investiture ceremony at Rashtrapati Bhavan, traditionally held on 29 August — National Sports Day.

Evaluation Focus Areas

  • Scale of Impact: Number of athletes identified, nurtured, or employed
  • Investment & Resources: CSR outlays and non-commercial contributions to sport
  • Infrastructure Development: Creation and upgradation of training facilities
  • Community Reach: Programmes that make sport accessible and inclusive
  • Longevity & Sustainability: Consistent commitment over the preceding three years

🕰️ Interactive Timeline

Trace the evolution of India's National Sports Promotion Award from its inception to the present day.

2009

Inception. The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports institutes the Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar. The first awards are conferred on 29 August at Rashtrapati Bhavan — Tata Steel and the Railways Sports Promotion Board are among the inaugural recipients.

2015

Scheme Revision. The award guidelines are revised, consolidating the original categories into four: budding-talent nurturing, CSR encouragement, sportsperson employment & welfare, and sports for development.

2018

Prestige Grows. The Puruskar is firmly established as one of the six core National Sports Awards, with prestigious recipients such as Rashtriya Ispat Nigam and Reserve Bank of India honoured across categories.

2021

Diverse Honourees. Manav Rachna Educational Institutions wins for over 25 years of talent nurturing, and sports foundations such as GoSports continue to receive recognition.

2023–24

Modern Era. Jain Deemed to be University and Odisha Mining Corporation win for 2023, continuing the award's annual investiture tradition before the President of India.

Today

Ongoing Legacy. With 55 awards across 42 organisations, the Puruskar remains India's definitive institutional honour for those who build the nation through sport.
