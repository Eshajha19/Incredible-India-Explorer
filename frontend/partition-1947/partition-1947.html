Editorial Note & Historical Framing

This presentation offers a factual, neutral, and respectful overview of the 1947 Partition of British India, focusing on political decisions, verified demographic scale, and nation-building aftermath. Content is compiled from peer-reviewed historical literature and official census archives. In accordance with strict educational guidelines, graphic depictions of violence are omitted to maintain an objective, dignified focus on human history and resilience.

The Mountbatten Plan (June 3, 1947)

Detailed analysis of the 3rd June Plan that structured the British exit and partition mechanism.

June 3, 1947 Announcement

Viceroy Lord Mountbatten announced the decision to transfer power to two sovereign dominions—India and Pakistan—advancing the British withdrawal date from June 1948 to August 15, 1947.

⏱️ Accelerated Timetable

Compressed the administrative transition window into 72 days, necessitating urgent division of treasury, military assets, and civil services.

🗳️ Assembly Votes & Referendums

Legislative assemblies of Punjab and Bengal voted on partition; referendums were ordered for Sylhet (Assam) and the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP).

📐 Twin Boundary Commissions

Established dual commissions for Punjab and Bengal, chaired by British barrister Sir Cyril Radcliffe, tasked with boundary demarcation in 5 weeks.

👑 Princely States Mandate

Terminated British paramountcy, giving 565 Princely States the legal choice to accede to either India or Pakistan.

The Radcliffe Line & Boundary Demarcation

Understated visual map and economic analysis of the Punjab and Bengal boundary commissions.

SVG visual representation of the dual Boundary Commission lines in Punjab (553 km) and Bengal (4,096 km).

🌾 Punjab Boundary Commission

Chairman: Sir Cyril Radcliffe

Districts Divided: Gurdaspur, Amritsar, Lahore, Firozpur, Jalandhar

Economic Impact: Bisected rich agricultural canal colonies, separating wheat fields from central distribution centers and irrigation networks.

🌿 Bengal Boundary Commission

Chairman: Sir Cyril Radcliffe

Districts Divided: Nadia, Jessore, Dinajpur, Malda, Sylhet

Economic Impact: Separated 80% of raw jute growing regions in East Bengal from all 108 jute processing mills located around Kolkata.

Refugee Crisis & Rehabilitation Initiatives

Overview of relief camps, ration management, and pioneer model townships.

Tent City

Kurukshetra Relief Camp

Location: Haryana (East Punjab)

Capacity: 300,000 residents

Constructed with over 50,000 military tents, complete with central kitchens, sanitation units, and field hospitals.

Urban Transit

Purana Qila & Humayun's Tomb Camps

Location: New Delhi

Capacity: 100,000 residents

Historical fort complexes temporarily repurposed as safe transit sanctuaries for displaced families awaiting housing.

Eastern Hub

Ranaghat & Cooper's Camp

Location: Nadia, West Bengal

Capacity: 150,000 residents

Primary transit hub providing immediate relief, rations, and medical checks for displaced persons arriving in Bengal.

Model Township

Faridabad & Ulhasnagar Townships

Location: Haryana & Maharashtra

Capacity: 250,000 resettled

Planned industrial model cities constructed via self-help cooperative housing initiatives.
