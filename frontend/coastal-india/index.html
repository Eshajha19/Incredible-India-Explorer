Geographic Coverage

India boasts a vast coastline of approximately 7,516.6 km, flanked by the Arabian Sea to the west, the Bay of Bengal to the east, and the Indian Ocean to the south.

It touches nine states (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal) and four Union Territories.

Marine Ecosystems

The Indian coastline is teeming with biodiversity. It hosts rich Coral Reefs (Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep), dense Mangrove Forests (Sundarbans, Bhitarkanika), and expansive Estuaries.

These ecosystems act as crucial nurseries for marine life and provide a natural buffer against cyclones and tsunamis.

The Blue Economy

India's coastal and marine sectors are pillars of its economy. Over 14 million people are employed in fishing and aquaculture.

With 12 major ports (like Kandla, Mumbai, and Chennai) handling over 90% of the country's trade by volume, the coastline is the lifeline of Indian maritime trade.

Conservation Challenges

Despite their ecological and economic value, India's coasts face severe threats from climate change, rising sea levels, coastal erosion, and marine pollution. Efforts are underway to establish Marine Protected Areas (MPAs) and promote sustainable coastal tourism to protect these fragile environments for future generations.
