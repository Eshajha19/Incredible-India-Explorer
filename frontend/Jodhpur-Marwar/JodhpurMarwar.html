Kingdom of Jodhpur (Marwar)

The Rathore Rajputs carved an empire from the sun-baked Thar Desert. For seven centuries their fortress city stood unconquered — a beacon of courage, artistry, and the indomitable desert spirit.

Historical Overview

The Rathore Rajputs of Marwar trace their descent from the Gahadavala kings of Kanauj. After Prithviraj Chauhan's defeat at the Second Battle of Tarain (1193 CE), Siyaji Rathore migrated westward and settled in the Marwar region, beginning the clan's association with the Thar Desert. By 1226 CE, Rao Chunda had captured Mandore from the Parihar Rajputs, establishing it as the first permanent Rathore capital.

In 1459 CE, Rao Jodha Rathore selected an imposing 150-metre sandstone rock — the Bhakurcheeria — for a new impregnable capital, laying the foundation of Mehrangarh Fort on 12 May 1459. The city of Jodhpur grew below the fort's walls and rapidly became the most significant commercial and political centre of western Rajputana. Rao Maldeo (r. 1532–1562) expanded Marwar to its greatest territorial extent, controlling over 300 parganas and earning recognition from Sher Shah Suri as the mightiest Hindu king of the age.

Architectural Heritage

Marwar's monuments — from Mehrangarh's sheer cliff-face battlements to Umaid Bhawan's Art Deco grandeur — represent 500 years of Rathore ambition in sandstone and marble.

Cultural Legacy

Marwar's arts, music, dance, craft, and cuisine reflect centuries of Rathore patronage — from Manganiyar musicians performing inside Mehrangarh to blue pottery glazed in the Blue City's workshops.
