Gandhian Movement · 1917

Champaran Satyagraha

Gandhi's first satyagraha on Indian soil — where he stood with indigo cultivators against the exploitative tinkathia system, and discovered his life's mission.

🌱 The Neel System

The Tinkathia Indigo System

Tinkathia Explained

The tinkathia system required cultivators (ryots) to plant indigo on 3 out of every 20 parts of their land (kathi) — roughly 15% — for the European planter. This obligation was enforced through a contract called satta.

Indigo was a cash crop used to dye British textiles. The ryots bore the cost of cultivation but received only a fraction of the profits. The planter set the price, often below cost, making indigo cultivation a losing proposition for farmers.

Conditions of Cultivators

Champaran's indigo ryots lived in extreme poverty. They were forced to pay illegal cesses (abwabs), perform unpaid labour (begari), and accept below-market prices for their indigo.

Attempts to resist were crushed by the planter's private armed retainers (lathiyals). The colonial police and courts sided with planters. Ryots were trapped in a feudal system backed by imperial power.

⚖️ Why Satyagraha

Causes of the Movement

Exploitative Tinkathia

The forced indigo cultivation system trapped ryots in loss-making contracts. They could not switch to food crops even when indigo prices crashed.

Synthetic Indigo Threat

German synthetic indigo (post-1897) crashed world prices. Planters still forced cultivation, but ryots now bore losses. Planters demanded compensation from ryots to release them from contracts.

Illegal Cesses (Abwabs)

Planters imposed over 40 illegal cesses: tawan (compensation), sharabeshi (rent enhancement), and various arbitrary fees. These became a major grievance.

Forced Labour (Begari)

Ryots and their families had to perform unpaid labour for planters: carrying luggage, providing carts, working in the factory without wages. This feudal oppression bred resentment.

Colonial Collusion

British officials, police, and courts sided with planters. European planters held magisterial powers. Ryots' petitions were routinely dismissed; they had no access to justice.

Failure of Existing Channels

Earlier attempts — petitions to local officials, press campaigns by Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi and Pir Muhammad Munis — had failed. The system was rigged; only mass resistance could work.

👥 The Key People

Key People of Champaran

RKS

Raj Kumar Shukla

Ryot · Catalyst

A peasant from Narkatiaganj who met Gandhi at the Lucknow Congress (1916) and insisted he visit Champaran. Shukla's persistence brought Gandhi to Bihar, making him the architect of India's satyagraha tradition.

MK

Mahatma Gandhi

Leader · Satyagrahi

Gandhi arrived in Champaran on 10 April 1917, having just ended the South African campaigns. His decision to disobey the British order to leave, his patient inquiry, and his moral authority transformed Indian politics.

RPR

Rajendra Prasad

Lawyer · Future President

A young Patna lawyer who accompanied Gandhi. Prasad organised the inquiry, recorded testimonies, and later became India's first President. Champaran launched his national career.

JB

J.B. Kripalani

Professor · Organiser

A Sindhi professor teaching at a Bihar college who joined Gandhi on day one. Kripalani helped record testimonies and later became Congress President (1946) during Independence.

MB

Mazharul Haque

Lawyer · Muslim leader

A senior Patna lawyer who gave Gandhi accommodation and legal advice. Haque represented Hindu-Muslim unity and later served as a nationalist leader in Bihar.

CD

Charles Freer Andrews

British ally

Gandhi's English friend and Christian missionary who supported the movement. Andrews symbolised Gandhi's belief that justice transcended race. He mediated between Gandhi and authorities.

KAS

Kasturba Gandhi

Companion · Women's work

Kasturba accompanied Gandhi and led women's sanitation and education work. She taught hygiene to village women, breaking caste barriers. Her presence made the movement family-centred.

BVB

Braj Kishore Prasad

Senior Lawyer

A senior Patna lawyer who initially doubted Gandhi's approach but became a key supporter. He used his legal expertise to challenge planter claims in the Champaran Agrarian Committee.

🚂 The Journey

Gandhi's Movement Route

1

Calcutta → Patna (April 1917)

Gandhi arrives in Calcutta after being invited by Raj Kumar Shukla. He travels to Patna, where Rajendra Prasad welcomes him. The plan: visit Champaran to investigate planter atrocities.

2

Patna → Muzaffarpur (10 April)

Arrives in Muzaffarpur, meets Professor Kripalani and lawyer Mazharul Haque. News of his arrival spreads. Peasants begin gathering from distant villages.

3

Muzaffarpur → Motihari (15 April)

Gandhi reaches Motihari, the district headquarters. He visits Jasaulipatti village where a ryot has been beaten. He receives the British order to leave Champaran.

4

"I will not leave" (18 April)

At Motihari court, Gandhi refuses to leave Champaran and reads his historic statement: "I have entered the country to render humanitarian and national service." The case is dropped; victory without confrontation.

5

Village Visits (April–June)

Gandhi visits villages across Champaran: Bettiah, Narkatiaganj, Turkaulia, Ratanpur. He records testimonies from 8,000+ ryots, documenting planter atrocities in meticulous detail.

6

Champaran Agrarian Committee (July–Oct)

Gandhi serves on the government-appointed inquiry committee. He presents evidence gathered from ryots. The committee's report, largely based on his work, recommends abolition of tinkathia.

📅 Chronology

Timeline of the Satyagraha

Dec 1916

Lucknow Congress

Raj Kumar Shukla, a Champaran peasant, insists Gandhi visit his district to see the planter atrocities. Gandhi agrees to visit after the Congress session.

10 Apr 1917

Gandhi arrives in Patna

Gandhi arrives in Patna and meets Rajendra Prasad. He decides to visit Champaran to investigate the conditions of indigo cultivators.

15 Apr 1917

Arrival in Motihari

Gandhi reaches Motihari. He visits Jasaulipatti where a ryot has been assaulted. He receives a British notice ordering him to leave Champaran immediately.

18 Apr 1917

Court appearance

Gandhi appears in Motihari court and refuses to obey the order. He reads his famous statement. The magistrate, unsure how to proceed, postpones the case. Government eventually drops the charges.

Apr–Jun 1917

Mass testimony campaign

Gandhi and his team (Rajendra Prasad, Kripalani, others) record 8,000+ testimonies from Champaran ryots. This systematic documentation becomes the evidence base for the inquiry committee.

Jun 1917

Champaran Agrarian Committee

The Bihar government appoints a committee of inquiry with Gandhi as a member. He presents the ryots' testimonies and negotiates with planters and officials.

Oct 1917

Committee report

The committee's report, largely based on Gandhi's evidence, recommends abolition of tinkathia, refund of 25% of tawan (illegal cesses), and protections for ryots.

1918

Champaran Agrarian Act

The Champaran Agrarian Act is passed, abolishing the tinkathia system and providing protections for cultivators. This is Gandhi's first legislative victory in India.

Post-1917

Schools & social work

Gandhi establishes six primary schools in Champaran, introduces basic education, and begins sanitation work. Kasturba leads women's welfare activities. The movement becomes a model for constructive work.

📜 Victory

Outcomes & Government Negotiations

Champaran Agrarian Act (1918)

The Act abolished the tinkathia system, freeing ryots from forced indigo cultivation. Planters agreed to refund 25% of illegal tawan cesses collected from ryots — a significant financial victory.

The Act also provided legal protections for cultivators against planter exploitation. It became a model for subsequent agrarian reforms in Bihar.

Birth of Satyagraha in India

Champaran established the template for all future Gandhian movements: moral authority over force, systematic investigation, willingness to suffer, and constructive work alongside protest.

The term satyagraha (truth-force) was used for the first time on Indian soil here. It would be applied at Kheda (1918), Ahmedabad (1918), and Non-Cooperation (1920).

🌟 Significance

Long-Term Significance

Gandhi's National Arrival

Champaran transformed Gandhi from a returning South African campaigner into India's leading mass leader. It proved his methods worked in Indian conditions.

Model for Future Movements

The Champaran template — patient inquiry, moral authority, civil disobedience, constructive work — was applied at Kheda (1918), Ahmedabad (1918), and the national Non-Cooperation Movement (1920).

Birth of Indian Satyagraha

The word satyagraha became synonymous with Indian freedom politics. Champaran showed that non-violent resistance could defeat imperial power.

Leadership Pipeline

Champaran launched the careers of Rajendra Prasad (future President), J.B. Kripalani (Congress President), and Mazharul Haque. It built the team that would lead India to freedom.

Constructive Work Model

Gandhi's schools, sanitation work, and women's empowerment programmes in Champaran became the template for the Constructive Programme — the "inner" side of the freedom movement.

Agrarian Reform Precedent

The Champaran Agrarian Act set the pattern for post-independence land reforms in Bihar and beyond. It established that peasant grievances could force colonial reform.
