🏔️ Garhwal Himalayas, Uttarakhand

Nanda Devi Explorer

Rising to 7,816 meters in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, Nanda Devi is India's second-highest mountain and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Surrounded by a ring of towering peaks, its inner sanctuary was once considered unclimbable — a fortress of ice and rock that guarded one of the most pristine ecosystems on Earth.

Geography, Glaciers & History

The Goddess of the Mountains

Nanda Devi, standing at 7,816 meters, is the second-highest mountain in India and the highest peak located entirely within the country. Situated in the Chamoli and Pithoragarh districts of Uttarakhand, the mountain is part of the greater Himalayan range and the Nanda Devi massif.

The name "Nanda Devi" translates to "Bliss-Giving Goddess" in Sanskrit. The mountain is deeply revered in Hindu mythology as the embodiment of Goddess Parvati, consort of Lord Shiva. It holds immense spiritual significance for the people of Kumaon and Garhwal, who consider it sacred.

In 1936, mountaineers Eric Shipton and H.W. Tilman made the first successful ascent on June 29, becoming the first to climb a peak above 7,620 meters. The mountain was also the site of a tragic 1939 German expedition led by Karl Wien, which was lost in a glacial avalanche — their bodies remained undiscovered until 1959.

Biodiversity

Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve

A UNESCO World Heritage Site harboring some of the most pristine alpine ecosystems in the Himalayas.

🐆

Snow Leopard

The elusive apex predator of the high Himalayas. The Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve provides critical habitat for this endangered feline, with an estimated population of 30-40 individuals in the wider region.

Endangered
🐐

Himalayan Tahr

A large ungulate adapted to rugged alpine terrain. Found across the rocky slopes and meadows of the reserve, the Himalayan Tahr is an iconic species of the Nanda Devi ecosystem.

Near Threatened
🌸

Alpine Flora

The reserve hosts over 800 plant species including rare Himalayan blue poppies, brahma kamal (the state flower of Uttarakhand), and ancient juniper forests that have survived for millennia.

Rich Diversity
🦅

Golden Eagle

Soaring above the high-altitude meadows and ridges, the Golden Eagle is among the apex avian predators of the reserve, hunting marmots and small mammals across the alpine zone.

Apex Predator
🐻

Himalayan Black Bear

Inhabiting the lower forested zones of the biosphere, this omnivorous bear feeds on berries, acorns, and insects. It is a key species in seed dispersal across the reserve.

Vulnerable
🌿

Temperate Forests

Dense forests of oak, rhododendron, and deodar cedar blanket the lower elevations, giving way to birch and juniper at higher altitudes before transitioning to alpine meadows.

Adventure

Trekking Guide

Explore the legendary trails and routes that lead to the base of the Goddess of Mountains.

🗺️

Nanda Devi Inner Sanctuary Trek

The most iconic trek leading through the Rishi Gorge into the legendary inner sanctuary — a natural amphitheater surrounded by a 7,000 m ring of peaks. Requires a special permit from the Forest Department.

📏 12-15 Days ⬆️ 4,300 m ⚡ Challenging
🌲

Nanda Devi Base Camp Trek

A popular trek starting from Lata village through alpine meadows, dense forests, and high-altitude passes with stunning views of the Nanda Devi massif and surrounding peaks.

📏 8-10 Days ⬆️ 4,150 m ⚡ Moderate
🦅

Roopkund Trek

The famous Skeleton Lake trek passes through the Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve, offering views of the mountain and the mysterious lake containing ancient human skeletal remains.

📏 7-9 Days ⬆️ 5,029 m ⚡ Challenging
🌸

Best Season & Permits

Best seasons: May-June (pre-monsoon wildflowers) and September-October (clear post-monsoon skies). Inner sanctuary requires Forest Department permit and registered guide.
