HISTORICAL OVERVIEW

A Pandya-era gateway to the sea

Alagankulam is an archaeological site on the banks of the Vaigai in Ramanathapuram district, around three kilometres from the coast. Tamil Nadu's Department of Archaeology identifies it as a major excavation site with evidence of long-distance exchange.

Research on excavated material places the site's early historic occupation broadly within the first-millennium BCE and early centuries CE, with later strata preserving evidence of contact with the Roman and wider Mediterranean world. It is best understood as part of a network linking inland production, river transport and Indian Ocean commerce.

ARCHAEOLOGICAL FINDINGS

Evidence preserved in the soil

🏺

Amphora fragments

Imported transport-vessel fragments point to Mediterranean connections. Amphorae were commonly used for commodities such as wine and oil.

◌

Rouletted ware

Rouletted pottery appears among the excavated ceramic assemblages, alongside Black-and-red ware, grey ware and local pottery.

𐀀

Tamil-Brahmi

Inscribed potsherds with Tamil-Brahmi characters provide evidence for literacy and cultural activity in the early historic settlement.

◈

Beads & shells

Excavations have produced glass and semi-precious-stone beads, shell objects and other craft-related finds.

🪙

Roman coins

Tamil Nadu's Archaeology Department records Roman coins from the site, including a coin associated with Emperor Valentinian II.

🧱

Built remains

Bricks, perforated tiles, iron objects and other structural materials add evidence of settlement and craft activity.

ROMAN TRADE EVIDENCE

Where archaeology meets the Indian Ocean

The strongest case for overseas contact comes from a combination of imported Mediterranean ceramics and coins rather than any single artefact. Scientific studies identify imported, locally produced and imitated forms, helping reconstruct trade circulation.

  • Mediterranean amphora fragments
  • Rouletted and other fine wares
  • Roman coins from excavated contexts
  • Local pottery alongside imports and possible imitations

TIMELINE

Layers of an ancient port

4th–3rd c. BCE

Early occupation

Scientific research reports AMS dates indicating an Early Historic phase beginning in the 4th–3rd centuries BCE.

5th–1st c. BCE

Expanding ceramic traditions

Excavated Period I includes Black-and-red ware, NBP ware, Rouletted ware, grey ware, beads and inscribed sherds.

1st c. BCE–1st c. CE

Indian Ocean exchange

Imported amphorae and Mediterranean ceramic evidence appear alongside local production and possible imitations.

4th–5th c. CE

Later Roman/Byzantine contacts

Later finds include Roman coin evidence associated with the late Roman/Byzantine period.

Today

Archaeological heritage

Government-led excavation and research continue to document Alagankulam's place in Tamil maritime history.
