Swargarohini Explorer

Swargarohini is a dramatic, multi-peaked mountain massif rising to 6,252 meters in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand. Renowned for its steep rock faces and jagged ridges, it holds a legendary place in mythology as the literal 'Pathway to Heaven' traversed by the Pandavas.

Geography, Glaciers & Mythology

The Mythological Stairway

Swargarohini consists of four distinct peaks, with Swargarohini I being the highest and most challenging. It towers directly above the Tons River valley and the alpine meadows of Har Ki Dun. The snows of Swargarohini feed the Supin river, a major tributary of the Tons, which eventually joins the Yamuna.

In Hindu epic lore, Swargarohini represents the path of ascent to heaven (Swarga). According to the Mahabharata, the Pandava brothers and their wife Draupadi attempted to scale this peak to reach heaven in human form. Only Yudhishthira, accompanied by a loyal dog, completed the journey, while the others fell along the slopes.

First climbed in 1974 by an Indian team, the peak is notoriously technical due to the steep hanging glaciers and crumbling rock ridges. It remains a coveted prize for advanced alpine mountaineers looking for sheer vertical challenges.

Technical Specifications

  • Elevation: 6,252 m (20,512 ft)
  • Prominence: 1,555 m
  • Coordinates: 31°06'00"N 78°30'58"E
  • Climbing Grade: Extreme (Alpine Grade V/VI)
  • First Ascent: October 25, 1974
  • Region: Uttarkashi, Uttarakhand

Routes, Trails & Har Ki Dun Valley

Details on viewing the massif and scaling the high-altitude trails surrounding Swargarohini.

🥾

The Har Ki Dun Approach

Most trekkers view the majesty of Swargarohini from the Har Ki Dun valley (3,566 m). Starting from Sankri, the route winds through Taluka, Osla, and Seema along the Supin River, presenting a dramatic backdrop of Swargarohini's snow-covered south face.

⛺

Ruinsara Tal Trek

To get closer to the base camp, trekkers follow the branch trail toward Ruinsara Tal, a pristine high-altitude glacial lake at 3,500 m. This trail goes directly along the base of the Swargarohini range and serves as the launchpad for climbing expeditions.

🗓️

Best Seasons

The pre-monsoon season of May–June and post-monsoon of September–November are ideal. The valley turns green in spring with alpine wild flowers, while autumn offers crystal-clear blue skies and unobstructed views of the snow-clad peaks.

📋

Difficulty & Preparedness

While the valley trek to Har Ki Dun is of moderate difficulty, attempting the peak itself is extremely technical, requiring ice axes, crampons, rope teams, and seasoned alpine climbing skills due to steep crevasses and ice walls.
