🏛️ Haryana, India · Indus Valley Civilization

Rakhigarhi Explorer

Discover Rakhigarhi, one of the largest and most significant Harappan Civilization metropolises in the world. Spanning multiple excavated mounds across the Ghaggar basin, explore its advanced urban planning, meticulous drainage systems, ancient DNA studies, and vast trade networks.

Heart of the Ghaggar Basin

A Megalopolis of the Indus Valley Civilization

Located in the Hisar district of Haryana, Rakhigarhi sits within the ancient Sarasvati-Ghaggar river basin. Spanning an immense area across multiple interconnected mounds, recent excavations establish Rakhigarhi as larger than Harappa and Mohenjo-daro, making it arguably the largest known Harappan settlement in the world.

The site presents a complete evolutionary timeline from early Mature Harappan settlements to urbanized metropolitan planning featuring structured streets, sophisticated water management, and specialized craft centers.

🏛️ Site at a Glance

  • Location: Hisar district, Haryana, India.
  • Basin: Ghaggar-Hakra river system (ancient Sarasvati valley).
  • Scale: Sprawls across multiple massive mounds (RGR-1 to RGR-7).
  • Significance: Recognized as one of the world's largest Harappan metropolises.
Excavated Mounds

The Mound Explorer & Settlement Architecture

Rakhigarhi is divided into multiple distinct mounds (designated RGR-1 through RGR-7), each serving specialized administrative, residential, and industrial roles in Harappan urban life.

🏛️

Mound RGR-1 (Acropolis)

The largest mound, believed to serve as the administrative and elite residential core of the ancient city with massive mud-brick structures.

🏘️

Mound RGR-2 (Residential Sector)

Extensive residential quarters displaying grid-pattern planning, multi-room houses, courtyards, and domestic ovens.

⚙️

Mound RGR-3 & RGR-4 (Craft Workshops)

Industrial zones dedicated to bead-making, pottery kilns, copper working, and shell ornament production.

⚱️

Mound RGR-5 & RGR-6 (Burial & Cemeteries)

Designated burial grounds revealing distinctive Harappan funerary customs, pottery grave goods, and skeletal positioning.

📐 Engineering Marvels

  • Streets & Drainage: Meticulously laid out intersecting roads with covered terracotta brick drains.
  • Houses: Standardized burnt and mud bricks with built-in bathing platforms and soakage jars.
  • Agriculture: Cultivation of wheat, barley, pulses, and evidence of pastoral cattle rearing.
  • Crafts: Exquisite terracotta figurines, steatite seals with Harappan script, and intricate agate/jasper bead work.
Advanced Urbanity

Streets, Drainage, Craft & Economy

The urban layout of Rakhigarhi showcases legendary Harappan engineering. Roads intersected at right angles, dividing the city into well-organized rectangular blocks. Sophisticated covered drainage systems channeled wastewater away from residential zones into soak pits, illustrating an exceptional standard of civic sanitation.

Economically, the city thrived on intensive agriculture along the Ghaggar basin, supplemented by robust domestic and long-distance trade networks exchanging copper, semi-precious stones, and terracotta artifacts across the greater Indus civilization.

Chronology

Settlement Timeline & Phases

Tracing Rakhigarhi from early agricultural settlements to peak metropolitan glory.

c. 3300 – 2600 BCE

Early Harappan (Sothi-Siswal Phase)

Initial agrarian settlements establishing pastoral villages and early ceramic traditions in the Ghaggar valley.

c. 2600 – 1900 BCE

Mature Harappan Metropolis

Peak urbanization: massive brick structures, advanced drainage, standardized weights, seals, and extensive craft specialization.

c. 1900 – 1300 BCE

Late Harappan / Transitional Phase

Gradual de-urbanization, shifting river patterns, and transformation into smaller rural settlements.

Scientific Breakthroughs

Archaeological Discoveries & Ancient DNA Studies

Groundbreaking genetic research and artifact unearthing that redefine our understanding of South Asian ancestry and Harappan heritage.

🧬

Ancient DNA (aDNA) Research

Landmark genetic studies on skeletal remains from Rakhigarhi burials providing deep insights into the ancestral lineage of the Indus population.

🏷️

Harappan Seals & Inscriptions

Steatite seals featuring classic Harappan animal motifs and undeciphered script, pointing to administrative and trade record-keeping.

🏺

Pottery & Terracotta Art

Exquisitely crafted painted pottery, terracotta animal toys, bangles, and mother goddess figurines reflecting rich artistic expression.

⚖️

Weights & Measures

Standardized chert stone cubical weights confirming a uniform system of commerce and taxation across the Indus realm.
