Purana Qila Explorer

Delhi's Ancient Fortress - Where Emperors Built and Conquered

Standing majestically on the ancient site of Indraprastha, Purana Qila (Old Fort) represents the architectural brilliance of both the Mughal Empire and the Sur Empire. Built by Emperor Humayun and later enhanced by Sher Shah Suri, this fortress witnessed the rise and fall of empires, making it one of Delhi's most significant historical landmarks.

Historical Background

Fortress of Empires

The fascinating history of Delhi's oldest surviving fort.

Purana Qila (Old Fort) is one of the oldest forts in Delhi, with a history spanning over 500 years. The fort is located on the ancient site of Indraprastha, the legendary capital of the Pandavas from the Mahabharata epic, making it a site of immense mythological and historical significance.

The construction of the current fort was begun by Emperor Humayun in 1533 as part of his new capital city, Dinpanah. However, Humayun's reign was interrupted when Sher Shah Suri defeated him in 1540 and expelled him from India. Sher Shah Suri then renamed the fort Shergarh and completed its construction, adding several architectural elements.

After Sher Shah Suri's death in 1545, Humayun recaptured the fort in 1555 and made modifications to the structure. The fort has since witnessed numerous historical events and has been associated with several significant figures in Indian history, including the later Mughal emperors and the British colonial period.

Architectural Heritage

Defensive Grandeur

A stunning example of medieval Indian military architecture.

🧱

Fortification Walls

The fort is surrounded by massive walls that stretch over 2 kilometers, rising to a height of 18 meters. The walls are built from red sandstone and feature bastions at regular intervals for defensive purposes.

🚪

Three Main Gates

The fort has three grand gates: the Bara Darwaza (Big Gate) facing west, the Humayun Darwaza (Humayun's Gate) facing south, and the Talaqi Darwaza (Forbidden Gate) facing north.

🕌

Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque

The magnificent mosque built by Sher Shah Suri features a beautiful prayer hall with five arches and intricate geometric patterns, representing the finest example of Sur dynasty architecture.

🏛️

Octagonal Tower

The Sher Mandal is an octagonal tower within the fort, believed to have been used as a library and observatory. It was from this tower that Humayun accidentally fell to his death in 1556.

Architectural Elements

  • Construction Material: Red sandstone and marble
  • Wall Height: 18 meters with projecting bastions
  • Gate Design: Double-storeyed with ornate decorations
  • Interior Layout: Palaces, mosques, and gardens
  • Decorative Elements: Geometric patterns, calligraphy, and ornamental brackets
  • Water Management: Baoli (stepwell) and water channels
Major Gates

Grand Entrances

The magnificent gateways that define the fort's imposing presence.

Bara Darwaza (Big Gate)

The western gate is the main entrance to the fort and the largest of the three gates. It features a double-storeyed structure with ornate decorations and defensive features. The gate is built from red sandstone and showcases the architectural brilliance of the period.

Humayun Darwaza (Humayun's Gate)

The southern gate is named after Emperor Humayun and is believed to have been built during his initial construction phase. This gate is distinguished by its elegant proportions and decorative elements that reflect early Mughal architectural style.

Talaqi Darwaza (Forbidden Gate)

The northern gate is known as the "Forbidden Gate" and is steeped in legend. According to local folklore, the gate was sealed after a prophecy stated that anyone leaving through this gate would never return. The gate features intricate carvings and defensive elements.

Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque

The Grand Mosque

Sher Shah Suri's architectural masterpiece within the fort.

The Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque (Mosque of the Old Fort) is one of the finest examples of Sur dynasty architecture and was built by Sher Shah Suri in 1541. The mosque is located inside the fort complex and represents the synthesis of Islamic and Indian architectural traditions.

The mosque features a large prayer hall with five elegant arches, each adorned with intricate geometric patterns and calligraphy. The central mihrab (prayer niche) is particularly ornate, showcasing the skilled craftsmanship of the period. The mosque's interior is decorated with colored tiles and marble inlay work.

The mosque's architecture reflects Sher Shah Suri's vision of creating a structure that was both functional for prayer and aesthetically magnificent. The use of red sandstone combined with white marble creates a striking visual contrast that has stood the test of time.

Architectural Features

  • Prayer Hall: Five-arched facade with decorative elements
  • Central Mihrab: Ornately decorated prayer niche
  • Materials: Red sandstone with marble inlay
  • Decorations: Geometric patterns and Quranic calligraphy
  • Courtyard: Large open space for congregational prayer
  • Minarets: Corner towers for traditional Islamic architecture

Emperor Humayun

The Mughal Visionary

The emperor who laid the foundation of Purana Qila.

Humayun's Reign

Nasir-ud-Din Muhammad Humayun (1508-1556) was the second Mughal Emperor who ruled over territories in what is now Afghanistan, Pakistan, and parts of northern India. He was the son of Babur, the founder of the Mughal Empire.

Humayun began the construction of Purana Qila in 1533 as part of his new capital city, Dinpanah (Refuge of the Faithful). He envisioned the fort as a symbol of Mughal power and architectural sophistication in India.

Exile and Return

Humayun's reign was interrupted when Sher Shah Suri defeated him at the Battle of Chausa in 1539 and the Battle of Kannauj in 1540, forcing him into exile for 15 years. During this period, Sher Shah Suri took over the fort and renamed it Shergarh.

Humayun returned to India in 1555 and recaptured Delhi, including Purana Qila. Tragically, his reign was cut short when he accidentally fell from the steps of the Sher Mandal library within the fort in 1556.

Sher Shah Suri

The Sur Emperor

The visionary ruler who completed and enhanced the fort.

Sher Shah Suri (1486-1545), born Farid Khan, was the founder of the Sur Empire in northern India. He defeated Humayun and established his rule over Delhi, completing the construction of Purana Qila and renaming it Shergarh (Fort of Sher).

Sher Shah Suri was known for his administrative reforms and architectural achievements. During his brief reign of five years, he completed the fort's construction, built the magnificent Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque, and added the Sher Mandal tower. His architectural vision combined Islamic design principles with Indian craftsmanship.

Sher Shah Suri's contributions to Purana Qila represent the pinnacle of Sur dynasty architecture. The fort stands as a testament to his military genius and administrative capabilities, showcasing how he transformed Humayun's incomplete structure into a formidable fortress.

Architectural Legacy

Sher Shah Suri's architectural contributions to Purana Qila include:

  • Completion of the fortification walls
  • Construction of Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque
  • Building of Sher Mandal tower
  • Integration of defensive features
  • Addition of ornamental decorations

Historical Significance

Cultural and Heritage Importance

The lasting legacy of this architectural masterpiece.

Archaeological Importance

Purana Qila holds immense archaeological significance as the site has revealed evidence of continuous habitation from 1000 BC. Excavations have uncovered Painted Grey Ware pottery and other artifacts that confirm the site's identification with ancient Indraprastha.

The fort represents a unique synthesis of Mughal and Sur architectural styles, showcasing how power transitions influenced architectural development in medieval India. It serves as an important case study for understanding the evolution of Indo-Islamic architecture.

Heritage Status

Today, Purana Qila is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) and serves as:

  • Historic Monument: Protected site of national importance
  • Tourist Destination: Major attraction for domestic and international visitors
  • Archaeological Site: Ongoing excavations revealing ancient history
  • Cultural Venue: Hosts sound and light shows depicting Delhi's history
  • Heritage Symbol: Represents Delhi's rich historical legacy

Archaeological Findings

Excavations at Purana Qila have revealed layers of history spanning several millennia. Archaeologists have discovered artifacts from the Mahabharata period, including Painted Grey Ware pottery (c. 1000 BC), as well as structures from the Mauryan, Shunga, Kushana, Gupta, Rajput, and medieval periods. These findings confirm the site's continuous habitation and its importance as a strategic location throughout Indian history.
