Alluri Sitarama Raju Explorer

Manyam Veerudu — the Hero of the Jungle who took on an empire with a bow and arrow.

In the misty hill tracts of the Godavari and Visakhapatnam Agencies, a saffron-robed sanyasi gathered the adivasis of the Eastern Ghats and rose against the British Empire. Alluri Sitarama Raju's Rampa Rebellion — the Manyam Rebellion of 1922–24 — raided police stations, seized rifles, and for two years outmanoeuvred a colonial army with whistles, drums, and guerrilla cunning. His name became a rallying cry for the tribal right to the forest and the land.

Biography

The Sanyasi Who Became Manyam Veerudu

From a village near the coast of Andhra to the forests of the Eastern Ghats — a life of barely twenty-seven years that armed a people against an empire.

Alluri Sitarama Raju was born on 4 July 1897 at Pandrangi, near Visakhapatnam in the Madras Presidency, into a middle-class Telugu family. Born Alluri Rama Raju, he is said to have prefixed the name "Sita" to his own in memory of a girl he had loved in his youth and lost. Raised partly by his uncle after his father's early death, he grew up in the hill country around Visakhapatnam, learning horse-riding and the ways of the forest that would later become his battlefield.

At around eighteen, Alluri renounced worldly life and took up sannyasa, wandering the Godavari and Visakhapatnam Agency tracts — a land of adivasis, bamboo groves and podu fields. There he became a charismatic, almost messianic figure to the tribal people, a leader the British called a "religious fanatic" and the tribes came to see as a deliverer. Inspired by the Non-Cooperation Movement sweeping the country, Alluri urged the tribals to boycott colonial courts and seek justice through their own panchayats — and when the forest laws strangling their livelihoods left no door open, he turned to armed revolt.

Milestones

Chronology of the Manyam Rebellion

From a boy on the coastal plains to the hunted, unbeaten heart of the Eastern Ghats.

4 Jul 1897 🌱

Born at Pandrangi

Alluri Sitarama Raju is born at Pandrangi, near Visakhapatnam, into a middle-class Telugu family. Raised in the hill country of the Agency, he grows up with the forests that will one day shelter his war.

c. 1915 🪔

The Sanyasi Takes the Forest

At about eighteen, Alluri renounces the world and becomes a sanyasi. He roams the Godavari and Visakhapatnam Agency tracts, living among the tribal people and gaining their trust, love — and belief in his invincibility.

1921 📢

The Non-Cooperation Spark

Carried by the tide of Gandhi's Non-Cooperation Movement, Alluri urges the tribals to boycott colonial courts and settle disputes in their own panchayats — the first open defiance of the Raj's authority in the hills.

Feb 1922 👁️

On the Raj's Radar

Alluri comes to the attention of the colonial police, who begin to suspect the young sanyasi of plotting armed resistance in the Agency hills. The empire has no idea what is coming.

22 Aug 1922 ⚡

Chintapalli: The Rebellion Begins

Over 300 tribals under Alluri storm the Chintapalli police station, seizing weapons and ammunition. Over the following days, the stations at Krishnadevipeta and Rajavommangi fall too — the Rampa Rebellion has begun.

24 Sep 1922 ⚔️

Blooding the Reserve Police

British officers Scott and Heiter fall in battle with the rebels near the hill passes, and reinforcements rushed from Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry and Koraput are repeatedly mauled in the forests.

Oct 1922 💥

An Empire Takes Him Seriously

"The situation was pretty serious," confesses Bracken, the District Collector of East Godavari. The British impose martial law, raise the specially trained Malabar Special Police, and place a bounty of Rs 10,000 on Alluri's head.

1923 🥁

The Jungle at War

For a year and more, Alluri's guerrillas — armed with bows, arrows, spears and captured rifles, coordinated by whistles and drums across the peaks — keep the colonial police and army at bay across the Agency tracts.

Apr–May 1924 🪢

Trapped in the Chintapalli Forests

Deputed by the Government of India to crush the rising, Thomas George Rutherford hunts Alluri through the hills. In early May 1924, the police close in on him in the forests of Chintapalli.

7 May 1924 🕊️

Martyrdom at Koyyuru

Captured and tied to a tree in the village of Koyyuru, the twenty-six-year-old Manyam Veerudu is executed by a firing squad. His last remains are enshrined in a mausoleum at Krishnadevipeta — and the rebellion he lit never truly went out.

The Rampa Rebellion

A tribal war over forest, land and dignity — led by a sanyasi the empire could not cage.

Why They Rose

The Forest Act That Starved a People

The trigger was the Madras Forest Act of 1882. It criminalised podu, the shifting slash-and-burn cultivation by which the Koya and other tribal communities of the Eastern Ghats had fed themselves for generations, and shut off the forests that were their granary, pharmacy and sacred ground. The state commercialised the timber; the tribal people were left with forced labour on roads and little else.

Under this twin yoke of famine and coercion — with the hereditary muttadars stripped of their authority too — the Agency tracts simmered. Alluri Sitarama Raju gave that discontent a voice, a leader and a plan: a people's war against the Raj itself.

⚔️ Rebellion at a Glance

  • Rampa Rebellion — the Manyam Rebellion of 1922–24 in the Godavari & Visakhapatnam Agencies.
  • Trigger — the 1882 Madras Forest Act and the ban on podu shifting cultivation.
  • Weapons — bows, arrows, spears and rifles seized from raided police stations.
  • Signals — a rebel courier system run on whistles and drums.
  • Bounty — Rs 10,000 for Alluri's head, Rs 1,000 for each lieutenant.
  • End — Alluri's capture and execution in May 1924; Gam Malludora and Gam Gantamdora carried the banner on.
🏹

Guerrilla War

Refusing pitched battle on British terms, Alluri's men struck police stations and patrols from the hills, vanishing back into the bamboo. It took the empire's specially raised forces to even begin to answer them.

🌳

The Forest as Fortress

The Eastern Ghats themselves were the rebels' ally — trackless, dense and known to the adivasis. Whistles and drums passed messages across the peaks faster than any colonial patrol could move.

⚖️

Justice by the Panchayat

Before the guns were raised, Alluri's quiet revolution was legal: boycott the colonial courts, settle disputes in tribal panchayats, and reclaim justice for the forest people on their own terms.

🥁

One People, One Leader

To the adivasis Alluri was a messenger of the gods; to the empire a "religious fanatic". That devotion, not firepower, was what made the Rampa Rebellion the most serious armed rising of its time in the south.

Legacy

The Hero the Nation Never Forgot

Executed at twenty-six, Alluri Sitarama Raju is honoured today in stamps, statues, a district and a nation's memory.

📬

The 1986 Commemorative Stamp

India Post issued a stamp in Alluri's honour in 1986 in the series "India's Struggle for Freedom" — one of the first official recognitions of the Manyam Veerudu by the Indian state.

🗓️

A State Festival in Andhra Pradesh

His birthday, 4 July, is celebrated annually as a state festival in Andhra Pradesh, keeping his memory alive in the land whose hills he fought for.

🗺️

A District Named for Him

In 2022, Andhra Pradesh carved out the Alluri Sitharama Raju district from Visakhapatnam, with Paderu as its headquarters — a homeland of the agency hills, named for its hero.

🏛️

A Statue in Parliament

In 2017 the Government of India approved a statue of Alluri Sitarama Raju in the precincts of Parliament — a revolutionary of the forests, honoured in the halls of power.

✈️

An Airport and a Stadium

The Alluri Sitarama Raju International Airport at Bhogapuram and the Alluri Sitarama Raju Cricket Stadium in Eluru carry his name into the everyday life of Andhra Pradesh.

🎬

A Story in Every Art

Ballads, burra katha folk theatre, biographies and finally S. S. Rajamouli's blockbuster RRR (2022) carried the Manyam Veerudu's story to the world — from the hills of Andhra to the global screen.

🏹

Tributes to a Hero

Jawaharlal Nehru called him "one of those few heroes that could be counted on fingers". Subhas Chandra Bose said his courage and sacrifice for the people would ensure him a place in history. In 2022, a 30-foot bronze statue was unveiled at Bhimavaram to mark his 125th birth anniversary — a century after he raised the Rampa Rebellion.

Sources Behind the Story

The scholarly studies of the Gudem–Rampa risings, the colonial record, and the modern works that frame Alluri in the wider story of India's freedom struggle.

📖

Arnold, David (1982)

"Rebellious Hillmen: The Gudem–Rampa Risings, 1839–1924", in Ranajit Guha (ed.), Subaltern Studies I. Oxford University Press. The classic scholarly account of the risings Alluri's rebellion crowned.

📖

Murali, Atlury (1984)

"Alluri Sitarama Raju and the Manyam Rebellion of 1922–24", Social Scientist 12(4). A foundational study of Raju's leadership and the social base of the rebellion.

📖

Hemingway, F. R. (1915)

Godavari District Gazetteer, Volume I. A colonial gazetteer documenting the Agency tracts and the pressures that provoked the tribal risings.

📖

Mangamma, J. (1979)

"Gudem Rebellion of 1922", Proceedings of the Indian History Congress 40. A history-congress study of the rebellion that Alluri led into its final phase.

📖

PIB, Government of India (2022)

Press release on the 125th birth anniversary of Alluri Sitarama Raju and the centenary of the Rampa Rebellion, recording the modern state's honouring of the Manyam Veerudu.
