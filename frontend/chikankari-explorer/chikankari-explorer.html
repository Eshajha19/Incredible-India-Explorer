Chikankari Embroidery

Discover the delicate, handcrafted white-on-white embroidery tracing back to the Mughal courts, defining elegance and grace.

Explore the Craft
—  Legacy & Heritage  —

A Tale of Threads

The exact origins of Chikankari are steeped in legend, but historical consensus heavily credits the Mughal Empress Nur Jahan for introducing and patronizing the craft in the 17th century. Inspired by Persian aesthetics, the delicate floral motifs began as fine white cotton thread stitched over sheer white muslin.

As the Mughal empire waned, artisans migrated to the regional courts of Awadh. Under the patronage of the Nawabs of Lucknow, the art flourished and absorbed the city's refined cultural ethos. Today, Lucknow remains the undisputed heart of Chikankari, representing an enduring symbol of Indian sartorial heritage and painstaking craftsmanship.

—  The Canvas  —

Fabric Showcase

☁️

Muslin (Mulmul)

Texture: Ultra-fine, sheer, and incredibly soft cotton.

Use: The traditional and most authentic base for Chikankari, allowing the delicate stitches to stand out beautifully without puckering.

🎐

Georgette & Chiffon

Texture: Lightweight, slightly crinkled, and highly flowy.

Use: Modern favorites that provide an elegant drape, making them perfect for sarees and festive wear adorned with intricate embroidery.

🌿

Linen & Cotton

Texture: Breathable, slightly textured, and sturdy.

Use: Ideal for daily wear (kurtas). The sturdier weave holds heavier embossed stitches well.

—  The Process  —

Embroidery Techniques

1

Fabric Preparation

The chosen fabric is cut into the desired garment shape (kurta, saree) and washed to remove any starch.

2

Design Transfer (Chhapayi)

The design is stamped onto the fabric using intricately carved wooden blocks dipped in temporary dyes like indigo or safeda (white clay).

3

Stitching (Takayi)

Artisans use needle and thread to trace the stamped patterns using a combination of over 32 distinct Chikankari stitches.

4

Motif Formation

Floral patterns (paisleys, lotus, jasmine) and the signature Jaali work are painstakingly filled in to create depth and texture.

5

Finishing & Washing (Dhulayee)

The embroidered fabric is carefully washed multiple times to remove the ink traces, starched, and ironed for a pristine look.

—  Preservation  —

Care Guide

Washing

Chikankari-specific: Always dry-clean heavy garments or sheer fabrics like Georgette. For daily cottons, gentle hand wash in cold water using a mild detergent is recommended to protect the threads.

Drying

Chikankari-specific: Never wring the embroidered fabric. Squeeze gently and dry in the shade to prevent the colored threads (if any) from fading and the cotton from weakening.

Ironing

Chikankari-specific: Iron on the reverse side of the embroidery. This prevents the embossed stitches (like Murri or Phanda) from flattening out and losing their 3D texture.

Storage

Chikankari-specific: Store wrapped in a soft muslin cloth. Avoid hanging delicate georgette pieces for long periods, as the heavy embroidery can stretch the fabric.
