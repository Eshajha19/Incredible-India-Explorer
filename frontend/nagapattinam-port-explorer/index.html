Nagapattinam Ancient Port Explorer

Discover Nagapattinam (ancient Nagai), the legendary naval capital and maritime gateway of the Imperial Cholas. Explore its trans-oceanic trade routes to Southeast Asia and China, the royal Chudamani Vihara monastery, and rich Coromandel heritage.

Naval Hub of the Cholas

The Imperial Gateway of Coromandel

Nagapattinam, known historically as Nagai or Pattinam (and recorded as Nikama by Greco-Roman geographer Ptolemy), served as the principal maritime harbour of the medieval Chola Empire (9th to 13th century CE).

Located on the Bay of Bengal coastline in Tamil Nadu, Nagapattinam was the central launch pad for Chola naval fleets and international merchant guilds. Under visionary monarchs like Raja Raja Chola I and Rajendra Chola I, the port transformed into a prosperous global metropolis connecting India with Malaya, Sumatra, Java, and the Song Empire of China.

📜 Port Profile

  • Ancient Names: Nagai, Nagapattinam, Nikam.
  • Geographic Location: Coromandel Coast, Tamil Nadu (Bay of Bengal).
  • Primary Imperial Era: Golden Age of the Imperial Chola Dynasty (c. 985 – 1279 CE).
  • Key Heritage Monument: Chudamani Vihara & Kayarohanaswamy Temple.
Trans-Oceanic Commerce

Chola Maritime Trade & Srivijaya Expeditions

Nagapattinam anchored international trade routes connecting South Asia with China and the Indonesian archipelago.

⚔️

Srivijaya Naval Campaign (1025 CE)

In 1025 CE, Rajendra Chola I launched a historic naval armada from Nagapattinam across the Bay of Bengal, securing maritime trade passages through the Malacca Straits against the Srivijaya Empire.

🏯

Song Dynasty Trade Delegations

Chola embassies dispatched from Nagapattinam maintained official commercial ties with China. High-value exports included fine Coromandel cotton muslins, spices, ivory, and gemstones in return for Chinese silk and porcelain.

📜

Merchant Guilds (Ainurruvar)

Powerful autonomous merchant guilds like the Ayyavole 500 (Ainurruvar) and Manigramam operated from Nagapattinam, managing overseas warehouses, security guards, and credit letters.

☸️ The Chudamani Vihara Legacy

  • Royal Patronage: Built in 1006 CE by Sri Vijayan King Mara Vijayottungavarman of the Sailendra Dynasty.
  • Anaimangalam Charter: Raja Raja Chola I granted the revenues of Anaimangalam village for its upkeep.
  • Nagapattinam Bronzes: Over 350 exquisite bronze Buddha and Bodhisattva statues unearthed in excavations.
  • Syncretic Center: Coexisted peacefully alongside the historic Dravidian Kayarohanaswamy Shiva Temple.
Religious Harmony & Heritage

Cultural Significance & Buddhist Patronage

Nagapattinam was an extraordinary cosmopolitan center where Hinduism, Buddhism, and international cultures flourished side-by-side. It stood as one of the last major active strongholds of Theravada and Mahayana Buddhism in South India.

The famous Chudamani Vihara monastery was constructed under the joint patronage of the Sailendra rulers of Indonesia and the Chola emperors. The royal Anaimangalam Copper Plates record this legendary cross-border diplomatic alliance, reflecting Nagapattinam's global open-door heritage.

Chronology

Timeline of Nagapattinam's Maritime History

Tracking Nagapattinam from classical antiquity through the Chola era to modern times.

1st – 2nd Cent. CE

Ptolemy's Nikama

Mentioned by Greco-Roman geographer Ptolemy as Nikama and early Sangam poets as a thriving Bay of Bengal port.

1006 CE

Chudamani Vihara Foundation

Sailendra King of Srivijaya builds the Chudamani Vihara with royal grants sanctioned by Raja Raja Chola I.

1025 CE

Rajendra Chola's Srivijaya Expedition

Launch of the victorious Chola naval armada across the Bay of Bengal, establishing maritime supremacy.

1660 CE

Dutch Capital of Coromandel

Dutch East India Company captures Nagapattinam, making it the fortified administrative capital of Dutch Coromandel.

Modern Era

Port & Archaeological Reserve

Modern coastal port preserving invaluable Chola epigraphs, Buddhist bronzes, and maritime heritage monuments.
