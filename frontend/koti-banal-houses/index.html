Koti Banal Architecture

The ancient earthquake-resistant multi-story marvels of Uttarakhand.

Introduction to Koti Banal

Originating in the Rajgarh area of Uttarkashi district, Uttarakhand, Koti Banal is a highly sophisticated vernacular architecture style that has stood the test of time—and tectonic shifts—for over a thousand years. The name translates to "Koti" (forty) and "Banal" (forest), referring to the massive amount of timber used in its construction. These structures are essentially wooden cages filled with stone, designed specifically to survive the highly active seismic zone of the Himalayas.

Earthquake-Resilience Principles

🏗️ Base Isolation

The massive, broad stone plinth acts as a solid anchor, while the timber frame above it is not rigidly fixed to the ground, allowing it to slide slightly during high-magnitude tremors.

🪵 Flexible Joints

The wooden beams are interlocked using traditional joinery without iron nails. This allows the joints to rotate and absorb kinetic energy without snapping.

🪨 Energy Dissipation

The loose stone infill rubs against the timber frame during an earthquake. This friction dissipates massive amounts of seismic energy, protecting the structural integrity.

Materials Documented

  • Rai Wood (Banj Oak): Extremely dense and durable hardwood used for the primary load-bearing frame.
  • Quartzite & Slate: Used for the thick walls and heavy roofing, providing a low center of gravity.
