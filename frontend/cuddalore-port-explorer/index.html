Cuddalore Ancient Port Explorer

Discover Cuddalore (ancient Koodaloor), the historic estuary harbour at the confluence of the Gadilam and Uppanar rivers. Explore its textile trade legacy, Fort St. David fortifications, 7th-century Chola temples, and vibrant coastal heritage.

Confluence of Rivers & Commerce

The Ancient Estuarine Gateway of South Arcot

Cuddalore derives its Tamil name Koodaloor from its location at the confluence (*Koodal*) of the Gadilam and Pennaiyar/Uppanar rivers into the Bay of Bengal. Since classical times, this natural estuarine location provided sheltered anchorage for riverine and coastal vessels.

Celebrated in early Tamil devotional Tevaram literature as Thirupathiripuliyur, Cuddalore evolved from an ancient temple town under the Pallavas and Cholas into a major Coromandel commercial port. Its strategic position attracted successive ruling dynasties—including the Vijayanagara Empire, Nayaks of Gingee, Marathas, and European trading companies.

📜 Port Profile

  • Etymology: Koodaloor (Tamil for "Confluence of Rivers").
  • Geographic Location: Mouth of Gadilam & Uppanar Rivers, Coromandel Coast, Tamil Nadu.
  • Historical Rulers: Pallavas, Cholas, Nayaks of Gingee, Marathas, British & French.
  • Key Landmark: Fort St. David & Padaleeswarar Temple.
Maritime Commerce

Trade Significance & Handloom Weaving

Cuddalore connected South Indian weaver guilds with international merchant networks across the Bay of Bengal.

🧵

Coromandel Handloom Textiles

Cuddalore was a world-famous weaving and dyeing hub producing fine cotton muslins, piece goods, and chintz exported across Southeast Asia, Sri Lanka, and Europe.

🌿

Spices, Indigo & Crops

Merchant fleets exported high-grade indigo dye, groundnuts, spices, and teak timber from South Arcot forests to Ceylon, Malaya, and Burma.

⚔️

Fort St. David & Colonial Trade

Acquired by the British East India Company in 1690 CE, Fort St. David in Cuddalore became the headquarters of English settlements on the Coromandel Coast, rivaling French Pondicherry.

🏛️ Coromandel Heritage Landmarks

  • Fort St. David (1690 CE): Historic British brick-and-stone fortress on the Gadilam River.
  • Padaleeswarar Temple: 7th-century Chola-Pallava Shiva shrine sung by Saint Appar.
  • Silver Beach: Long sandy coast witnessing centuries of maritime commerce.
  • Pichavaram Mangroves: World's 2nd largest mangrove ecosystem located nearby.
Monuments & Ecosystems

Nearby Heritage Sites of Cuddalore

Beyond its busy harbour, Cuddalore is surrounded by extraordinary historical and natural monuments.

The 7th-century Padaleeswarar Temple stands as a monument to ancient Dravidian stone architecture. Nearby Fort St. David preserves colonial ramparts where Robert Clive served. Just down the coast, the pristine Pichavaram Mangrove Forests form a labyrinth of green waterways protecting the estuarine bay.

Chronology

Timeline of Cuddalore Port

Key historical milestones shaping Cuddalore across two millennia.

7th Cent. CE

Pallava Devotional Era

Celebrated in Saint Appar's Tevaram hymns as Thirupathiripuliyur; active estuarine trade port.

10th – 13th Cent. CE

Chola Commercial Zenith

Flourishing textile weaving center exporting Coromandel fabrics to Southeast Asian ports.

1690 CE

Fort St. David Purchase

British East India Company purchases Fort St. David from Maratha ruler Rajaram, expanding coastal trade.

1746 – 1752 CE

Coromandel Headquarters

Fort St. David serves as the capital of English Coromandel Presidency during Anglo-French conflicts.

Modern Era

Fishing & Coastal Heritage Port

Active commercial maritime harbour and coastal heritage destination preserving centuries of history.
