Lok Sabha — House of the People

The Lok Sabha is the lower house of India's bicameral Parliament. Members are directly elected by the people.

🗳️ Election Process

Members elected from single-member constituencies using first-past-the-post. Every citizen aged 18+ can vote.

⏱️ Term & Dissolution

Normal term is 5 years, but can be dissolved earlier by the President on advice of the Council of Ministers.

💪 Powers

Controls the money bill — only Lok Sabha can introduce money bills. The Council of Ministers is responsible to Lok Sabha.

👑 Speaker

Elected from among its members. Presides over sessions, maintains order, and decides on disqualification.

Rajya Sabha — Council of States

The Rajya Sabha is the upper house of Parliament. It represents the states and union territories of India.

🔄 Permanent Body

Cannot be dissolved. One-third retire every 2 years, each serving a 6-year term.

📊 Composition

Maximum 250 members: 238 elected + 12 nominated by the President.

👔 Chairman

The Vice President of India is the ex-officio Chairman.

⚡ Special Powers

Can authorize laws on State List subjects. Can delay non-money bills for up to 14 days.

Key Roles in Parliament

Understanding the key positions that keep Parliament functioning smoothly.

⚖️

Speaker of Lok Sabha

Elected from Lok Sabha members

  • Presides over Lok Sabha sessions
  • Maintains order and decorum
  • Casts deciding vote in tie
  • Decides on Anti-Defection disqualification
Current: Om Birla
👔

Chairman of Rajya Sabha

Vice President of India (ex-officio)

  • Presides over Rajya Sabha
  • Maintains order
  • Does not vote ordinarily
  • Refers bills to committees
Current: Jagdeep Dhankhar
📋

Leader of the House

Head of the ruling party/coalition

  • Represents the government
  • Announces policies
  • Manages legislative business
🗣️

Leader of Opposition

Largest opposition party leader

  • Scrutinizes government actions
  • Raises public issues
  • Participates in committee appointments
Note: Requires 10% seats

Parliamentary Sessions

Parliament meets in three sessions every year.

Feb — May

Budget Session

The longest and most important session.

  • President's Address
  • Union Budget presentation
  • Demands for Grants
Jul — Aug

Monsoon Session

Second session, usually the shortest.

  • Key legislative bills
  • Question Hour & Zero Hour
  • Calls Attention motions
Nov — Dec

Winter Session

Third session covering year-end business.

  • Pending bills completion
  • Year-end review
  • Constitutional amendments

Key Parliamentary Procedures

Question Hour

First hour of every sitting. MPs ask ministers about government functioning.

Zero Hour

Unscripted period after Question Hour. Urgent matters without prior notice.

Adjournment Motion

Disrupts normal business to draw attention to urgent public matters.

No-Confidence Motion

Requires 50+ members' support. If passed, Council of Ministers must resign.
