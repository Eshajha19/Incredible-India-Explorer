Kavaratti Island

The administrative capital of Lakshadweep — a coral island ringed by a calm turquoise lagoon, home to vivid reefs, a well-loved marine aquarium, historic mosques and a close-knit island community.

🌊 Lagoons

Kavaratti is wrapped by a wide, shallow lagoon with calm, clear turquoise water that is protected from the open sea by a surrounding reef. The lagoon's gentle currents make it one of the safest and most inviting in Lakshadweep.

Glass-bottom boat rides across the lagoon are a popular way to view the reef and marine life below without getting into the water, and the lagoon's calm shallows are also ideal for swimming and kayaking.

  • Wide, shallow lagoon with calm turquoise water
  • Protected from the open sea by the surrounding reef
  • Popular for glass-bottom boat rides, swimming and kayaking

🐠 Marine Aquarium

Kavaratti is home to one of Lakshadweep's few marine aquariums, showcasing the colourful reef fish, corals and other lagoon life found in the surrounding waters, giving visitors a close look at the ecosystem without diving.

The aquarium serves as both a tourist attraction and an educational resource, helping visitors and locals alike understand the reef habitats that the island's economy and culture depend on.

  • One of the few marine aquariums in Lakshadweep
  • Displays reef fish, corals and lagoon species
  • Doubles as an educational resource on reef ecology

🪸 Coral Reefs

The reef encircling Kavaratti's lagoon supports a rich variety of coral formations and reef fish, making the island a popular spot for snorkelling and scuba diving in relatively calm, shallow water.

Like other Lakshadweep reefs, Kavaratti's corals are sensitive to rising sea temperatures and bleaching events, and are part of wider conservation efforts across the archipelago to protect these coral ecosystems.

  • Fringing reef with varied coral formations and reef fish
  • Popular for snorkelling and scuba diving
  • Subject to coral reef conservation efforts across Lakshadweep

🎎 Culture

As the administrative capital, Kavaratti blends the traditional Lakshadweep way of life with the island's role as a centre of governance. The local community is predominantly Muslim, and daily life is closely tied to fishing, coconut cultivation and the sea.

Traditional music, boat races and local cuisine built around fish, coconut and rice reflect the island's close relationship with the surrounding ocean.

  • Predominantly Muslim island community
  • Daily life closely tied to fishing and coconut cultivation
  • Traditional boat races and coastal cuisine

🏝️ Tourism

Kavaratti is a key entry point for tourism in Lakshadweep, offering lagoon activities like kayaking, glass-bottom boat rides and snorkelling, alongside visits to the marine aquarium and the island's historic mosques.

Because Lakshadweep tourism is regulated to protect its fragile ecosystem, visits to Kavaratti typically require permits, and eco-friendly practices are encouraged to preserve the island's lagoon and reefs.

  • Lagoon activities: kayaking, boating and snorkelling
  • Entry point for visits to the marine aquarium and mosques
  • Permit-based, eco-conscious tourism to protect the ecosystem

🕌 Mosques

Kavaratti is known for having a large number of mosques for its size, with over 50 spread across the small island, reflecting the deep-rooted Islamic heritage of the Lakshadweep community.

The Ujra Mosque is among the most notable, admired for its traditional coral-stone architecture and carved wooden ceiling, and stands as a symbol of the island's long-standing religious and architectural traditions.

  • More than 50 mosques across the small island
  • Ujra Mosque known for coral-stone architecture and carved ceiling
  • Reflects the island's long-standing Islamic heritage
