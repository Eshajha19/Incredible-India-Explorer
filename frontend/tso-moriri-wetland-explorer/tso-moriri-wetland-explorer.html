Tso Moriri Wetland

High-Altitude Ramsar Wetland of Ladakh

Changthang Plateau, Ladakh, India

Elevation: ~4,595 m

Explore Ecosystem View Map
Overview

A Vital Himalayan Ecosystem

High-Altitude Wetland

Formed from glacial melt, this unique freshwater to brackish lake features seasonal freezing and thawing, supporting a fragile alpine wetland ecosystem vital for regional biodiversity.

Ramsar Site Designation

Recognized internationally under the Ramsar Convention for its immense ecological significance, biodiversity protection, and sustainable management practices.

Cold Desert Climate

Characterized by a harsh climate with freezing winters and low rainfall. The brief summer tourism season offers mild temperature variations despite high UV exposure.

Life at 4,500m

Changpa Nomads & Rare Fauna

A sustainable coexistence in the harsh Changthang landscape.

Changpa Community

Culture

A nomadic pastoral lifestyle deeply connected to nature. They herd yaks and Pashmina goats, preserving traditional culture and sustainable coexistence.

Black-necked Crane

Vulnerable

Tso Moriri is an incredibly important breeding habitat and nesting area for these migrating birds, heavily protected by local conservation efforts.

Snow Leopard

Vulnerable

The "Ghost of the Mountains" prowls the higher ridges surrounding the lake. Extremely rare and a pinnacle of the Himalayan food web.

Himalayan Marmot

Least Concern

These robust rodents can often be seen sunbathing near their extensive burrow systems across the alpine meadows.

Other Notable Wildlife: Tibetan Wolf, Kiang (Wild Ass), Bar-headed Goose, Brown-headed Gull, and Himalayan Hare.

Facts

Did You Know?

Tso Moriri is one of India's highest lakes at nearly 4,595 meters.

It is located entirely within the sprawling Changthang Wildlife Sanctuary.

The lake is famous worldwide for its mesmerizing, crystal-clear blue waters reflecting the barren peaks.
