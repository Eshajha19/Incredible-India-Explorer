Satopanth Explorer

Standing tall at 7,075 meters, Satopanth is a majestic and complex mountain in the Gangotri range of the Garhwal Himalayas in Uttarakhand. Known for its formidable climbing routes and technical knife-edge ridges, this snow-cloaked peak is a classic mountaineering expedition destination.

Geography, Glaciers & History

The Sacred Route

Satopanth is located in the Gangotri region of Uttarakhand, near the border with Tibet. The name 'Satopanth' translates directly to 'Path of Truth' or 'Pure Path' in Sanskrit, reflecting its deep connections to regional mythology and sacred paths followed by ancient pilgrims.

The mountain lies between the Gangotri and Satopanth glaciers, just northeast of the holy town of Badrinath. It is flanked by high-altitude features including the triangular Satopanth Tal, a high glacier lake revered for its pristine waters and spiritual significance.

The peak was first climbed in 1947 by a Swiss team led by René Dittert, marking a major milestone in Himalayan mountaineering. The route features challenging technical elements, particularly on the knife-edge North-East Ridge and the steep ice-slopes of the South Face.

Technical Specifications

  • Elevation: 7,075 m (23,212 ft)
  • Prominence: 1,250 m (4,101 ft)
  • Coordinates: 30°50′45″N 79°17′09″E
  • Climbing Difficulty: Grade VI (Extreme / Alpine)
  • First Ascent: August 1, 1947
  • Expedition Team: Swiss Expedition
