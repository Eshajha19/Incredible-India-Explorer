⛵ Deccan & Andhra · c. 1st Century BCE – 2nd Century CE

Satavahana Dynasty Explorer

Step into the golden age of the Deccan — where Satavahana kings ruled from Pratishthana and Amaravati, minted ship-type lead coins, forged Indian Ocean trade routes with Imperial Rome, and patronized magnificent Buddhist stupas.

Rulers of Dakshinapatha

The Lords of the Southern Trade Routes

The Satavahanas (also known as the Andhras in Puranic texts) were an ancient Indian dynasty that ruled over large parts of the Deccan plateau, modern Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Karnataka. Emerging after the fall of the Mauryan Empire, they established their early capital at Pratishthana (Paithan) and later at Amaravati (Dharanikota).

They served as a vital cultural and economic bridge between Northern and Southern India, administering trade networks, promoting Prakrit literature, and issuing the earliest indigenous Indian coins carrying portraits of kings and ship symbols.

🏛️ Satavahana At a Glance

  • Era: c. 1st Century BCE – 2nd Century CE
  • Capitals: Pratishthana (Paithan), Amaravati, Junnar
  • Languages: Prakrit, Early Telugu/Kannada substratum
  • Coinage: Lead, Potin, and Silver coins featuring ship motifs & royal portraits
Monarchs & Conquerors

Major Satavahana Rulers

Key kings who expanded the empire's borders, repelled foreign Sakas, and patronized trade and literature.

👑

Simuka (c. 1st Century BCE)

Founder of the Satavahana dynasty who established independent rule in the Deccan post-Mauryan and Kanva decline.

⚔️

Satakarni I

Expanded the empire through military campaigns across Central India and performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice to claim imperial status.

🛡️

Gautamiputra Satakarni (r. c. 106–130 CE)

The greatest Satavahana monarch, who crushed the Western Kshatrapas (Sakas), revived Satavahana power, and claimed the title 'Lord of Dakshinapatha'.

⛵

Vasisthiputra Pulumavi (r. c. 130–159 CE)

Son of Gautamiputra who shifted imperial focus eastwards to Amaravati and expanded maritime commerce along the Coromandel coast.

⚓

Yajna Sri Satakarni (r. c. 167–196 CE)

Rebuilt naval strength and issued famous silver and lead coins featuring two-masted ships, symbolizing maritime supremacy.

📜

Hāla (r. c. 1st Century CE)

Philosopher king credited with compiling the Gāthā Saptasatī, an anthology of 700 Prakrit poems celebrating rural life and romance.

🌐 Deccan Trade Network

  • Port Towns: Bharuch (Barygaza), Sopara, Kalyan, Motupalli.
  • Exports: Cotton textiles, spices, semi-precious stones, ivory, and timber to Rome and Southeast Asia.
  • Imports: Roman gold coins (denarii), wine amphorae, and glassware.
  • Guild System: Wealthy merchant guilds (Shrenis) financed Buddhist cave complex constructions.
Commerce & Navigation

Deccan Trade Routes & Roman Linkages

The Satavahanas controlled the crucial trade passes (Ghats) linking the inland agricultural valleys of the Godavari and Krishna with major Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal ports. The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea details how ships sailed from Egypt and Rome to Satavahana ports.

Wealth generated from trade enabled the dynasty to issue extensive coinage and subsidize massive stone art projects across Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh.

Chronology

Satavahana Dynasty Timeline

Key historical milestones from origin to regional fragmentation.

c. 1st Century BCE

Founding by Simuka

Establishment of Satavahana sovereignty in the upper Godavari valley post-Mauryan decline.

c. 70 BCE

Expansion under Satakarni I

Military dominance across Malwa and Northern Deccan; performance of Vedic sacrifices.

c. 106 CE

Resurgence under Gautamiputra Satakarni

Defeat of Nahapana and Saka rulers; restoration of Satavahana hegemony recorded in Nasik cave inscriptions.

c. 150 CE

Flourishing of Amaravati Stupa

Vasisthiputra Pulumavi patrons magnificent limestone carvings at the Great Stupa of Amaravati.

c. 220 CE

Fragmentation

Empire splits into smaller successor lineages like the Ikshvakus, Abhiras, and Kadambas.
