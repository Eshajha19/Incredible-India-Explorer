Indian Lacquerware Explorer

Lacquerware is one of India's oldest lathe-turned wood crafts, born from the resinous secretion of the lac insect. Across Channapatna's toy workshops, Etikoppaka's riverside lathes, Nirmal's gilded ateliers, and Jaipur's bangle lanes, artisans press molten lac against spinning wood to create glossy, jewel-toned heirlooms that have delighted households, temples, and children for generations.

A Resin Turned into Art

What Is Lacquerware?

Lac is a natural resin secreted by the tiny lac insect (Kerria lacca) on the branches of host trees such as palas, ber, and kusum. Once harvested and refined, this resin is melted into sticks, tinted with mineral or vegetable pigments, and pressed against wood spinning rapidly on a lathe. Friction heat softens the lac, allowing it to bond and spread into a smooth, glossy coating as the craftsperson shapes and colours the piece by hand.

The technique ? sometimes called lac-turnery or turned-wood lacquerware ? is thought to have travelled along ancient trade routes between Persia, Central Asia, and India, blending with indigenous wood-turning skills to produce a distinctly Indian craft tradition found today in Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Rajasthan, Punjab, and Uttar Pradesh.

?? Lacquerware Snapshot

  • Core Material: Lac resin from the Kerria lacca insect
  • Key Tool: Foot- or power-driven wood lathe
  • Leading Centres: Channapatna (Karnataka), Etikoppaka & Nirmal (Andhra Pradesh/Telangana), Jaipur (Rajasthan)
  • Typical Products: Toys, bangles, boxes, furniture legs, bowls, idols
  • Recognition: Channapatna toys and Etikoppaka toys both carry Geographical Indication (GI) tags
From Raw Resin to Radiant Sheen

How Lacquerware Is Made

Click on any step to see how a plain block of wood becomes a polished, colourful piece of lacquerware.

01

Wood Selection & Seasoning

Soft, fine-grained woods such as ivory-wood (hale mara), rubber wood, or local softwoods are cut, dried, and seasoned to prevent cracking.

02

Lathe Turning

Seasoned blocks are mounted on a hand- or power-driven lathe and shaped with chisels into toys, beads, bangles, or vessel forms.

03

Lac Preparation

Raw lac is cleaned, melted, and mixed with vegetable or mineral pigments and a resin like beroza to form coloured lac sticks.

04

Lac Application

Coloured lac sticks are pressed firmly against the spinning wood; friction heat melts the resin so it spreads into an even coat.

05

Polishing

A dried palm leaf, wood shaving, or wax cloth is pressed against the still-spinning, lac-coated surface to buff it to a glassy shine.

06

Detailing & Finishing

Pieces are parted from the lathe, hand-finished, and ? in styles like Nirmal ? further embellished with gold-leaf motifs or paintwork.

One Craft, Many Traditions

Regional Variations

Lacquerware wears a different face in every state that practises it. Explore the distinct materials, motifs, and histories below.

Karnataka

Channapatna Toys

Known as "Gombegala Ooru" (Toy Town), Channapatna's craft dates to the late 18th century, when Tipu Sultan is said to have invited Persian artisans to teach lacquer techniques to local wood-turners. Artisans use hale mara (ivory-wood, Wrightia tinctoria) and colour it with lac blended with vegetable dyes like turmeric and indigo. The craft received a GI tag in 2005 and today supports hundreds of home workshops around Ramanagara district.

Andhra Pradesh

Etikoppaka "Tharani" Toys

In the village of Etikoppaka on the banks of the Varaha river near Visakhapatnam, artisans practise "Tharani" or turned-wood lacquerware, documented since at least the 1830s. Toys, spinning tops, and boxes are coloured with lac mixed into natural, non-toxic dyes, giving the craft its reputation as one of India's most eco-conscious toy-making traditions. It nearly disappeared in the 20th century before revival efforts led by craftsman C. V. Raju restored it to prominence; it now carries its own GI tag.

Telangana

Nirmal Lacquerware & Toys

The town of Nirmal is celebrated for wooden toys and decorative figures finished in lac and further embellished with fine gold-leaf detailing, a technique historically linked to Mughal-era craftsmen. Nirmal artisans are also known for softwood carving using "Poniki" wood, paired with the same lac-turnery finishing seen elsewhere in the Deccan.

Rajasthan

Jaipur Lac Bangles

In Jaipur's Maniharon ka Rasta lane, the Manihar community melts lac into a pliable core that is shaped into bangles and encrusted with mirrors, stones, and meenakari-style enamel work. Deeply tied to ritual life, lac bangles ("lakh ki chudiyan") are worn by brides and are considered auspicious for festivals such as Teej, Gangaur, and Karva Chauth.

The Hands Behind the Craft

Artisan Communities

?????

Chitragar & Local Artisans, Channapatna

Historically practised by the Chitragar community, the craft is now carried on by a wider mix of artisans, including many working from home workshops and the government-supported Lacquerware Crafts Complex in Channapatna.

??

Vishwabrahmin & Allied Communities, Etikoppaka

Around 200 artisans from communities including Vishwabrahmin, Devanga, Gouda, Padmasali, and Kapu sustain the Etikoppaka tradition, many organised through cooperative and revival initiatives since the late 1980s.

??

Manihar Community, Jaipur

The predominantly Muslim Manihar community, whose name derives from the Sanskrit "mani" (jewel), has shaped Jaipur's lac bangle trade for generations, with lineages of artisans concentrated along Maniharon ka Rasta.
