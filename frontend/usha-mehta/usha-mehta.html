Usha Mehta

Voice of the Underground

Secret Congress Radio and the Quit India Movement

Biography

A Life of Defiance

Usha Mehta (1920–2000) was a prominent Gandhian and freedom fighter of India. She is most remembered for organizing the Secret Congress Radio, an underground radio station, which functioned for a few months during the Quit India Movement of 1942.

Born in a village near Surat, she was deeply influenced by Mahatma Gandhi from an early age. When the Quit India Movement was launched in August 1942, most top Congress leaders were arrested. In this leadership vacuum, Usha Mehta, then a 22-year-old student, helped establish the secret transmission system to keep the spirit of the movement alive, broadcasting messages of freedom, news of protests, and speeches by leaders.

She was eventually arrested and imprisoned in Yerwada Jail until 1946. After independence, she led an active academic life, eventually receiving the Padma Vibhushan in 1998 for her contributions.
