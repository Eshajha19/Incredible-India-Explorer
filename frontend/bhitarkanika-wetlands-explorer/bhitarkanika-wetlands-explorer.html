Bhitarkanika Wetlands

India's Mangrove Paradise and Ramsar Wetland

Kendrapara District, Odisha, India

Discover Wildlife View Map
Ecosystem

A Thriving Estuarine Network

Mangrove Ecosystem

India's second-largest mangrove ecosystem features dense tidal forests that provide vital coastal ecology benefits, act as a massive carbon sink, and serve as an essential natural barrier preventing coastal erosion during cyclones.

Ramsar Designation

Recognized globally as a Ramsar Site of International Importance. The wetlands are crucial for biodiversity protection, wetland conservation, and demonstrate sustainable ecosystem management practices.

Estuarine Biodiversity

A complex network of rivers, estuaries, creeks, and mudflats supports immense fish diversity, crustaceans like crabs, molluscs, and forms a highly productive aquatic ecosystem.

Wildlife Sanctuary

Apex Predators and Avian Wonders

Home to the world's largest living reptile and vibrant bird colonies.

Saltwater Crocodile

Conservation Success

The world's largest living reptile. Bhitarkanika's intensive crocodile conservation programme has led to a remarkable population recovery, making it one of the best breeding and nesting habitats globally.

Asian Openbill

Least Concern

A prominent resident of the heronries. During monsoon, thousands gather to breed, creating one of the largest nesting colonies in India.

Black-capped Kingfisher

Least Concern

A stunning coastal kingfisher often spotted diving into the tidal creeks for fish and small crustaceans.

White-bellied Sea Eagle

Least Concern

This majestic raptor patrols the expansive estuaries, relying on the rich fish diversity of the mangroves.

Also home to: Lesser Adjutant Stork, Egrets, Herons, and thousands of Migratory Waterfowl taking advantage of excellent birdwatching opportunities.

Conservation

Preserving the Wild

Designated as a National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary to safeguard its fragile ecosystem.

  • Crocodile Conservation Programme: A globally lauded initiative that successfully revived the critically endangered saltwater crocodile population.
  • Mangrove Restoration: Active community participation in planting and preserving the tidal forests.
  • Eco-Tourism: Sustainable boat safaris strictly follow safety guidelines to minimize impact on wildlife while providing economic benefits to locals.
Facts

Did You Know?

  • One of India's largest and most diverse mangrove ecosystems, second only to the Sundarbans.
  • Famous for its massive population of saltwater crocodiles, including individuals exceeding 20 feet in length.
  • Recognized as a Ramsar Site of International Importance in 2002.
  • Its name means 'Inner Beauty' in Odia ('Bhitar' meaning interior, 'Kanika' meaning extraordinarily beautiful).
  • Supports an incredible density of both resident and migratory bird species.
