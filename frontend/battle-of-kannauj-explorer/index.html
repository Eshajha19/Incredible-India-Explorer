⚔️ Uttar Pradesh · 17 May 1540 CE

Battle of Kannauj (Bilgram)

On the plains near Kannauj, Sher Shah Suri's Sur forces delivered the decisive blow against Mughal Emperor Humayun — a victory that ended the first Mughal reign in India, established Sur dominance across the Gangetic heartland, and ushered in an era of sweeping administrative, military, and architectural reforms.

After the Ganges Trap of Chausa

The Clash That Ended a Dynasty

The Battle of Chausa (1539) had shattered Humayun's army and sent the Mughal emperor fleeing across the Ganges. But Sher Shah Suri did not pursue immediately — instead, he pressed his advantage, seizing key forts and cities along the Ganges corridor while Humayun licked his wounds in the east. By early 1540, Humayun, desperate to recover his prestige and his capital, marched out from Bengal with a reorganised but still depleted force.

The two armies met near Kannauj at a site known as Bilgram. Sher Shah, ever the tactician, chose the battlefield with care — anchoring his line on the river and deploying his light Afghan cavalry and mountain guns to lethal effect. In the engagement that followed, the Mughal centre was rolled up, Humayun's artillery proved ineffective against Suri positional tactics, and the emperor was forced into a precipitate flight — this time without hope of a swift return.

🏛️ At a Glance

  • 17 May 1540 CE — Fought near Kannauj at Bilgram, Uttar Pradesh.
  • Sur Victory — A decisive battlefield win that ended Humayun's first reign.
  • Sher Shah Suri — The Afghan ruler who became the de facto sovereign of North India.
  • Sur Empire Born — The victory led to Sher Shah's coronation and a transformative 5-year reign.
Forces in the Field

The Belligerents

Two rival claimants to the throne of Delhi clashed on the Kannauj plain — the exiled Mughal emperor against the rising Suri dynasty.

🛡️

Sur Empire (Sher Shah Suri)

  • Commander — Sher Shah Suri
  • Strength — ~50,000 men, Afghan cavalry, and mountain artillery
  • Tactics — Positional defence, river-anchored line, and mobile cavalry strikes
🛡️

Mughal Empire (Humayun)

  • Commander — Humayun (Mughal Emperor)
  • Strength — ~30,000 men, artillery-heavy but demoralised after Chausa
  • Tactics — Traditional Mughal artillery barrages and centralised charge

Why it mattered: Unlike the conspiracies at Plassey or the night raids at Chausa, Kannauj was a full-scale set-piece battle. It demonstrated that the Mughals could be defeated in open combat — and that Sher Shah's reforms had created an army capable of doing so.

⚔️ The Verdict of Kannauj

  • Decisive Sur Victory — Humayun's army was routed; the emperor fled with little more than his life.
  • Casualties — Mughal losses were severe; many guns and standards captured. Sur losses were comparatively light.
  • End of the First Reign — Humayun retreated to Persia, beginning a 15-year exile.
  • Sher Shah Supreme — The Suri dynasty became the uncontested power in northern India.
The Sur Empire Ascendant

Why Kannauj Was the Turning Point

The defeat at Kannauj was the final nail in the coffin of the first Mughal experiment in India. Humayun's 15-year exile meant that the Mughals — heirs to Babur's legacy — were removed from the stage of North Indian politics just as they were beginning to consolidate their rule.

In Humayun's absence, Sher Shah Suri stepped into the imperial vacuum. He crowned himself Sultanat-uz-Zaman in Delhi, and over the next five years launched a sweeping programme of reform — of administration, taxation, trade, and architecture — that left an indelible mark on the subcontinent.

Legacy of the Victory

Historical Significance

The Battle of Kannauj did not merely change rulers — it reshaped the administrative, economic, and cultural landscape of the subcontinent.

1540–1545

The Sur Reign

Sher Shah Suri ruled for barely five years, yet his reign introduced reforms that outlived his dynasty. The Grand Trunk Road was rebuilt, the silver Rupiya was minted, and a new system of local governance took root.

1540

Currency Revolution

The Silver Rupiya, introduced during the Sur period, became the subcontinent's standard coin for centuries — adopted by the Mughals and persisting well into the British Raj.

1546

Architectural Legacy

Sher Shah's buildings — the Quwwat-ul-Islam complex, the Buland Darwaza, the Rohtas Fort, and the Sher Shah Suri Gate of Lahore — set architectural standards that the Mughals would emulate.

📜 Lasting Implications

  • End of Early Mughal Rule — Humayun's exile delayed Mughal restoration by over a decade; Akbar would inherit a transformed North India.
  • Administrative Reform — Sher Shah's Diwan (revenue), his subah-dar (provincial governor), and his chain-of-justice reforms influenced Mughal governance for generations.
  • Religious Tolerance — Unlike many contemporary Islamic rulers, Sher Shah patronised Hindu temples and allowed continued local customs — a model Akbar would refine.
  • Infrastructure — The Grand Trunk Road, inns (sarai), and bridges built under Sher Shah remained the arteries of north Indian commerce and communication for centuries.
  • Legendary Status — To this day, Sher Shah Suri is remembered in North India as the just ruler who, through a single battle, turned a regional chieftain into an emperor.
Milestones

Chronology of the Battle of Kannauj

From the Ganges trap at Chausa to Sher Shah's coronation — the road to Sur supremacy.

26 June 1539

Battle of Chausa

Sher Shah Suri's surprise night assault routs Humayun's Mughal camp at Chausa, near Buxar. Humayun escapes across the Ganges.

1540 (early)

Consolidating Power

Sher Shah captures Agra, Delhi, and key forts along the Ganges corridor while Humayun retreats to eastern India with a shattered army.

17 May 1540

The Battle of Kannauj (Bilgram)

Sher Shah Suri's forces decisively defeat Humayun's reorganised Mughal army near Kannauj. The emperor flees once more — this time into exile.

1540

Sher Shah Crowned

Humayun's defeat allows Sher Shah to enter Delhi triumphantly and assume the title Sultanat-uz-Zaman, founding the Sur Empire.

1 May 1545

Sher Shah Dies

Sher Shah Suri dies in Delhi after a brief but transformative five-year reign, leaving a legacy of reforms, coins, and monuments.

1555

Mughal Restoration

After 15 years of exile, Humayun returns with Akbar still a child — and the Sur Empire's foundations are already embedded in North Indian governance.
