📖 Introduction

The High-Range Cascade

A Hidden Gem of Munnar

Lakkam Waterfalls is located near Marayoor in the Idukki district of Kerala, approximately 35 km from the popular hill station of Munnar. Situated within the Anaimalai Hills — the highest range in South India — it represents one of Kerala's most elevated and pristine waterfalls.

The falls lie within the Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary buffer zone, surrounded by unique shola-grassland ecosystems and tea estates that define the Munnar high-range landscape. Unlike the more commercial Athirappilly, Lakkam offers a serene, off-the-beaten-path experience.

Water Source & Geography

The waterfall is fed by the Pambar River, which originates from the Anamudi peak (2,695 m) — South India's highest mountain. The river flows through the Eravikulam National Park before cascading at Lakkam, carrying pure glacial and spring water from the high-altitude shola forests.

Geologically, the falls occur where the river crosses a laterite escarpment formed by the tectonic uplift of the Western Ghats. The combination of high elevation, heavy monsoon rainfall (4,000-5,000 mm annually), and unique geology creates this spectacular cascade.

Structure Facts

  • Type: Multi-tiered cascade
  • Height: Approximately 20-25 metres in main drop
  • Total descent: ~100 metres through multiple tiers
  • River: Pambar (tributary of Amaravati)
  • Rock type: Laterite and charnockite
  • Ecosystem: Shola-grassland mosaic

🌧️ Seasonal Variation

When to Visit

Peak

Monsoon (Jun–Sep)

Waterfall at its most magnificent with full volume from heavy monsoon rains. Shola forests lush and green. Frequent mist creates dramatic atmosphere. Trails can be slippery. Leeches common.

Best

Post-Monsoon (Oct–Feb)

Ideal visiting period with healthy water flow and pleasant weather. Clear skies offer excellent views of surrounding peaks. Perfect for trekking and photography. Recommended season.

Low

Summer (Mar–May)

Water flow reduces to gentle stream. Some upper tiers may dry. Hotter days though cooler than plains. Tea estates remain green. Still beautiful but less spectacular.

🌳 Surrounding Ecosystem

High-Range Biodiversity

🌲

Shola Forests

Stunted evergreen forests in valleys above 1,800m. Unique to the Western Ghats with 80% endemic plant species. Home to rare species like Syzygium travancoricum.

🐐

Nilgiri Tahr

Endangered mountain ungulate found only in Western Ghats. Eravikulam NP has world's largest population (~750). Often visible on grassland ridges near the falls.

🦅

High-Altitude Birds

Endemic species including Nilgiri pipit, Nilgiri laughingthrush, black-and-orange flycatcher, and Kerala laughingthrush restricted to high ranges.

🌸

Neelakurinji

The legendary Strobilanthes kunthiana blooms once every 12 years, covering hillsides in violet-blue. Next mass flowering expected around 2030.

🐾

Rare Mammals

Brown palm civet, Nilgiri marten, lion-tailed macaque, and Indian giant squirrel inhabit the shola forests. Occasional tiger and leopard sightings.

🌾

Montane Grasslands

Rolling grasslands on ridges alternate with shola valleys. Support endemic grasses, orchids, and herbs. Critical for watershed conservation.

📍 Nearby Attractions

Places to Explore

Eravikulam National Park

UNESCO World Heritage Site home to endangered Nilgiri Tahr. Features Rajamalai tourism zone with stunning views. Entry by permit only. Closed during calving season (Feb-Mar).

15 km away

Marayoor Sandalwood Forest

One of India's few natural sandalwood forests. Government sandalwood depot and museum. Prehistoric dolmens (muniyaras) scattered in surrounding landscape.

8 km away

Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary

Unique dry deciduous forest ecosystem in rain-shadow region. Home to grizzled giant squirrel, star tortoise, and 40+ mammal species. Excellent for wildlife safaris.

25 km away

Top Station

Highest point on Munnar-Kodaikanal road at 1,880m. Panoramic views of Western Ghats and Tamil Nadu plains. Famous for Neelakurinji blooms every 12 years.

40 km away

Anamudi Peak

South India's highest mountain at 2,695 metres. Trekking requires permission from forest department. Spectacular views and endemic species. Adventure destination.

20 km away

Kundala Lake

Scenic artificial lake surrounded by tea estates and cherry blossoms. Boating facilities available. Nearby golf course adds to the charm. Popular picnic spot.

30 km away
