⚔️ Srirangapatna, Karnataka · April–May 1799

Siege of Seringapatam

Step onto the historic ramparts of Srirangapatna. Explore the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War's climax, where British-led coalition forces breached the island fortress capital, leading to the death of Tipu Sultan and the integration of Mysore.

The Fourth Anglo-Mysore War

The Campaign Against the Tiger of Mysore

By 1799, the British East India Company, led by Governor-General Richard Wellesley, was determined to crush the Kingdom of Mysore. Tipu Sultan's alliance with Revolutionary France threatened British colonial interests in India. Aligning with the Nizam of Hyderabad, the British launched a two-pronged invasion of Mysore.

General George Harris marched from Madras, while the Bombay Army advanced from the west. They joined forces to lay siege to the island fortress of Seringapatam, situated on the Kaveri River. Defended by Tipu's royal guards and pioneers of Mysorean rocket artillery, the fort resisted for several weeks before the final assault.

🏛️ At a Glance

  • Combatants: Kingdom of Mysore vs. British East India Company and the Hyderabad Nizam forces.
  • Location: Srirangapatna, Mandya District, Karnataka.
  • Outcome: Decisive British coalition victory.
  • Significance: Fall of the Independent Kingdom of Mysore; death of Tipu Sultan.
Opposing Commanders

Commanders and Belligerents

Learn about the notable military leaders and advisors who clashes at Srirangapatna.

👑

Tipu Sultan

The ruler of Mysore, known for his defiance of British rule and his implementation of advanced military rockets. He was killed defending the northern Water Gate.

💂

General George Harris

The commander-in-chief of the British forces who planned the grand encirclement and coordinated the multi-pronged artillery bombardments.

⚔️

Maj. Gen. David Baird

Led the final storming party that crossed the Kaveri River and breached the outer ramparts. He had previously been imprisoned by Tipu for four years.

🛡️

Arthur Wellesley

The future Duke of Wellington commanded the reserve column and spearheaded early outpost assaults, later appointed military governor of Seringapatam.

📈 Strategic Impact

  • End of French Alliance Threat: Tipu's death destroyed all potential French military collaborations in Southern India.
  • Restoration of the Wodeyars: British placed the child Maharaja Krishnaraja Wadiyar III on the throne, signing a subsidiary alliance.
  • Territorial Partition: Mysore was carved up, with the British gaining Kanara, Wayanad, and Coimbatore, and Hyderabad receiving northern districts.
  • Development of Rocketry: Tipu's metal-cylinder gunpowder rockets were sent to Woolwich for study, inspiring William Congreve to develop the Congreve rockets.
The Fall of Mysore

The Death of Tipu Sultan and Regional Annexation

On the morning of 4 May 1799, the British artillery bombardment succeeded in breaching the stone wall on the northern face of the fortress. Major General David Baird led the storming column across the river beds, securing the ramparts within hours. Tipu Sultan rushed to the breach, fighting alongside his soldiers, and was shot near the Water Gate.

The death of the "Tiger of Mysore" ended organized resistance. The British dismantled Tipu's administration and restored the ancient Wodeyar dynasty under a subsidiary alliance, making Mysore a princely state. Seringapatam fell into ruin, but it remained a milestone of British imperial consolidation.

Milestones

Siege Chronology

Follow the campaign events of the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War.

March 1799

The Encirclement

British Bombay and Madras armies converge, enclosing the island fortress from the north-west and south.

17 April 1799

Siege Commences

The British establish artillery batteries and begin regular shelling of the fortress ramparts.

2 May 1799

The Wall Breach

Heavy artillery fire breaches the outer curtain wall on the northern rampart.

4 May 1799 (Midday)

Storming & Fall

Baird's columns cross the Kaveri and storm the breach. Tipu Sultan is killed in action, and the fortress surrenders.
