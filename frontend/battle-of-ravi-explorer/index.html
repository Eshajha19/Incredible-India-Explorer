⚔️ Punjab · 1306 CE

Battle of Ravi Explorer

On the banks of the Ravi River, the armies of Alauddin Khalji's Delhi Sultanate — led by the general Malik Kafur — destroyed the last Mongol invasion of India. Kopek's Chagatai host was shattered, ending a generation of Mongol raids and securing the Sultanate's northern frontier once and for all.

The Storm from the Steppes

A Battle That Ended a Mongol Age

For nearly a decade, the Chagatai Khanate — the Mongol realm of Central Asia — had hurled invasion after invasion at the Delhi Sultanate. Alauddin Khalji met each one with fire: Jaran-Manjur (1298), Sivistan (1298), Kili (1299), Delhi (1303), and finally Amroha (December 1305), where some 20,000 Mongols were killed.

Enraged by the disaster at Amroha, the Chagatai ruler Duwa Khan sent one last great army in 1306 to avenge his fallen soldiers. It crossed the Indus and surged into the Punjab, ransacking the land as it advanced to the banks of the Ravi River. Alauddin, in response, dispatched an army commanded by his most trusted general — Malik Kafur — supported by Malik Tughluq and other senior commanders, and promised his soldiers a full year's salary as a battle bonus.

🏛️ At a Glance

  • 1306 CE — Fought on the banks of the Ravi River, in the Punjab.
  • Decisive Sultanate Victory — The Chagatai invasion was annihilated.
  • Kopek Captured — The Mongol commander-in-chief was taken prisoner.
  • Last Mongol Invasion — No Mongol army dared invade India again during Alauddin's reign.
Forces in the Field

The Belligerents

A disciplined Sultanate army of cavalry and commanders against the feared mounted archers of the Chagatai Khanate.

🛡️

Delhi Sultanate

  • Sultan — Alauddin Khalji (at Delhi)
  • Commanders — Malik Kafur, Malik Tughluq, Shahnah-i-Bargah, Malik Alam
  • Tactics — Disciplined cavalry, rapid forced marches, and the vanguard tactics of Malik Tughluq
🏹

Chagatai Khanate (Mongols)

  • Ruler — Duwa Khan (who ordered the expedition)
  • Commanders — Kopek, Iqbalmand, Tai-Bu
  • Strength — Isami claims up to 100,000 horsemen; likely an exaggeration
  • Tactics — Steppe cavalry, mounted archery, and rapid raiding

Why it mattered: The Mongols had conquered most of Asia, yet the Delhi Sultanate was the one great power they could never break. At the Ravi, that record was sealed — the Chagatai Khanate's ambitions in India ended in ruin.

Men Who Decided the Day

The Commanders

Brilliant generals on both sides faced each other across the Ravi — but only one army had Malik Kafur.

Sultanate · Commander-in-Chief

Malik Kafur

Alauddin's most trusted general. At the Ravi, his army scattered under Kopek's initial charge, but Kafur rallied his men and routed the entire Mongol host, capturing Kopek himself. He would go on to lead the Sultanate's greatest southern campaigns, down to Madurai.

Sultanate · Vanguard

Malik Tughluq

Governor of Dipalpur and commander of the vanguard, Tughluq spotted the Mongol scouts and guided the army to the battlefield. After the victory he adopted an aggressive frontier policy, raiding Kabul, Ghazni, Kandahar, and Garmsir — and in 1320 founded the Tughluq dynasty as Sultan Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq.

Sultanate · Command

Alauddin Khalji

The Sultan of Delhi who planned the campaign, promised his soldiers a year's salary as a bonus, and kept the Mongol horde at bay for his entire reign. His fort-building, standing army, and revenue reforms made the Sultanate a fortress against invasion.

Chagatai · Commander-in-Chief

Kopek (Kubak)

Duwa's chosen general. Kopek led the invasion to the banks of the Ravi, launched the attack that briefly scattered Kafur's soldiers, but was defeated, captured, and taken prisoner by the Delhi army.

⚔️ The Verdict of the Ravi

  • Decisive Sultanate Victory — Malik Kafur rallied his army and routed the Mongol host completely.
  • Kopek Captured — The Mongol commander-in-chief was taken prisoner on the field.
  • Massive Losses — Tens of thousands of Mongols were killed; captives were brought to Delhi, where they were executed or sold into slavery.
  • Invaders Routed — Iqbalmand and Tai-Bu fled across the Indus when they heard of Kopek's defeat.
The Frontier Secured

Why the Ravi Was the Turning Point

The Battle of Ravi was the fourth and final attempt by the Chagatai Khanate to conquer India. After the disaster, Amir Khusrau records, the Mongols retreated to the mountains of Ghazni and never launched another expedition during Alauddin's reign.

The victory ended the greatest external threat the Delhi Sultanate had ever faced. From then on, the roles reversed: Alauddin's generals — especially Malik Tughluq — took the war into Mongol territory itself, plundering Kabul and Ghazni and levying tribute on the frontiers, until Duwa's death in 1306–07 left the Chagatai Khanate too weak to threaten India for years to come.

Milestones

Chronology of the Battle of Ravi

From the first Mongol raids to the final, crushing defeat on the banks of the Ravi.

1297–1303

Repelled Invasions

Alauddin Khalji defeats successive Mongol attacks at Jaran-Manjur (1298), Sivistan (1298), Kili (1299), and Delhi (1303), where the invaders besiege but cannot break the under-construction Siri Fort.

December 1305

Battle of Amroha

Alauddin's 30,000-strong cavalry under Malik Nayak destroys a Mongol army in the Gangetic plains, killing about 20,000 invaders.

1306

Duwa's Revenge

Enraged by Amroha, Chagatai ruler Duwa Khan dispatches Kopek, Iqbalmand, and Tai-Bu with a vast army. The Mongols cross the Indus, ravage the Punjab, and advance to the Ravi River.

1306

The Battle of Ravi

Malik Kafur's army, guided by Tughluq's vanguard, meets the Mongols on the riverbank. Kopek's charge scatters Kafur's soldiers, but the Sultanate forces rally and annihilate the invaders, capturing Kopek.

1306–07

Invaders Routed, Duwa Dies

Iqbalmand and Tai-Bu flee across the Indus pursued by the Delhi army. Duwa Khan dies and his successors' power struggles end the Chagatai raids on India.

After 1306

War Carried to the Frontier

Alauddin's governor Malik Tughluq raids Kabul, Ghazni, Kandahar, and Garmsir annually, levying tribute on the Mongol frontier — and in 1320 founds the Tughluq dynasty.
