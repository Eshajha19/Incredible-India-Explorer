Tagore Award for Cultural Harmony

Discover India's international award honouring outstanding contributions to cultural harmony, universal brotherhood, and the values that Gurudev Rabindranath Tagore — poet, philosopher, and Nobel laureate — stood for throughout his life.

2011 Established Year
₹1 Cr Prize Money

📜 History of the Award

The Tagore Award for Cultural Harmony was instituted in 2011 by the Government of India as part of the nationwide commemoration of the 150th birth anniversary of Gurudev Rabindranath Tagore (1861–1941). The award is administered by the Ministry of Culture and conferred for exceptional contributions to the promotion of cultural harmony and universal values.

Genesis of the Award

The idea of instituting an award in Tagore's name was first raised at the inaugural meeting of the National Committee for the birth-anniversary commemoration, held on 20 May 2010 under the Chairmanship of the Prime Minister with 36 eminent personalities. The National Implementation Committee, chaired by the then Union Finance Minister, approved the proposal on 14 September 2010 and tasked the Ministry of Culture with working out the operational details.

Announcement and First Award

The institution of the award was announced in the Union Budget Speech on 28 February 2011, with prize money of ₹1 crore — the same as the Gandhi Peace Prize and the Jawaharlal Nehru Award for International Understanding. The first recipient, Pandit Ravi Shankar, the legendary Indian sitar maestro, was announced on 7 May 2012 during the closing ceremony of the commemoration. Since Pandit Ravi Shankar passed away on 11 December 2012, the award was presented to his wife, Smt. Sukanya Shankar, by the President of India at Rashtrapati Bhavan on 7 March 2013.

An International Honour

From the outset, the award was conceived as a prestigious international award — open to all persons regardless of nationality, race, language, caste, creed, or gender — reflecting Tagore's belief that humanity forms one universal family. The award carries an amount of ₹1 crore (convertible to foreign currency), a citation in a scroll, a plaque, and an exquisite traditional handicraft/handloom item.

💡 Inspiration Behind the Award

The award draws its inspiration from the life and philosophy of Rabindranath Tagore — the first Asian to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1913) for Gitanjali — and his unwavering faith in universal brotherhood, harmony, and the unifying power of art, music, and literature.

🌍

Universal Brotherhood

Tagore dreamed of a world "not broken up into fragments by narrow domestic walls" — a vision of humanity united across nations, cultures, and faiths that the award seeks to celebrate and propagate.

🎼

Art Unites Humanity

Tagore believed that music, poetry, and art speak a universal language that dissolves barriers of race and nationality — the very spirit recognized by this award for cultural harmony.

🏫

Visva-Bharati & Santiniketan

At Santiniketan, Tagore founded Visva-Bharati — "where the world makes its home in a single nest" — a living experiment in intercultural learning that remains a symbol of his faith in free spirit and creative power.

🕊️

Peace Through Creativity

Tagore was a passionate believer in universal peace, renouncing his knighthood in 1919 in protest against the Jallianwala Bagh massacre — a moral stand that exemplifies the courage the award honours.

🪔

Unity in Diversity

The award reflects India's timeless ethos of unity in diversity — celebrating intercultural dialogue, preserving cultural heritage, and fostering peace through creative expression.

📖

A Living Legacy

Nearly a century after his passing, Tagore's writings, songs, and ideals continue to inspire global movements for harmony, making this award a living tribute to his enduring legacy.

📋 Eligibility Criteria

True to Tagore's universalist vision, the award is designed to be open and inclusive, welcoming contributors from every corner of the world.

🌏 Open to All Nationalities

The award is open to all persons regardless of nationality, race, language, caste, creed, or gender — a genuinely international honour drawing nominations from across the globe.

👥 Individuals & Institutions

The award may be conferred upon individuals, associations, institutions, or organizations whose work epitomizes the high universal ideals that Tagore stood for.

📅 Ten-Year Contribution Window

Normally, contributions made during the ten years immediately preceding the nomination are considered. Older contributions may also be considered if their significance has become apparent only recently.

📚 Published Works

A written work, in order to be eligible for consideration, must have been published during the last ten years — ensuring the award celebrates contemporary relevance alongside timeless values.

What the Award Recognizes

Outstanding achievement in fostering harmony and universalism and the values of cultural harmony — especially in conflict or extreme situations — through innovative systems and strategies that have an enduring and transformational impact.

⚙️ Selection Process

The selection process is designed to be transparent, rigorous, and global, ensuring that only the most deserving contributors to cultural harmony receive the honour.

1

Global Nominations

Nominations are invited annually from eminent persons and institutions worldwide, in accordance with the Code of Procedure, by 31 August each year.

2

Selection Committee Sifting

All nominations received are sifted and shortlisted by a Selection Committee, which evaluates each contribution against the award's criteria of cultural harmony and universal values.

3

High-Level Jury Deliberation

The shortlist is placed before a high-level Jury chaired by the Prime Minister of India. The inaugural Jury comprised the Chief Justice of India, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and Shri Gopalkrishna Gandhi.

4

Announcement & Conferment

The award is announced on 7 May — the birth date of Gurudev Rabindranath Tagore — and conferred by the President of India at a ceremony at Rashtrapati Bhavan.

Selection Highlights

  • Transparency: A well-documented Code of Procedure governs nominations and evaluation
  • Global Reach: Nominations drawn from every part of the world, not just India
  • Highest Level: Jury chaired by the Prime Minister; conferred by the President
  • Enduring Impact: Emphasis on contributions with transformational, lasting effect

🏅 Award Recipients

Since its institution, the Tagore Award for Cultural Harmony has honoured a distinguished and diverse line of laureates — musicians, dancers, sculptors, cultural institutions, and writers — reflecting the award's universal spirit.

2012
🪕

Pandit Ravi Shankar

Hindustani Classical Sitar Maestro (1920–2012)

First recipient, announced on 7 May 2012. A Bharat Ratna awardee whose renditions internationalized Indian classical music; the award was received on his behalf by his wife, Smt. Sukanya Shankar.

2013
🎼

Maestro Zubin Mehta

Western Classical Conductor (b. 1936)

Conferred the 2013 award on 6 September 2013. Music Director for Life of the Israel Philharmonic, celebrated for humanitarian concerts at Sarajevo's ruins and Buchenwald.

2014
💃

Rajkumar Singhajit Singh

Doyen of Manipuri Dance (b. 1931)

Honoured for a lifetime of elevating Manipuri dance to national and international prominence, preserving and popularizing one of India's most exquisite classical art forms.

2015
🎭

Chhayanaut

Cultural Organization, Bangladesh (est. 1961)

The first non-Indian recipient — a pioneering cultural organization from Dhaka that has nurtured Bengali music, dance, and theatre for over six decades, embodying cross-border cultural harmony.

2016
🗿

Ram V. Sutar

Sculptor & Architect (b. 1925)

Principal architect of the Statue of Unity — the world's tallest statue — celebrated for sculpting icons of national unity and cultural pride that inspire millions.

2020
📖

Raj Kamal Jha

Novelist (b. 1966)

Honoured for his novel "The City and the Sea" — a work that explores the bonds that hold a fractured world together, echoing Tagore's vision of harmony.

A Truly Universal Honour

From an Indian sitar maestro to a Bangladeshi cultural organization, the roll of honour demonstrates that the Tagore Award truly belongs to the world — celebrating harmony without borders, exactly as Tagore envisioned.

🌏 Cultural Significance

More than a prize, the Tagore Award for Cultural Harmony is a statement of India's civilizational values and a global call for unity in an increasingly divided world.

🪔

Honouring Tagore's Legacy

The award keeps alive the ideals of the first Asian Nobel laureate — a poet, philosopher, composer, and educator whose vision of "the universal man" transcends time and borders.

🤝

Intercultural Dialogue

By honouring voices from India, Bangladesh, and beyond, the award actively promotes dialogue between cultures, faiths, and artistic traditions across the world.

🏛️

Preserving Cultural Heritage

Recognition of institutions like Chhayanaut and masters like Singhajit Singh reinforces the preservation and propagation of living cultural heritage for future generations.

🕊️

Peace Through Expression

The award celebrates creative expression — music, dance, art, and literature — as instruments of peace, healing, and reconciliation in conflict-affected societies.

🌏

Unity in Diversity

As an international award open to all nationalities, it embodies India's timeless ethos of unity in diversity and its commitment to inclusive cultural dialogue.

⭐

Transformational Impact

Recognizing contributions with enduring, transformational impact — especially in conflict or extreme situations — the award highlights culture as a force for change.

The Award's Enduring Message

In Tagore's own words — a world where "the mind is without fear and the head is held high" — the Tagore Award for Cultural Harmony reminds humanity that art, music, and shared values remain our most powerful bridges across every divide.
