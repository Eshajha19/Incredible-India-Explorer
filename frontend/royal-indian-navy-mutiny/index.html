⚓ India · 18–23 February 1946

Royal Indian Navy Mutiny

A hunger strike over rotten food aboard a single Bombay shore establishment grew, within days, into a fleet-wide uprising of 20,000 ratings across 78 ships and 20 shore bases — and shook Britain's confidence that it could hold India by force.

Causes

Conditions Faced by Naval Ratings

Indian "ratings" — the Royal Indian Navy's non-commissioned sailors — served under conditions sharply unequal to their British counterparts. Pay, promotion prospects and living quarters all lagged behind, while officers were overwhelmingly British and frequently treated Indian personnel with open contempt.

By early 1946, months after the Second World War had ended, thousands of ratings recruited for wartime service faced uncertain demobilisation, unresolved pay disputes, and daily indignities that made the navy's racial hierarchy impossible to ignore.

⚠️ Discrimination and Grievances

  • Racial hierarchy: British officers held almost all senior positions; Indian ratings were routinely addressed with racial slurs.
  • Poor food: Rotten, inadequate rations were a recurring and immediate grievance.
  • Unequal pay: Indian ratings were paid and promoted on markedly worse terms than British sailors.
  • Political awareness: The 1945–46 INA trials of Indian National Army officers had sharpened nationalist sentiment among servicemen.
18 February 1946

HMIS Talwar and the Initial Protest in Bombay

The mutiny began not on a warship but at a shore-based signals training establishment in Bombay.

📡

A Signals Training Base

HMIS Talwar trained ratings in signalling; months earlier, rating B. C. Dutt had been court-martialled for painting "Quit India" slogans on its walls.

🍽️

The Hunger Strike Begins

On 18 February 1946, ratings refused food in protest at its quality, quickly escalating into a full strike and refusal of duty.

📣

Demands Widen

What began as a food complaint broadened within hours into demands for equal pay, better conditions, and release of INA prisoners.

🔥

Rapid Escalation

News of the strike spread through Bombay harbour overnight, drawing in ratings from neighbouring ships and establishments the next morning.

By the Numbers

Ships and Shore Establishments Involved

🚢

78 Ships

Vessels across Bombay, Karachi, Calcutta, Madras, Vishakhapatnam and Cochin joined the strike within days.

🏗️

20 Shore Establishments

Signals schools, barracks and training bases across India stopped work in solidarity with HMIS Talwar.

👥

20,000 Ratings

An estimated 20,000 naval ratings took part at the mutiny's peak — the largest such uprising in the Royal Indian Navy's history.

🏴

HMIS Hindustan, Karachi

Ratings aboard HMIS Hindustan in Karachi harbour exchanged fire with British troops, among the mutiny's most violent episodes.

✊ Forms of Solidarity

  • General hartal: Bombay's mills, shops and transport shut down in sympathy.
  • Student strikes: Students across the city joined processions supporting the ratings.
  • Street clashes: Crowds clashed with British troops and police in several parts of Bombay.
  • Communist Party support: The Communist Party of India openly backed the mutineers, organising solidarity strikes.
Bombay Rises With the Ratings

Civilian Solidarity

The mutiny drew Bombay's civilian population into open confrontation with the colonial state. Mill workers, students and ordinary residents launched a citywide hartal, and in several neighbourhoods, crowds clashed violently with British troops sent to restore order.

Casualty estimates from the accompanying riots across Bombay and other cities run into the hundreds, making the civilian toll of the surrounding unrest far higher than that of the naval mutiny itself.

A Fractured Response

Political Responses

Indian political leadership was divided over how to treat an uprising it had not initiated and could not fully control.

🏛️

Indian National Congress

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel urged the ratings to surrender, wary of a violent uprising outside Congress's control jeopardising ongoing independence negotiations.

🕌

Muslim League

M. A. Jinnah likewise appealed for calm and surrender, sharing Congress's concern that the mutiny could derail constitutional talks with Britain.

🚩

Communist Party of India

Alone among major parties, the CPI backed the mutiny directly, organising solidarity strikes and civilian support.

⚔️

Naval Central Strike Committee

Led by M. S. Khan and Madan Singh, the Committee coordinated the ratings' demands and represented them in negotiations.

Show of Force

British Military Response

The colonial government treated the mutiny as a direct threat to imperial authority. Royal Navy warships and British Army units were mobilised around Bombay and Karachi harbours, with artillery and armed launches deployed against the striking ships.

Commanders threatened to bomb ships that did not stand down, and troops opened fire on both mutineers and civilian demonstrators in the accompanying unrest, contributing to a heavy toll among the largely unarmed crowds ashore.

🪖 Measures Taken

  • Warships and troops mobilised around Bombay and Karachi.
  • Threats to shell or bomb mutinous ships into surrender.
  • Firing on demonstrators during the accompanying civilian unrest.
  • Martial law-like measures imposed in the worst-affected areas.
23 February 1946

Negotiations and Surrender

🤝

Political Mediation

Vallabhbhai Patel and M. A. Jinnah both engaged directly with the Naval Central Strike Committee to press for an end to the standoff.

📜

Assurance Against Victimisation

The Committee agreed to surrender on the understanding that ratings would not be victimised for their part in the strike.

🏳️

Surrender, 23 February

Ratings stood down and returned to barracks, ending five days of mutiny across the Royal Indian Navy.

⚖️

A Promise Broken

Despite the assurance, many ratings were later dismissed from service, court-martialled, or otherwise penalised.

🇮🇳 Why It Mattered

  • Demonstrated that Indian servicemen's loyalty could no longer be assumed.
  • Followed closely on the 1945–46 INA trials, compounding pressure on British military confidence.
  • Cited by later historians as a factor accelerating Britain's decision to leave India.
  • Exposed the limits of Congress and League control over a rapidly radicalising public mood.
A Warning Britain Could Not Ignore

Historical Significance

The mutiny's importance lay less in its immediate outcome — a negotiated surrender followed by quiet reprisals — than in what it revealed. For the first time, a substantial section of Britain's own armed forces in India had turned against it, echoing, for many contemporaries, the memory of 1857.

Coming so soon after the INA trials had already inflamed nationalist sentiment among Indian servicemen, the mutiny reinforced a conclusion British officials were increasingly reaching on their own: that continued rule in India could no longer depend on the reliability of Indian troops and sailors, hastening the political calculations that led to independence in 1947.

Chronology

Royal Indian Navy Mutiny Timeline

Five days that spread from one Bombay shore establishment to ports across India.

18 Feb 1946

Strike Begins at HMIS Talwar

Ratings refuse food and duty in Bombay, protesting conditions and demanding equal treatment.

19 Feb 1946

Spreads Across Bombay Harbour

Ships and shore establishments across Bombay join the strike; a Naval Central Strike Committee forms.

20 Feb 1946

Karachi Joins, Violence Erupts

Ratings aboard HMIS Hindustan in Karachi exchange fire with British troops sent to retake the ship.

21 Feb 1946

Bombay Erupts in Solidarity

A citywide hartal and street clashes between civilians and British troops leave many dead and injured.

22 Feb 1946

Mutiny Reaches Other Ports

Calcutta, Madras, Vishakhapatnam, Cochin and Delhi establishments join in sympathy strikes.

23 Feb 1946

Surrender

Following negotiations involving Patel and Jinnah, the Strike Committee agrees to stand down on assurances against victimisation.

1946–1947

Reprisals and Aftermath

Many ratings are dismissed or court-martialled despite assurances, even as the mutiny's political shock reverberates toward independence.
