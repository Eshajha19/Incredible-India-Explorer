⚓ Indian Navy Gallantry Honour

Nao Sena Medal Explorer

Conferred upon all ranks of the Indian Navy for such individual acts of exceptional devotion to duty or courage as have special significance for the Navy.

Instituted: 1960 Awarded By: President of India Equivalent To: Sena Medal · Vayu Sena Medal

History & Genesis

🗓️

Instituted in 1960

The Nao Sena Medal ("Navy Medal") was instituted in 1960 by a notification of the President's Secretariat (No. 17-Pres/60 dated 26 January 1960). It was established alongside five other service decorations — the Vishisht Seva Medal series, Sainya Seva Medal, Videsh Seva Medal, Sena Medal (Army) and Vayu Sena Medal (Air Force) — to create a structured recognition system for distinguished and gallant service across the three armed forces.

⚓

Purpose & Special Significance

The medal is awarded for "such individual acts of exceptional devotion to duty or courage as have special significance for the Navy." It serves as the Navy's principal commendation, honouring sea-going gallantry, hazardous diving and salvage work, counter-piracy action, and outstanding devotion to duty that sets a benchmark for naval personnel.

🤝

A Tri-Service Family of Medals

The Nao Sena Medal is the naval counterpart of the Sena Medal (Indian Army) and the Vayu Sena Medal (Indian Air Force). All three share the same institutional framework, hierarchy and award philosophy, ensuring parity in how gallantry and devotion to duty are recognised across the Indian Armed Forces.

Eligibility Criteria

  • All Ranks of the Navy: Every rank of the Indian Navy is eligible, from sailors and divers to commissioned officers and flag officers.
  • Individual Acts: Awarded specifically for individual acts of exceptional devotion to duty or courage with special significance for the Navy.
  • Gallantry & Devotion to Duty: Conferred in two streams — Nao Sena Medal (Gallantry) for acts of bravery and Nao Sena Medal (Devotion to Duty) for outstanding peacetime service.
  • Posthumous Awards: The medal may be awarded posthumously to personnel who laid down their lives in the line of duty.
  • Repeated Recognition: A bar is authorised for every subsequent award of the medal to the same individual, worn on the ribbon.
  • Monetary Allowance: Gallantry awardees receive a monthly monetary allowance, enhanced with each bar to the medal.
  • Presidential Prerogative: The President of India may cancel and annul an award, and may also restore it subsequently.

Medal Design & Insignia

🔷

Distinctive Pentagon Shape

The medal is prescribed as pentagonal in shape with curved sides, struck in standard silver. Its distinct silhouette instantly differentiates the naval decoration from the circular Army medal and the star-shaped Air Force medal of the same family.

🎖️

Obverse: The Naval Crest

The obverse bears the Naval Crest, the emblem of the Indian Navy, symbolising the service's maritime identity. The mounting is a fixed ring attached to a metal stripe 3 mm wide ornamented with Ashoka leaves.

🔱

Reverse: Trident & Rope

The reverse features a trident within a circle and rope, with the inscription "Nao Sena Medal" embossed in Hindi along the upper rim — a design reflecting the Navy's ancient maritime heritage.

🎗️

Ribbon & Bars

The riband is navy blue with a white thin silver stripe down the centre (32 mm wide — 15 mm navy blue, 2 mm white, 15 mm navy blue). Additional awards are denoted by bars attached to the ribbon.

Naval Operations & Acts of Valour

The Nao Sena Medal has been earned across decades of naval history — from daring wartime operations to hazardous peacetime rescues. Here are some of the defining missions associated with the award.

🚀

Operation Trident (1971)

The Indian Navy's missile-boat attack on Karachi harbour on 4–5 December 1971. Missile boat attacks and subsequent strikes (Operation Python, 8 December) neutralised the Pakistani fleet, and numerous personnel were recognised for conspicuous gallantry in the high-stakes sea operations of the 1971 war.

🛳️

INS Khukri & the 1971 Blockade

During the 1971 Indo-Pak War, the Indian Navy enforced a tight naval blockade on East Pakistan while the frigate INS Khukri was tragically sunk off the coast of Karachi on 9 December 1971 with the loss of 18 officers and 176 sailors. Acts of courage during these operations feature prominently among Nao Sena Medal citations.

🛡️

Anti-Piracy Operations

From 2008 onwards, Indian Navy warships have conducted sustained anti-piracy patrols off the coast of Somalia and the Gulf of Aden, boarding vessels, foiling hijackings and rescuing hostages — operations that have produced numerous Nao Sena Medal (Gallantry) awardees.

🤿

Diving & Salvage Operations

The medal's earliest inspiration came from hazardous underwater work — such as Leading Seaman Lal Bahadur Mishra's bravery during diving operations at Bhakra Dam in September 1959. Naval diving teams continue to earn the honour through salvage, mine-clearing and submarine rescue work.

🌊

Humanitarian & Evacuation Missions

Navy personnel have repeatedly been recognised for rescue operations during natural disasters and conflict zones — including tsunami relief (Operation Sahayta, 2004), non-combatant evacuations such as Operation Sukoon (Lebanon, 2006) and Operation Rahat (Yemen, 2015), and coastal rescue operations.

Selection Process

Recommendations flow through the naval chain of command, are scrutinised at Naval Headquarters, and are finally approved and announced by the President of India. Names of recipients are published in the Navy Fleet Orders.

1

Recommendation

Commanding officers put forward personnel for individual acts of exceptional courage or devotion to duty.

2

Vetting & Scrutiny

Citations are examined and validated by Naval Headquarters within the Ministry of Defence.

3

Presidential Approval

Gallantry awards are approved by the President and announced on Republic Day (26 Jan) and Independence Day (15 Aug).

4

Publication

Awardee names are published in the Navy Fleet Orders and official gazette notifications.

5

Investiture

Medals are presented at investiture ceremonies at Rashtrapati Bhavan or by senior naval authorities in the field.

Timeline of the Nao Sena Medal

1959

Act of Bravery at Bhakra Dam

Leading Seaman Lal Bahadur Mishra displays outstanding bravery during hazardous diving operations at Bhakra Dam — a landmark act of naval courage.

1960

Medal Instituted

President's Secretariat Notification No. 17-Pres/60 (26 January 1960) institutes the Nao Sena Medal; designs are approved by the President later that year.

1961

Statutes Revised

The medal's statutes are modified (15 May 1961), refining eligibility and design specifications.

1971

Indo-Pak War

Operation Trident and Operation Python strikes on Karachi and the naval blockade of East Pakistan produce numerous Nao Sena Medal (Gallantry) citations.

1999

Monetary Allowance Introduced

From 1 February 1999, the Government sets a monthly stipend for gallantry awardees of the medal, later enhanced further.

2008+

Anti-Piracy Era

Sustained Indian Navy counter-piracy patrols off Somalia and the Gulf of Aden generate a new wave of gallantry awards for boarding operations and rescues.

2021

Trailblazing Recipients

Surgeon Rear Admiral Sheila Samanta Mathai — the first woman in the Indian Navy to reach Rear Admiral rank — receives the Nao Sena Medal (Devotion to Duty).
