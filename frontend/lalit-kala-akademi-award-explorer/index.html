📜 History of the Lalit Kala Akademi

The Lalit Kala Akademi (National Academy of Art) is India's national academy of fine arts, established in New Delhi on 5 August 1954 by the Government of India. It was inaugurated by the then Minister of Education, Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, and registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 on 11 March 1957.

It is the youngest of the three national Akademies founded in independent India — after the Sangeet Natak Akademi (performing arts, 1952) and the Sahitya Akademi (letters, 1954) — and is dedicated solely to the visual arts. The Akademi was born of Jawaharlal Nehru's vision of a national cultural identity, with the state supporting not just art but artists themselves.

In his inaugural address, Maulana Azad declared: "The Akademi must work to preserve the glorious traditions of the past and enrich them by the work of our modern artists. It must also seek to improve standards and refine public taste."

Mandate & Objectives

  • To encourage and promote study and research in the creative arts such as painting, sculpture, and graphics.
  • To promote and propagate an understanding of Indian art both within the country and abroad.
  • To coordinate the activities of State Lalit Kala Akademies and regional art organisations.
  • To encourage cooperation among artists and the development of art associations.
  • To organise exhibitions, seminars, conferences, and exchange programmes between schools of art.

Headquarters & Regional Centres

The Akademi is headquartered at Rabindra Bhawan, Ferozeshah Road, New Delhi, and is funded by the Union Ministry of Culture. It operates regional centres at Bhubaneswar, Chennai, Garhi (Delhi), Kolkata, Lucknow, and Shimla, along with a North-East Regional Centre at Agartala.

Flagship Events

The Akademi's most prestigious annual event is the National Exhibition of Art (NEA), begun in 1955, which showcases the year's advances in visual arts across the country. It also organises Triennale India, a major international exhibition of contemporary art first held in 1968, and publishes the bilingual journal Contemporary.

📋 Eligibility & Honour Structure

The Lalit Kala Akademi confers several levels of national recognition in the visual arts. Each honour recognises distinguished, sustained, and original achievement.

🏅 National Award (National Academy Award)

Conferred annually on artists whose works are selected in the National Exhibition of Art. Winners receive a plaque, a shawl, and a cash purse of ₹2 lakh. In the 64th National Exhibition (2024–25), 20 artists were honoured, with the awards conferred by the President of India.

💎 Lalit Kala Akademi Fellowship (Ratna)

The highest honour in the fine arts conferred in India. Given to eminent artists for their lifetime achievements in the visual arts, it carries a citation, a plaque, and a purse. The first Fellow, elected in 1955, was painter Jamini Roy.

🎨 Eligible Art Forms

Original works of painting, sculpture, printmaking (graphic art), and ceramics — together with applied arts, and, in recent editions, newer media such as installation and digital art. Folk and tribal traditions are showcased through dedicated programmes.

🇮🇳 Who Can Participate

Entries to the National Exhibition of Art are open to artists across India of any age or background. Submissions must be original works, not previously shown in the NEA, and are subject to selection by national juries.

🏛️ What is Recognised

Excellence in creative expression, technical mastery, and original vision. The Akademi celebrates artists who preserve glorious traditions of the past while enriching them through modern artistic practice.

📅 Annual Recognition

The National Exhibition of Art is held annually since 1955, with awards given for the best exhibits — from ten prizes of ₹1,000 each in 1955, to the Akademi's Gold Plaque (introduced 1956) and today's National Awards of ₹2 lakh.

🖌️ Painting — The Heart of the Akademi

Painting has been at the core of the Lalit Kala Akademi since its founding. The Akademi's constitution originally defined the "creative arts" as painting, sculpture, and graphics, and painters have formed the largest body of its Fellows and National Award winners.

Modern Masters

The Akademi's first Fellow, Jamini Roy (1955), drew on Bengali folk and Kalighat traditions, while Nandalal Bose (1956) brought the Bengal School's lyrical line into the national canon. The progressive generations — M. F. Husain, S. H. Raza, Ram Kumar, and Arpita Singh — were all honoured as Fellows.

Diverse Traditions

From Bengal School wash paintings and Santiniketan naturalism to the Baroda and Bombay modernists, the Akademi's exhibitions celebrate the extraordinary diversity of Indian painting — figuration, abstraction, and everything between.

National Exhibition of Art

Paintings form the largest segment of the annual National Exhibition of Art. The best works receive the prestigious National Award, plaque, and cash purse, and are often acquired for the Akademi's permanent collection.

🗿 Sculpture — Three Dimensions of Expression

Sculpture has enjoyed equal standing in the Akademi's vision, honouring India's deep sculptural heritage — from temple bronzes and stone carving to modern experiments in metal, wood, and mixed media.

Pioneering Sculptors

Fellows of the Akademi include D. P. Roy Choudhury (1962), one of the first principals of Government College of Art, Kolkata; Sankho Chaudhuri and Prodosh Dasgupta (1982), founders of the sculptors' collective; and Dhanraj Bhagat (1978).

Material & Method

Honoured works span bronze casting, stone and wood carving, welded and folded metal, and assemblage. Himmat Shah, one of the most recent Fellows (2021), is renowned for his intimate terracotta heads and printmaking.

Public Monuments

Many honoured sculptors have shaped India's public art landscape — from statues and memorials to contemporary site-specific installations, carrying the Akademi's mandate of improving standards and refining public taste.

🖨️ Printmaking — The Graphic Arts

Printmaking, or graphic art, was one of the three "creative arts" named in the Akademi's original 1954 constitution. The Akademi championed the medium through the National Exhibition of Art, dedicated print biennales, and by honouring India's foremost graphic artists.

Master Printmakers

Jeram Patel (Fellow 2007) revolutionised Indian printmaking with his bold, dark ink-wash and etching experiments. K. G. Subramanyan (Fellow 1985) worked across painting, printmaking, and glass, while Jyoti Bhatt (Fellow 2021) is celebrated for serigraphy and photography.

Techniques Celebrated

The Akademi recognises mastery across etching, engraving, woodcut, linocut, serigraphy (silkscreen), lithography, and collagraphy — both traditional hand-printing and contemporary digital print media.

Print Biennale India

The Akademi organises the Print Biennale India, bringing international printmaking to Indian audiences and connecting the country's graphic artists with the global contemporary print community.

🏺 Ceramics — Earth, Fire & Form

Ceramics has grown into a celebrated category of the Lalit Kala Akademi's honours, recognising artists who transform earth and fire into sculptural and functional art — from studio pottery to monumental ceramic sculpture.

Studio Pottery & Sculpture

Ceramic artists explore glazes, slips, and firing techniques to create both wheel-thrown vessels and hand-built sculptural forms. The Akademi showcases these works prominently in its National Exhibition of Art.

Indigenous Traditions

Indian ceramic and terracotta traditions — from the terracotta horses of Bankura and the studio-pottery movement to contemporary porcelain — connect rural craft heritage with modern fine-art practice.

Scholarships & Open Studios

Through scholarships and open-studio programmes at its regional centres, the Akademi supports emerging ceramic artists, preserving the medium's future while honouring its masters.

🎯 Selection Process

The selection of National Awards is anchored in the annual National Exhibition of Art, which has been curated and juried with care since 1955. The process blends open submission with rigorous, multi-tier expert evaluation.

1

Call for Entries

The Akademi issues an annual call inviting original works of painting, sculpture, printmaking, ceramics, and other visual media from artists across India.

2

Open Submission

Artists submit their works along with documentation. Submissions must be original and must not have been exhibited in a previous National Exhibition of Art.

3

Screening Juries

Regional and zonal juries of eminent artists, critics, and curators screen entries and forward shortlisted works to the national selection committee.

4

National Selection Committee

A national jury selects the works for display and shortlists the most outstanding exhibits for awards, assessing originality, technical mastery, and artistic vision.

5

General Council Approval

Recommendations are placed before the Akademi's General Council and Executive Board, which function under its constitution, for final approval.

6

Award Ceremony

National Awards — a plaque, shawl, and ₹2 lakh purse — are conferred at an official ceremony, traditionally graced by the President of India or the Vice-President.

Evaluation Focus Areas

  • Originality & Vision: A distinct personal artistic voice and innovative ideas
  • Technical Mastery: Command over medium, material, and craft
  • Cultural Depth: Engagement with India's artistic traditions and contemporary life
  • Aesthetic Excellence: Formal quality, composition, and refinement
  • Contribution: The artist's wider impact on the visual arts in India

🏆 Notable Awardees & Fellows

Since 1955, the Akademi has honoured the giants of modern Indian art through its Fellowship (Ratna) and National Awards. These artists shaped the very idea of Indian modernism.

🖌️

Jamini Roy

Fellow · 1955 · Painting

The first Fellow of the Lalit Kala Akademi, whose folk-inspired, flat-lined paintings of Bengal became a cornerstone of Indian modernism.

🎨

Nandalal Bose

Fellow · 1956 · Painting

The master of Santiniketan, whose works adorned the original handwritten manuscript of the Indian Constitution.

🗿

D. P. Roy Choudhury

Fellow · 1962 · Sculpture

Pioneering sculptor and first principal of Government College of Art, Kolkata, known for his monumental public sculptures.

🖌️

M. F. Husain

Fellow · 1978 · Painting

The most celebrated painter of modern India, whose dynamic, colour-drenched canvases defined the Bombay Progressive movement.

🎨

S. H. Raza

Fellow · 1984 · Painting

A founding Progressive whose signature Bindu paintings fused Indian cosmology with Western abstract painting.

🖨️

K. G. Subramanyan

Fellow · 1985 · Painting & Printmaking

Artist, theorist, and educator whose experiments in painting, printmaking, and glass inspired generations at Santiniketan and Baroda.

🎨

Ram Kumar

Fellow · 2011 · Painting

A lyrical abstractionist whose landscapes of Benares and the Himalayas rank among modern India's most poetic paintings.

🖌️

Arpita Singh

Fellow · 2014 · Painting

One of India's foremost women artists, whose narrative, often subversive paintings chronicle the everyday and the political.

🗿

Himmat Shah

Fellow · 2021 · Sculpture

A sculptor and printmaker celebrated for his contemplative terracotta heads, inked images, and decades-long creative practice.

🖨️

Jyoti Bhatt

Fellow · 2021 · Printmaking

A pioneer of Indian printmaking and photography, honoured for his serigraphs and his visual documentation of Indian folk and tribal culture.

Impact of the Honours

  • Fellows of the Akademi are regarded as national treasures of Indian art
  • National Awards shine a spotlight on the finest works in the annual National Exhibition of Art
  • Honoured artists' works enter the Akademi's permanent collection for the nation
  • The honours connect India's regional art scenes into a single national conversation
  • They inspire emerging artists to pursue excellence in painting, sculpture, printmaking, and ceramics
