Linguistic Classification

The Tani Language Family Tree

Classified under the Trans-Himalayan (Sino-Tibetan) phylum, Tani languages share striking grammatical and lexical continuities.

🌲

Western Tani

Languages: Nyishi, Apatani, Tagin, Hill Miri (Sanga), Damu.

Characterized by glottal stops, retention of initial voiced stops, and strong Western Subansiri connections.

🌊

Pre-Tani / Central Tani

Language: Galo.

Bridges Western and Eastern Tani features; famous for strict patrilineal naming chains linking every Galo individual back to Abotani.

🏞️

Eastern Tani

Languages: Adi (Padam, Minyong, Bokar, Shimong, Pasium), Mising.

Spoken along the Siang valley down to the Brahmaputra riverine plains; features rich epic Ponung chants.

Orthography & Scripts

Writing Systems & Script Innovations

From legacy oral chants to Latin diacritic orthographies and the indigenous Tani Lipi script created by Tony Koyu.

📜

Oral Tradition & Nyibu Agom

Historically without a written alphabet, Tani literature flourished through oral epics (*Nyibu Agom* & *Abe*) memorized by shamanic bards and passed through father-to-son naming chains.

🔤

Standardized Latin Orthographies

Literary bodies like Nyishi Elite Society (NES) and Adi Agom Kébang (AAK) adopted Latin-based systems with diacritics for central unrounded vowels (/ɨ/, /ɯ/).

✍️

Tani Lipi Script (Indigenous)

Developed by scholar Tony Koyu, Tani Lipi is a dedicated indigenous writing system created specifically to represent Tani pitch accents, glottal stops, and vowel length without Latin diacritics.
