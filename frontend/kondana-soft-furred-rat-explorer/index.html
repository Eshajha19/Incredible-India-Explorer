Introduction

The Kondana Soft-furred Rat is a small, nocturnal rodent found only on the Sinhgad plateau near Pune, Maharashtra. Covering roughly one square kilometre, this makes it one of the most range-restricted mammal species anywhere in the world.

First discovered in 1975, the species remains poorly known, in part because its only confirmed population lives on and around the popular Sinhgad Fort, a site now visited by large numbers of tourists and trekkers each year.

Scientific Name & Classification

Millardia kondana Mishra & Dhanda, 1975. Also known as the Kondana rat or large metad.

Distribution & Habitat

🌍 Ultra-Endemic Distribution

Known from a single locality: the Sinhgad plateau in the northern Western Ghats. Its area of occupancy is less than 1 km². Sinhgad is designated an Alliance for Zero Extinction (AZE) site.

🌿 Specialized Habitat

Found in subtropical dry forest and dry lowland grassland. It digs burrows in rocky, grassy terrain and struggles to persist where grassland becomes fragmented or converted to agriculture.

Physical Traits & Behaviour

  • Appearance: Resembles the Soft-furred Field Rat but has larger cranial measurements, six distinct plantar (foot) pads, and proportionally small ears.
  • Nocturnal & Fossorial: Active mainly at night and lives in burrows within grassland and rocky slopes, making field monitoring difficult.
  • Diet: Primarily granivorous (grains, seeds) with opportunistic insect consumption.

Conservation Status

Critically Endangered EDGE of Existence Top 100

Uplisted to Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List in 2008. It is recognized by the Zoological Society of London's (ZSL) EDGE of Existence programme as one of the world's most evolutionarily distinct and globally endangered mammals.

Primary Threats

Habitat Loss & FragmentationHigh
Tourism & Recreational DisturbanceHigh
Overgrazing by LivestockMedium

💡 Interesting Facts

  • Its entire known global range is smaller than many city parks (< 1 km²).
  • Researchers use integrative studies (DNA analysis + morphometrics) to distinguish it from its common look-alike, M. meltada.
  • Conservation teams from the Bombay Natural History Society (BNHS) actively monitor its grassland habitat at Sinhgad.
