Kondana Soft-furred Rat

One of the world's most range-restricted mammals - found nowhere on Earth except a single hilltop near Pune.

Introduction

The Kondana Soft-furred Rat is a small, nocturnal rodent found only on the Sinhgad plateau near Pune, Maharashtra - an area covering roughly one square kilometre. This makes it one of the most range-restricted mammal species anywhere in the world.

First discovered in 1975, the species remains poorly known, in part because its only confirmed population lives on and around the popular Sinhgad Fort, a site now visited by large numbers of tourists and trekkers each year.

Scientific Name

Millardia kondana Mishra & Dhanda, 1975

Also known as the Kondana rat or large metad, the species was first described in 1975 by researchers Mishra and Dhanda based on specimens collected at Sinhgad.

The genus Millardia ("soft-furred rats") contains four species, all but one endemic to the Indian subcontinent: the widespread Soft-furred Field Rat (M. meltada), the Sand-coloured Soft-furred Rat (M. gleadowi), the Kondana Soft-furred Rat (M. kondana), and the Burmese Soft-furred Rat (M. kathleenae) of Myanmar.

Distribution

The Kondana Soft-furred Rat is known from a single locality: the Sinhgad plateau in the northern Western Ghats, near Pune, Maharashtra. Its estimated extent of occurrence and area of occupancy are both less than one square kilometre, making it one of the most narrowly distributed mammals ever recorded.

Because of this, Sinhgad has been designated an Alliance for Zero Extinction (AZE) site - a global classification reserved for locations that are the last remaining home of one or more highly threatened species.

Habitat

  • Found in subtropical/tropical dry forest, dry lowland grassland, and adjoining urban or semi-urban areas around Sinhgad Fort.
  • Prefers grasslands with shrub cover, which provide shelter from predators and from harsh weather.
  • Digs burrows within the fort's grassy, rocky terrain, requiring fairly large, continuous patches of grassland habitat to survive.
  • Research has shown the species struggles to persist where grassland habitat becomes too fragmented or converted to agriculture, unlike many other small mammals that tolerate such disturbance.

Physical Characteristics

  • A small rodent closely resembling the widespread Soft-furred Field Rat (Millardia meltada), which has historically made field identification difficult.
  • Distinguished from related Millardia species by larger cranial and external body measurements.
  • Has six distinct plantar (foot) pads, along with proportionally small ears - subtle features used by researchers to separate it from its close relatives.
  • Soft, dense fur typical of the genus, suited to a burrowing lifestyle.

Diet

As with other soft-furred rats in the genus Millardia, the Kondana Soft-furred Rat is believed to feed on a mix of grains, seeds, and other plant material available in its grassland habitat, supplemented opportunistically with insects - a typical diet pattern for ground-dwelling murid rodents.

Behaviour

  • Nocturnal: Active mainly at night, which along with its burrowing habits makes it difficult to study and monitor.
  • Fossorial: Digs and lives in burrows within the grassland and rocky slopes of the Sinhgad plateau.
  • Habitat Specialist: Field studies using live-trapping found that while the species can tolerate some habitat disturbance, it disappears from areas where grassland becomes highly fragmented or is converted to farmland - unlike many generalist small mammals in the same landscape.

Conservation Status

The Kondana Soft-furred Rat was first assessed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List in 1996, and was uplisted to Critically Endangered in 2008 following further research into its extremely restricted range.

  • Sinhgad, its only known locality, is recognised as an Alliance for Zero Extinction (AZE) site.
  • The species has also been included among the Zoological Society of London's (ZSL) EDGE of Existence list of the world's top 100 most evolutionarily distinct and globally endangered mammals.

Threats

  • Habitat Loss: Conversion of surrounding grassland to agriculture and settlements, driven by high human population density around Pune.
  • Overgrazing: Livestock grazing degrades the grassland vegetation the species depends on for shelter and food.
  • Tourism & Recreational Disturbance: Sinhgad Fort is a highly popular trekking and tourism destination, and increasing visitor activity disturbs the rat's already tiny habitat.
  • Extremely Restricted Range: With its entire known population confined to under one square kilometre, any single disturbance event carries an outsized risk to the species' survival.
  • Taxonomic Uncertainty: Its close resemblance to the common Soft-furred Field Rat has historically complicated accurate population monitoring and conservation assessment.

Interesting Facts

  • Its entire known global range covers less than one square kilometre - smaller than many city parks.
  • It lives in and around the walls and grassy slopes of Sinhgad Fort, a historic hilltop fortress and popular tourist site near Pune.
  • It is included on the Zoological Society of London's EDGE of Existence list, which highlights mammals with few close evolutionary relatives, making their loss especially significant for the tree of life.
  • Researchers have used integrative studies - combining body measurements, DNA analysis, and habitat modelling - to confirm it is a genuinely distinct species from its common look-alike relative.
  • Conservation researchers, including teams from the Bombay Natural History Society, continue to study how grassland fragmentation affects its survival at Sinhgad.
