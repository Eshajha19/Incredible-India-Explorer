Explore Ujjain

Journey into the Ancient City of Astronomy, Trade, and Spirituality located in Madhya Pradesh.

Geographic & Political Importance

Situated on the eastern bank of the Kshipra River, Ujjain (ancient Ujjayini) was the capital of the Avanti kingdom, one of the sixteen Mahajanapadas. It emerged as a political hub under Ashoka, who served as its viceroy before becoming the Mauryan Emperor.

Astronomy & Mathematics

Ujjain is the Greenwich of India. The Surya Siddhanta, an ancient astronomical treatise, defined Ujjain's meridian as the prime meridian (zero longitude) for Indian calendars. Legendary astronomers and mathematicians like Brahmagupta and Bhaskaracharya worked here.

Commercial Role & Trade Routes

Ujjain was strategically located on the Dakshinapatha (the Southern Route) connecting northern India to the Deccan and western ports like Bharuch. This made it a bustling center for commercial trade in textiles, gems, and spices.

Religious Significance & Monuments

Known for the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga, Ujjain hosts the Kumbh Mela every 12 years. The city is also rich in archaeological evidence from the Mauryan and Shunga periods, including the Vedh Shala (Jantar Mantar) observatory built later by Jai Singh II.
