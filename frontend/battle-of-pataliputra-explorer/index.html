⚔️ Pataliputra, Magadha · c. 322 BCE

Battle of Pataliputra Explorer

The Battle of Pataliputra (c. 322 BCE) marked the overthrow of the formidable Nanda Empire by Chandragupta Maurya and his strategist Chanakya (Kautilya). By combining guerrilla strikes, diplomatic alliances with Himalayan kingdoms, and a multi-pronged siege of Magadha’s capital city, Chandragupta founded the Mauryan Empire—India’s first Pan-Subcontinental empire.

Mauryan Conquest

The Siege of Magadha’s Wealthy Capital

Under Emperor Dhana Nanda, Magadha possessed immense wealth and a staggering standing army of 200,000 infantry, 80,000 cavalry, 8,000 war chariots, and 6,000 war elephants. However, oppressive taxation and tyranny alienating the populace provided Chanakya and Chandragupta the opportunity to strike.

After initial setbacks attempting direct assaults on the core of Magadha, Chanakya devised a perimeter strategy: conquering outer provinces first, securing alliances with King Parvataka, and cutting off Pataliputra’s supply lines before encircling the city walls.

🏛️ Historical Snapshot

  • Date: c. 322 BCE
  • Location: Pataliputra (Modern Patna, Bihar)
  • Belligerents: Mauryan Rebels & Himalayan Allies vs Nanda Dynasty
  • Mauryan Commanders: Chandragupta Maurya, Chanakya (Kautilya), King Parvataka
  • Nanda Commanders: Dhana Nanda, Prime Minister Rakshasa, Commander Bhadrasala
  • Outcome: Fall of Pataliputra; establishment of Mauryan Empire.
Military Leaders

Commanders & Opposing Forces

Meet the visionary strategists and commanders involved in the siege of Pataliputra.

Mauryan Confederacy

Mauryan & Himalayan Forces

Leaders: Chandragupta Maurya & Chanakya
  • Chandragupta Maurya: Charismatic military genius and founder of the empire.
  • Chanakya (Kautilya): Master strategist, author of Arthashastra, and mastermind of espionage.
  • King Parvataka: Himalayan ruler who supplied elite mountain cavalry and mercenary foot soldiers.
  • Tactical Approach: Outer ring subjugation, covert subversion inside Pataliputra, and siege warfare.
Nanda Dynasty

Imperial Nanda Army

Leaders: Dhana Nanda & Bhadrasala
  • Dhana Nanda: Last ruler of the Nanda Dynasty, notorious for vast treasuries and heavy taxes.
  • General Bhadrasala: Chief military commander who directed the defense of Pataliputra.
  • Minister Rakshasa: Fiercely loyal prime minister known for political intrigue and administrative skill.
  • Defensive Strength: Massive fortification moats fed by the Ganges and Son rivers.
Key Events

Campaign Timeline

Chronological milestones in the overthrow of the Nanda Dynasty.

Phase 1: c. 324 BCE

Pact of Taxila

Chanakya and Chandragupta forge an alliance with King Parvataka to raise a unified liberation force.

Phase 2: c. 323 BCE

Border Campaign

Chandragupta captures border garrisons of Magadha, gradually isolating the capital city of Pataliputra.

Phase 3: c. 322 BCE

Siege of Pataliputra

Mauryan coalition besieges Pataliputra while Chanakya orchestrates internal rebellions among citizens.

Phase 4: c. 322 BCE

Capitulation & Coronation

Dhana Nanda surrenders. Chandragupta ascends the throne of Magadha, marking the birth of the Mauryan Empire.

Legacy

Strategic Significance & Outcome

The dramatic shift in subcontinental geopolitics following the battle.

👑 Rise of Mauryan Hegemony

Chandragupta consolidated rule over Magadha, creating the foundation for expansion across modern India, Pakistan, and Afghanistan.

📖 Practical Statecraft

The siege demonstrated principles later codified in the Arthashastra: spy networks, economic warfare, and strategic alliances.

🏛️ Capital of Empire

Pataliputra grew into one of the largest and most prosperous cities of the ancient world, as described by Megasthenes.
