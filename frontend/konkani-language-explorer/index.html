🌳 Language family

Where Konkani sits in the Indo-Aryan family

Konkani belongs to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European family — a close cousin of Marathi, shaped by centuries of contact with Kannada, Malayalam, Portuguese and Arabic.

🗺️ Where it's spoken

Along the Konkan coast — and beyond

Konkani speakers stretch in a narrow band down India's western coastline, plus diaspora communities further afield.

Goa

Konkani's official home — the state's official language since 1987, written mainly in Devanagari, with Roman script also in wide everyday use.

Coastal Karnataka

Mangalore, Udupi and Karwar (the Canara coast) — home to large Konkani-speaking communities, often using the Kannada script.

Kerala

Communities around Kasaragod and Kochi, where Konkani is sometimes written in the Malayalam script.

Maharashtra

The Konkan coastal belt (Sindhudurg, Ratnagiri) north of Goa; Konkani Muslim communities here have historically used the Perso-Arabic script.

📖 Literary & cultural heritage

A living, layered literary tradition

~2nd century CE

Earliest known inscription

A rock inscription at Aravalem, Goa, in the now-extinct Goykanadi script is among the earliest traces of written Konkani.

15th century

Krishnadas Shama

Born in Quelossim, Goa, he is regarded as the first writer in Konkani literary history.

16th century onward

Roman Konkani tradition

Romi Konkani becomes the oldest continuously preserved literary tradition in the language, still used by an estimated half a million people today.

1987

Official language of Goa

Devanagari is declared Konkani's official script under Goa's Official Language Act.

1992

Eighth Schedule recognition

Konkani is added to the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution, becoming one of India's official languages.

Today

Tiatr and the Sahitya Akademi

Konkani literature is eligible for the Sahitya Akademi Award, while Tiatr — Goa's lively musical theatre tradition — keeps the spoken language on stage.
