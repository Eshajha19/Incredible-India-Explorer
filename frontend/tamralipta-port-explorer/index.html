Tamralipta Ancient Port Explorer

Step onto the ancient estuarine shores of Tamralipta (modern Tamluk in West Bengal). Discover eastern India’s principal maritime gateway that linked the Mauryan and Gupta Empires with Sri Lanka, Suvarnabhumi, and China.

Gateway of Bengal & Magadha

The Ancient Maritime Capital of Bengal

Tamralipta (also known as Tamluk, Tamralipti, or Tamalites in Ptolemy's Geographia) was located near the mouth of the Rupnarayan River in Purba Medinipur district, West Bengal.

Flourishing from the 6th century BCE, Tamralipta served as the main sea outlet for the inland Magadhan dynasties including the Maurya, Sunga, Gupta, and Pala Empires. It was mentioned in epic literature like the Mahabharata, Kathasaritsagara, and Jataka tales as a wealthy metropolis bustling with merchants, ships, and monastical institutions.

🏺 Tamralipta At a Glance

  • Location: Tamluk, Purba Medinipur, West Bengal (Rupnarayan river).
  • Dynasties: Maurya, Sunga, Gupta, and Pala Dynasties.
  • Literary Mentions: Mahabharata, Ptolemy's Geographia, Kathasaritsagara.
  • Famous Pilgrims: Faxian (410 CE), Xuanzang (639 CE), Yijing (673 CE).
Milestones through Antiquity

Location & Historical Timeline

Tracing Tamralipta's journey across twelve centuries of oceanic commerce and Buddhist pilgrimage.

c. 6th Century BCE

Urban Emergence & Early Riverine Trade

Tamralipta establishes itself as the primary river-sea port of Bengal, trading terracotta, copper, and textiles.

3rd Century BCE

Imperial Mauryan Gateway

Emperor Ashoka sends his son Prince Mahendra and daughter Princess Sanghamitra from Tamralipta to Sri Lanka with a sacred Bodhi tree sapling.

410 CE

Faxian's 2-Year Stay & Voyage

Chinese pilgrim Faxian resides at Tamralipta writing Buddhist sutras before embarking on a merchant vessel for Sri Lanka.

639 CE

Xuanzang's Record of Monasteries

Xuanzang visits Tamralipta, describing 10 flourishing Buddhist monasteries, 1,000 monks, and sea vessels laden with rare merchandise.

c. 8th - 9th Century CE

River Siltation & Shift to Satgaon

Heavy siltation of the Rupnarayan river channel restricts ocean ship access, gradually shifting Bengal trade to Satgaon and Chittagong.

Bay of Bengal Network

Trade Significance & Maritime Silk Route

Tamralipta connected northern and eastern India with Southeast Asian kingdoms and China.

🌏

Maritime Silk Route Terminus

Served as the vital eastern junction of the Silk Route by sea, receiving Chinese silks and porcelain while exporting Indian textiles, gems, and spices.

🚢

Suvarnabhumi Trade Expeditions

Sea captains sailed directly across the Bay of Bengal to Suvarnabhumi (Golden Land of Java, Sumatra, and Malaya) carrying Indian goods and cultural traditions.

🧵

Bengal Muslin & Silk Exports

Renowned worldwide for fine Gangetic muslin fabrics, indigo dyes, silk garments, and copper artifacts crafted in Bengal workshops.

🏺

Sunga-Gupta Terracotta Art

Produced exquisite molded terracotta plaques, figurines, and ritual pottery exported throughout ancient South and Southeast Asia.

☸️ Buddhist Pilgrimage Voyages

  • Emperor Ashoka's Mission: Prince Mahendra and Sanghamitra departed from Tamralipta to propagate Buddhism in Sri Lanka.
  • Faxian's Account (410 CE): Recorded 24 monasteries and spent two years copying sacred sutras and painting holy images.
  • Xuanzang's Description (639 CE): Described Tamralipta as a thriving coastal city where sea trade coexisted with Buddhist learning.
  • Yijing's Stay (673 CE): Chinese scholar Yijing landed at Tamralipta to study Sanskrit before proceeding to Nalanda University.
Spiritual & Scholarly Center

Religious & Cultural Importance

Beyond its economic might, Tamralipta was a world-famous center of Buddhist learning, art, and monastic scholarship. It functioned as the primary departure point for Buddhist monks propagating the Dhamma across Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, and East Asia.
