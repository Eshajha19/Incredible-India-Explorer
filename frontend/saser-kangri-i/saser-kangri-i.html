Saser Kangri I Explorer

Rising to 7,672 metres in the remote Saser Muztagh subrange of the Karakoram in Ladakh, Saser Kangri I is the highest peak of the Saser Muztagh and one of the most formidable summits in the Saser Glacier region. Its massive ice-clad flanks guard the western approaches to the Saser Glacier, making it a peak of immense geographical and strategic significance.

Geography, Glaciers & Significance

The Guardian of Saser Glacier

Saser Kangri I stands in the Saser Muztagh, a major subrange of the eastern Karakoram in Ladakh. It is the highest peak of the Saser Muztagh and overlooks the extensive Saser Glacier system in the eastern Karakoram. The peak forms the western wall of the Saser Glacier, the world's longest non-polar glacier at 76 km, and it lies in the remote eastern Karakoram close to the upper Nubra Valley and has long been an important landmark for high-altitude expeditions. — making it one of the most geopolitically sensitive summits on Earth.

The glaciers surrounding Saser Kangri I feed the Nubra River basin, which ultimately joins the Shyok and Indus river systems. The mountain dominates the Saser Muztagh and is one of India's highest and most prominent Himalayan summits.

The first recorded ascent was achieved in 1962 by an Indo-Japanese expedition, a landmark achievement in high-altitude mountaineering. Because of its extreme remoteness, harsh weather, and technical climbing routes, expeditions to Saser Kangri I remain rare.

Technical Specifications
