Aga Khan Palace Explorer

Where the Father of the Nation was imprisoned and the Mother of the Nation breathed her last.

In the heart of Pune stands a majestic palace that became the prison of Mahatma Gandhi, his wife Kasturba Gandhi, and other freedom fighters during the Quit India Movement. Built in 1892 by Sultan Aga Khan III, this Italianate architectural gem witnessed the final days of Kasturba Gandhi and remains a powerful symbol of India's struggle for independence.

Palace History

A Palace Born of Compassion

From a charitable gesture to a national monument - the story of Aga Khan Palace.

Aga Khan Palace was built in 1892 by Sultan Muhammad Shah Aga Khan III, the spiritual head of the Nizari Ismaili Muslims. The palace was constructed with a noble purpose - to provide employment and relief to the people of Pune who were severely affected by the famine of 1890-1892.

The palace spans 19 acres and took five years to complete. It was designed by British architect Claude Batley in the Italianate architectural style, featuring elegant arches, spacious halls, and beautiful gardens. The construction cost approximately ₹12 lakhs at the time - a princely sum that reflected the Aga Khan's commitment to humanitarian causes.

In 1969, the palace was donated to the Government of India by Aga Khan IV as a tribute to Gandhi and his ideals. Today, it stands as a national monument and houses a museum dedicated to the freedom struggle, preserving the memory of those who sacrificed for India's independence.

Architectural Heritage

Italianate Grandeur in Indian Soil

A masterpiece of Victorian-Italianate architecture adapted for the Indian climate.

🏛️

Italianate Style

The palace follows the Italianate architectural style popular in Victorian England, characterized by low-pitched roofs, wide eaves, and elegant arches. The design was adapted for the Indian climate with high ceilings and large ventilation openings.

🌿

Garden Layout

The 19-acre complex features beautifully landscaped gardens with manicured lawns, flower beds, and tree-lined pathways. The gardens were designed to provide a serene environment and remain a key feature of the palace.

🏠

Spacious Halls

The palace contains several large halls with high ceilings and polished floors. These halls were used for various purposes during Gandhi's imprisonment, including prayer meetings, spinning sessions, and political discussions.

🔲

Structural Elements

Built with stone and brick, the palace features semi-circular arches, Corinthian columns, and decorative cornices. The architecture blends European elegance with Indian practicality, creating a unique heritage structure.

Key Architectural Features

  • Five Suites: The palace originally had five residential suites, each with its own bathroom and dressing room.
  • Italian Marble: The floors and certain decorative elements use imported Italian marble, showcasing the luxury of the era.
  • Ventilation System: Designed with numerous windows and high ceilings for natural air circulation in Pune's climate.
  • Decorative Elements: Elaborate cornices, moldings, and plasterwork adorn the interiors, reflecting Victorian aesthetic sensibilities.
Freedom Movement

Palace of Prisoners

How a royal residence became the most important prison during the Quit India Movement.

On 9 August 1942, Mahatma Gandhi launched the Quit India Movement at the All-India Congress Committee session in Bombay. The British response was swift and brutal - Gandhi and other top leaders were arrested within days.

The British chose Aga Khan Palace as the detention center for Gandhi and his associates. The palace's isolation, spacious grounds, and secure location made it ideal for holding high-profile prisoners while maintaining some degree of comfort.

From August 1942 to May 1944, the palace housed:

  • Mahatma Gandhi and Kasturba Gandhi
  • Their secretary Mahadev Desai
  • Gandhi's personal physician Dr. Sushila Nayar
  • Other freedom fighters and Congress leaders

During this imprisonment, Gandhi continued his political activities through letters, maintained his daily routine of prayer and spinning, and transformed the palace into a center of resistance despite being under house arrest.

Quit India Movement Context

The Quit India Movement (Bharat Chodo Andolan) was the final major nationalist campaign launched by Gandhi during World War II. It demanded an end to British rule immediately, marking a decisive shift towards complete independence.

Gandhi Association

The Father and Mother of the Nation

The personal tragedies and triumphs that marked Gandhi's stay at Aga Khan Palace.

Mahatma Gandhi's Imprisonment

Gandhi spent 21 months at Aga Khan Palace, the longest period of imprisonment in his life. Despite being under house arrest, he maintained his daily routine:

  • Morning Prayers: Held daily at 4:30 AM with fellow prisoners
  • Spinning: Continued his practice of spinning khadi daily
  • Writing: Authored important letters and documents including the "Letter to Every Indian"
  • Fasting: Undertook several fasts as political protest

It was here that Gandhi received the news of his son Devdas's death and dealt with the declining health of his wife Kasturba.

Kasturba Gandhi's Final Days

Kasturba Gandhi (1869-1944), Gandhi's wife of 62 years, was also imprisoned at the palace. Her health deteriorated during their imprisonment, and she breathed her last on 22 February 1944 at the age of 74.

Kasturba had been Gandhi's pillar of support throughout the freedom struggle. Her death devastated Gandhi, who famously said, "I cannot imagine life without Ba." Her samadhi (memorial) stands in the palace gardens, adjacent to Gandhi's own memorial.

"She was the embodiment of selfless service and sacrifice for the nation."
— Mahatma Gandhi on Kasturba

Other Historical Figures Associated

Mahadev Desai

Gandhi's personal secretary and lifelong companion who died at the palace on 15 August 1942, just days after their imprisonment began. His death was a severe blow to Gandhi.

Dr. Sushila Nayar

Gandhi's personal physician who was also imprisoned at the palace. She attended to Kasturba during her final illness and documented their prison life.

Sarojini Naidu

The Nightingale of India was also imprisoned at various times during the Quit India Movement and visited the palace during Gandhi's detention.

Mirabehn (Madeleine Slade)

A British disciple of Gandhi who devoted her life to the Indian freedom movement. She spent significant time at the palace during Gandhi's imprisonment.

Memorial Significance

Sacred Ground of the Freedom Struggle

The samadhis and memorials that make Aga Khan Palace a pilgrimage site for freedom lovers.

The Samadhis

The palace grounds house two important samadhis (memorials) that are the heart of the complex:

Kasturba Gandhi's Samadhi

Kasturba Gandhi's memorial is marked by a simple yet elegant structure where her mortal remains were interred. The samadhi is surrounded by well-maintained gardens and is a place of quiet contemplation. Visitors pay their respects to the woman who stood by Gandhi through every trial.

Mahadev Desai's Samadhi

Located near Kasturba's memorial is the samadhi of Mahadev Desai, Gandhi's faithful secretary who died just days after their imprisonment began. His memorial stands as a tribute to unsung heroes of the freedom struggle.

Museum Collections

The palace houses a museum that preserves and displays:

  • Personal Belongings: Gandhi's charkha (spinning wheel), eating bowl, and personal items
  • Photographs: Rare photographs of Gandhi, Kasturba, and other freedom fighters
  • Letters: Original correspondence from Gandhi's prison period
  • Newspapers: Contemporary newspapers covering the Quit India Movement
  • Artifacts: Items from the freedom struggle era

The museum serves as an educational resource, helping visitors understand the sacrifices made for India's independence.

National Importance

Aga Khan Palace was declared a national monument in 1969 when it was donated to the Indian government by Aga Khan IV. It is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) and serves as:

  • A tourist destination for those interested in Indian history
  • An educational center for students learning about the freedom struggle
  • A memorial site where people pay respects to Gandhi and Kasturba
  • A venue for cultural events related to Gandhi's philosophy
  • A symbol of national unity and the values of the freedom movement

Construction Period

Sultan Aga Khan III commissions the palace to provide employment during the famine. Construction takes five years at a cost of ₹12 lakhs.

1892 🏛️

Palace Completion

Aga Khan Palace is completed and becomes a symbol of charitable architecture. The palace serves as a residence for the Aga Khan during his visits to Pune.

Quit India Movement Launched

Gandhi launches the Quit India Movement in Bombay. The British respond with mass arrests of Congress leaders.

Aug 1942 ⛓️

Gandhi Imprisoned at Palace

Gandhi, Kasturba, Mahadev Desai, and others are imprisoned at Aga Khan Palace. The palace becomes their prison for the next 21 months.

Mahadev Desai's Death

Gandhi's secretary Mahadev Desai dies of a heart attack at the palace, just days after their imprisonment begins. A devastating loss for Gandhi.

Kasturba Gandhi breathes her last at the palace after a prolonged illness. Her samadhi is created in the palace grounds.

Due to failing health, Gandhi is released from imprisonment. He leaves the palace after 21 months of detention.

1969 🏛️

Present 🏛️

The palace serves as a museum and heritage site, attracting thousands of visitors who come to pay homage to the freedom struggle heroes.

Interesting Facts

Fascinating Details About Aga Khan Palace

Lesser-known facts that make this palace unique in India's heritage landscape.

01

Famine Relief Project

The palace was built specifically to provide employment during the 1890-1892 famine. Over 1,000 workers found work and wages through its construction.

02

Gandhi's Longest Imprisonment

Gandhi's 21-month stay at Aga Khan Palace was the longest period of continuous imprisonment in his entire life.

03

Letter to Every Indian

While imprisoned here, Gandhi wrote his famous "Letter to Every Indian" appealing for unity and non-violent resistance.

04

Personal Tragedies

Gandhi suffered two major personal losses here - the death of his secretary Mahadev Desai and his wife Kasturba Gandhi.

05

Italian Marble Floors

The palace features imported Italian marble flooring, showcasing the luxury and elegance of the era when it was built.

06

Architectural Innovation

The palace was one of the first buildings in Pune to use modern construction techniques combined with traditional Indian elements.

07

Museum Since 1980

The palace museum was formally established in 1980 and houses rare photographs, artifacts, and personal belongings of Gandhi.

08

Film Location

The palace has been featured in several films and documentaries about Gandhi and the Indian freedom struggle due to its historical significance.
