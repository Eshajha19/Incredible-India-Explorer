Bangaram Island

An uninhabited coral atoll in the Lakshadweep archipelago, ringed by a shallow turquoise lagoon and a living reef — one of India's most pristine escapes for divers, snorkellers and eco-travellers.

120 acres Total Land Area
125 sq km Shared Lagoon Area
~8 km From Agatti Island
20–30 m Lagoon Visibility

🪸 Coral Atoll

Bangaram is the largest island in a small coral atoll that also includes Thinnakara, Parali I, Parali II, Parali III and Kalpitty, all sharing a single reef system. The atoll sits atop the same submerged mountain range as the Chagos–Maldives–Lakshadweep ridge, and is linked to the neighbouring Agatti reef by a shallow submarine ridge.

The island itself covers roughly 120 acres of coconut groves and scrub, with a long brackish pond at its centre fringed by screw pine and palm trees. Unlike most of Lakshadweep, Bangaram has no permanent civilian population — its only residents are resort staff and visiting travellers.

  • Part of a six-island atoll sharing one coral reef
  • Connected to Agatti by a shallow submarine ridge
  • A central brackish pond bordered by screw pine and coconut palms

🌊 Lagoon

A shallow, glassy lagoon encloses Bangaram and its neighbouring islets, held in place by the surrounding coral reef. The combined lagoon of the atoll spreads across roughly 125 square kilometres, making it one of the largest in Lakshadweep.

Underwater visibility inside the lagoon can reach 20 to 30 metres on a calm day, letting snorkellers see coral gardens directly from the shoreline without a boat. Glass-bottomed boat rides and lagoon cruises are a popular way to take in the scenery without getting wet.

  • Roughly 125 sq km of shared lagoon across the atoll
  • Exceptional 20–30 metre visibility on good days
  • Glass-bottom boat tours and lagoon cruises available

🐠 Marine Biodiversity

The reefs around Bangaram host a striking range of coral fish, including angelfish, clownfish, butterflyfish, surgeonfish and groupers, alongside black coral formations. Larger marine life such as reef sharks, manta rays, stingrays, moray eels and sea turtles are regularly sighted by divers and snorkellers.

Coral cover in the region was affected by a major bleaching event linked to the 1998 El Niño, and recovery has been gradual due to shallow, sun-warmed waters over the atoll's submerged ridge. Small patches of healthy coral persist across most dive sites, and the island community follows a strict hands-off policy toward coral and shells to support the reef's slow recovery.

  • Angelfish, clownfish, butterflyfish, surgeonfish and groupers
  • Reef sharks, manta rays, stingrays, moray eels and sea turtles
  • Ongoing coral recovery after historic bleaching events

🤿 Diving

Bangaram is home to one of Lakshadweep's best-known dive centres, offering both introductory dives for beginners and full certification courses for experienced divers, with all equipment provided on-site. Dive sites around the atoll include sheer reef walls, coral gardens and shallow inter-atoll bridges, shaped by coral growth keeping pace with rising sea levels over thousands of years.

Warm water temperatures around 30°C make for comfortable year-round diving conditions, with the best visibility and calmest seas typically between October and May.

  • Dive centre offering intro dives and certification courses
  • Reef walls, coral gardens and inter-atoll bridges to explore
  • Best diving season: October to May

🌱 Eco-tourism

Tourism on Bangaram is deliberately small in scale — the island's resort consists of a limited number of beach-front cottages under the palms, with no large lobby, conference facility or crowds. Visitors need a Lakshadweep entry permit in advance, and the island follows plastic-free practices with an emphasis on coral-safe snorkelling and minimal disturbance to marine life.

Because the island is uninhabited outside the resort, its ecology remains largely undisturbed, and conservation-minded practices — from reef-safe sunscreen guidance to leaving coral and shells untouched — are central to how the island is presented to visitors.

  • Small-scale, low-impact resort stays rather than mass tourism
  • Advance Lakshadweep permit required for all visitors
  • Plastic-free island with coral-safe snorkelling guidelines

🏖️ Beaches

Bangaram's shoreline is lined with powdery white sand and swaying coconut palms, offering a quiet contrast to its energetic reef just offshore. Beach-based activities include swimming, beach games, deep-sea fishing trips and simple walks along the water's edge.

After dark, bioluminescent plankton occasionally washes ashore, giving the waterline a faint bluish glow — one of the more magical, low-key sights the island is known for among overnight visitors.

  • Soft white-sand beaches fringed by coconut palms
  • Swimming, beach games and deep-sea fishing
  • Occasional bioluminescent plankton glow at night
