🇮🇳 From 1857 to 1947 · Women at Every Front

Women & the Freedom Struggle

Across every phase of India's freedom movement, women were not bystanders — they were combatants, organisers, spies, poets, and symbols of defiance. From Rani Lakshmibai on the battlefield to the Rani of Jhansi Regiment marching into Burma, from sarli-leaf protests in the bazaars to secret radio transmissions in Delhi bedrooms, women wove courage into the fabric of the nation.

Forgotten Warriors, Untold Stories

The Women Who Shaped India's Freedom

Women's participation in the freedom struggle spans the full arc of resistance — from the battlefield heroism of Rani Lakshmibai during the Revolt of 1857, to the intellectual leadership of Sarojini Naidu and Annie Besant during the Swadeshi era, the grassroots organisation of Kasturba Gandhi and Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay during Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience, and the revolutionary fire of Aruna Asaf Ali and Captain Lakshmi Sahgal during Quit India and the INA.

From the tribal uprising led by Matangini Hazra in Bengal to the Karrani Kingdom's resistance in the east, from the Rani of Jhansi Regiment marching through Burma to Usha Mehta's clandestine radio in Delhi — women stitched the flag of freedom with threads of sacrifice, courage, and unwavering resolve.

🧭 At a Glance

  • Timeline span: 1857 Revolt to 1947 Independence — 90 years of continuous resistance.
  • Geographic spread: Bengal, Awadh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Bengal, Assam, and the Northeast.
  • Movement types: Armed rebellion, boycott, civil disobedience, revolutionary sabotage, and cultural resistance.
  • Key roles: Military leaders, political organisers, underground couriers, poets, and symbols of defiance.

By Movement

Women in Every Phase of Resistance

From the battlefield to the streets, women's roles evolved but never wavered. Explore how women participated across each major movement.

⚔️

Revolt of 1857

1857 · Jhansi, Kanpur, Lucknow

Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi led cavalry into battle. Begum Hazrat Mahal organised resistance in Awadh. In Delhi, women fought alongside men in the Red Fort. These queen-ruled kingdoms became symbols of armed female defiance.

🏴

Swadeshi Movement (1905)

1905–1908 · Bengal, Madras

Sarojini Naidu delivered her first political speech in Barisal. Annie Besant championed boycott at the Madras session. University women organised spinning sessions and promoted khadi. The movement created the first generation of politically conscious women leaders.

✊

Non-Cooperation (1920–1922)

1920–1922 · Nationwide

Kasturba Gandhi led protests in Ahmedabad and Delhi. Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay organised harijan women. Women picketed liquor shops and foreign cloth stores. Over 12,000 women were jailed in 1921 alone.

🏖️

Civil Disobedience (1930)

1930–1934 · Dandi, across India

Women led the Salt March extensions — Sarojini Naidu at the forefront in Gujarat. Thousands joined picketing and protests. Kasturba Gandhi was imprisoned multiple times. In South India, women formed the Madras Mahila Congress Committee.

📢

Quit India Movement (1942)

1942 · Nationwide

Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the Congress flag at the Gowalia Tank Maidan. Usha Mehta ran the Congress Radio underground. Students and housewives took to streets. Over 10,000 women were imprisoned — the largest detention of women in the freedom struggle.

🪖

INA & Rani of Jhansi Regiment

1941–1945 · Singapore, Burma

Captain Lakshmi Sahgal formed the all-women INA regiment that marched into Burma. Over 1,500 women volunteered. They carried weapons, treated the wounded, and became symbols of a new kind of woman warrior — equal to any man on the battlefield.

Titans of Courage

Major Women Leaders

From queens who ruled kingdoms to students who stormed streets — explore the remarkable women whose courage defined an era.

Rani Lakshmibai

Queen of Jhansi · 1828–1858

The warrior queen who became the icon of Indian resistance. Armed and mounted, she led her soldiers into battle during the 1857 Revolt, refusing to surrender her kingdom to the British.

Begum Hazrat Mahal

Queen of Awadh · 1823–1889

Wife of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, she led the resistance in Awadh after the 1857 Revolt, organised troops, raised funds, and continued the fight even after her husband's death.

Sarojini Naidu

Poet & Politician · 1877–1949

The Nightingale of India, a poet laureate who became a formidable political leader. She led the Salt March in Gujarat and was the first woman President of the Indian National Congress.

Aruna Asaf Ali

Freedom Fighter · 1905–1996

Hoisted the Congress flag during Quit India at Gowalia Tank Maidan. A graduate of Delhi University, she went underground, ran secret radio broadcasts, and became a symbol of defiant youth.

Captain Lakshmi Sahgal

INA Officer · 1927–2012

Doctor and officer in the Indian National Army. She formed and commanded the all-women Rani of Jhansi Regiment that marched into Burma, proving women could fight as equals on the battlefield.

Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay

Social Reformer · 1869–1972

A pioneer of the freedom struggle, social reformer, and champion of women's education. She led non-cooperation picketing and later became a founder of the All India Women's Conference.

Milestones

Timeline of Women's Resistance

From the warrior queens of 1857 to the underground radio operators of 1942 — a chronology of women whose courage changed history.

1857

The Warrior Queens Rise

Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, Begum Hazrat Mahal of Awadh, and other queen-ruled kingdoms lead the Revolt of 1857. Women fight alongside men in Delhi's Red Fort.

1905

Swadeshi Sparks

The anti-Partition protests see Sarojini Naidu's political debut in Barisal. Annie Besant champions the boycott in Madras. University women organise spinning and khadi promotion.

1921

Non-Cooperation Arrests

Kasturba Gandhi leads protests in Ahmedabad. Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay organises women's picketing. Over 12,000 women are jailed for defying British laws.

1930

The Salt March Women

Women lead Dandi March extensions. Sarojini Naidu commands the protest at the salt pans. Thousands of women are jailed for making salt in defiance of the law.

1942

Quit India's Fearless Women

Aruna Asaf Ali hoists the Congress flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan. Usha Mehta runs the underground Congress Radio. Students and housewives flood the streets.

1945

The Rani of Jhansi Regiment

Captain Lakshmi Sahgal leads the all-women INA regiment into Burma. Over 1,500 women serve as soldiers, medics, and instructors — rewriting the code of women's courage.
