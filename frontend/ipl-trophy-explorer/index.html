01 · Origins

How the league began

The Indian Premier League was launched by the BCCI in 2008 as a franchise Twenty20 tournament, reshaping how the sport is played, sold and watched.

2007

The spark

Fresh off India's win at the inaugural T20 World Cup, the BCCI moved to build a city-based T20 league with an open player auction — a first for cricket.

2008

Season one

Eight franchises, bought at auction for a combined $723.59 million, took the field. Shane Warne's Rajasthan Royals won the very first title.

2011–13

Expansion & churn

The league grew to ten teams, then contracted again as Deccan Chargers, Kochi Tuskers Kerala and Pune Warriors India all folded within a few seasons.

2022–now

Ten-team era

Gujarat Titans and Lucknow Super Giants joined in 2022, restoring a ten-team league now valued among the world's richest sporting properties.

The trophy itself has barely changed since it was redesigned in 2011: a golden globe — representing cricket's global reach — balanced on three silver columns styled as stumps and bails, standing for batting, bowling and fielding. The seam running across the globe is set at a tilt, echoing the axial tilt of the Earth.

Around its base runs a line of Sanskrit: "Yatra Pratibha Avsara Prapnotihi" — "where talent meets opportunity." The winning captain lifts the original each final; a replica engraved with that year's result is what the franchise actually keeps, since the master trophy returns to the BCCI.

Reported dimensions vary by source — most accounts put it around 60 cm tall and roughly 6–11 kg of sterling silver with gold plating, since the BCCI has never published exact official specifications.

03 · The teams

Participating franchises

Ten city-based franchises currently compete for the trophy. Five earlier franchises have folded, been rebranded or replaced along the way.

No longer in the league: Deccan Chargers (2008–2012, champions 2009), Kochi Tuskers Kerala (2011 only), Pune Warriors India (2011–2013), Gujarat Lions (2016–2017) and Rising Pune Supergiant(s) (2016–2017, runners-up 2017). Delhi Daredevils were rebranded Delhi Capitals in 2019; Kings XI Punjab became Punjab Kings in 2021.
04 · Roll of honour

Champions by titles won

Mumbai Indians and Chennai Super Kings share the record with five titles each — but eight different franchises have lifted the trophy since 2008.

05 · Individual honours

Orange Cap & Purple Cap

Alongside the team trophy, two caps track the tournament's leading individual performers all season long — and change hands match by match until the final ball is bowled.

Orange Cap

Awarded to the season's leading run-scorer. The cap physically passes to whoever holds the top spot after every match, so the "current" holder can change mid-tournament before the season's final owner is confirmed at the end of the league.

Purple Cap

The bowling equivalent, given to the season's leading wicket-taker. Like the Orange Cap, it is a running award — spinners have taken it more often than raw pace, with six of the IPL's ten all-time leading wicket-takers being spin bowlers.
