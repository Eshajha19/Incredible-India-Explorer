Sandi Bird Sanctuary

Ramsar Wetland & Freshwater Sanctuary

Hardoi District, Uttar Pradesh, India

View Bird Species Explore Map
Heritage & Status

A Protected Wetland Ecosystem

Historical Development

Sandi Bird Sanctuary originated as a natural freshwater marsh. Recognizing its immense importance for local biodiversity and migratory flocks, it was brought under strict wildlife conservation initiatives, evolving into a premier protected sanctuary in Uttar Pradesh.

Ramsar Designation

Internationally recognized under the Ramsar Convention, Sandi is a wetland of global significance. Its conservation objectives focus on maintaining sustainable management practices to protect its rich biodiversity and vital hydrological functions.

Wetland Habitat

The sanctuary is characterized by shallow lakes, freshwater marshes, dense reed beds, and grasslands. This intricate ecological food web provides vital ecosystem services, buffering against floods and maintaining local water tables.

Avian Paradise

Resident & Migratory Birds

A critical stopover and breeding habitat on the Central Asian Flyway.

Central Asian Flyway

During the winter migration, Sandi becomes a bustling hub of avian activity. Seasonal arrivals use these extensive feeding grounds as a crucial stopover habitat, offering unparalleled birdwatching opportunities for visitors and researchers alike.

Sarus Crane

Vulnerable (Resident)

The world's tallest flying bird, the state bird of UP, finds excellent nesting habitats in the sanctuary's marshes.

Painted Stork

Near Threatened

Forms large, noisy breeding colonies in the trees surrounding the shallow wetland waters.

Purple Heron

Resident/Migrant

An expert at camouflage, this heron wades stealthily through the dense reed beds hunting for fish.

Asian Openbill

Resident

Named for its unique bill structure designed to extract snails, a staple of the sanctuary's muddy banks.

Other prominent species include: Black-necked Stork, Little Egret, Grey Heron, White Ibis, Cormorants, and various Kingfishers.

Conservation

Protecting Sandi

  • Habitat Restoration: Dedicated programs to manage aquatic vegetation and restore optimal water levels.
  • Pollution Prevention: Strict monitoring of incoming water to ensure a pristine environment for aquatic life.
  • Eco-Tourism & Awareness: Fostering community awareness through responsible eco-tourism and wildlife protection initiatives.
Facts

Did You Know?

  • It is one of Uttar Pradesh's Ramsar-designated wetlands.
  • Located directly on the Central Asian Flyway, making it a critical migratory hotspot.
  • Supports hundreds of resident and migratory bird species annually.
  • Serves as an important breeding habitat for several waterbird species, including the Sarus Crane.
