Odisha • 1078 - 1434 CE

Eastern Ganga Dynasty

Builders of the Konark Sun Temple and champions of Odishan culture and maritime trade.

Historical Overview

The Eastern Ganga dynasty ruled the region of Kalinga (modern-day Odisha) from the late 11th century to the mid-15th century. They were known for their fierce resistance against Muslim invasions from the north and their patronage of art, religion, and monumental architecture. The dynasty reached its peak under Anantavarman Chodaganga and Narasimhadeva I.

They were great patrons of the Jagannath cult and played a pivotal role in shaping the religious and cultural identity of Odisha. Their naval prowess allowed them to maintain trade links with Southeast Asia, continuing the legacy of ancient Kalinga mariners.

Major Rulers

👑

Anantavarman Chodaganga

1078 - 1150 CE

Founded the imperial Ganga power. He built the Jagannath Temple at Puri and introduced the Eras of the Ganga calendar.

⚔️

Narasimhadeva I

1238 - 1264 CE

The greatest ruler of the dynasty. He commissioned the Konark Sun Temple and successfully fought the Muslim rulers of Bengal.

Architectural Legacy

  • Konark Sun Temple: A UNESCO World Heritage site designed as a colossal chariot for the Sun God Surya, with 24 carved wheels and drawn by seven horses.
  • Jagannath Temple, Puri: One of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites, initiated by Anantavarman Chodaganga. It remains the center of the Jagannath cult.
  • Madhukesvara Temple: Located in Mukhalingam, showcasing the early Kalinga architectural style.

Historical Timeline

1078 CE

Anantavarman Chodaganga establishes the Eastern Ganga rule over Kalinga.

1100s CE

Construction of the Jagannath Temple at Puri begins.

1243 CE

Narasimhadeva I defeats the Muslim governor of Bengal, asserting military dominance.

1255 CE

Construction of the Konark Sun Temple is completed.

1434 CE

The dynasty falls to the Gajapati Empire under Kapilendra Deva.
