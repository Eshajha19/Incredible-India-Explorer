📜 History of the Awards

The National Energy Conservation Awards (NECA) were instituted in 1991 by the Ministry of Power, Government of India, to give national recognition to industries and establishments that have taken special efforts to reduce energy consumption while maintaining their production.

Genesis and Vision

The scheme was launched to encourage energy efficiency as a national priority. The first awards were presented on December 14, 1991, a date that was declared as National Energy Conservation Day — an annual occasion to raise awareness about energy efficiency and conservation across the country.

Evolution Over the Years

Following the enactment of the Energy Conservation Act, 2001, the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) was constituted in March 2002 and became the nodal agency implementing the awards on behalf of the Ministry of Power. Since NECA 2017, the participating sectors have been categorized into three groups, with each group corresponding to a specific year and the cycle repeating every three years — allowing fair rotation of sectors.

Significance and Impact

The awards are presented every year on National Energy Conservation Day (14 December) by the nation's highest functionaries — the Hon'ble President, Hon'ble Prime Minister, and Hon'ble Union Minister of Power. Participation has grown year after year, with 558 applications received for NECA 2025, reflecting the awards' growing prestige and the industry's deepening commitment to energy conservation.

Institutional Framework

The awards are administered by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) under the Ministry of Power, with the sector framework approved annually by the Award Committee chaired by the Secretary (Power) and technical evaluation conducted by a Technical Committee headed by the Member (Thermal), Central Electricity Authority (CEA).

📋 Eligibility Criteria

The NECA is open to a wide spectrum of entities — from large industrial complexes to individual champions of efficiency. Eligible participants include:

🏭 Industrial Units

Large, medium, and small industrial units across sectors such as chemicals, steel, textiles, sugar, petrochemicals, edible oils, rubber, aluminium, cement, fertilizers, and thermal power plants.

🏢 Buildings & Institutions

Commercial buildings and establishments, hotels, hospitals, colleges and universities, schools, government buildings, residential buildings, and academic institutions demonstrating exemplary energy performance.

🚆 Transport Sector

Zonal railways, metro railway stations, railway stations, ports, and other transport operators that have achieved significant energy savings in their operations.

🔌 Appliance Manufacturers

Manufacturers of energy-efficient appliances and equipment — ceiling fans, refrigerators, room air conditioners, deep freezers, pump-sets, washing machines, and electric storage water heaters.

🏙️ Municipalities & Urban Bodies

Municipalities and urban local bodies recognized for energy-efficient street lighting, water pumping, and public service delivery.

👥 Individuals & Organizations

Individuals and organizations promoting energy efficiency, builders and architects for green building projects, and state governments competing for the State Performance Award.

Core Evaluation Themes

Entries are assessed on the basis of specific energy consumption reduction, adoption of energy-efficient technologies, integration of renewable energy, innovation in conservation practices, and maintenance of production or service quality while cutting energy use.

What Winners Receive

Recipients are presented trophies and certificates of merit by the Hon'ble President, Hon'ble Prime Minister, or Hon'ble Union Minister of Power during the National Energy Conservation Day celebrations held on 14 December every year.

⚙️ Selection Process

The selection process is rigorous, transparent, and merit-based, ensuring that only the most deserving energy-efficient performers are honoured.

1

Sector Approval

The Award Committee, chaired by the Secretary (Power), reviews and approves the eligible sectors for each award cycle, ensuring alignment with national energy priorities.

2

Call for Applications

An advertisement is published in prominent national newspapers, and the call for applications is promoted through official websites and social media platforms, inviting submissions from eligible entities.

3

Application Submission

Eligible units submit detailed applications with energy consumption data, conservation measures, and performance evidence before the deadline — NECA 2025 received 558 applications.

4

Technical Committee Evaluation

A Technical Committee headed by the Member (Thermal), Central Electricity Authority (CEA) — with representatives from the Ministry of Railways, CEA, Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs, EESL, National Productivity Council, industry associations, and BEE — evaluates each application and shortlists awardees.

5

Award Committee Approval

The Technical Committee's recommendations are submitted to the Award Committee chaired by the Secretary (Power) — with the Chairman CEA, Additional Secretaries of the Ministry of Power, DG BEE, MD EESL, and Secretary BEE as members — for final consideration and approval.

6

Felicitation on 14 December

Selected awardees are honoured and felicitated on National Energy Conservation Day (14 December) by the nation's highest dignitaries, in the presence of top government officials and industry leaders.

Evaluation Matrix

  • Energy Savings: Tangible reduction in specific energy consumption while maintaining production or service levels
  • Technology Adoption: Deployment of energy-efficient equipment, processes, and best available technologies
  • Renewable Integration: Use of solar, wind, and other clean energy sources to offset conventional consumption
  • Innovation: Novel conservation practices and energy management systems
  • Sustainability: Long-term viability and replicability of the conservation measures

🌱 Energy Conservation Initiatives

Beyond honouring achievers, the NECA framework is part of a wider national ecosystem of energy efficiency initiatives driven by the Ministry of Power and the Bureau of Energy Efficiency.

⚖️

Energy Conservation Act, 2001

The legislative foundation of India's energy efficiency movement, establishing the Bureau of Energy Efficiency and empowering the government to set efficiency standards for appliances and designated consumers.

🏷️

Standards & Labelling Programme

A landmark scheme (launched 2006) that rates appliances from 1 to 5 stars, enabling consumers to make informed choices and pushing manufacturers toward ever-higher efficiency.

🏢

Energy Conservation Building Code

The ECBC (2007) and Eco-Niwas Samhita (2018) set minimum energy performance requirements for commercial and residential buildings, shaping energy-efficient construction nationwide.

📈

Perform, Achieve & Trade (PAT)

A market-based mechanism that assigns energy-saving targets to large industries and enables trading of energy-saving certificates, turning efficiency into a tradable commodity.

💡

UJALA Programme

The world's largest LED distribution programme (launched 2015 by EESL), distributing billions of LED bulbs, tube lights, and fans at affordable prices to cut household electricity bills.

🎨

National Painting Competition

Organized by the Ministry of Power since 2005 for school students across three stages — school, state, and national levels — with over 80 lakh students participating in 2025.

🏛️

State Designated Agencies

State-level agencies that implement BEE schemes locally, enforce energy codes, and promote conservation awareness, competing for the annual State Performance Award.

🌍

Carbon Trading Framework

The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, introduced provisions for a domestic carbon credit trading scheme, aligning energy efficiency with India's climate commitments.

A Legacy of Excellence

Since 1991, thousands of industrial units, buildings, transport operators, and institutions have earned national recognition. Each awardee becomes a benchmark for its sector, inspiring peers to adopt energy-efficient practices and contributing to India's journey toward energy independence and a sustainable future.

⏳ Timeline

1991

Awards Instituted

The National Energy Conservation Awards are launched by the Ministry of Power, and the first awards are presented on 14 December — declared as National Energy Conservation Day.

2001

Energy Conservation Act

Parliament enacts the Energy Conservation Act, providing a comprehensive legal framework for energy efficiency in India.

2002

BEE Takes Charge

The Bureau of Energy Efficiency is constituted in March 2002 and becomes the nodal agency administering the awards under the Ministry of Power.

2005

Painting Competition Launched

The Ministry of Power launches the National Painting Competition on Energy Conservation for school students, nurturing conservation habits from a young age.

2006

Standards & Labelling

The star-rating programme for appliances begins, empowering consumers and transforming the market toward energy-efficient products.

2007

Building Code Introduced

The Energy Conservation Building Code sets minimum energy performance requirements for commercial buildings across the country.

2012

PAT Scheme

The Perform, Achieve and Trade mechanism assigns efficiency targets to large industries, creating a vibrant market for energy-saving certificates.

2015

UJALA Programme

EESL launches the world's largest LED distribution programme, cutting household lighting costs and electricity demand dramatically.

2017

Rotating Sector Groups

NECA sectors are reorganized into three rotating groups, with each cycle covering its sectors once every three years for fair participation.

2022

Amendment Act & Carbon Trading

The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act introduces provisions for carbon credit trading, linking efficiency efforts with climate action.

2025

Record Participation

NECA 2025 receives 558 applications across sectors, with 17 Best Performing Units and 21 Certificates of Merit conferred on National Energy Conservation Day.
