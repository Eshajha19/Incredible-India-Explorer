Namesake & Pioneer

Syed Mushtaq Ali: The Flamboyant Maestro

Syed Mushtaq Ali (1914–2005) was an iconic Indian opening batsman known for his charismatic swagger and aggressive strokeplay long before the T20 era. In 1936 at Old Trafford, Manchester, he carved his name in gold by scoring 112 against England — becoming the first Indian cricketer to score a Test century on overseas soil.

Renowned for charging down the pitch to fast bowlers and spinners alike, Mushtaq Ali received the prestigious Padma Shri in 1964 and C. K. Nayudu Lifetime Achievement Award in 1998. In 2008, BCCI officially rebranded its flagship Inter-State T20 tournament in his honor.

"He played cricket the way it was meant to be played — with joyous freedom, boundless audacity, and infectious passion."
— Wisden Cricketers' Almanack
Tournament History & Format

Evolution of India's T20 Championship

Launched by the BCCI in 2006–07 as the Inter-State T20 Championship (won by Tamil Nadu with Dinesh Karthik as captain), the tournament was renamed the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy in 2008–09.

  • Current Format: 38 teams divided into 5 elite groups (A, B, C, D, E) followed by Pre-Quarterfinals, Quarterfinals, Semifinals, and Grand Final.
  • Innovation: First domestic tournament in world cricket to successfully trial the Impact Player rule and two bouncers per over rule before IPL adoption.
  • Talent Pipeline: Crucial showcase where scouts from all 10 IPL franchises identify domestic powerhouse talent right ahead of the annual IPL Mega Auctions.
