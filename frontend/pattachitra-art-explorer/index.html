Pattachitra Art

The sacred scroll paintings of Odisha, weaving epics into visual tapestries.

The Tradition of Pattachitra

Pattachitra derives its name from the Sanskrit words 'Patta' (cloth) and 'Chitra' (painting). Originating in the 12th century in Odisha, it is deeply intertwined with the cult of Lord Jagannath in Puri. Traditionally, these paintings served as visual aids for traveling bards (Chitrakars) who sang the epics from village to village.

The art form is characterized by bold outlines, vibrant flat colors, and a lack of perspective or depth, giving it a distinctly two-dimensional, graphic quality that is instantly recognizable.

Mythological Narratives

The central theme of almost all Pattachitra paintings is the 'Badhia Thakura'—a stylized depiction of Lord Jagannath, his brother Balabhadra, and sister Subhadra. Beyond this, artists depict:

Dashavatara

The ten incarnations of Lord Vishnu.

Krishna Leela

The playful childhood and divine acts of Lord Krishna.

Ramayana & Mahabharata

Key battle scenes and moral dilemmas from the great epics.

Gita Govinda

The romantic and spiritual verses of Jayadeva's poetry.

Brushes and Natural Colors

The creation of a Pattachitra is a meticulous, multi-stage process:

  1. Canvas Prep: Two pieces of cloth are glued together using tamarind seed paste and coated with a mixture of chalk powder and clay.
  2. Brushes: Made from the hair of domestic animals (like buffalo neck hair) or keya plant roots, tied to bamboo handles.
  3. Natural Pigments:
    • White: Conch shells
    • Red: Hingula stone
    • Yellow: Haritala stone
    • Blue: Indigo or crushed lapis lazuli
    • Black: Lamp soot
