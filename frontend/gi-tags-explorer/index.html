Geographical Indication Tags of India

A GI tag is a promise that a product genuinely comes from the place whose name it carries — and that the place is the reason it is good. From Darjeeling Tea to Kolhapuri Chappals, explore the products India has legally tied to their soil, their looms and their artisans.

The Basics

What Is a Geographical Indication?

A sign used on products that have a specific geographical origin, and qualities or a reputation that are due to that origin.

Some products cannot be made anywhere else. Darjeeling Tea tastes the way it does because of altitude, mist and a particular soil. Bidriware turns black because of the soil inside Bidar fort. Aranmula Kannadi is cast from an alloy known to a handful of families in one Kerala village.

A Geographical Indication is the legal recognition of exactly that link. It marks a product as belonging to a place, and it gives the producers of that place the right to stop others from selling imitations under the same name.

Unlike a trademark, a GI is a collective right. No single company owns it. It cannot be sold, licensed or moved. It belongs to everyone who makes the product, in the place where the product is from — and it stays there.

  • Governed by the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999
  • Administered by the GI Registry in Chennai
  • Applied for by producer associations, never by individuals
  • Valid for 10 years and renewable without limit
🏷️

Why It Matters

Before GI protection, a Kashmiri weaver had no legal way to stop a mill-made shawl from being sold as "pashmina". Registration turns a reputation built over centuries into a right that can actually be enforced.
