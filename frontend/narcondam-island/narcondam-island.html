Narcondam Island

A tiny, densely-forested volcanic island rising sharply from the Bay of Bengal, cut off from the rest of the Andamans by open sea — and the only place on Earth where the Narcondam Hornbill is found.

~6.8 sq km Total Land Area
~710 m Peak Elevation
~120 km From Main Andaman Group
1977 Declared Wildlife Sanctuary

🗺️ Geography

Narcondam is a small, roughly circular island in the Bay of Bengal, lying about 120 km east of the main Andaman archipelago and closer to the coast of Myanmar than to Port Blair. Administratively it falls under the North Andaman district of the Andaman & Nicobar Islands.

Unlike most of the Andamans, Narcondam is an isolated volcanic peak rather than part of a connected island chain, surrounded by deep open sea on all sides. Its coastline is mostly steep and rocky, with very limited flat ground for landing.

  • Located roughly 120 km east of the main Andaman group
  • Falls under the North Andaman administrative district
  • Small, steep, roughly circular island with a rocky shoreline

🦜 Narcondam Hornbill

The island's best-known resident is the Narcondam Hornbill (Rhyticeros narcondami), a species found nowhere else on Earth. It nests in the island's tall forest trees and feeds mainly on fruit, playing an important role in dispersing seeds across the forest.

Because its entire population is confined to this single small island, the hornbill is considered highly vulnerable — any disturbance to the forest or introduction of predators could put the whole species at risk. This has made its protection a key conservation priority.

  • Endemic to Narcondam Island — found nowhere else in the wild
  • A fruit-eating hornbill that helps disperse forest seeds
  • Entire global population confined to one small island

🌋 Extinct Volcano

Narcondam Island is the exposed summit of an extinct volcano, rising steeply to a peak of around 710 metres above sea level. There are no records of the volcano having erupted in recorded history, and it is generally classed as extinct rather than dormant.

The island's volcanic origin has shaped its steep terrain and rocky coastline, leaving very little level ground and making the interior accessible mainly along a few narrow forest trails.

  • The exposed summit of an extinct volcanic cone
  • Peak elevation of roughly 710 metres
  • Steep, rugged terrain with little flat land

🛡️ Conservation

Narcondam was declared a Wildlife Sanctuary in 1977, primarily to protect the Narcondam Hornbill and its forest habitat. Public access to the island is tightly restricted, and only a small forest department and police presence is stationed there.

Because the hornbill's entire population lives on this one island, conservation efforts focus on keeping the forest cover intact and preventing the introduction of species — such as goats or predators — that could disturb the fragile ecosystem.

  • Declared a Wildlife Sanctuary in 1977
  • Access to the island is tightly restricted
  • Focus on protecting forest cover and excluding invasive species

🌿 Flora & Fauna

The island is covered in dense tropical evergreen forest, which supports the Narcondam Hornbill along with other birds, reptiles and insects adapted to its isolated environment. The surrounding waters are also home to a variety of reef fish and marine life.

Because of its isolation, Narcondam supports a self-contained mini ecosystem where the forest canopy, birdlife and coastal waters are closely interlinked — making the health of the forest directly tied to the survival of its most famous resident.

  • Dense tropical evergreen forest covering most of the island
  • Home to birds, reptiles and insects adapted to isolation
  • Surrounding reef waters support varied marine life
