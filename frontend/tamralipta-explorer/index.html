Explore Tamralipta

Journey into the ancient port city of Tamluk, West Bengal, where merchants set sail for Southeast Asia and Chinese pilgrims came to study.

Geographic Setting

Tamralipta (modern Tamluk in West Bengal) was strategically located near the mouth of the Rupnarayan River (close to the Ganges delta) and the Bay of Bengal. It served as a critical nexus connecting the landlocked Mauryan and Gupta empires with the lucrative maritime routes of the Indian Ocean.

Maritime Trade & Foreign Connections

Tamralipta was a premier port facilitating trade with Suvarnabhumi (Southeast Asia), Sri Lanka, and as far as China. Exports included indigo, silk, and copper, while imports included spices and precious stones.

Buddhist Connections & Archaeology

Historical accounts from Chinese monks Fa-Hien (5th century CE) and Xuanzang (7th century CE) describe Tamralipta as a thriving Buddhist centre with numerous monasteries. Archaeological excavations have yielded Rouletted ware, amphorae, terracotta figurines, and punch-marked coins, validating its cosmopolitan nature.
