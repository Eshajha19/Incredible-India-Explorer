Dholavira: The Harappan City of Water

Explore the ancient metropolis of Khadir Bet in the Rann of Kutch, Gujarat (3000 BCE – 1500 BCE) — renowned for its monumental stone architecture, tripartite city layout, and world-class water management engineering.

Hydraulic Engineering

Reservoirs, Dams & Water-Flow System

Situated in an arid zone between two seasonal streams (Manhar & Mansar), Dholavira built 16 massive stone-cut reservoirs storing up to 250,000 cubic meters of water.

🌊

Stone-Cut Reservoirs

Massive rock-cut tanks surrounding the city, connected by dams and underground masonry channels, capable of harvesting every drop of monsoon runoff.

🪜

Cascading Siltation Basins

Water passed through series of stone settling tanks to filter out silt before entering deep step-wells and drinking water reservoirs.

🏰

Fortified Dams & Aqueducts

Thick masonry check-dams constructed across the Manhar and Mansar rivulets diverted floodwaters into underground stone conduits.

Archaeological Evidence

The Dholavira Signboard & Discoveries

The 10-Symbol Inscription Signboard

Found near the Northern Gate of the Citadel, featuring ten large Indus script symbols (approx 37 cm high) made of crystalline gypsum inlay. Believed to be a public advertisement or ceremonial billboard.

Craft & Trade Artifacts

Copper workshops, shell bangles, semi-precious carnelian beads, gold ornaments, and Harappan seals indicating thriving trade networks with Mesopotamia and Oman.

End of Metropolis

Decline, Abandonment & Archaeological Sources

Climatic Aridity & Decline

Drying up of seasonal streams, shifting monsoon patterns, and seismic disruptions around 1900 BCE led to gradual depopulation and late-Harappan rural transformation.

Archaeological Evidence vs. Interpretation

Discovered by J. P. Joshi in 1967 and excavated extensively by Dr. R. S. Bisht (ASI) from 1990 to 2005. UNESCO declared Dholavira a World Heritage Site in 2021.
