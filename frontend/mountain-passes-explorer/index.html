Mountain Passes of the Himalaya

A pass is not scenery. It is the only place a range can be crossed, which is why passes decide where trade goes, where armies go, where languages meet, and where borders end up being drawn. The Silk Route ran over the Karakoram Pass. Nathu La carried most of Tibet's trade until 1962. Zoji La is why Ladakh is cut off for months every year.

The Idea

What a Pass Is, and Why It Decides Things

Geomorphologically a col on a ridge. Historically, the reason a town exists where it does.

A pass is the low point on a ridge between two summits — the place where a range can be crossed without climbing it. In the Himalaya that geometry has done more to shape human settlement than any amount of fertile ground, because a range with one crossing concentrates everything through that crossing: trade, armies, pilgrims, languages, and eventually the border itself.

The evidence is in the towns. Leh was a caravan city because the Karakoram Pass route ran through it, and stopped being one in 1949 when that route closed. Kalimpong grew into a substantial entrepôt on the Kalimpong–Lhasa wool road over Nathu La and Jelep La, and has not recovered its position since 1962. The Bhotiya villages of the upper Garhwal valleys were built around a seasonal movement over Mana, Niti and Lipulekh, and emptied when those passes shut.

Passes are also where climate changes abruptly. Walk over Hampta from Kullu into Lahaul and you go from wet forest to high desert in a day. The range is the rain barrier, and the pass is the door through it.

  • Passes concentrate trade, armies and language contact through a single point
  • Closing one can end a town's economy — Leh in 1949, Kalimpong in 1962
  • The vocabulary changes with the language, not the altitude: la, jot, khal, ghati
  • Tunnels are now removing the seasonality that shaped all of this

On the Elevations

Sources routinely disagree by 50–150 m about the same pass, because "height" may mean the road summit or the geographic col, and older figures came from less accurate surveys. Khardung La is the standard example — a signboard claiming 18,380 ft against survey work putting it near 5,359 m. Every figure here is the commonly cited value and should be read as approximate.

Altitude Safety

Almost every serious altitude incident in Ladakh follows the same pattern: someone gained too much height too fast, by vehicle or by air.

⚠️

Fitness does not protect you. Altitude illness is not a measure of how fit someone is — well-trained people get it, and sometimes get it worse because they ascend faster. The only reliable protections are gradual ascent, a rest day every three or four days, and descending at the first sign of anything more than a mild headache. If in doubt, go down.
