Hokersar Wetland

Jammu & Kashmir's Ramsar Wetland and Himalayan Bird Sanctuary

Near Srinagar, Jammu & Kashmir, India

View Migratory Birds Explore Map
Heritage & Status

A Vital Himalayan Ecosystem

Historical Significance

Historically acting as a natural flood absorption basin for the Kashmir Valley, Hokersar has been crucial to the local communities for centuries. Over time, it evolved from a traditional wetland into a highly protected wildlife sanctuary, reaching major wetland management milestones in recent decades.

Ramsar Designation

Recognized globally under the Ramsar Convention, Hokersar is celebrated for its outstanding conservation importance. This international status underscores the imperative to protect its biodiversity and has catalyzed numerous wetland restoration initiatives.

Himalayan Hydrology

This dynamic freshwater marsh is largely fed by Himalayan snowmelt and seasonal flooding from the Jhelum River basin. Its extensive reed beds and marsh vegetation perform essential ecological services, buffering the region against severe climate influences.

Avian Paradise

Winter Migrants & Flyways

A critical stopover and wintering ground on the Central Asian Flyway.

Bird Migration Routes

Hokersar serves as a vital winter stopover habitat along the Central Asian Flyway. As temperatures drop in Siberia, Central Asia, and Northern Europe, millions of birds use the Himalayan migration corridors to reach the relatively warmer Kashmir Valley, making this wetland an area of immense global conservation significance.

Greylag Goose

Winter Migrant

Large flocks arrive in early winter, filling the crisp Himalayan air with their distinctive honking calls.

Northern Pintail

Winter Migrant

Elegant dabbling ducks that feed extensively in the shallow, vegetation-rich waters of the marsh.

Purple Heron

Resident/Migrant

Elusive waders that perfectly camouflage themselves among the dense reed beds of the wetland.

Marsh Harrier

Raptor

These birds of prey can frequently be seen flying low over the reeds hunting for small birds and amphibians.

Other prominent species include: Common Teal, Eurasian Wigeon, Gadwall, Mallard, Eurasian Coot, and Little Egret.

Conservation

Safeguarding Hokersar

  • Water Management: Dredging and scientific water regulation ensure optimal water levels are maintained during critical winter months.
  • Pollution & Invasive Species: Rigorous pollution control measures and manual removal of invasive weeds help restore native marsh vegetation.
  • Community Participation: Engaging local populations in sustainable eco-tourism and anti-poaching programs, supported by robust government conservation initiatives.
Facts

Did You Know?

  • It is Jammu & Kashmir's only recognized Ramsar wetland.
  • Dominated almost entirely by natural freshwater marshes and expansive reed beds.
  • Supports upwards of 500,000 migratory waterfowl during a good winter season.
  • Located just 14 km from Srinagar, making it highly accessible for bird research.
  • The wetland is snow-covered during peak winter, creating stunning, stark scenery.
