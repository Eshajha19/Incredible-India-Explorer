The Lifeline of India: The Monsoons

🌊 Southwest Monsoon (June - Sept)

The primary rainy season for most of India. Driven by the intense heating of the Tibetan Plateau and the Indian subcontinent, it draws moisture-laden winds from the Indian Ocean. It splits into two branches: the Arabian Sea branch and the Bay of Bengal branch. It is vital for India's Kharif crops.

🌬️ Northeast Monsoon (Oct - Dec)

Often referred to as the 'Retreating Monsoon'. As the landmass cools, the wind direction reverses, blowing from the northeast. While it brings dry, cool air to most of the country, it picks up moisture over the Bay of Bengal, delivering heavy, crucial rainfall to the southeastern coast (Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh).

Climate Zones Overview

Due to its vast geography, India experiences a wide range of climates:

  • Alpine & Mountainous: Himalayas (Freezing winters, cool summers).
  • Arid & Semi-Arid: Thar Desert and parts of the Deccan Plateau (Extreme heat, very low rainfall).
  • Tropical Wet: Western Ghats and Northeast India (Heavy year-round rainfall, high humidity).
  • Tropical Wet & Dry: Most of peninsular India (Distinct wet and dry seasons).
