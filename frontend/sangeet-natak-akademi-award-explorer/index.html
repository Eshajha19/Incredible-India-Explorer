📜 History of the Sangeet Natak Akademi

The Sangeet Natak Akademi (National Academy of Music, Dance and Drama) is India's national academy for the performing arts — the first National Academy of the arts set up by the Republic of India. It was created by a resolution of the Ministry of Education, Government of India, dated 31 May 1952, and became functional the following year with the appointment of its first Chairman, Dr. P. V. Rajamannar.

Dr. Rajendra Prasad, the first President of India, inaugurated the Akademi on 28 January 1953 in a special function held in the Parliament House. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, then Union Minister for Education, in his opening address defined its calling: "It is not the function of an academy to impose its own canons of art; it should, on the contrary, help the artist and the scholar to develop in freedom."

The Akademi was established to preserve and promote the vast intangible heritage of India's diverse culture expressed in the many forms of music, dance, and drama. It is registered as a society, with its registered office at Rabindra Bhavan, 35 Ferozeshah Road, New Delhi, and functions as an autonomous body of the Ministry of Culture.

Mandate & Objectives

  • To preserve, promote, and propagate India's performing arts — music, dance, and theatre.
  • To give awards and honours recognizing individual artistic and scholarly achievement.
  • To organize performances, festivals, seminars, and conferences across the country.
  • To document the performing arts in one of the largest audio-visual archives in the country.
  • To advise and assist the Government of India in formulating policies for the performing arts.

Headquarters & Regional Presence

The Akademi is headquartered at Rabindra Bhavan, New Delhi and works through a national General Council representing every region of India. It collaborates with State academies and major cultural institutions, coordinates India's nominations to the UNESCO Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, and maintains a reference library, a gallery of musical instruments, and one of the largest audio-visual archives of the performing arts in the world.

Flagship Honours

The Akademi confers the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship (Akademi Ratna) — its highest honour — the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award (Akademi Puraskar) for practising artists, gurus and scholars, and, since 2006, the Ustad Bismillah Khan Yuva Puraskar for young artists. Over 1,200 artists have been honoured with the Akademi Award since the first were conferred in 1952.

📋 Eligibility & Honour Structure

The Sangeet Natak Akademi confers several levels of national recognition in the performing arts. Each honour recognizes sustained, distinguished, and original achievement in music, dance, or drama.

🏅 Sangeet Natak Akademi Award

The highest national recognition conferred on practising artists, gurus, and scholars of music, dance, and theatre. It carries a cash purse of ₹1 lakh, a tamrapatra (brass plaque), and an angavastram (shawl). Around 40 awards are conferred annually, honouring artists of all disciplines without distinction of nationality, race, caste, religion, creed, or sex.

💎 Akademi Fellowship (Akademi Ratna)

The highest honour of the Akademi, conferred for lifetime achievement in the performing arts. Fellowship seats are limited — 30 at any time until 2008, increased to 40 thereafter. The Fellowship carries a purse of ₹3 lakh, a tamrapatra, and an angavastram. Ordinarily only artists of 50 years and above are considered.

🎓 Ustad Bismillah Khan Yuva Puraskar

Instituted in 2006, this honour recognizes outstanding young artists in music, dance, and theatre, encouraging the next generation of performers in the footsteps of the legendary shehnai maestro.

🎨 Eligible Art Forms

Honours span Hindustani and Carnatic music, instrumental and percussion music, the classical dance traditions, folk and tribal traditions, theatre acting and direction, playwriting, puppetry, and other traditional performing arts.

🗳️ How Honourees Are Chosen

Recommendations flow from eminent practitioners, the current Fellows, and members of the General Council. Screening by expert panels precedes approval by the Akademi's Executive Board and General Council.

🎪 What is Recognised

Sustained excellence and contribution to the performing arts — as performer, guru, scholar, director, or designer — that has enriched India's musical, dance, and theatrical heritage.

💃 Dance Categories

India's classical dance traditions — rooted in the Natya Shastra — are among the most celebrated performing arts in the world. The Akademi honours every major classical form as well as creative, experimental, and folk dance.

Classical Dance

The eight classical traditions — Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Odissi, and Sattriya — each honoured in its own category, alongside the martial-folk Chhau traditions.

Creative & Experimental Dance

From Uday Shankar's pioneering fusion of Indian and Western dance to modern choreography, the Akademi has a dedicated category for creative and experimental dance.

Folk & Tribal Dance

Honours for the vibrant folk and tribal dance traditions of every state — from Garba and Bhangra to the martial dances of the east — celebrating India's living grassroots heritage.

🎶 Music Categories

From the ragas of Hindustani music to the rhythmic geometry of Carnatic music, the Akademi's music honours celebrate India's two great classical systems alongside instrumental, percussion, and folk traditions.

Classical Vocal

The Hindustani (North Indian) and Carnatic (South Indian) vocal traditions, honoured in separate categories. Early honours for these disciplines were conferred as Presidential Awards from 1951, before the Akademi was set up.

Instrumental

Honours for plucked, bowed, and wind instruments — the sitar, sarod, veena, violin, shehnai, flute, and santoor — celebrating the master instrumentalists of both traditions.

Percussion & Folk Music

Recognition for the tabla, mridangam, ghatam, and pakhavaj virtuosos, and for the rich folk music traditions — Baul, Rajasthani, Bihu, and countless regional forms — that nourish the classical repertoire.

🎭 Theatre Categories

Theatre is the third pillar of the Akademi's name and mandate. From the classical Natya Shastra tradition to the modern Indian stage, the Akademi honours the actors, directors, playwrights, and designers who bring drama to life.

Acting

Honours for outstanding stage actors — from the pioneers of the modern Indian theatre to today's leading performers — celebrating command of language, gesture, and presence.

Direction

Recognition for directors who have reshaped Indian theatre — from Sombhu Mitra, Ebrahim Alkazi, and Habib Tanvir to the contemporary stage — for their vision, staging, and ensemble building.

Playwriting & Design

Awards for playwriting in Indian languages — celebrated by writers such as Girish Karnad and Chandrashekhara Kambara — as well as for set, lighting, and costume design.

🎯 Selection Process

The selection of honours is a rigorous, multi-tier process combining peer nomination with expert evaluation, all governed by the Akademi's Rules and Regulations under its Memorandum of Association.

1

Nominations

Recommendations are invited from eminent artists and scholars, the current Fellows of the Akademi, and members of the General Council, for each discipline of music, dance, and theatre.

2

Expert Screening

Expert panels of senior practitioners and scholars in each field screen the nominations and shortlist the most distinguished candidates based on sustained artistic achievement.

3

Committee Deliberation

The shortlisted names are deliberated by the Akademi's Awards Committee, which weighs the artist's body of work, contribution to the field, and national significance.

4

General Council Approval

Recommendations are placed before the General Council — the Akademi's all-India governing body — for final approval.

5

Investiture

Honourees are conferred the tamrapatra, angavastram, and cash purse at an annual investiture ceremony celebrating India's performing arts.

Evaluation Focus Areas

  • Artistic Excellence: Mastery of the form and distinction of performance
  • Sustained Contribution: A lifelong body of work, not a single achievement
  • Guru-Shishya Legacy: Transmission of tradition to the next generation
  • Scholarship: Contribution to theory, research, and documentation of the arts
  • National Impact: Elevation of India's performing arts at home and abroad

🏆 Notable Awardees & Fellows

Since 1952, the Akademi has honoured the giants of Indian music, dance, and theatre. These artists defined the very idea of modern Indian performing arts.

🎶 Music

🎤

M. S. Subbulakshmi

The nightingale of Carnatic music, whose divine voice earned her the Bharat Ratna in 1998 — the first musician ever to receive it.

🎺

Bismillah Khan

Award · 1956 · Shehnai

The maestro who made the shehnai a concert instrument, playing at Red Fort on Independence Day — the inspiration behind the Yuva Puraskar.

🎸

Ravi Shankar

Award · 1962 · Sitar

The sitar virtuoso who carried Indian classical music to world concert halls, collaborating with Yehudi Menuhin and George Harrison.

🎻

Bhimsen Joshi

Award · 1976 · Hindustani Vocal

The Kirana gharana titan whose khayal and bhajans stirred millions, and whose voice opened the Republic Day parades for years.

The abhinaya queen of Bharatanatyam, descended from the devadasi tradition of the Tanjore court, later honoured as an Akademi Fellow.

The great Lucknow gharana exponent who preserved the courtly elegance of Kathak and shaped its teaching at India's premier arts institutions.

The guru who revived Odissi from temple tradition to world stage, celebrated for the sculptural grace of his choreography.

A master of the Kalamandalam style of Kathakali, celebrated for his magnificent portrayal of the great epic roles.

🎭 Theatre

🎬

Sombhu Mitra

Award · 1959 · Direction

The founder of Bohurupee and a towering figure of the Bengali theatre movement, honoured for a lifetime of direction and acting.

🎬

The RADA-trained director who reshaped Indian theatre as head of the National School of Drama, staging over fifty landmark productions.

The Kannada playwright of Tughlaq and Nagamandala, whose history-soaked dramas transformed modern Indian playwriting.

Theatre and cinema director who brought folk music and natural staging into the modern Indian theatre, and led the NSD Repertory.

Impact of the Honours

  • The Akademi Fellowship (Akademi Ratna) is the highest honour in the performing arts in India
  • Over 1,200 artists have been honoured with the Akademi Award since 1952
  • The Award carries a ₹1 lakh purse, tamrapatra, and angavastram
  • Honourees include legendary musicians, dancers, actors, and directors of every Indian region
  • The honours connect India's regional performing arts into a single national conversation

⏳ Timeline

A journey through the milestones of India's national academy of the performing arts.

1952

Akademi Established

Created by a resolution of the Ministry of Education dated 31 May 1952 — the first national academy of the arts set up by the Republic of India. Early music awards begin as Presidential Awards.

1953

Inauguration

President Dr. Rajendra Prasad inaugurates the Akademi on 28 January 1953 at Parliament House; first Chairman Dr. P. V. Rajamannar appointed.

1954

First Fellows

The Akademi elects its first Fellows, and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Awards become the national honours for practising artists, replacing the earlier Presidential Awards.

1961

Rabindra Bhavan & Registration

The Akademi registers as a society, with its registered office at Rabindra Bhavan, New Delhi — its permanent home and cultural hub.

1986

Akademi Ratna

The honour for lifetime achievement is formalized as the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship (Akademi Ratna Sadasyata), capped at 30 Fellows at any time.

2006

Yuva Puraskar

The Ustad Bismillah Khan Yuva Puraskar is instituted for young artists, nurturing the next generation of musicians, dancers, and theatre makers.

2010

Expanded Fellowship

Following an amendment to the Rules and Regulations, ten additional Fellowship seats are approved, to be filled in phases of two per year.
