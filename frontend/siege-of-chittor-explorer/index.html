⚔️ Rajasthan · 1567–1568

Siege of Chittor Explorer

For four months, Akbar's great army ringed the fortress of Chittorgarh — the proud capital of the Sisodia house of Mewar. Its eight thousand defenders, led by Jaimal and Patta, turned siegecraft into legend, until the third jauhar of Chittor ended one of the most famous sieges in Indian history.

The Proud House of Mewar

Why Akbar Marched on Chittor

Chittorgarh was the greatest fortress of the Sisodia Rajputs — the one Rajput house that had never submitted to Mughal authority. Its capture mattered strategically: it guarded the road between Delhi, Malwa, and Gujarat, and opened the way to the Deccan.

By 1567 Akbar had crushed the Uzbek revolts and was free to turn on Rajputana. Rana Udai Singh II — the son of Rana Sanga, who had defied even Babur — refused to bend the knee and gave refuge to the fugitive Afghan rebel Baz Bahadur of Malwa. Enraged, Akbar declared the campaign against Mewar a jihad, and marched from Agra with his army and artillery.

🏛️ At a Glance

  • 23 October 1567 – 23 February 1568 — A siege of over four months.
  • Akbar in Person — The Mughal emperor commanded the siege himself.
  • ~8,000 Defenders — Jaimal and Patta held the fort against a vast imperial host.
  • Third Jauhar of Chittor — The women of the fort chose fire over capture.
Men Who Decided the Day

The Major Commanders

An emperor who led from the front against a band of Rajput chiefs who chose death over surrender.

Mughal · Emperor

Emperor Akbar

Third Mughal emperor, in command throughout the siege. He directed the sabat, the mines, and the great siege gun — and on 22 February 1568 personally shot Jaimal with his matchlock, breaking the defence's morale.

Mewar · Commander-in-Chief

Jaimal Rathore of Badnore

A famed warrior of the Rathore clan, given the command of Chittor's defence. He led the garrison for four months and was killed by Akbar's shot while repairing the breached wall at night — his death shattered the defenders' will.

Mewar · Command of the Fortress

Patta Sisodia of Kelwa

The young Sisodia chief who took command after Jaimal fell. He led the final saka — the last saffron-clad charge of the garrison — and died fighting, trampled by a Mughal war elephant.

Mewar · Rana (absent from the fort)

Rana Udai Singh II

The Rana of Mewar who withdrew to the hills of the Girwa valley with his family, leaving the fortress to Jaimal and Patta. He founded Udaipur and kept resisting the Mughals until his death in 1572.

The Art of the Siege

The Battle Strategy

Chittor's walls could not be stormed. The siege was won by engineers, miners, and gunners — and by one accurate shot.

1

The Investment

Akbar encamped on the plains before the fort and, over a month of careful reconnaissance, ringed Chittor with siege lines and batteries. The garrison's sallies cost the Mughals dearly before a direct assault was abandoned.

2

The Sabat and the Mines

Some 5,000 builders, stonemasons, and carpenters constructed a great covered approach trench — the sabat, wide enough for ten men abreast — while sappers dug two mines beneath the walls. On 17 December 1567 the mines exploded and breached the rampart, but the defenders sealed the gap.

3

The Great Gun

A massive siege cannon was cast on site and brought forward under the shelter of the sabat, joining three artillery batteries that bombarded the fortress. Under the cover of the trench, Akbar himself took position and fired his matchlock against the defenders.

4

The Breach and the Shot

On 22 February 1568 the guns opened several breaches at once. As Jaimal supervised repairs by torchlight, Akbar's musket shot struck him down — the single stroke that decided the siege and doomed the garrison.

⚔️ The Fall of Chittor

  • 23 February 1568 — The fort fell on the day of Holi, after over four months of siege.
  • Jauhar and Saka — On the night of 22 February the women immolated themselves; the saffron-clad garrison, led by Patta, charged to their deaths in the final saka.
  • Massacre — Akbar ordered a general slaughter; around 30,000 inhabitants were killed.
  • Mughal Victory — The last great independent Rajput fortress had fallen to the empire.
The Third Sack

Why Chittor Was the Turning Point

Chittorgarh had been sacked twice before — by Alauddin Khalji in 1303 and by Bahadur Shah of Gujarat in 1535. The siege of 1567–68 was the third and final time the fortress fell, and its capture broke the back of independent Rajput resistance.

Akbar acknowledged the valour of his fallen foes in an extraordinary way: he had stone statues of Jaimal and Patta carved and set at the gates of his own palace at Agra — honouring the two commanders whose resistance had made the siege famous across India.

The Aftermath

Historical Impact

The fall of Chittor was one of the defining moments of Akbar's reign — and of Mughal empire-building in North India.

🏰

The Chain of Conquest

The great fortresses fell one by one: Ranthambhor (1569) and Kalinjar (1569) followed Chittor. Akbar's sieges of 1567–69 became the template of Mughal expansion.

🤝

The Rajput Alliance

With Chittor gone, almost every major Rajput state — Jodhpur, Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Bundi, Sirohi, Dungarpur — accepted Mughal overlordship, ushering in the era of Rajput generals, marriages, and mansabdars in Akbar's service.

🗺️

A Road to the Deccan

The route to Malwa and Gujarat now lay open, and Mughal armies could march southward at will — preparing the way for the empire's expansion into the Deccan.

🕯️

The Unconquered Spirit

Mewar's defiance became a legend. The Rana's house, though it lost its capital, kept its independence and reclaimed Chittor from the Mughals in 1616, when Jahangir returned it — a symbol that never faded.

Milestones

Chronology of the Siege

From the march on Mewar to the fall of the fortress on the day of Holi.

1562–1567

Defiance of Mewar

While most Rajput houses submit, Rana Udai Singh II holds out, sheltering the fugitive Baz Bahadur. Amber's ruler Bharmal, by contrast, seals an alliance by sending his daughter to the Mughal harem.

September–October 1567

The March and the Investment

Akbar leaves Agra, and by 23 October encamps before Chittor. Udai Singh withdraws to the hills, leaving Jaimal and Patta with about 8,000 men to hold the fortress.

17 December 1567

Two Mines Exploded

Fifty-eight days into the siege, the imperial sappers reach the walls. The mines breach the rampart, but the defenders repair the gap; the assault stalls.

22 February 1568

The Fatal Shot

A coordinated artillery barrage opens several breaches. As Jaimal supervises the night repairs, Akbar's matchlock shot kills him — the morale of the garrison breaks.

23 February 1568

Jauhar, Saka, and Fall

On Holi, the women perform jauhar while the saffron-clad Rajputs, led by Patta, make their last charge. Patta falls and the fort is taken by night.

After 1568

The Empire Consolidated

Ranthambhor and Kalinjar fall in 1569. Akbar raises statues to Jaimal and Patta at Agra, and the Rajput states of Rajasthan enter the Mughal fold.
