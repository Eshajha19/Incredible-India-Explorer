⚔️ Indus River & Northwestern Frontier · 305–303 BCE

Mauryan–Seleucid War Explorer

In 305 BCE, Seleucus I Nicator marched east across the Indus to reclaim Alexander the Great’s former Indian satrapies. He was met by Emperor Chandragupta Maurya, founder of India’s first pan-subcontinental empire. The resulting conflict and subsequent peace treaty shifted four satrapies to Mauryan control, supplied 500 war elephants to the Seleucid army, and initiated centuries of Greco-Indian diplomatic relations.

Historical Conflict

The Clash of Ancient Superpowers

Following the death of Alexander the Great in 323 BCE, his vast empire fractured among his generals (the Diadochi). Seleucus I Nicator consolidated rule over Babylon, Persia, and Central Asia, establishing the Seleucid Empire. Meanwhile in India, Chandragupta Maurya—guided by the master strategist Chanakya (Kautilya)—overthrew the Nanda Dynasty in 321 BCE and annexed the Macedonian garrisons in the Punjab and Northwest.

Seeking to restore Macedonian control over the Indian provinces, Seleucus crossed the Indus River around 305 BCE. Confronted by Chandragupta’s massive army of infantry, cavalry, and hundreds of battle-trained war elephants, Seleucus recognized the impossibility of an outright conquest while his western borders were threatened by rival Diadochi. The two rulers concluded a landmark peace treaty in 303 BCE that redefined ancient geopolitics.

🏛️ War Summary

  • Dates: 305–303 BCE
  • Location: Indus River Valley & Northwestern Frontier (Modern Pakistan & Afghanistan)
  • Primary Rulers: Emperor Chandragupta Maurya vs King Seleucus I Nicator
  • Treaty Terms: Ceding of 4 Hellenistic satrapies, 500 war elephants exchanged, marital alliance (*Epigamia*).
  • Diplomatic Legacy: Appointment of Greek ambassador Megasthenes to the Mauryan court at Pataliputra.
Chronology of Events

Timeline of the War & Treaty

Follow key historical milestones from the fall of Alexander’s garrisons to the establishment of Greco-Indian diplomatic alliances.

323 BCE

Death of Alexander the Great

Alexander dies in Babylon. Power struggles erupt among his generals (the Diadochi). Macedonian control in northwestern India weakens rapidly as native rebellions gain momentum.

321 BCE

Rise of the Mauryan Empire

Chandragupta Maurya and Chanakya overthrow Dhana Nanda, establishing the Mauryan Empire with its capital at Pataliputra. Chandragupta subsequently expels Macedonian satraps from the Punjab and Indus regions.

312 BCE

Establishment of Seleucid Rule

Seleucus I Nicator captures Babylon and consolidates control over Syria, Mesopotamia, and Persia, founding the Seleucid Empire.

305 BCE

Seleucus Crosses the Indus

Seleucus leads a Macedonian army across the Indus River into Northwestern India. Chandragupta mobilizes his grand army, leading to military engagements and a strategic standoff.

303 BCE

The Indus Peace Treaty

The two monarchs sign a historic treaty. Seleucus cedes Arachosia, Gedrosia, Paropamisadae, and parts of Aria to Chandragupta in exchange for 500 war elephants and a marriage alliance (*Epigamia*).

302 BCE

Megasthenes Embassy at Pataliputra

Seleucus sends Megasthenes as an official ambassador to the Mauryan capital at Pataliputra. Megasthenes writes Indika, providing the earliest detailed Greek account of Indian society and administration.

301 BCE

Battle of Ipsus (Phrygia)

Seleucus deploys his 500 Mauryan war elephants at the Battle of Ipsus in Asia Minor, decisively defeating Antigonus Monophthalmus and securing Seleucid hegemony over the Middle East.

Leadership & Diplomats

Key Commanders & Figures

Profiles of the emperors, strategists, and ambassadors who shaped the conflict and peace treaty.

Mauryan Emperor

Chandragupta Maurya

Founder of the Mauryan Empire (r. 321–297 BCE)

Architect of the first unified Indian empire. Unanimously recognized by classical Greek sources (as Sandrokottos) for commanding an army of 600,000 infantry, 30,000 cavalry, and 9,000 war elephants. His tactical discipline compelled Seleucus to seek peace.

Seleucid Emperor

Seleucus I Nicator

Founder of the Seleucid Empire (r. 305–281 BCE)

Former elite general (Somatophylax) of Alexander the Great. Having conquered Persia and Central Asia, he sought to extend Macedonian authority into India but pragmatically negotiated peace to secure his eastern border and acquire war elephants.

Chief Strategist

Chanakya (Kautilya)

Prime Minister & Author of Arthashastra

Legendary political thinker who guided Chandragupta's rise and statecraft. Formulated foreign policy principles of Sāma, Dāma, Bheda, Danḍa (Persuasion, Purchasing, Division, Force) that governed the treaty negotiations.

Greek Ambassador

Megasthenes

Envoy to Pataliputra & Author of Indika

Greek diplomat dispatched by Seleucus to reside at the Mauryan court. His treatise Indika recorded detailed observations on Pataliputra's wooden palisade walls, Indian caste divisions, governance, and royal elephant corps.

Peace Terms & Geography

Territorial Changes & Treaty Outcomes

The Treaty of the Indus (303 BCE) permanently redrew the borders of South Asia and the Near East.

🗺️

Ceding of Four Hellenistic Satrapies

Seleucus ceded control of four eastern satrapies to Chandragupta Maurya:

  • Arachosia: Region surrounding modern Kandahar, Afghanistan.
  • Gedrosia: Coastal region covering modern Balochistan.
  • Paropamisadae: Region surrounding Kabul and the Hindu Kush.
  • Aria: Southeastern territories surrounding Herat.
🐘

500 Indian War Elephants

In exchange for the western provinces, Chandragupta supplied Seleucus with 500 war-trained Indian elephants along with experienced mahouts. These elephants proved decisive at the Battle of Ipsus (301 BCE), allowing Seleucus to block Antigonus's cavalry and win the battle.

💍

Dynastic Marriage Alliance (Epigamia)

The treaty established an Epigamia (right of intermarriage between Greeks and Indians or a royal marital alliance). Historical sources record that a daughter of Seleucus was married into the Mauryan royal family, cementing peace between the dynasties.

📜

Greco-Indian Diplomatic Axis

The settlement opened permanent diplomatic channels. Subsequent Hellenistic rulers (Deimachus sent by Antiochus I, Dionysius sent by Ptolemy II) continued as resident ambassadors at Pataliputra during Bindusara and Ashoka's reigns.

Bibliography & Sources

References & Ancient Texts

Primary Greco-Roman historical accounts and authoritative modern historical scholarship.

Syriaca (The Syrian Wars)

Author: Appian of Alexandria

Primary Greek history documenting Seleucus's campaign across the Indus, the transfer of 500 war elephants, and the marital alliance.

Geographica (Book XV)

Author: Strabo

Comprehensive geographical treatise detailing the ceding of Arachosia, Gedrosia, Paropamisadae, and Aria to Chandragupta Maurya.

Epitome of Pompeius Trogus

Author: Justin (Marcus Junianus Justinus)

Latin history recounting Sandrokottos's (Chandragupta's) overthrow of the Nanda Dynasty and his treaty with Seleucus.

Asoka and the Decline of the Mauryas

Author: Romila Thapar

Authoritative modern study on Mauryan imperial borders, administrative organization, and diplomatic relations with Hellenistic kingdoms.
