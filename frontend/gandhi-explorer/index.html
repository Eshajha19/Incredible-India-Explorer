📜 Biography

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (2 October 1869 – 30 January 1948) was an Indian lawyer, anti-colonial nationalist, and political ethicist who led India's successful campaign for independence from British rule. Revered as "Mahatma" (Great Soul) and affectionately called "Bapu" (Father), he inspired movements for civil rights and freedom across the world through his philosophy of nonviolence.

Born in Porbandar, Gujarat, to Karamchand Gandhi and Putlibai, he trained as a barrister at the Inner Temple in London before moving to South Africa in 1893, where he spent over two decades developing his method of nonviolent civil resistance, Satyagraha (truth-force), while campaigning against discriminatory laws targeting Indian residents.

Returning to India in 1915, Gandhi gradually assumed leadership of the Indian National Congress, reshaping it into a mass movement. He championed the use of homespun cloth (Khadi) and the boycott of foreign goods (Swadeshi) as tools of economic self-reliance and symbolic defiance of British rule.

He led national campaigns for easing poverty, expanding women's rights, building religious and ethnic amity, ending untouchability, and above all for achieving Swaraj (self-rule). His most celebrated campaigns included the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22), the Salt Satyagraha or Dandi March (1930), and the Quit India Movement (1942), each escalating pressure on British colonial authority through disciplined, nonviolent mass action.

Gandhi's life was cut short on 30 January 1948, when he was assassinated in New Delhi. His philosophy of Ahimsa and Satyagraha would go on to directly influence later civil rights and freedom movements around the world, including those led by Martin Luther King Jr. and Nelson Mandela.

    1869

    Born in Porbandar, Gujarat

    Born on 2 October to Karamchand and Putlibai Gandhi, into a Vaishnava Hindu family in the coastal town of Porbandar, then part of a princely state.

    1888

    Sails to London to study law

    Travelled to England to train as a barrister at the Inner Temple, adapting to a new culture while holding firmly to vows made to his mother.

    1893

    Moves to South Africa

    Took up legal work in South Africa, where firsthand experience of racial discrimination shaped his early political consciousness.

    1906

    Develops Satyagraha

    Formulated and first applied the philosophy of Satyagraha (truth-force) in a campaign against discriminatory registration laws in the Transvaal.

    1915

    Returns to India

    Came back to India after two decades abroad and, under the guidance of Gopal Krishna Gokhale, began travelling the country to understand its people.

    1920

    Launches Non-Cooperation Movement

    Called on Indians to withdraw cooperation from British institutions following the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, marking his rise as the movement's principal leader.

    1930

    Salt Satyagraha (Dandi March)

    Led a 240-mile march to the coastal village of Dandi to defy the British salt tax monopoly, drawing global attention to the independence struggle.

    1942

    Quit India Movement

    Launched the Quit India Movement with the rallying cry "Do or Die," demanding an end to British rule and triggering the movement's final phase.

    1947

    Indian Independence

    Witnessed India's independence on 15 August, while also working to quell communal violence surrounding the Partition.

    1948

    Assassination

    Assassinated on 30 January in New Delhi, bringing his life to an end but cementing his legacy as the Father of the Nation.

    ✊ Major Movements

    The three defining campaigns of nonviolent resistance that Gandhi led against British colonial rule.

    🤝

    Non-Cooperation Movement

    1920 – 1922

    Launched in response to the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and the Rowlatt Act, this campaign called on Indians to withdraw cooperation from British courts, schools, titles, and goods — turning noncooperation into a mass, nationwide movement for the first time.

    🧂

    Salt Satyagraha (Dandi March)

    1930

    A 240-mile, 24-day march from Sabarmati Ashram to the coastal village of Dandi, undertaken to defy the British monopoly on salt production. The act of making salt from seawater became a powerful symbol of defiance that galvanized civil disobedience across India.

    ✊

    Quit India Movement

    1942

    Launched at the height of World War II with the call to "Do or Die," this movement demanded an immediate end to British rule. It led to the mass arrest of Congress leadership, yet marked the final, decisive phase of the independence struggle.

    🕊️ Legacy & Impact

    The enduring imprint of Bapu's philosophy across India and the world.

    A Global Philosophy

    Gandhi's philosophy of Ahimsa (nonviolence) and Satyagraha (truth-force) went on to directly inspire civil rights and independence movements far beyond India — most notably Martin Luther King Jr.'s leadership of the American civil rights movement and Nelson Mandela's early activism in South Africa.

    Self-Reliance & Khadi

    His promotion of Khadi (homespun cloth) and village-level self-reliance shaped India's economic thinking well beyond independence, and the humble charkha (spinning wheel) remains a national symbol of self-sufficiency.

    Social Reform

    Gandhi campaigned tirelessly against untouchability, for women's participation in public life, and for religious harmony between India's communities — ideals enshrined in India's Constitution and continually invoked in its public life.

    Remembering Bapu

    Gandhi Jayanti (2 October) is a national holiday in India and is observed internationally as the United Nations International Day of Non-Violence. Statues, memorials, roads, and institutions across the world bear his name and legacy.

    🕊️ Remembering the Father of the Nation

    From Porbandar to Dandi to Delhi, Mahatma Gandhi's life was a sustained experiment in truth and nonviolence. Every act of peaceful protest that follows his example carries forward the conviction that even an empire can be moved without raising a fist.
