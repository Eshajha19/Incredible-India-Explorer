📜 History of the Sahitya Akademi

The Sahitya Akademi (National Academy of Letters) is India's national academy for literature. The idea of a National Cultural Trust for India was first accepted in principle in 1944, on a proposal from the Royal Asiatic Society of Bengal, envisaging three academies — one for letters, one for the visual arts, and one for dance, drama and music.

After Independence, the Government of India constituted the Sahitya Akademi by a resolution dated 15 December 1952, and it was formally inaugurated on 12 March 1954 in the Central Hall of Parliament, New Delhi. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad welcomed the gathering, and Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, then Vice President of India, delivered the inaugural address.

The first General Council was presided over by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, the Akademi's first Chairman, with Dr. Radhakrishnan elected its Vice-Chairman. The Government explained that Nehru was chosen "not because he is Prime Minister, but because he has carved out for himself a distinctive place as a writer and author." The Akademi was registered as a society on 7 January 1956 under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, and functions as an autonomous body of the Ministry of Culture, headquartered at Rabindra Bhavan, 35 Ferozeshah Road, New Delhi.

Mandate & Objectives

  • To work actively for the development of Indian letters and to set high literary standards.
  • To foster and co-ordinate literary activities in all the Indian languages.
  • To promote, through literature, the cultural unity of the country.
  • To honour outstanding books of literary merit with the annual Sahitya Akademi Awards.
  • To publish original works, translations, and reference books, and to document Indian literature.

Headquarters & National Presence

The Akademi is headquartered at Rabindra Bhavan, New Delhi — the same complex that houses the Sangeet Natak Akademi and the Lalit Kala Akademi. It works through a General Council, an Executive Board, and a Finance Committee representing every region of India, maintains regional offices, and has published over 7,000 books across its 24 recognized languages.

Flagship Honours

The Akademi confers the Sahitya Akademi Fellowship — its highest honour, reserved for the "immortals of literature" — the Sahitya Akademi Award for the most outstanding books published each year, and, since the 1980s and 2010s, the Prize for Translation, the Yuva Puraskar, the Bal Sahitya Puraskar, and the Bhasha Samman.

📅 Year Instituted

The Sahitya Akademi was constituted by a Government of India resolution on 15 December 1952 and formally inaugurated on 12 March 1954. The Sahitya Akademi Award itself was first conferred in 1955, and has been given every year since to the most outstanding books of literary merit published in the languages recognized by the Akademi.

📅 Akademi Established

Constituted by Government resolution dated 15 December 1952; formally inaugurated on 12 March 1954 in the Central Hall of Parliament, New Delhi.

🏅 First Awards

The first Sahitya Akademi Awards were conferred in 1955, honouring outstanding books published in the preceding year in languages such as Hindi, Bengali, and Assamese.

💵 Purse Today

Each Award carries an engraved copper plaque, a shawl, and a cash purse of ₹1,00,000 — raised progressively from ₹5,000 at inception to the present amount in 2009.

🖋️ The Plaque

The award plaque was designed by the renowned filmmaker Satyajit Ray, and today holds an engraved citation to the winning book and author.

📋 Eligibility & Award Details

The Sahitya Akademi Award recognizes the most outstanding book of literary merit published in any of the 24 languages recognized by the Akademi. The award honours the book itself, and hence the author who wrote it.

🏅 What is Honoured

The most outstanding original book of literary merit published during the year preceding the award, in any of the 24 recognized Indian languages.

📚 Eligible Genres

Fiction, poetry, drama, essays and literary criticism, biography and autobiography, travel literature, and other creative and scholarly prose of high literary merit.

👥 The Jury

Each language has a three-member jury of eminent writers and scholars who screen the books and make unanimous or majority selections.

🗳️ Final Approval

Jury selections are placed before the Akademi's Executive Board, which declares the awards on the basis of the jury's unanimous or majority recommendations.

🎁 Award Components

A casket containing an engraved copper plaque designed by Satyajit Ray, a shawl, and a cheque for ₹1,00,000 — the amount in force since 2009.

🌍 No Barriers of Creed

The Award is open to writers of Indian nationality in the recognized languages, without distinction of caste, creed, religion, or region.

🌐 Languages Covered

The Sahitya Akademi recognizes 24 languages — the 22 languages of the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India, together with English and Rajasthani. Awards are conferred for the most outstanding book published in each language.

Growing the Family of Languages

  • From the original languages at inception, the Akademi has steadily added Dogri, Maithili, Manipuri, Rajasthani, Konkani, Nepali, and Sindhi over the decades.
  • Bodo and Santhali were the most recent additions, completing the 24 languages in 2005.
  • English has been honoured since 1960, when R. K. Narayan's The Guide became the first English book to win.

🎯 Selection Process

The selection of the Sahitya Akademi Awards is a disciplined, multi-tier annual process that moves from publication to presentation, guided by three-member juries in each language.

1

Publication

Books of literary merit published in the preceding year in the 24 recognized languages are brought into consideration by the Akademi.

2

Submission & Collection

Authors and publishers submit copies of eligible books, and the Akademi collects the year's significant publications in each language.

3

Language Juries

A three-member jury of eminent writers and scholars in each language screens the books and recommends the most outstanding work.

4

Executive Board Approval

The selections — made unanimously or by majority vote — are placed before the Akademi's Executive Board, which approves and declares the awards.

5

Festival of Letters

The awards are presented at the Akademi's annual Festival of Letters (Akhil Bharatiya Sahityotsav), where the engraved plaque, shawl, and cheque are conferred.

Evaluation Focus Areas

  • Literary Merit: Distinction of language, craft, and imagination
  • Originality: Freshness of form, thought, and expression
  • Linguistic Excellence: Mastery of the language of the work
  • New Trends: Works that acknowledge and shape new literary directions
  • National Significance: Contribution to the literature of India as a whole

🗂️ Categories & Honours

Beyond the flagship Sahitya Akademi Award, the Akademi confers a family of honours recognizing writers, translators, young authors, children's literature, and languages beyond the recognized 24.

📖 Sahitya Akademi Award

The flagship honour since 1955 — given annually to the most outstanding book in each of the 24 recognized languages, carrying a plaque, shawl, and ₹1,00,000.

💎 Sahitya Akademi Fellowship

The highest honour of the Akademi — reserved for the "immortals of literature", and limited to twenty-one Fellows at any given time.

🔁 Prize for Translation

Instituted in 1989 for outstanding translations among the 24 languages, carrying a purse of ₹50,000 — celebrating the bridges between India's literatures.

🌱 Yuva Puraskar

Instituted in 2011 for writers aged 35 and below, nurturing the next generation of Indian writers with a plaque and ₹50,000.

🧒 Bal Sahitya Puraskar

Instituted in 2010 for outstanding contributions to children's literature, encouraging the earliest readers of Indian languages.

🗣️ Bhasha Samman

Instituted in 1996 for writers and scholars contributing to languages beyond the recognized 24, and to classical and medieval literature — carrying ₹1,00,000.

Impact of the Honours

  • The Sahitya Akademi Award is considered India's most prestigious literary award after the Jnanpith
  • Awards are conferred for books in 24 languages — 22 Eighth Schedule languages plus English and Rajasthani
  • Each Award carries a plaque, shawl, and ₹1,00,000 purse
  • Honourees include poets, novelists, playwrights, and scholars of every Indian region
  • The honours connect India's regional literatures into a single national conversation

⏳ Timeline

A journey through the milestones of India's national academy of letters.

1944

Seed of an Idea

The Royal Asiatic Society of Bengal proposes a National Cultural Trust; the Government accepts the idea of a National Academy of Letters in principle.

1952

Constituted

The Government of India constitutes the Sahitya Akademi by a resolution dated 15 December 1952.

1954

Inauguration

Formally inaugurated on 12 March 1954 in the Central Hall of Parliament; PM Jawaharlal Nehru chairs the first General Council as the first Chairman.

1955

First Awards

The first Sahitya Akademi Awards are conferred for outstanding books published in the preceding year.

1956

Registered as a Society

The Akademi is registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, on 7 January 1956, cementing its autonomy.

1959–1977

Family of Languages Grows

Sindhi (1959), Maithili (1966), Dogri (1970), Manipuri (1973), Rajasthani (1974), and Konkani and Nepali (1977) are brought into the awards.

1989

Prize for Translation

The Akademi institutes an annual Prize for Translation for outstanding translations among the 24 recognized languages.

1996

Bhasha Samman

The Bhasha Samman is instituted for contribution to languages beyond the recognized 24, and to classical and medieval literature.

2005

24 Languages Complete

Bodo and Santhali are added, completing the 24 recognized languages of the Akademi.

2009

Purse Raised to ₹1 Lakh

The Award purse is raised to ₹1,00,000, up from ₹5,000 at inception in 1955.

2010–2011

New Honours for New Readers

The Bal Sahitya Puraskar (2010) for children's literature and the Yuva Puraskar (2011) for writers under 35 are instituted.

💡 Interesting Facts

Curious corners of the Sahitya Akademi's story — from wartime plaques to the pen behind the awards.

🖋️ Designed by Satyajit Ray

The award plaque was designed by the legendary filmmaker and writer Satyajit Ray.

💼 Plaques of Marble

Early plaques were occasionally made of marble, a practice discontinued because of their excessive weight.

🪖 Savings Bonds in Wartime

During the Indo-Pakistan War of 1965, the plaque was substituted with national savings bonds.

👑 A Prime Minister First

PM Jawaharlal Nehru was the first Chairman — chosen, in the Government's words, for being a writer, not a Prime Minister.

💎 Twenty-One Immortals

The Sahitya Akademi Fellowship — the highest honour — is limited to just 21 Fellows at any time.

🎪 Festival of Letters

Awards are presented each year at the Akhil Bharatiya Sahityotsav (Festival of Letters) in New Delhi.

🏛️ Three Academies, One Roof

Rabindra Bhavan houses all three national academies — Sahitya, Sangeet Natak, and Lalit Kala.

📚 7,000 Books and Counting

The Akademi has published over 7,000 books and produced over 145 documentaries on eminent Indian writers.
