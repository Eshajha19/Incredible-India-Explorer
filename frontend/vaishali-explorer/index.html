The Vajji Confederacy & Licchavi Democracy

Vaishali, situated in modern-day Bihar, was the capital of the Vajji Confederacy (a union of eight clans, of which the Licchavis were the most prominent) during the 6th century BCE. It is widely recognized as the world's first democratic republic.

Unlike the absolute monarchies of the Magadha or Kosala empires, the Licchavis practiced a form of republicanism where power was held by an assembly of chiefs (Rajas) representing various clans.

Sacred Spiritual Crossroads

Vaishali holds profound religious significance for both Buddhism and Jainism. Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism, was born in Kundagrama (a suburb of Vaishali), where he spent his early years.

For Buddhists, Vaishali is where Gautama Buddha preached his last sermon, admitted women into the monastic order (Sangha) for the first time by ordaining Mahaprajapati Gautami, and where the historic Second Buddhist Council was convened a century after his Mahaparinirvana.
