Warli Art

The ancient tribal language of Maharashtra told through circles and triangles.

Ancient Origins

Warli painting is a style of tribal art practiced by the Warli tribe of Maharashtra. It is believed to date back to 2500-3000 BCE, making it one of the oldest surviving art forms in India. Unlike many other Indian folk arts that depict mythological deities, Warli art focuses strictly on the secular, everyday life of the tribe.

Visual Language

The art is strikingly minimalist. It uses only basic geometric shapes—circles, triangles, and squares—derived from observing nature. The circle represents the sun and moon, the triangle represents mountains and conical trees, and the square represents a sacred plot of land.

Decoding the Symbols

⭕

The Circle

Represents the celestial bodies (Sun and Moon) and the cycle of life and death. There is no straight line in nature, hence the prominence of the circle.

🔺

The Triangle

Derived from the observation of mountains and trees. Two triangles joined at the top represent a tree, while joined at the bottom, they form the human body.

⬛

The Square (Chauk)

The most sacred motif. The "Chauk" is drawn during weddings and harvest festivals. It always contains the goddess Palaghata (the goddess of fertility) in the center.

〰️

The Tarpa Dance

A common motif showing villagers dancing in a spiral formation around a Tarpa (trumpet-like instrument) player, symbolizing community and the cycle of life.

Traditional Materials

Warli art is traditionally painted on the mud walls of tribal huts. The preparation is deeply connected to the earth:

  • Base: A mixture of cow dung and mud creates the rough, brown canvas.
  • Pigment: Rice paste mixed with water and a pinch of gum acts as the white paint.
  • Brush: A simple chewed bamboo stick or a twig from the date palm tree.
