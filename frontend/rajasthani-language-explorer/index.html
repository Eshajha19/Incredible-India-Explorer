राजस्थानी

A cluster of Western Indo-Aryan language varieties native to Rajasthan, spoken by over 50 million people and carrying centuries of desert folklore, bardic poetry, and regional pride.

Greeting

खम्मा घणी

Khamma Ghani

pronounced roughly khum-maa gha-nee

Traditionally offered with folded hands and a slight bow. Khamma traces to Sanskrit for respect and well-wishing, and ghaṇī means "a lot" — together, "many greetings." The customary reply is घणी खम्मा (ghaṇī khammā).

Language family

Indo-European Indo-Iranian Indo-Aryan Western Indo-Aryan Rajasthani

Rajasthani descends from Shauraseni Prakrit and is traditionally written in the Devanagari script, though a historic Mahajani (Modiya) script was once used by merchant communities. It is spoken across Rajasthan and spills into neighbouring Haryana, Gujarat, the Malwa and Nimar regions of Madhya Pradesh, Pakistan's Bahawalpur division, and Sindh — carried further afield by generations of Marwari traders.

Literature and culture

Rajasthani's literary voice grew out of the Charani bardic tradition, split broadly into Dingal — vigorous heroic poetry praising Rajput valour — and Pingal, its more lyrical counterpart. The best-known performance tradition is the phad: a long painted scroll depicting a folk deity's life, sung by Bhopa performers to the sound of a ravanhatha fiddle. Pabuji ki Phad, recounting the folk hero-deity Pabuji, is the most celebrated of these epics.

In the modern era, writer Vijaydan Detha ("Bijji") built Bataan ri Phulwari, a fourteen-volume collection retelling Rajasthani folk tales, and co-founded Rupayan Sansthan to document the region's oral heritage. India's Sahitya Akademi recognises Rajasthani for its own literary awards, even though the language is not yet included in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution — a status long sought by language activists in the state.
