Khajuraho Explorer

Step into the Khajuraho Group of Monuments — the UNESCO World Heritage temple complex of the Chandela dynasty, where Nagara-style towers, intricate sandstone sculptures and centuries of heritage rise above the plains of Madhya Pradesh.

Where Stone Meets the Sky

An Introduction to the Khajuraho Complex

Khajuraho is a historic town in the Chhatarpur district of Madhya Pradesh, set in the Bundelkhand region of central India. Between the 10th and 12th centuries CE, the Chandela dynasty built an extraordinary complex of Hindu and Jain temples here, celebrated today for their soaring Nagara-style shikharas and the remarkable density of sculpture that covers their walls.

An estimated 85 temples once stood at Khajuraho; around 25 survive, grouped into the Western, Eastern and Southern clusters. The temples are masterpieces of medieval Indian art and are protected by the Archaeological Survey of India as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

A Legacy in Stone

History of the Khajuraho Temples

The temples were built during the reign of the Chandela dynasty of Jejakabhukti, who made Khajuraho one of their capitals. The complex is thought to have been a spiritual, cultural and educational hub, and in its prime served as a meeting ground for traditions of Shaivism, Vaishnavism, Jainism and the Tantric sects.

🏰

A Capital of the Chandelas

The Chandelas rose from feudatory status under the Gurjara-Pratiharas to become sovereign rulers of Jejakabhukti, with Khajuraho (known in inscriptions as Kharjuravahaka) serving as a major Chandela capital alongside Kalinjar and Mahoba.

🛕

Generations of Temple Building

Successive Chandela kings added to the complex over roughly two centuries. The Chausath Yogini Temple is among the earliest survivors, while the Lakshmana and Vishvanatha temples mark the reigns of Yashovarman and Dhanga, and the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple crowns the era of Vidyadhara.

🗺️

Rediscovery in the Modern Era

After centuries of obscurity, the temples were brought to the wider world's attention in 1838 by the British engineer T. S. Burt, whose reports of the site drew archaeological and public interest that eventually led to restoration and World Heritage recognition.

🛕 Nagara Style at a Glance

  • Shikhara: Soaring curvilinear tower rising in tiers above the sanctum.
  • Sandstone: Built from locally quarried sandstone blocks, laid without mortar.
  • Plan: Sanctum, vestibule, halls and entrance porch aligned on an east–west axis.
  • Ornament: Walls wrapped in densely carved bands of deities, dancers and celestial figures.
The Northern Style Perfected

Temple Architecture at Khajuraho

The Khajuraho temples are built in the Nagara style characteristic of northern India. Their distinctive feature is the shikhara — a tall, curvilinear tower that rises in gradually narrowing tiers above the sanctum, crowned by an amalaka, a ribbed disc-like finial. The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple carries the tallest surviving shikhara at the site, soaring about 31 metres high.

Each temple is raised on a plinth and laid out along an east–west axis, typically moving from an entrance porch through halls to the inner sanctum. Because the sandstone blocks are set without mortar, careful weight distribution keeps these structures standing — and their builders had no room for error, since a wall's stability relied entirely on precision of cut and placement.

Gems of the Complex

Major Temples of Khajuraho

From the towering Kandariya Mahadeva to the granite circle of the Chausath Yogini, each surviving temple contributes its own chapter to the story of Khajuraho.

⛰️

Kandariya Mahadeva

The largest and most ornate temple at Khajuraho, built in the mid-11th century CE under Vidyadhara. Its 31-metre shikhara and densely carved walls make it the artistic climax of the complex.

🦅

Lakshmana Temple

Dedicated to Vishnu and begun under Yashovarman in the 10th century CE, this Vaishnava temple features a richly sculpted band of architecture and friezes celebrating the deeds of its patron.

🔱

Vishvanatha Temple

Commissioned by Dhanga around 999 CE and dedicated to Shiva, this temple introduced the fully developed Nagara shikhara that later temples, including the Kandariya Mahadeva, would magnify.

☀️

Chitragupta Temple

A late 10th-century temple in the Western Group dedicated to Surya, the sun god, with notable sculptural panels including a large image of Surya driving his chariot.

🕉️

Parshvanatha Temple

The largest of the Jain temples at Khajuraho, its carvings rival the Hindu temples in quality while serving a distinctly Jain sanctuary dedicated to the tirthankara Parshvanatha.

🧘

Chausath Yogini Temple

Dedicated to the 64 yoginis and thought to be the earliest surviving monument at Khajuraho, this granite, open-air temple stands on a hilltop in the Western Group.

Stone Given Life

Sculptures and Carvings

Khajuraho is celebrated above all for its sculpture. The walls of the great temples carry thousands of figures carved in sandstone — deities, celestial nymphs known as apsaras, musicians, dancers, warriors, lovers and scenes of daily and courtly life. The carvings are arranged in horizontal bands that wrap around the exterior, with the density reaching its peak on the Kandariya Mahadeva.

Far from being purely decorative, these images carry deep religious meaning: they embody the Indian artistic tradition of depicting the entire span of human experience, from the mundane to the divine, as part of a sacred whole. The celebrated erotic panels form only a small part of this vast sculptural programme, which scholars such as Devangana Desai have interpreted within the Tantric and Shaiva traditions of the time.

🗿 Sculptural Themes

  • Deities: Shiva, Vishnu, Surya, the goddesses and their celestial attendants.
  • Apsaras: Graceful celestial nymphs in dancing, adorning and toilette poses.
  • Music & Dance: Drummers, cymbal players, dancers and entire orchestras in stone.
  • Daily Life: Teachers, scribes, wrestlers, processions and royal court scenes.
  • Mithuna: Couples in loving poses, a small but famous part of the repertoire.
Faiths Side by Side

Cultural and Religious Heritage

Khajuraho is one of the finest surviving expressions of medieval Indian pluralism — a single complex where Hindu and Jain sanctuaries stood together for generations.

🔱

Shaivism

Shiva temples dominate the complex, from the Vishvanatha to the Kandariya Mahadeva, reflecting the Chandela court's strong Shaiva patronage alongside Tantric traditions.

🦅

Vaishnavism

Vishnu temples such as the Lakshmana and the Chaturbhuja temple show that Vaishnava worship flourished equally under the same dynasty.

🕉️

Jainism

Jain temples, including the Parshvanatha and Adinatha temples of the Eastern Group, attest to a thriving Jain community and royal tolerance across faiths.

💃

The Khajuraho Dance Festival

Every year, the Khajuraho Dance Festival sets classical Indian dance performances against the temple backdrop — a living continuation of the tradition of music and dance carved into the stones.

🌍 UNESCO World Heritage

  • Inscribed: 1986.
  • Criteria: (i) — a masterpiece of human creative genius; (iii) — an exceptional testimony to a cultural tradition.
  • Area: The core zone covers the Western, Eastern and Southern groups of temples.
  • Management: Conserved by the Archaeological Survey of India.
A Global Treasure

UNESCO Recognition

In 1986, the Khajuraho Group of Monuments was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognised as a masterpiece of human creative genius and an exceptional testimony to the cultural traditions of medieval India. The nomination acknowledged both the architectural achievement of the Nagara-style temples and the outstanding sculptural artistry that covers them.

Today the site is a symbol of Indian heritage worldwide, drawing visitors from every continent and placing Bundelkhand's medieval temples among the most celebrated monuments of Asia.

Milestones

Chronology of the Khajuraho Temples

From the first Chandela shrines to World Heritage recognition, the key moments in the life of the complex.

c. 9th–10th century CE

The Earliest Shrines

The Chausath Yogini Temple and other early monuments are raised at Khajuraho, marking the beginnings of the complex.

c. 950 CE

The Lakshmana Temple

Yashovarman commissions the Lakshmana Temple, one of the finest early monuments of the complex.

c. 999 CE

The Vishvanatha Temple

Dhanga dedicates the Vishvanatha Temple, which sets the fully developed Nagara form for later builders.

c. 1025–1050 CE

The Kandariya Mahadeva

Vidyadhara commissions the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple, the tallest and most richly carved monument at Khajuraho.

12th century CE

Decline of the Chandela Age

Temple building tapers off as Chandela power fades; the site gradually recedes from public memory.

1838

Rediscovery

The British engineer T. S. Burt reports the overgrown temples, sparking fresh interest in Khajuraho.

1986

World Heritage Inscription

The Khajuraho Group of Monuments is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

Did You Know?

Interesting Facts

85 Temples, 25 Survivors

Inscriptions and accounts suggest that about 85 temples once stood at Khajuraho; only around 25 survive today.

No Mortar, No Error

The temples were built from sandstone blocks fitted together without mortar — each course had to be cut with total precision.

No Two Alike

Every temple at Khajuraho differs from every other in plan, proportion or ornament — a hallmark of the builder's individual genius.

Hindu and Jain Together

The complex uniquely preserves Hindu and Jain temples standing side by side, built by the same dynasty for different faiths.

Granite Beginning

The oldest surviving monument, the Chausath Yogini Temple, is built of granite — a rare departure from the sandstone of later temples.

Heritage in Motion

The annual Khajuraho Dance Festival keeps the complex a living cultural venue, with classical dance performed before the temple walls.
