👑 Magadha · Pataliputra, Modern-Day Bihar

Nanda Dynasty Explorer

Step into the empire that ruled Magadha before the Mauryas — a dynasty of low-caste origin that raised the largest army in the Indian subcontinent, filled the treasury of Pataliputra, and made even Alexander the Great's soldiers refuse to march further.

From Shishunaga Vassals to Sole Rulers

The First Great Empire of Magadha

The Nanda dynasty ruled the kingdom of Magadha in eastern India for roughly two to three decades in the mid-to-late 4th century BCE, with its capital at Pataliputra, near present-day Patna. It came to power after overthrowing the ruling Shishunaga dynasty, and is remembered as the first dynasty to weld a large part of northern India into a single, centrally administered empire.

Puranic tradition holds that the dynasty's founder, Mahapadma Nanda, was the son of the last Shishunaga king and a woman of low social standing — Jain sources and the Greek historian Curtius go further, describing his father as a barber. Whatever the exact truth, ancient texts agree that the Nandas rose from outside the traditional Kshatriya ruling class, which made their conquests all the more remarkable, and earned Mahapadma the title "Sarva-Kshatriyantaka," destroyer of Kshatriyas.

📜 At a Glance

  • Region: Magadha, with a capital at Pataliputra (modern Patna, Bihar).
  • Founder: Mahapadma Nanda, c. mid-4th century BCE.
  • Last ruler: Dhana Nanda, overthrown c. 321 BCE.
  • Known for: A vast standing army, centralised taxation, and the empire's fall to Chandragupta Maurya.
Nine Kings in a Single Line

Major Rulers of the Nanda Dynasty

Ancient sources disagree on the exact number and names of Nanda kings, but Buddhist, Jain and Puranic traditions together describe a short but dominant dynasty bookended by two towering figures.

🏹

Mahapadma Nanda

The dynasty's founder overthrew the Shishunaga rulers of Magadha and expanded his territory from the Kuru region in the northwest to the Godavari valley in the south, reportedly ruling for around 28 years and unifying much of northern India for the first time.

👑

The Later Nandas

Buddhist tradition names eight successors after Mahapadma, sometimes called the "Nava Nandas" (nine Nandas) collectively. Their individual reigns are only sketchily recorded, but Puranic and Buddhist sources agree the dynasty as a whole lasted a few decades.

💰

Dhana Nanda

The last Nanda king was renowned even among the Greeks — recorded as "Agrammes" or "Xandrames" — for his immense wealth and an army so large it reportedly discouraged Alexander's own soldiers from advancing on Magadha. He was eventually deposed by Chandragupta Maurya.

⚔️ The Nanda War Machine

  • Infantry: Greek accounts credit the Nandas with around 200,000 foot soldiers.
  • Cavalry: An estimated 20,000 to 80,000 horsemen, depending on the source.
  • War elephants: Between 3,000 and 6,000 elephants, the single most feared element of the army.
  • Chariots: Roughly 2,000 to 8,000 four-horse war chariots.
The Army That Turned Back Alexander

A Deterrent Even to the Greeks

When Alexander the Great's army reached the Hyphasis (the modern Beas River) in 326 BCE after defeating King Porus, his exhausted soldiers mutinied and refused to march further east. Greek historians such as Diodorus, Curtius and Plutarch record that fear of the Nanda and Gangaridai armies — described as fielding tens of thousands of cavalry, hundreds of thousands of infantry, and thousands of war elephants — was a decisive factor in the retreat.

Whatever the precise numbers, the episode reflects how formidable the Nanda military reputation had become by the late 4th century BCE, marking the easternmost point Alexander's campaign ever reached in the Indian subcontinent.

An Administrative Blueprint

Legacy: Governing a Vast Empire

Beyond their army, the Nandas are remembered for building the administrative machinery that later Mauryan rulers inherited and refined.

🏛️

Centralised Administration

The Nandas built on the administrative foundations of their Haryanka and Shishunaga predecessors, creating a more centralised bureaucracy capable of governing a much larger territory from Pataliputra.

📊

Systematic Taxation

Ancient sources describe a well-organised system for assessing and collecting taxes, which filled the Nanda treasury with legendary wealth — though it also made the dynasty unpopular with its subjects.

🪙

A Model for the Mauryas

Many of the Nandas' fiscal and administrative methods were carried forward largely unchanged by the Mauryan Empire, which inherited both the Nanda capital of Pataliputra and much of its governing apparatus.

Milestones

Chronology of the Nanda Dynasty

Tracing the Nandas from Mahapadma's seizure of Magadha to Dhana Nanda's fall before Chandragupta Maurya.

c. mid-4th century BCE

Mahapadma Overthrows the Shishunagas

Mahapadma Nanda deposes the last Shishunaga ruler of Magadha and founds the Nanda dynasty at Pataliputra.

c. 345–338 BCE

Expansion Across Northern India

Mahapadma's campaigns extend Nanda territory from the Kuru region in the northwest to the Godavari valley in the south, and east to Kalinga.

326 BCE

Alexander's Army Halts at the Hyphasis

Fear of the Nanda and Gangaridai armies contributes to the mutiny of Alexander's soldiers at the Beas River, ending his advance into the Gangetic plains.

c. 329–321 BCE

Reign of Dhana Nanda

The last Nanda king rules Magadha from Pataliputra, commanding immense wealth and, by Greek accounts, the largest standing army in the region.

c. 321 BCE

Fall to Chandragupta Maurya

Chandragupta Maurya, guided by his advisor Chanakya, raises an army that defeats Dhana Nanda and captures Pataliputra, ending Nanda rule and founding the Mauryan Empire.
