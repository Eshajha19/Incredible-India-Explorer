Meru Peak Explorer

Rising dramatically as a stunning three-peaked massif to 6,660 meters, Meru Peak is one of the most stunning and visually striking peaks in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand. Its sheer vertical granite walls and complex ice ridges make it home to the 'Shark's Fin' and a legendary alpine climbing arena.

Geography, Glaciers & History

The Sacred Sanctuary

Meru Peak is located in the Gangotri Group of peaks in the western Garhwal Himalaya, guarding the source of the holy Ganges. Situated just opposite the Bhagirathi massif, it towers directly above Tapovan, a serene, high-altitude meadow renowned for meditation and spiritual pilgrimage.

The mountain rises as a single peak It is world-renowned for its three distinct summits: South (6,660m), Central (6,310m), and North (6,450m), with the Central peak hosting the infamous Shark's Fin.

The highest Southern peak was first climbed in 1980 by a Japanese team, while the technically daunting Shark's Fin (Central Peak) saw its first successful ascent in 2011 by an American team. Meru Peak has since seen legendary alpine climbs, winning global acclaim for pioneers of modern climbing styles.

Technical Specifications

  • Elevation: 6,660 m (21,850 ft)
  • Prominence: 1,046 m (3,432 ft)
  • Coordinates: 30°52'05"N 79°01'56"E
  • Climbing Difficulty: Grade V/VI (Extreme / Alpine)
  • First Ascent: 1980 (South Peak) / 2011 (Shark's Fin)
  • Expedition Team: Japanese Team (1980) / Anker, Chin, Ozturk (2011)
