Arishina Gundi Falls

📖 Introduction

The Turmeric Pool of Kollur

A Hidden Forest Jewel

Arishina Gundi Falls is located near Kollur in the Udupi district of Karnataka, nestled deep within the pristine evergreen forests of the Western Ghats. The name "Arishina Gundi" translates to "turmeric pit" in Kannada — referring to the golden-yellow hue the pool takes on after heavy rains when mineral-rich runoff flows into it.

Unlike the commercialized waterfalls of Karnataka, Arishina Gundi remains largely undiscovered by mainstream tourism, preserving its pristine character. Reaching the falls requires a moderate trek through dense forest, which naturally limits visitor numbers and helps maintain its ecological integrity.

Water Source & Location

The waterfall is fed by seasonal streams originating in the higher reaches of the Western Ghats. These streams are recharged by the heavy southwest monsoon rainfall (4,000-5,000 mm annually) that characterizes the coastal Karnataka region. The falls are part of the Someshwara Wildlife Sanctuary buffer zone.

Located approximately 12 km from Kollur (famous for the Mookambika Temple) and 40 km from Kundapur, the falls sit at an elevation of approximately 500 metres in the foothills of the Western Ghats. The surrounding forest is part of one of the world's eight biodiversity hotspots.

🌳 Forest Ecosystem

Western Ghats Wilderness

🌿

Tropical Evergreen

Dense canopy with trees reaching 40+ metres. Rich in endemic Dipterocarpaceae species, Myristica swamps, and rare medicinal plants. Epiphytes and climbers create multi-layered structure.

🐆

Large Carnivores

Leopards, sloth bears, and wild dogs (dholes) inhabit the forest. Tigers present but rarely seen. Important corridor connecting to Kudremukh National Park.

🐘

Herbivores

Gaur (Indian bison), sambar deer, barking deer, wild boar, and Indian giant squirrel. Lion-tailed macaque — one of world's rarest primates — occurs in nearby forests.

🦜

Endemic Birds

Over 200 bird species including Malabar trogon, great pied hornbill, Nilgiri wood pigeon, and Malabar grey hornbill. Important Bird Area designated by BirdLife International.

🦎

Reptile Diversity

King cobra, Malabar pit viper, Travancore tortoise, and numerous endemic frogs. The moist forest environment supports exceptional herpetofauna diversity including caecilians.

🦋

Butterfly Paradise

Over 250 butterfly species including endemic Malabar banded peacock, southern birdwing (India's largest), blue nawab, and Tamil lacewing. Riparian zones particularly rich.

🥾 Trek Route

Trail Visualization

1

Trailhead: Kollur Road

Begin at the designated parking area on the Kollur-Baindoor road. Look for the small forest department sign. The trail starts through areca nut plantations before entering forest.

📍 Distance: 0 km🕐 Start point
2

Forest Entry

Enter the dense evergreen forest. The trail becomes narrower with roots and rocks. Canopy closes overhead, creating cool shaded conditions. Watch for endemic birds.

📍 Distance: 0.3 km⏱️ 15 min
3

Stream Crossing

Cross a small perennial stream — the first sign of water. Rocks may be slippery, especially during monsoon. Good spot to refill water bottles with flowing stream water (filter recommended).

📍 Distance: 0.6 km⏱️ 30 min
4

Descent Section

Trail descends steeply through moss-covered rocks. Use rope supports where provided. The sound of falling water becomes audible. Most challenging section of the trek.

📍 Distance: 0.9 km⏱️ 45 min
5

Arishina Gundi Falls

Arrive at the hidden cascade plunging into the natural pool. Surrounded by moss-covered boulders and dense forest. The pool is swimmable during safe water levels. A magical hidden paradise.

📍 Distance: 1.0 km⏱️ 60 min
🌧️ Seasonal Behaviour

When to Visit

Peak Flow

Monsoon (Jun–Sep)

Waterfall at full glory with heavy flow. The "turmeric" golden hue is most pronounced. Forest is lush and green. Trekking trails very slippery with leeches. Swimming dangerous due to strong currents.

Best Time

Post-Monsoon (Oct–Jan)

Ideal visiting period with healthy flow and safe trekking conditions. Forest still green. Swimming safe in the pool. Clear weather with pleasant temperatures. Recommended season for most visitors.

Low Flow

Summer (Feb–May)

Water reduces to gentle stream. Pool remains but shallower. Trekking easiest but hot conditions. Less spectacular but peaceful experience. Some years may see complete drying.

🌍 Responsible Tourism

Visiting Ethically

✅ Do

  • Stay on marked trails only
  • Carry all waste back with you
  • Use reusable water bottles
  • Respect wildlife from a distance
  • Hire local guides when available
  • Use eco-friendly soaps if needed
  • Support local homestays and eateries
  • Photograph without disturbing nature

❌ Don't

  • Litter or leave any waste behind
  • Use plastic bottles or bags
  • Play loud music in the forest
  • Feed or approach wildlife
  • Pick plants, flowers, or seeds
  • Light fires or cigarettes
  • Carve names on rocks or trees
  • Use harsh soaps in the pool

⚠️ Safety

  • Never trek alone — go in groups
  • Inform someone of your trek plan
  • Check weather before visiting
  • Avoid swimming during monsoon
  • Carry first-aid and water
  • Watch for leeches (salt helps)
  • Turn back if conditions worsen
  • Respect forest department rules

🙏 Cultural Respect

  • Many forest areas are sacred to locals
  • Ask permission before photographing people
  • Dress modestly, especially near temples
  • Learn basic Kannada greetings
  • Support community-based tourism
  • Don't disturb ritual sites
  • Buy local handicrafts
  • Share positive experiences responsibly
📍 Nearby Attractions

Places to Explore

Kollur Mookambika Temple

Ancient temple dedicated to Goddess Mookambika, one of the most revered Shakti shrines in South India. Located on the banks of Souparnika River. Pilgrimage centre with daily rituals.

12 km away

Kudremukh National Park

UNESCO World Heritage Site known for rolling grasslands and shola forests. Named after "horse-face" shaped peak. Habitat for lion-tailed macaque and other endemics. Permit required.

60 km away

Maravanthe Beach

Pristine beach where the Souparnika River meets the Arabian Sea. Unique geography with river on one side, sea on other. Spectacular sunsets. NH66 runs alongside.

30 km away

Kodachadri Peak

Highest peak in coastal Karnataka at 1,343 metres. Famous for sunrise and sunset views. Trekking permitted with forest department guide. Shola forests and grasslands.

55 km away

Ottinene Temple

Cliff-top temple with panoramic views of the Arabian Sea. Dedicated to Lord Shiva. Beautiful sunset point. Less crowded alternative to other coastal attractions.

40 km away

Barkur Ancient Port

Historic port town with ruins of 12th-century temples and palace. Once capital of Alupa dynasty. Archaeological site showcasing coastal Karnataka's maritime heritage.

50 km away
