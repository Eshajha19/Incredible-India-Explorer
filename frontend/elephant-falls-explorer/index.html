Why "Elephant" Falls?

Two names, one waterfall

The story is usually told on a weathered signboard partway down the walk — reproduced here in spirit.

  • LocationUpper Shillong, East Khasi Hills district, Meghalaya — about 12 km from central Shillong, opposite Mattilang Park in Laitmynsaw, within the Eastern Air Command grounds.
  • Coordinates25.5379° N, 91.8221° E — roughly 1,600–1,900 m up on the Shillong Plateau.
  • Water sourceA monsoon-fed hill stream draining the Khasi Hills plateau, one of the wettest highland regions on earth, feeding the falls in three successive drops rather than one sheer plunge.
  • AccessA paved, railed stairway descends past all three tiers, with benches at the first two levels. Entry ₹20, camera permit ₹20, parking ₹20.

Trailside Notice

Local Khasi memory calls this fall Ka Kshaid Lai Pateng Khohsiew — the three-step waterfall — for the way the stream drops in three distinct stages down the hillside.

British officers stationed nearby renamed it "Elephant Falls" after a boulder beside the third tier that resembled an elephant. The rock was destroyed in the great earthquake of 12 June 1897 — but the colonial name outlasted the stone that inspired it.

The First Fall

The entry point of the cascade: wide rather than tall, with the stream spreading across a broad rock shelf that's largely screened from view by surrounding trees until you're close to the railing.

The Second Fall

A narrower, more modest drop connecting the first tier to the third. This is the stage that changes the most with the season — a strong ribbon in the monsoon, reduced to thin threads of water (or nearly dry) by winter.

Most seasonal of the three

The Third Fall

The final and tallest drop, where the stream sheets over dark, clinging rock in a near-milky curtain. This is the stage closest to where the elephant-shaped boulder once stood, and the one most visitors photograph.

Interactive · Seasonal Comparison

The same falls, two different moods

Guides disagree on a single "best" month because the falls reward different things at different times of year — raw volume in the monsoon, clarity and comfort in winter.

Monsoon · June to September

All three stages run at full volume, fed directly by rain over one of the wettest highland regions on earth. The second fall — thin or absent in the dry months — becomes a strong, continuous ribbon. Expect louder water, more mist, and slicker stone steps.

Relative water volume
Photo: Wikimedia Commons — full-volume cascade view

Winter · December to February

Water levels drop noticeably; the second tier can shrink to thin threads or near-stillness. In exchange, the air clears, skies stay bright, and the stone stairway is drier and easier underfoot — many visitors find this the more comfortable window for photography.

Setting

Forest, rock, and the plateau beneath it

Forest surrounding the East Khasi Hills · Wikimedia Commons

Vegetation

The stairway down to the falls passes through fern-draped rock and subtropical pine and broadleaf forest characteristic of the Khasi Hills — moss-slick boulders, hanging ferns, and, higher up the plateau, wild orchids typical of Meghalaya's cloud-forest belt.

Geological setting

The falls sit on the Shillong Plateau, a raised block of ancient crystalline and basaltic rock in the Khasi Hills. Centuries of monsoon-fed stream erosion cut the stream's path into three separate rock steps rather than one sheer drop.

A seismic footnote

On 12 June 1897, one of the most powerful earthquakes on record in the region struck the Shillong Plateau, reshaping hillsides across the Khasi Hills. Among the casualties was the elephant-shaped boulder beside the third fall — the very rock that gave these falls their English name.

Finding the falls

Elephant Falls sits in Upper Shillong, just off the Shillong–Nongthymmai road, roughly 12 km from the city centre and close to Shillong Peak.

Worth combining with your visit

Most visitors pair Elephant Falls with one or two nearby stops on the same day out from Shillong.

~10 km

Shillong Peak

The highest point in the area, with panoramic views over Shillong and the surrounding hills, plus an on-site Air Force Museum.

~12 km

Ward's Lake

A man-made lake in the city centre with a wooden footbridge and paddle boating — a calmer, flatter contrast to the falls.

~13 km

Lady Hydari Park

A landscaped garden with a small mini-zoo, popular for an easy walk after the falls' stairway.

~14 km

Don Bosco Centre for Indigenous Cultures

A museum devoted to the tribal cultures of Northeast India, including the Khasi communities who named the falls.

~25 km

Mawphlang Sacred Forest

A Khasi sacred grove protected by centuries of tradition — a living counterpart to the falls' own forest setting.

~15 km

Umiam Lake

A large reservoir popular for kayaking, boating, and lakeside picnics on the road toward Guwahati.
