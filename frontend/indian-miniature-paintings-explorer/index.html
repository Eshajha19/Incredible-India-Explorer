Historical overview

Small paintings, expansive worlds

Indian miniature painting describes a broad family of finely detailed paintings produced in the Indian subcontinent, often on paper or manuscript folios. The tradition developed across many courts, religious settings and regional workshops rather than following one single style.

Important traditions include Mughal, Rajasthani, Pahari and Deccani painting. They differ in palette, composition, subject matter and treatment of landscape, architecture and figures, while sharing an emphasis on controlled detail.

Miniature paintings recorded court life, literary stories, religious narratives, nature studies, portraits and historical events. Their small scale encouraged close viewing and rewarded careful observation.
