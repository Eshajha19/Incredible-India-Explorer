📍 Kashmir, India

Kashmiri Papier-mâché

A delicate decorative art form born from recycled paper and shaped into breathtakingly ornate boxes, trays, and vases.

Explore the Craft
—  History & Origins  —

The Journey to Kashmir

Papier-mâché was introduced to Kashmir in the 15th century by the Persian mystic Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani. Originally used to decorate book covers and create exquisite pen stands (Kalamdans), it soon evolved into a rich cultural tradition. The craft elegantly weaves together Persian, Central Asian, and local Kashmiri aesthetics.

Today, Kashmiri papier-mâché stands as a symbol of the valley's resilience and artistic brilliance. Its evolution from functional items to highly sought-after contemporary decor speaks to its timeless relevance and the profound cultural importance it holds in preserving Kashmiri heritage.

—  The Craft  —

Step-by-Step Making Process

1

Paper Preparation

Waste paper is soaked in water for several days until it breaks down into a soft, malleable state.

2

Pulp Formation (Sakhtsazi)

The soaked paper is pounded and mixed with an adhesive solution of rice flour and glue.

3

Moulding

The pulp is carefully applied over wooden or brass moulds and allowed to set.

4

Drying

The moulded forms are left to dry naturally under the sun, which hardens them into solid shapes.

5

Surface Preparation

The dried forms are smoothed using files and coated with a layer of gypsum (zamin) to create a flawless canvas.

6

Painting (Naqashi)

Master artists outline intricate patterns and fill them in with vibrant organic colors using fine brushes.

7

Detailed Decoration

Gold and silver foil highlights are added to emphasize the luxuriousness of the motifs.

8

Finishing

A final coat of lacquer is applied to seal the colors, adding durability and a glossy shine.

—  Design Elements  —

Decorative Motifs

🌺

Gul-e-Hazara (Thousand Flowers)

An intricate and dense floral pattern displaying a mesmerizing array of tiny, vibrant flowers.

🍃

Chinar Leaf

A deeply rooted Kashmiri symbol inspired by the majestic Chinar trees found throughout the valley.

🦜

Hazara and Birds

Traditional floral designs intermingled with beautiful birds like kingfishers or bulbuls.

⚜️

Persian Arabesque

Reflecting its Central Asian roots, these geometric and swirling motifs are timeless and elegant.

— Artisan Spotlight —

Syed Amjad Ali

📍 Srinagar, Kashmir

Master of Naqashi

Syed Amjad comes from a lineage of Papier-mâché craftsmen stretching back over five generations. Known for his exquisite 'Gul-e-Hazara' detailing, he continues to pass down the intricate painting techniques to young apprentices in his Srinagar workshop, ensuring the survival of this delicate art.
